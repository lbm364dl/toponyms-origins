# "Correspondencia con la Historia" - Metro de Madrid x Real Academia de la Historia

**Date of research:** 2026-03-25
**Program launch:** 16 April 2015
**Institutions:** Metro de Madrid + Real Academia de la Historia (RAH)
**Total stations announced:** 58
**Stations documented:** 55 confirmed (25 Phase 1 + 30 Phase 2); 3 remaining stations unconfirmed

---

## Program Overview

"Correspondencia con la Historia" (Correspondence with History) is a cultural collaboration between Metro de Madrid and the Real Academia de la Historia (Royal Academy of History). Launched on 16 April 2015, the program installed biographical vinyl panels in metro stations named after historical figures. Each panel provides a brief biography of the person the station is named after, along with a QR code linking to the full entry in the RAH's *Diccionario Biografico Espanol* (Spanish Biographical Dictionary) at https://dbe.rah.es/.

The program was announced with a target of 58 stations total and rolled out in phases:
- **Phase 1 (April 2015):** 25 stations received panels
- **Phase 2 (June 2016):** 30 additional stations (convention renewed at Ventura Rodriguez station on 3 June 2016)
- The remaining 3 stations to reach the announced total of 58 have not been clearly documented in available sources

Multiple sources confirm the program was announced with a target of 58 stations. With 25 (Phase 1) + 30 (Phase 2) = 55 confirmed, 3 additional stations remain unaccounted for. Based on cross-referencing sources, the following stations are strong candidates for the remaining 3, as they are consistently mentioned in the context of the "Correspondencia con la Historia" program and as stations with biographical panels:

| 57? | Garcia Noblejas | L7 | The five Garcia-Noblejas brothers | Early 20th c. | Falangist activists | -- | Five brothers who were Falangist activists against the Second Republic. Three died during the Spanish Civil War. The station's naming has been subject to historical memory debates. |

**Alternative candidates** (mentioned in program context but could also be part of the confirmed 55 above):
- Vicente Aleixandre (L6) - Vicente Pio Marcelino Cirilo Aleixandre y Merlo (1898-1984), poet, Nobel Prize in Literature 1977 (Q134644)
- Severo Ochoa (L12, as Hospital Severo Ochoa) - Severo Ochoa de Albornoz (1905-1993), biochemist, Nobel Prize in Physiology/Medicine 1959 (Q233957)
- Blasco Ibanez (ML1) - Vicente Blasco Ibanez (1867-1928), novelist, journalist, politician (Q219646)
- Antonio Saura (ML1) - Antonio Saura Atares (1930-1998), painter, writer, illustrator (Q528511)
- Menendez Pelayo (L1) - Marcelino Menendez y Pelayo (1856-1912), writer, philologist, literary critic (Q716799)
- Abrantes (L5) - References the Duke of Abrantes title, connected to the Lancastre/Alencastre noble family

**Note:** The discrepancy between 55 documented and 58 announced may also reflect that: (a) the Telemadrid article said "29 additional stations" + Ventura Rodriguez = 30 for Phase 2, but one earlier source mentioned the Phase 1 had "25 stations that already inform travelers" and 28 were originally planned for Phase 1; or (b) 3 stations were added in a later undocumented phase; or (c) the original announcement of "58 stations" was an aspiration that was implemented as 55.

## Academic Study

An academic paper analyzing the gender distribution of these historical figures was published:

- **Title:** "Personajes ilustres del Metro de Madrid: una aproximacion desde los estudios de genero"
- **Authors:** Sara Gallego Trijueque, Vanesa Cejudo Mejias, Carlos Oliva Maranon, Pilar Vicente-Fernandez
- **Source:** Available via Dialnet (https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=9949995) and UNIR repository (https://reunir.unir.net/handle/123456789/16067)
- **Key finding:** Out of 73 "personajes ilustres" (illustrious figures) with stations across the entire metro network, only 8 (10.95%) are women, while 89.05% are men. The study covered the entire 278-station network (at the time), not just the 58 "Correspondencia" stations.
- **Professions breakdown (from the study):** Politicians (23.29%), writers (13.70%), military (12.33%), saints (10.96%), aristocrats (6.85%), painters (5.47%), conquistadors/physicians/musicians/bishops (4.11% each), engineers (2.74%), urbanists/sportspersons/virgins/activists/actors/architects (1.37% each).
