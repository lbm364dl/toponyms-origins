## Tier 1: Genuinely Disputed -- Already Well-Sourced

These 7 stations have extensive scholarly sources. "Uncertain" is the correct confidence level because the etymology is genuinely debated among linguists.

### 1. Lavapies (metro_009)

**Theories:**
1. **Topographical** (favoured by Lorenzo Arribas, CVC 2008; Bazaco Palacios et al. 2013): Rainwater rushing down the steep barranco toward Embajadores literally washed pedestrians' feet
2. **Jewish fountain**: Jews washed feet before entering synagogue. Rejected by Urgorri (1954 census shows Jews lived dispersed, not in a ghetto)
3. **Posada/inn**: Travellers washed feet before sharing beds
4. **Underground springs** (Moratín, dismissed by Mesonero Romanos)

**Key sources:**
- Lorenzo Arribas, CVC 2008: cvc.cervantes.es/el_rinconete/anteriores/noviembre_08/21112008_01.htm
- Bazaco Palacios, Lorenzo Arribas & Real Gallego 2013 (academia.edu/4588123)
- Earliest document: 1441 land sale (Archivo de Villa); variants "Alavapies"/"Avapies" (1441-1498)

### 2. Carabanchel (metro_055)

**Theories:**
1. **Pre-Roman *kar-/*karav-** = "stone" (Menéndez Pidal 1953): "stony ground"
2. **Celtic anthroponym Caravantius** with diminutive Carabancilius (Caridad Arias 2004)
3. **Arabic "carab"** = cultivable land owner (Oliver Asín, CSIC)
4. **"Garbanzal"** = chickpea field (A.M. Canto)
5. **"Karavan"** = caravans for commerce

**Key sources:**
- Earliest document: 1 January 1181 (Archivo Catedral de Toledo, original parchment)
- Menéndez Pidal, Toponimia Prerromana Hispana 1953
- Caridad Arias, Anales IEM 44, 2004, pp. 821-830 (dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=3012173)
- Oliver Asín, Historia del nombre "Madrid" (CSIC 1959)

### 3. Barajas (metro_065)

**Theories:**
1. **"Bar Axa"**: Philip II's 1575 Relaciones record: "un moro llamado Baraja, hijo de una mora llamada Aja"
2. **Latin *varalia*** = fence of crossbars (Nieto Ballester 1997)
3. **Latin *uaralia*** = quarrel/litigation (Gutierre Tibón)
4. **Pre-Roman** pan-European toponym (Caridad Arias, Anales IEM XLVII, 2007, pp. 689-697)

**Key sources:**
- Philip II Relaciones topográficas 1575
- Covarrubias, Tesoro de la lengua 1611
- Nieto Ballester, Breve diccionario de topónimos españoles 1997
- Caridad Arias (Dialnet: dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/3017273.pdf)

### 4. Getafe (metro_235 + cercanias_019)

**Theories:**
1. **Arabic "jata"** = "something long" → Xatafi = "long street" along Madrid-Toledo road. Most widely cited. Documented in Philip II's 1575 Relaciones Topográficas
2. **Arabic "xenan/yinam"** = "huerta" (orchard). Jiménez de Gregorio, Anales IEM 44 (2004) pp. 799-820
3. **Basque substrate "gypsum quarry"**: *an igueltz* → *gueta be* → *getafe* (Goitia Blanco, eukele.com). Non-mainstream but geologically grounded (Cerro de los Angeles gypsum quarries documented)

García Sánchez (CVC Rinconete Part III, 2007): "presenta muchos problemas" -- "poco puede decirse de él sin dudar"

**Key sources:**
- García Sánchez, CVC Part III: cvc.cervantes.es/el_rinconete/anteriores/julio_07/09072007_01.asp
- Sánchez González, "De Alarnes a Getafe" (Ayto. Getafe, 1989)
- León Pinelo, Anales de Madrid (17th c.): BNE bdh.bne.es/bnesearch/detalle/bdh0000135757
- Relaciones Topográficas Felipe II (1575): museo.getafe.es/omeka/files/original/RelacionesTopograficasDeFelipeII
- Documented name forms: Satafi/Xatafi c.1150, Xeetafee 1249, Xetafe 1576, Gettafe 1714, Getafe 1776+

### 5. Coslada (cercanias_036)

**Theories:**
1. **Celtic "coslo/cosla"** = hazelnut → "hazelnut grove" (Menéndez Pidal 1952). Parallels: Villa Coslus, Villa Cosla (France), German Kusel
2. **Latin "cos" + "late"** = "abundant flint" (Aurelio García López 1998), based on historical flint quarries
3. **Latin "cova ustulata"** = "burnt cave" -- dismissed as "mera hipótesis" by García Sánchez (CVC 2008)

**Key new finding**: García Sánchez (De Gruyter 2010, "En torno a la toponimia madrileña") calls even the Celtic theory **"más discutible"** (more debatable). This is stronger skepticism than in his 2007 CVC article.

**Key sources:**
- Menéndez Pidal, Toponimia Prerrománica Hispana (Gredos 1952)
- García López et al., Coslada (Lunwerg 1998, ISBN 84-7782-563-7)
- García Sánchez, CVC Part IV: cvc.cervantes.es/el_rinconete/anteriores/enero_08/28012008_01.htm
- García Sánchez, "En torno a la toponimia madrileña" (De Gruyter 2010): academia.edu/10965316

### 6. Valdemoro (cercanias_024)

**Theories:**
1. **Latin VALLE + MAURA/MAURU** = "dark/dusky valley" (Toponomasticon Hispaniae): Vallemoro > Valmoro > Valdemoro
2. "Valley of the Moor(s)" from Reconquista context
3. Arabic "Wadi al-murr" (bitter river)
4. "Valley of blackberries" (Latin morum)
5. Personal name Maurus
6. Latin *murus* (wall/ruin) -- per Historia de Valdemoro blog

**Key sources:**
- Toponomasticon Hispaniae (toponhisp.org)
- García Sánchez, CVC Part IV (2008): cvc.cervantes.es/el_rinconete/anteriores/enero_08/28012008_01.htm
- Alfonso VIII documents; documented as "Vallem Maurii"

### 7. Meco (cercanias_043)

**Theories:**
1. **Celtic "miccon"** = sheep (Celtiberian settlement)
2. **Arabic "maksusk"** = bare mountain -- majority scholarly view per amiaire.net
3. **RAE "meco"** = blackish-red colour (but this is a Mexicanism from chichimeca -- unrelated)
4. The popular identification with Roman mansio **Miacum** is REJECTED by modern archaeology: Rodríguez Morales places Miacum at Collado Mediano (El Beneficio site)

**Key sources:**
- Quadernillos (quadernillos.com/el-origen-del-nombre-de-torrejon-meco-daganzo-y-camarma)
- Rodríguez Morales on Miacum (academia.edu; researchgate.net/publication/349297806)
- RAE (dle.rae.es/meco)
- Felipe II Relaciones 1579 (earliest written record for Meco specifically)

## Tier 2: Enriched with New Scholarly Sources

### 8. Aravaca (cercanias_061)

**Key finding**: Caridad Arias (2004) provides extensive scholarly argument that Aravaca is an **ethnic designation** from the Celtiberian people the **Arevaci/Aravaci**, NOT an agricultural reference.

**Evidence cited by Caridad Arias:**
- Pliny N.h. 3, 27: "Arevacis nomen dedit flumen Areva"
- St. Jerome confirms Arevacan groups settled in Carpetanian territory
- Roman cavalry units *Aravacorum* documented in CIL III 3271 (Dalya, Raab, Roma, Funfkirchen, Efeso)
- Celtic names sharing the *Arabo-* radical: Arabo (Narbona), Arabius (Tivoli), Aravia (Roma)

**Alternative**: Isabel Gea's "Ara Val Cea" (valley of Cea, personal name Ceia/Keia)
**Folk etymology**: "ara, vaca!" (plough, cow!) -- from Felipe II's 1575 Relaciones. Lacks linguistic support.
**First documented**: 1202 Fuero de Madrid (Sexmo de Aravaca); current form 1427

**Source**: Caridad Arias, Anales IEM 44, 2004, pp. 821-830 (dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=3012173)

### 9. La Garena (cercanias_040)

**Key finding**: The scholarly source for the Lucena family is **Diago Hernando (1993)** in *Sefarad* (CSIC journal).

**Etymology**: Almost certainly from Old French *garenne* (Medieval Latin *warenna*) = seigneurial game park/rabbit warren. The variant spelling "La Garcena" in the c.1495 document may be an intermediate form.

**Historical context**: The Lucena family were conversos (converted Jews). Their estate "La Garena" later became a meeting place for alumbrados (illuminists) -- documented in Archivo Histórico Nacional (leg. 111, exp. 14).

**Sources:**
- Diago Hernando, "El protonotario Lucena en su entorno sociopolítico" (Sefarad 53(2), 1993: sefarad.revistas.csic.es)
- Reymundo Tornero, Datos históricos de Alcalá de Henares (1950), p. 818

### 10. Opanel (metro_149)

**Key finding**: Villar Liébana's 2002 article on *up-/*op- hydronyms does **NOT** mention Opanel by name. The connection is typological -- Opanel would fit the pattern (Op- prefix for water-related toponym) but no scholar has explicitly made this argument.

**Confirmed facts:**
- Arroyo de Opanel appears on the Facundo Cañada López plano of Carabanchel (1900) -- digitized at CSIC: digital.csic.es/handle/10261/28971
- Madrid/Carabanchel falls in the voiceless *op- variant zone per Villar's geographic distribution
- Villar lists Opaca (Huesca) as a parallel form

**Source**: Villar Liébana, "Los hidrónimos con *up- (*op-) agua, río" (Palaeohispanica 2, 2002, pp. 277-291: dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=670065)

### 11. Valdecarros (metro_248)

**Two theories:**
1. **"Valle de Carros"** (Valley of Carts) -- traditional/popular etymology
2. **"Valle de Navarros"** (Valley of Navarrese settlers) -- scholarly alternative. Supported by:
   - Parallel toponym Valdecarros in Salamanca province (also repopulation-era)
   - Cluster of *Naharros/Narros* place names in the region
   - Phonetic evolution: Val-de-Navarros > Valdenarros > Valdecarros

**First documented**: 21 April 1296 (purchase and sale document)
**Pellicer y Saforcada (1803)**: discussed the Vallecas "val-" toponymic cluster

**Sources:**
- valdecarroscultural.tripod.com/historia.html
- es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdecarros_(Salamanca)
- Pellicer y Saforcada, Disertación sobre el origen... de Madrid (1803)
- Iron Age archaeology: Cerro de la Gavia site excavated 2011 (academia.edu/11957879)

## Tier 3: Genuinely Unknown

### 12-13. Pitis (metro_167 + cercanias_083)

**Status**: NO scholarly publication has EVER treated this toponym. This is confirmed after exhaustive searching across CVC Cervantes, Dialnet, all scholarly databases, Menéndez Pidal, García Sánchez, Nieto Ballester, and Aparisi Laporta references.

**Hypotheses:**
1. **Greek "pitys" (pine)**: Circumstantial support from the documented "Arroyo de los Pinos" in adjacent Peñagrande neighbourhood and pine forests of Monte de El Pardo. But no mechanism for Greek → Castilian rural toponym.
2. **Personal name/nickname of a landowner**: Common pattern in the Fuencarral area (cf. Colonia Lacoma). No "Pitis" surname found in genealogical databases.
3. **"Pitis" as small coin (Malay/Chinese origin)**: No plausible connection to Madrid.

**Area c.1960**: Described as having "una frondosa alameda" (leafy poplar grove) with a stream.

**Unsearched sources that may resolve this:**
1. **Aparisi Laporta, Toponimia madrileña (2001)** vol. 2 -- at BNE. 20,000+ toponyms.
2. **Alonso Fernández-Checa, Metro de Madrid (2023)** -- Amazon, ~15 EUR.
3. **Catastro de Ensenada for Fuencarral** (1749-1753) -- at PARES: pares.cultura.gob.es
4. **IGN historical planimetries** (1870-1950) -- ign.es/wms/minutas-cartograficas
