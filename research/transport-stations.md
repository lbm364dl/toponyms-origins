**Date:** 2026-03-25
**Searches conducted:** 80+ distinct web queries in Spanish and English (extensive deep research covering all Cercanias and Metro Ligero stations)

## 1. BOOKS (Dedicated to station name origins)

### 1a. "Metro de Madrid. ¿Por qué sus estaciones se llaman así?"
- **Author:** José Felipe Alonso Fernández-Checa
- **Publisher:** Ediciones La Librería (Madrid)
- **Year:** 2023
- **Pages:** 276
- **ISBN:** 978-84-9873-497-3
- **Price:** ~€16.50
- **URL (Amazon):** https://www.amazon.es/Madrid-estaciones-llaman-HISTORIAS-MADRID/dp/8498734975
- **URL (Publisher):** https://edicioneslalibreria.com/tienda/metro-de-madrid-por-que-sus-estaciones-se-llaman-asi/
- **URL (Tirant):** https://editorial.tirant.com/es/libro/metro-de-madrid-por-que-sus-estaciones-se-llaman-asi-9788498734973
- **Contents:** Analyzes the reason behind EACH of the 302+ station names, providing historical curiosities and documenting all name changes over time. Covers questions like why artists, writers, actors, and politicians have stations named after them.
- **Quality/Completeness:** THE most comprehensive single resource. Covers ALL Metro de Madrid stations. This is the definitive reference book on the topic.

### 1b. "El alma del suburbano madrileño. Un viaje por las estaciones de la etapa clásica (1919-1944)"
- **Publisher:** Comunidad de Madrid / Metro de Madrid
- **Year:** 2025 (published April 2025)
- **Pages:** 565
- **URL (Metro Madrid):** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/press-release/2025-04-16/the-community-of-madrid-compiles-a-book-on-the-history-and-development-of-the-first-42-metro-stations
- **URL (Comunidad de Madrid):** https://www.comunidad.madrid/noticias/2025/04/16/comunidad-madrid-recopila-libro-historia-evolucion-42-primeras-estaciones-metro
- **Digital companion:** https://museosmetromadrid.es/el-alma/ (partial digital content)
- **Contents:** History and evolution of the first 42 Metro stations (1919-1944 "classic period"). Includes maps, photographs, descriptions of locations and surrounding urban environment, and significant changes from inauguration to present. Covers Chamberí, Sol, Ventas, plus 2 workshops and 3 auxiliary buildings.
- **Quality/Completeness:** Very high quality, official publication. Limited to the first 42 stations only.

## 2. OFFICIAL CAMPAIGNS & INSTITUTIONAL RESOURCES

### 2a. "Correspondencia con la Historia" - Metro de Madrid x Real Academia de la Historia
- **URL (RAH):** https://www.rah.es/biografias-metromadrid/
- **URL (Metro press release):** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/press-release/2015-04-29/metro-starts-up-a-campaign-to-get-the-meaning-of-the-names-of-25-stations-in-the-network-known
- **Contents:** Biographical panels installed in 58 metro stations named after historical figures. QR codes link to full biographies from the Real Academia de la Historia's Diccionario Biográfico Español. Rolled out in phases: 25 stations in 2015, then 30+ more in 2016.
- **Format:** Physical panels in stations + web biographies on rah.es
- **Quality/Completeness:** HIGH for the 58 stations with "nombres propios" (proper names). Official collaboration with Spain's Royal Academy of History. Does not cover stations named after places/geography.

### 2b. Metro de Madrid - "Estación del Mes" (Station of the Month) series
- **URL (example - Vinateros):** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/news/the-station-of-the-month-vinateros
- **URL (example - Lavapiés):** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/news/the-station-of-the-month-lavapies-on-l3
- **URL (example - Arganda del Rey):** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/node/6476
- **Contents:** Monthly feature on individual stations, including name origin, history, and local context. E.g., Vinateros: named because wine from Arganda arrived by wagon along this road.
- **Format:** Web articles on metromadrid.es
- **Quality/Completeness:** MEDIUM. Individual articles are high quality but covers only a selection of stations over time.

### 2c. Metro de Madrid - "Plano Literario" (Literary Map)
- **URL:** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/news/find-out-the-different-names-of-the-underground-stations-on-the-new-literary-map

### 2d. Metro de Madrid - "Estaciones emblemáticas" (Emblematic Stations)
- **URL:** https://www.metromadrid.es/en/quienes-somos/estaciones-emblematicas

### 2e. Museos Metro Madrid - "Estaciones con historia"

### 3f. El Español - "Así es como se eligen los nombres de las estaciones del Metro de Madrid"

### Cercanías Station Name Origins (COMPREHENSIVE - 84 stations documented):
Now fully documented in `/data/madrid_cercanias_stations.csv` (84 stations with etymology). Key findings by etymology type:

**Arabic origins:** Alcalá (al-qala, fortress), Getafe (Xatafi/Jata, long), Alcorcón (al-kur, hill), Azuqueca (sukayqa, little road), Guadalajara (wad al-hayara, valley of stones), Alpedrete (al-pedrete, the stony place)

**Latin/Romance origins:** Cercedilla (quercetum, oak grove), Fuenlabrada (fuente labrada, hewn fountain), Leganés (légamo, mud), Las Rozas (roza, cleared farmland), Ciempozuelos (cien pozuelos, many small wells), Valdemoro (vallem + moro), Pinto (punctum, crossing point), Móstoles (monisterium, monasteries), Collado Mediano (collado + mediano, medium-sized pass)

**Personal names:** Méndez Álvaro (physician), Clara Campoamor (feminist politician), Paco de Lucía (flamenco guitarist), Ramón y Cajal (neuroscientist), Fernando VI (king, San Fernando), Justa Freire (pedagogue), Príncipe Pío (Francisco Pío de Saboya)

**Descriptive/Geographic:** Delicias (pleasant promenade), Pirámides (obelisks), Fuencarral (fountain for carts), Majadahonda (deep sheepfold), Torrelodones (tower + hackberry trees), Cuatro Vientos (windy location), Cantoblanco (white rock), Colmenar Viejo (old apiary)

**Debated/Uncertain:** Chamartín (Basque/French?), Aranjuez (Basque/pre-Roman/Latin?), Parla (Roman name/Latin speech/swamp?), Coslada (flint/hazelnut/cave?), Robledo de Chavela (oak grove + multiple theories for Chavela)

### Metro Ligero Station Name Origins (COMPREHENSIVE - 52 stations documented):
Now fully documented in `/data/madrid_metro_ligero_stations.csv` (52 stations across ML1, ML2, ML3, Tranvía de Parla). Key findings:

**ML1 (Pinar de Chamartín - Las Tablas):** Street-named stations honour Spanish cultural figures: Antonio Saura (painter), Pérez Villaamil (romantic painter), Vicente Blasco Ibáñez (novelist), María Tudor (queen), and Palas de Rey (Galician town, from Latin Palatium Regis).

**ML2 (Colonia Jardín - Aravaca):** European-themed naming in Pozuelo (Bélgica, Berna, Avenida de Europa), plus historical references (Dos Castillas, Somosaguas, Campus de Somosaguas).

**ML3 (Colonia Jardín - Puerta de Boadilla):** Cinema/audiovisual theme in Ciudad de la Imagen area (José Isbert the actor, Ciudad del Cine), plus Boadilla historical references (Infante Don Luis, Nuevo Mundo, Siglo XXI).

**Tranvía de Parla (15 stations):** Mix of historical monarchs (Reyes Católicos, Isabel II, Jaime I), artists (Julio Romero de Torres), modern tributes (Miguel Ángel Blanco, ETA victim), and astronomy theme in Parla Este (Sistema Solar, Tierra, Venus, Estrella Polar).

## 19. NAMING PROCESS & GOVERNANCE

### 19a. Who decides station names?
- **Authority:** Comunidad de Madrid (since 1995)
- **Process:** Consults affected municipalities, local associations, and community entities
- **Criteria:**
  1. **Environment:** Named after the surrounding area (street, plaza, barrio, landmark)
  2. **Personalities:** Named to honor notable figures (cultural, artistic, political, social)
  3. **Special considerations:** Victims of terrorism, gender violence, historical memory
- **Source:** https://www.elespanol.com/madrid/sociedad/como-eligen-nombres-estaciones-metro-madrid/714928859_0.html

### 19b. Historical naming authority
- 1917-1931: Compañía Metropolitano Alfonso XIII (private company)
- 1931-1939: Compañía Metropolitano de Madrid
- 1939-1975: Franco regime (renamed by decree)
- 1975-1989: Compañía Metropolitano de Madrid
- 1989-1995: Metro de Madrid, S.A.
- 1995-present: Comunidad de Madrid

## 21. ASSESSMENT & GAPS (updated after deep research)

### What exists:
- **Book 1a (Alonso Fernández-Checa, 2023)** is the single most comprehensive resource covering ALL 302+ Metro station name origins. It is a physical book (not freely available online), 276 pages, ISBN 978-84-9873-497-3.
- **"Correspondencia con la Historia"** (RAH + Metro) covers 58 stations named after historical figures with official biographies from the Diccionario Biográfico Español.
- **Metro de Madrid official podcast** (10 episodes) directly addresses station name origins in audio format.
- **"Revista Madrid Histórico"** has an ongoing article series on individual station histories (at least Diego de León and Concha Espina published in 2024).
- **Multiple web articles** cover 10-20 station name origins each (El Independiente, España Fascinante, Muy Interesante, Madrid Secreto, etc.).
- **Aurora Moya's histories** (1990, 2009) provide the most comprehensive narrative context, though not etymology-specific.
- **Antonio Martínez Moreno's monographs** on Line 1 (2019, 2021) provide HIGH-quality etymology for the earliest stations.
- **"El alma del suburbano madrileño" (2025)** -- 565 pages on the first 42 stations, available as FREE PDF from COAM.
- **Free PDFs confirmed** for at least 11 key resources (see Section 16).
- **Street name reference books** by Gea Ortigas, Répide, Rodríguez-Checa, Montero Vallejo, and especially Aparisi Laporta (20,000+ toponyms) provide upstream etymology for most metro station names.
- **Open data portals** (CRTM, Renfe, Comunidad de Madrid) provide comprehensive station LISTS with coordinates but NO etymology/origin data.
- **CRTM Chronology PDFs** document all station openings, name changes, and dates.
- **Comunidad de Madrid expansion publications** (14 books, 1994-2011) document new station construction, some available as free PDFs.

### Key gaps (updated after comprehensive Cercanías/Metro Ligero research):
1. **No single free, structured digital dataset** exists that maps station names to their origins/etymology. This project is now building that dataset.
2. ~~**Metro Ligero station name origins** are essentially undocumented.~~ **NOW RESOLVED**: 52 Metro Ligero stations (ML1, ML2, ML3, Tranvía de Parla) fully documented in `madrid_metro_ligero_stations.csv`.
3. ~~**Cercanías station name origins** have very limited coverage.~~ **NOW RESOLVED**: 84 Cercanías stations fully documented in `madrid_cercanias_stations.csv` with etymologies researched from multiple sources.
4. **The Wikidata "named after" (P138) property** for Madrid Metro stations is partially populated and could serve as a starting point for a structured dataset.
5. **The RAH biography pages** for the 58 "Correspondencia con la Historia" stations are dynamically loaded and could not be scraped in this research session.
6. **Naming governance documentation**: While we know the Comunidad de Madrid decides since 1995, no specific BOCM decrees for individual station naming decisions were found online. The Archivo General de Metro de Madrid may contain primary source records of naming decisions.
7. **The "Revista Madrid Histórico" article series** on individual stations appears to be ongoing but only two articles (Diego de León, Concha Espina) were confirmed; the full extent of the series is unclear.
8. **A few station etymologies remain uncertain**: Pitis, La Garena, and El Goloso lack documented etymology. Some like Chamartín, Aranjuez, Parla, and Coslada have multiple competing theories without scholarly consensus.
