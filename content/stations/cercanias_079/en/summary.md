The Cercanías station name is geographical: it refers directly to the Puerto de Navacerrada, the high pass between Madrid and Segovia. Renfe identifies the station as Puerto de Navacerrada on line C-9, in the municipality of Cercedilla, and its own centenary account places the station in service since 1923. The older name Navacerrada is not simply a transparent description of the pass: Toponomasticon Hispaniae argues that the village most likely gave its name to the pass and the river. The first element, nava, is an old and widespread Iberian landscape word for a flat, often damp open area, but its origin and exact local meaning are debated. The second element, cerrada, may refer to an enclosed pasture, surrounding hills, difficult access, or dense vegetation, so the safest public explanation should present those possibilities rather than a single definitive story.
