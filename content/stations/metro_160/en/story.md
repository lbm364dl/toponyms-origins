Parque de Santa María station takes its name from the Santa María area of Hortaleza. The clearest official statement comes from the Community of Madrid’s page on the Line 4 extension: the station “serves the Barrio de Santa María” and takes its name from that barrio. In administrative terms, the station is in Pinar del Rey, not in a separate official Madrid neighbourhood called Parque de Santa María.

The local name is older than the Metro stop. Architectural documentation for Luis Cubillo de Arteaga records a 1970 “conjunto de viviendas en el Barrio de Santa María” on the streets Santa Susana and Santa Virgilia, exactly the streets around the station. Local reporting also treats Parque de Santa María as a recognisable Hortaleza neighbourhood, with 1960s housing built by Construcciones Residenciales y Sociales.

The Metro arrived on 15 December 1998, when Line 4 was extended from Mar de Cristal through San Lorenzo to Parque de Santa María. The station was the northern terminus of the line until the later extension toward Pinar de Chamartín opened in 2007. Contemporary press described the opening as bringing Metro service to the barrios of San Lorenzo and Santa María.

The name’s religious element is real, but it is indirect for the station. The nearby Parroquia Santa María del Parque is listed by Madrid City Council at Calle Santa Virgilia 13, in Pinar del Rey/Hortaleza, and the regional transport map places it close to the Metro accesses. However, the sources found prove coexistence and local association, not that the parish created the neighbourhood name.

For public wording, the safest reading is therefore place-name first: the station was named after the local Santa María/Parque de Santa María area. The Marian dedication probably explains the deeper religious wording, but the exact naming chain between housing development, park/open space, and parish remains insufficiently documented without consulting planning files, parish foundation records, or COAM archive files.
