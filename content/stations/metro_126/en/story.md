The station name is first of all geographical: Barrio del Pilar is the Metro stop for the neighbourhood of the same everyday name in Fuencarral-El Pardo. Madrid City Council lists the official municipal neighbourhood as "Pilar", and the regional transport consortium places the station on Line 9 with access from Ginzo de Limia and Melchor Fernández Almagro.

Behind that place name is one of Madrid's major post-war housing developments. In the early 1960s the developer José Banús Masdeu promoted a large residential district here, using inexpensive land that local-history sources describe as formerly belonging to the Society of Jesus. Madripedia's historical summary says that by 1970 the development had more than 45,600 residents in 13,872 dwellings, many under 60 square metres.

The name "Pilar" is often assumed to refer directly to Our Lady of the Pillar, especially because the neighbourhood celebrates fiestas around 12 October. The stronger local explanation, however, is different: Banús named the development after his wife, Pilar Calvo y Sánchez de León. A 2023 El Español/Porfolio article gives this version explicitly, and official BOE documentation for the later Fundación José Banús Masdeu y Pilar Calvo y Sánchez de León confirms both her full name and that she was Banús's widow.

The transport chronology is also precise. The station opened on 3 June 1983 as part of the separate northern Line 9B section between Herrera Oria and Plaza de Castilla; that section was integrated into Line 9 on 24 February 1986. Contemporary press coverage of the opening described a station called "Pilar", probably as shorthand, while later transport chronologies and current CRTM information use "Barrio del Pilar".

For public display, the safest formulation is that the station is named after the neighbourhood, and that the neighbourhood was probably named for Pilar Calvo y Sánchez de León. The direct station etymology is therefore a place name, with a likely personal origin one step behind it. The claim is strong, but not quite archival-proof without a planning, sales, or municipal naming document from the original Banús development.
