Parque de las Avenidas station takes its name from the place around it: the Parque de las Avenidas area of La Guindalera, in Madrid's Salamanca district. The Metro entrance is listed by the transport authority on Avenida de Bruselas, while the City Council identifies the municipal Parque de las Avenidas nearby on Avenida de Brasilia. In everyday Madrid usage, the name refers not only to a single park but also to the residential district around those avenues.

The older story behind the name is a modern urban-development story. The Colegio Oficial de Arquitectos de Madrid describes Parque de las Avenidas as the most important urban nucleus created in La Guindalera after the 1954 limited-rent housing legislation, promoted by the Compañía Inmobiliaria Organizadora del Hogar, S.A. (CIOHSA). Its planning is dated to 1956, with buildings developed mainly from the late 1950s into the 1960s.

Before that development, this edge of La Guindalera was not a formal park in the modern sense. Secondary Madrid reference sources describe a strip of about forty hectares between the Abroñigal stream and the small Canalillo, with market gardens, flower growing, tile works and older paths. The difficult slope toward the old Abroñigal valley helped reserve parts of the scheme for green space, which later made the word "parque" feel natural in the area's name.

The "avenidas" part is also literal. The estate is organised around broad roads such as Avenida de Bruselas, Avenida de Bonn and Avenida de Brasilia, with other nearby streets and squares named after cities, many beginning with the letter B. This gives the toponym a planned, mid-century flavour: it is a neighbourhood named for its avenues as much as for its greenery.

The current dataset's reference to Cecilio Rodríguez and a 1928-1933 park is not supported by the stronger Madrid sources. Cecilio Rodríguez was a major Madrid gardener and landscape designer, especially associated with El Retiro and other municipal gardens, but the authoritative architectural accounts of Parque de las Avenidas name Francisco Echenique Gómez and Luis Calvo Huedo for the 1956 urbanisation and later buildings. The station name can therefore be treated as probably verified as a local place name, while the Cecilio Rodríguez chronology should be removed.
