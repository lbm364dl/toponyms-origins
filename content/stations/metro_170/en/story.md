La Elipa station is named after the surrounding La Elipa area, around Avenida del Marqués de Corbera in Madrid’s Ciudad Lineal district. The name was already attached to the place before Metro arrived: the Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists the station as La Elipa on Line 2, and Madrid municipal material treats La Elipa as the neighbourhood area served by the stop.

Behind the modern neighbourhood is an older rural toponym. The clearest accessible documentary trail points to an estate called Helipa or Elipa: an eighteenth-century biographical work by José Antonio Álvarez y Baena, citing a 1510 mayorazgo founded by Diego de Luján, lists “la heredad de la Helipa, hoy Elipa” among the Vargas-Luján family properties. That gives the name a much deeper history than the 1950s housing estate that later made La Elipa a recognizable urban district.

The traditional explanation says that the estate name came from Phelipa or Felipa de Vargas, wife of Miguel Ximénez de Luján, and that Phelipa gradually became La Elipa. This appears in local histories and in Madrid toponymy references, but the accessible record is not equally strong at every step: it supports the old Helipa/Elipa estate and its link to the Vargas-Luján patrimony, while the exact story of Miguel naming the land after his wife remains a well-established tradition rather than a directly inspected primary proof.

The present-day neighbourhood took shape mainly in the 1950s and 1960s. Madrid’s Plan de Barrio notes that the territory belonged in the early nineteenth century to Vicálvaro, which was annexed to Madrid in 1951, and that the modern La Elipa housing area grew from the 1957 partial plan connected with social-housing policy. In other words, the station name compresses two histories: a medieval or early-modern rural estate name and a twentieth-century working-class neighbourhood.

Metro reached La Elipa on 16 February 2007, after a long local campaign for a station. It was the eastern terminus of Line 2 until 16 March 2011, when the line was extended toward Las Rosas. For public display, the safest wording is that the station is named for the La Elipa area, whose older name is probably connected with Phelipa de Vargas; calling that personal origin fully verified would go beyond the evidence inspected here.
