The station’s immediate name source is the La Elipa area, not a station-specific dedication. La Elipa was already an old rural estate name before the twentieth-century housing district, and the official historical Callejero records Calle del Arroyo de la Elipa from 1950: a 1510 mayorazgo reference cited by Álvarez y Baena mentions the estate as “Helipa, today Elipa.” Later local and toponymic tradition connects the name with Phelipa or Felipa de Vargas, wife of Miguel Ximénez de Luján, but the accessible evidence is stronger for the existence of the old Helipa/Elipa estate than for the exact naming act. The modern metro station opened on 16 February 2007 after long neighbourhood campaigns and served as Line 2’s terminus until the extension to Las Rosas opened on 16 March 2011.
