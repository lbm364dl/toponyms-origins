The station name is first of all geographic: Collado Mediano is the municipality served by the railway stop. Renfe places the station on the Villalba-Segovia railway and records that it entered service on 1 July 1888, when the Villalba-Segovia section of the old Villalba-Segovia-Medina del Campo line opened. The present Cercanias name therefore preserves the name of the town rather than commemorating a person or event.

The older name behind the station is a landscape word. The Real Academia Espanola derives collado from Latin collis, meaning a hill or height, and defines it as either land rising like a small hill or a gentle depression that allows passage across a mountain range. In a Guadarrama village, both senses matter: Collado Mediano sits in a mountain environment of slopes, dehesas, passes and routes between valleys.

The adjective mediano is less securely explained in historical sources. In ordinary Spanish it means intermediate or moderate, neither very large nor very small. A cautious public interpretation is therefore 'the middle, moderate or modest collado', rather than a precise technical label. Some local educational and secondary accounts connect the name with the pass between the Guadarrama and Manzanares basins, but the available sources did not include an authoritative primary naming act that fixes that explanation.

The town's history gives the name its setting. Local and regional accounts place Collado Mediano in the long medieval dispute between Segovia and Madrid over the Sierra de Guadarrama and in the later jurisdiction of the Real de Manzanares. The municipal history pages also stress livestock, sheep farming and drove roads as part of the local economy, which helps explain why pastoral readings of the name are attractive even when they are not fully proven as etymology.

The railway added a new layer without changing the name. The old stone station became one of the features of the elongated townscape along Calle Real, and municipal planning documents describe the railway's arrival in 1888 as a turning point that connected the town more closely with Madrid and helped the later summer-resort transformation of the sierra. The station name is therefore well verified as a place-name, while the exact nuance of Mediano and the livestock-enclosure theory remain matters for cautious wording.
