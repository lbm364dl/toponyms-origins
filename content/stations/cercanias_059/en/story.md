La Serna-Fuenlabrada is named for the place it serves: La Serna, a neighbourhood in the northern part of Fuenlabrada, near the boundary with Leganés. Official transport sources confirm both sides of the name: CRTM lists the stop as La Serna on line C-5, while Renfe's station sheet records the full name LA SERNA-FUENLABRADA, in the municipality of Fuenlabrada.

The older name behind the station is the agricultural word serna. In modern Spanish it is no longer an everyday term, but the RAE defines it as a portion of sown land. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains that the word survives widely in place-names across Castilian and Galician-Portuguese areas, often as La Serna, Las Sernas, Serna, or compounds with a second element to distinguish one place from another.

The deeper etymology is attractive but not completely simple. The RAE gives serna as Celtic *senara, a field worked apart, from elements meaning apart and plough. Toponomasticon Hispaniae is more cautious: it classifies *SĔNĂRA as pre-Roman, says a Celtic derivation has been proposed, and notes that the term's exact original sense is difficult to pin down because the explanation may partly depend on later definitions of the word.

For the station itself, the name belongs to the growth of Cercanías in the metropolitan south of Madrid. A contemporary El País report of 27 April 1983 says the transport minister had inaugurated the La Serna-Fuenlabrada halt, located at kilometre 17.200 of the Madrid-Cáceres line, and that Fuenlabrada's municipal corporation financed half of its 58-million-peseta cost. A municipal mobility diagnosis later describes La Serna as a halt in the neighbourhood of the same name, at the north of the municipality.

The safest public explanation is therefore local and layered: the stop is named after the La Serna neighbourhood, and that neighbourhood preserves an old rural word for sown or separately worked land. The input row is essentially correct, but it should avoid presenting a Vulgar Latin stage as certain and should flag the Celtic reconstruction as proposed rather than beyond dispute. A claimed earlier station name, Fuenlabrada Apeadero, appears in secondary material, but the 1983 press report already uses La Serna-Fuenlabrada, so that former-name claim remains unverified.
