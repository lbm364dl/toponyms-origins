The station name points first to Avenida de la Gavia, beside the Line 1 stop in the Ensanche de Vallecas, and then to the older Arroyo de La Gavia. Archaeological and municipal sources describe several local names in southeast Madrid as deriving from that stream, whose valley runs toward the Manzanares. In Spanish, gavia can mean a ditch opened in the ground for drainage or property boundaries, from Latin cavea. The same toponym also names the Cerro de La Gavia, a major archaeological site with Paleolithic material in the valley and a fortified Second Iron Age settlement above the lower Manzanares.
