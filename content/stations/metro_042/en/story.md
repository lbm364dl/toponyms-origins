Menéndez Pelayo is a station name borrowed from the city around it. The platforms lie under Avenida de la Ciudad de Barcelona, close to Avenida de Menéndez Pelayo, and the official transport access list still identifies exits on both even and odd sides of that avenue. In public use, the station therefore carries the name of the nearby street rather than the full biographical name of the man.

The older name behind the station is that of Marcelino Menéndez y Pelayo, born in Santander in 1856 and dead there in 1912. He was a philologist, critic, historian of literature and historian of ideas, remembered for works such as *Historia de los heterodoxos españoles* and *Historia de las ideas estéticas en España*. His name is often written with the conjunction y in biographies, while Madrid's avenue and Metro station normally shorten it to Menéndez Pelayo.

Metro's own historical file places the station in the second southern extension of Line 1, the Atocha-Puente de Vallecas section, opened to passengers on 8 May 1923. It also records the station's early urban setting: under the then Calle del Pacífico, now Avenida de la Ciudad de Barcelona, between Gutenberg and Menéndez Pelayo. The same file notes that the station was later lengthened in 1961, when Line 1 platforms were extended from 60 to 90 metres.

The avenue itself preserves a layer of Madrid street history. According to Madrid street-name tradition recorded by María Isabel Gea and reused by Madripedia, different sections had earlier local names: Calle de Muñoz near Alcalá and O'Donnell, Ronda de Vicálvaro farther south, and Ronda de Vallecas near the old road toward Vallecas. In 1915, those older references were gathered under the commemorative name Avenida de Menéndez Pelayo.

The etymology is well supported, but one common summary detail needs care: the avenue borders the eastern side of Retiro park, not the western side. It also runs from Calle de Alcalá to Avenida de la Ciudad de Barcelona, passing several Retiro and Salamanca neighbourhood contexts rather than simply connecting Atocha with Ibiza. The station name, however, has remained stable since its 1923 opening.
