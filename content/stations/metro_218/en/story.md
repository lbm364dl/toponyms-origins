San Francisco station is named for the San Francisco area of Carabanchel, not for a documented station-side street of that name. Madrid's own district mapping labels a **Colonia de San Francisco** in the area around Puerta Bonita and the station, and press coverage of the 2006 Line 11 extension described the new stops as serving the Colonia de la Prensa, San Francisco, Carabanchel Alto and the PAU of Carabanchel.

The deeper name behind the place is probably **Saint Francis of Assisi**. Francis, born in Assisi in 1181 or 1182 and dead there in 1226, became one of Catholicism's most venerated medieval figures and is known as the founder of the Franciscan religious orders. That makes the name religious in its ultimate reference, but the station's immediate naming layer is local geography.

The surrounding urban fabric helps explain why a neighbourhood name mattered for the Metro. Nearby, the older Colonia de la Prensa was laid out between Carabanchel Bajo and Carabanchel Alto in the early twentieth century for writers and journalists, with garden houses and a distinctive residential identity. San Francisco sits in the same Carabanchel landscape of colonies, older village centres and later extensions.

The transport chronology is important because the existing dataset date is wrong. San Francisco did not open in 2010; a Comunidad de Madrid Line 11 study lists the San Francisco-Carabanchel Alto-La Peseta section as opened in 2006, while the 2010 opening corresponds to the later La Fortuna extension. Contemporary reports give the opening day as 18 December 2006.

The safest public explanation is therefore: the station was named after the San Francisco locality or Colonia de San Francisco in Carabanchel, whose name probably refers to Saint Francis of Assisi. I found no strong evidence for the more specific claim that the Metro name came from a Calle de San Francisco, nor for a medieval Franciscan presence in Carabanchel being the reason for this station name.
