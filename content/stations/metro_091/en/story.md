The metro station is named after Plaza de Santo Domingo, the square at its original western access. Metro de Madrid's historical station sheet places the station on the first north-western extension of Line 2, opened on 21 October 1925 between Sol and Quevedo, and notes that the original access was planned at Plaza de Santo Domingo.

The square's name reaches back to the vanished monastery of Santo Domingo el Real. The convent stood in this part of the old Barrio de Palacio until its demolition in 1869, and the open space in front of it became the Santo Domingo place-name used by the square, the cuesta and finally the metro station.

The medieval origin is important, but it needs careful wording. A municipal heritage note describes the site as an early monastery of nuns and says it began as a house of friars before becoming a women's convent very soon afterward. Dominican and academic accounts agree on the link with Saint Dominic of Guzmán's passage through Madrid around 1219, but they vary in the details: some speak of a foundation in 1218, others of 1219, and the Dominican centenary page distinguishes the existing group in 1219 from enclosure in a proper building in 1220.

Santo Domingo el Real was not a minor chapel. Studies of its medieval heritage describe it as a powerful religious and aristocratic institution, tied to royal patronage, burials and major works of art. Its presence also helped form an arrabal, an early suburb, at the north-western edge of medieval Madrid; that urban memory survived even after the building disappeared.

The convent's end came in the political climate after the 1868 revolution. The old complex was demolished in 1869, causing the loss of significant medieval and Renaissance fabric, while the Dominican community later continued in a new Santo Domingo el Real on Calle de Claudio Coello. For the station name, the strongest evidence is therefore straightforward: the metro stop inherits a long-lived urban toponym from the square, and the square preserves the name of the former Dominican monastery.
