The station name is best explained as a place name inherited from the Atocha area of Madrid, not as a modern commemorative name. In Spanish, atocha means esparto grass, and RAE/ASALE derives it from Mozarabic and Hispano-Arabic aṭṭáwča, ultimately from a pre-Roman *taucia meaning thicket or shrubland. Corominas records the Madrid place-name forms Toia in 1202 and Tocha in 1219, connecting the word with western Iberian forms such as Galician-Portuguese touza. The transport name has shifted: the Metro stop formerly called Atocha Renfe was announced as simply Atocha on 19 December 2021 because Renfe was no longer the only rail operator at the interchange.
