Boadilla Centro takes its immediate name from where the stop is. Metro Ligero Oeste places it on the ML3 line beside the Majadahonda road, the M-516, close to the streets Alberca and José Antonio, and very near Boadilla del Monte’s commercial area, old town, and the El Palacio and Zoco shopping centres.

The older name behind the station is Boadilla del Monte. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats Boadilla as part of a broad family of forms such as Boadilla, Bovadilla and Bobadilla. Its specialist entry does not choose one single etymology: it says the name may come from Germanic *BÛWITHA, connected with vaults or arched structures and possibly extended to landscape description, or from Latin-Romance *BŎVĀTA, meaning a dehesa boyal or ox-grazing pasture.

The second half of the municipal name is less contested. Toponomasticon states that del Monte was added formally to avoid confusion with other places called Boadilla and that it refers to the montuous terrain where the town stands. It also notes that the Catastro de Ensenada still recorded the place in 1751 simply as Boadilla, while Sebastián de Miñano’s 1829 dictionary already used Boadilla del Monte.

The town’s history gives the name a strong local frame. Toponomasticon says Alfonso VIII incorporated the place into Madrid’s territory in 1208, while the municipal history book by José Montero Padilla cites 1208 documents with forms such as Bovadella and Bobadilla. In the eighteenth century the Infante Luis Antonio de Borbón acquired the lordship in 1761 and commissioned Ventura Rodríguez to transform the old palace complex into the Palacio del Infante Don Luis, one of the landmarks reached from the centre and nearby ML3 stops.

For the station itself, the evidence is straightforward: Boadilla Centro is a modern descriptive stop name, in service from the 2007 opening of Metro Ligero Oeste. The deeper etymology of Boadilla remains probable rather than fully settled, because the best linguistic source deliberately preserves two possible roots. The main correction is chronological: the input’s reference to the 1222 Fuero de Madrid should not be presented as the earliest documentation when 1208 forms are cited in the municipal history and Toponomasticon gives its own documentary sequence.
