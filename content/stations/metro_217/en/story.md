Carabanchel Alto station is named for the place around it: the historic Carabanchel Alto, today part of Madrid's Carabanchel district. The adjective 'Alto' is not decorative; it distinguishes the upper settlement from Carabanchel Bajo, the lower Carabanchel closer to old Madrid.

Older sources and local histories preserve the paired names Carabanchel de Arriba and Carabanchel de Abajo, or Carabanchel de Suso and de Yuso. A local historical account places the distinction around the churches of San Pedro and San Sebastián and gives a practical explanation: the Alto nucleus stood higher above sea level than the Bajo nucleus.

Carabanchel Alto was not always just a Madrid neighbourhood. The municipal archive page of Madrid City Council describes the former Ayuntamiento de Carabanchel Alto as part of the jurisdiction of the Villa y Tierra de Madrid during the Ancien Régime, with its own municipal documentation from the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. The decisive administrative change came with the decree of 9 January 1948 approving the annexation of Carabanchel Alto and Carabanchel Bajo to Madrid.

The Metro name arrived much later. The Carabanchel Alto station opened on Line 11 on 18 December 2006, when the line was extended through San Francisco, Carabanchel Alto and La Peseta. Contemporary press described the opening as the answer to a long neighbourhood demand for a Metro connection to the historic centre of Carabanchel Alto and the new PAU of Carabanchel.

The safest etymology for the station is therefore simple and well supported: it is named after the historic upper Carabanchel. The older word 'Carabanchel' is more contested. Joaquín Caridad Arias argued for a Celtic or pre-Roman personal-name base, while local summaries preserve other theories, including Oliver Asín's 'carab' landholder explanation, caravan-related folk etymology, and stony-ground or garbanzo associations. The former Carabanchel prison is essential to the area's twentieth-century memory, but it did not give the station its name.
