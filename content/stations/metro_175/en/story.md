The station name is local and literal: **Barrio del Puerto** is the Coslada neighbourhood served by this Line 7 stop. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes places the station at Avenida de España, 8, with its ticket hall also named Barrio del Puerto, confirming that the metro name follows the surrounding urban area.

The interesting part is the word **Puerto**. Coslada is inland, but the neighbourhood's name refers to the **Puerto Seco de Madrid-Coslada**, a dry port: a rail-linked logistics terminal where maritime freight can be transferred, processed and moved without being physically on the coast. In 1998, when Coslada was discussing a new neighbourhood connected with rehousing plans, El Pais reported that the destination would be the new Barrio del Puerto and that it would owe its name to the Puerto Seco to be installed in Coslada.

The dry port project belongs to the logistics transformation of Coslada in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries. A 2002 report traced the project back to 1995 and noted that it began operating in March 2001, when the Coslada terminal could connect with the ports of Valencia, Bilbao, Algeciras and Barcelona. The Port of Barcelona's current information still describes Puerto Seco de Madrid-Coslada as linked with Algeciras, Barcelona, Bilbao and Valencia and designed to move goods between Madrid and international maritime markets.

The neighbourhood reinforces that maritime theme in its urban language. The local residents' association describes a young neighbourhood with a 'Paseo de los descubridores', sculptures of explorers, references to oceans and nearby green space at El Humedal. The result is a deliberately nautical identity in a landlocked municipality whose real 'port' is logistical rather than coastal.

For the station itself, the naming date is best tied to the opening of MetroEste on **5 May 2007**, when the extended Line 7 began carrying passengers between Madrid, Coslada and San Fernando de Henares. The origin is well supported, but the safest public wording is that the neighbourhood was named for the Puerto Seco and that the station was then named for the neighbourhood.
