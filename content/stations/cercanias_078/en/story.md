Cercedilla station takes its name directly from the municipality of Cercedilla, in the Sierra de Guadarrama northwest of Madrid. Renfe identifies the station as serving Cercedilla and as part of the C-8 and C-9 Cercanías network, with the mountain railway to Navacerrada and Cotos beginning here.

The deeper origin of the place-name lies not in the railway but in the older settlement name. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains Cercedilla as a diminutive toponym formed from nearby Cerceda, only about 13 km away. In plain terms, Cercedilla can be read as a smaller or derived Cerceda, comparable to other Madrid-region pairs such as Villalba/Villalbilla or Valdemoro/Valdemorillo.

Cerceda itself is part of a wider group of Iberian place-names connected with oak woodland. The proposed linguistic chain goes back to forms related to Latin quercus, 'oak', and quercetum or comparable collective formations meaning an oak grove or wooded place. That makes the station name a modern railway label attached to a much older landscape word.

Historical spellings show the name already well established in the early modern period. Toponomasticon Hispaniae cites documents with forms such as Zerezedilla in 1544, Cerecedilla in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, and Cercedilla in later records. The same source notes the settlement's position near the old route over the Fuenfría pass, a corridor whose importance declined after the Navacerrada pass route was improved.

The railway added a second layer to the name. Cercedilla station was built in the late nineteenth or early twentieth century on the Madrid-Segovia railway, and in 1923 it became the starting point of the Ferrocarril Eléctrico del Guadarrama, today associated with line C-9. The etymology is strong, but the exact human story behind the diminutive, such as whether people from Cerceda founded a seasonal or semi-permanent settlement, remains an informed reconstruction rather than a directly documented fact.
