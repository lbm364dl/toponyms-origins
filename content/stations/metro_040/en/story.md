Ibiza station takes its name from Calle de Ibiza, the street beneath which the Line 9 platforms are located in Madrid's Retiro district. This is a local street-name transfer rather than a direct reference to the island by Metro itself: the station inherited the established name of its urban setting.

Behind the street name is Ibiza, or Eivissa in Catalan and in the island's official local form, one of the Balearic Islands in the western Mediterranean. In Madrid, the name appears as part of a small Balearic echo in the Retiro street grid: Calle de Ibiza and Calle de Menorca remain current streets, and historical callejero references indicate that today's Calle del Doctor Castelo was once Calle de Mallorca.

The neighbourhood's layout belongs to the broader nineteenth-century expansion of Madrid. Carlos María de Castro's Ensanche plan was approved by Royal Decree on 19 July 1860 and shaped new districts beyond the old city, including Retiro, with a more regular street pattern. That planning context explains the grid, but the evidence found does not prove that Castro's 1860 plan itself assigned the name Ibiza; the specific street-name date should be treated separately.

The Metro station arrived much later, when Line 9's missing central link was built between Avenida de América and Sainz de Baranda. The Community of Madrid's transport history describes this link as opening in February 1986 with three intermediate stations, while newspaper references give 24 February and one official regional page gives 25 February. Either way, the station name appears to have been fixed from opening because it matched the street above.

The strongest reading is therefore simple and place-based: Ibiza station is named after Calle de Ibiza, which is named after the Balearic island. The surrounding claim about a wider Balearic cluster is plausible if limited to Ibiza, Menorca, and the former Mallorca name; it should not include Formentera or Cabrera in this Retiro cluster unless a future archival source proves it.
