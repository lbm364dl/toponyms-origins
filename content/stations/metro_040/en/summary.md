Ibiza station is a straightforward place-name: Metro de Madrid named it after Calle de Ibiza, its immediate street location. The street is dedicated to Ibiza, one of the Balearic Islands, and sits in Madrid's Retiro district within the administrative barrio also called Ibiza. The surrounding urban fabric belongs to the nineteenth-century Ensanche planned by Carlos María de Castro, approved in 1860, although the street names themselves should not be presented as created by the plan without a municipal naming record. The official historical Callejero records Calle de Ibiza as active from 21 July 1880, and the name is often associated with nearby Calle de Menorca and the former Calle de Mallorca, now Calle del Doctor Castelo. The station opened with the central Line 9 link between Avenida de América and Sainz de Baranda in February 1986, with official and press references differing by one day.
