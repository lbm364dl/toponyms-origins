Canillas station takes its name from the barrio of Canillas, in the district of Hortaleza. The link is unusually direct: Memoria de Madrid, using material from Metro de Madrid's photographic archive, says the station opened on 27 April 1998 and "receives its name" from the barrio where it is located. In transport terms, the name arrived with the Line 4 extension from Esperanza to Mar de Cristal, which brought two new stations, Canillas and Mar de Cristal, to this part of northeast Madrid.

Behind the modern barrio is the older village and municipality of Canillas. The Community of Madrid's heritage record for the Ermita de San Blas places Canillas among the settlements that grew around Madrid after the conquest of Toledo by Alfonso VI in 1085 and the repopulation of the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. The oldest documentary reference it gives for the aldea of Canillas is a 1252 land-sale deed. Its early nucleus was around the present Ermita de San Blas, once the parish church of San Juan Bautista.

The sixteenth-century picture is of a small, poor agrarian settlement. Official heritage documentation says the Relaciones of Felipe II counted 50 vecinos, about 200 inhabitants, and described an economy based mainly on agriculture, some livestock, bread-making for Madrid residents, and laundry work. In the seventeenth century Canillas suffered heavy depopulation, then became a seigneurial holding: in 1680 Baltasar de Molinet Jijón bought its jurisdiction, lordship and vassalage, and in 1689 Charles II granted him the title of Count of Canillas.

Canillas survived into the modern period as an independent municipality on Madrid's edge. The municipal archive kept by the Archivo de Villa records documentation from 1857 to 1950 and notes that Canillas formed part of the jurisdiction of the Villa y Tierra de Madrid under the Old Regime. By the time of annexation it was no longer just a small village: municipal sources list 20,412 inhabitants spread between the villa and outlying barriadas such as La Concepción, Ciudad Lineal, San Pascual and Ventas.

The annexation story needs two dates. The Ministry of the Interior decree approving the annexation of Canillas to Madrid was dated 17 August 1949, but the incorporation was formalized on 30 March 1950. The station name, assigned when the 1998 Metro station opened, therefore preserves a medieval place-name through the modern administrative barrio. What is verified is the station's naming after the barrio and former municipality; what remains less secure is the ultimate linguistic origin of the word Canillas, with possible explanations requiring specialist toponymic confirmation.
