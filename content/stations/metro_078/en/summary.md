Usera station opened on Line 6 with the Pacífico-Oporto extension in 1981 and was named for the surrounding district. The district name comes from the older Usera neighbourhood, whose development was associated with Marcelo de Usera y Sánchez. According to the Real Academia de la Historia, Usera was born in Madrid in 1874, served in the army, reached the rank of lieutenant colonel, and later became active in urban development, agriculture, and livestock. The land behind the neighbourhood came through his wife, Carmen del Río Fernández, daughter of José del Río, the Tío Sordillo. Between about 1925 and 1930 those lands were parcelled and sold, and many local streets acquired names from the Usera family and its circle.
