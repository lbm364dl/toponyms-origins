The modern interchange was once the Estación del Norte, named for the railway company and line that linked Madrid with northern Spain and the French border. In December 1994, during the remodeling that connected the old Norte station with Metro lines 6 and 10, the station was renamed Príncipe Pío. The name points to the historic Príncipe Pío estate and Montaña del Príncipe Pío, associated with Francisco Pío de Saboya y Moura (1672-1723), Prince of San Gregorio and Marquess of Castel-Rodrigo. The hill later became famous as one of the execution sites after the uprising of 2 May 1808, remembered in Goya's painting of the 3 May shootings. The etymology is therefore personal in origin, but it reaches the station through a long-established Madrid place name.
