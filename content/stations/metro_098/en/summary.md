The Metro station name comes from the Plaza del Conde de Casal, beside the Line 6 station. Madrid's official street data gives the square's name as active from 29 September 1954, shortly after the death of Manuel Escrivá de Romaní y de la Quintana. The Instituto de Estudios Madrileños identifies him as the XII Count of Casal and records that the name replaced the unofficial Plaza del Marqués de Cerralbo. Escrivá de Romaní was a senator for Toledo, a Madrid municipal councillor and deputy mayor, a member of cultural academies, and a notable scholar and collector of ceramics. The station itself entered service on 11 October 1979 with the first Cuatro Caminos-Pacífico section of Line 6.
