The station name is a straightforward transfer from the municipality of Robledo de Chavela. Toponomasticon Hispaniae derives Robledo from Latin roboretum, an oak grove, while Chavela is explained as a compound personal name, Echa Vela. Echa is treated as a medieval form ultimately related to Basque aita, and Vela as a medieval name of Germanic origin. Historical spellings such as Robredo de Chavela and Robredo de Enchauela show both the older robredo form and the stability of the second element by the fourteenth century. Local tradition preserves other explanations, including 'ocho en vela', Isabel la Catolica, Latin/romance 'house of Vela', or Arabic djebel, but the Toponomasticon analysis is the strongest published source found.
