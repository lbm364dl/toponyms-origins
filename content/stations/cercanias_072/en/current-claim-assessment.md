The core claim is well supported: the station takes its name from the municipality, and Toponomasticon Hispaniae gives Robledo as an oak-grove term and Chavela as an anthroponymic compound from Echa + Vela. The wording should be tightened: Toponomasticon explains Aita/Echa as an affectionate form meaning 'little father' or 'daddy', not simply 'father/chief', and the rival theories are municipal or older local explanations rather than equally supported scholarly alternatives.
