The Madrid Metro station name is local before it is historical: **Sierra de Guadalupe** takes its public identity from the street and interchange area around Calle de la Sierra de Guadalupe in Villa de Vallecas. The official Madrid historical Callejero records the street in that district from 28 May 1952, and regional transport documentation for the Line 1 extension identifies Sierra de Guadalupe as one of the three new stations built on the Miguel Hernández-Congosto section.

Behind the Madrid street name is a mountain toponym from Extremadura. The Sierra de Guadalupe is commonly associated with the Sierra de las Villuercas, in the province of Cáceres, a rugged landscape around the town of Guadalupe. In public use, the name evokes not only a mountain range but also the historic devotional geography of Guadalupe.

That association became famous through the Royal Monastery of Santa María de Guadalupe. Official tourism and UNESCO sources place the monastery in Cáceres and connect it with King Alfonso XI, the 14th century, the Battle of Salado in 1340, pilgrimage, and a long architectural history. The traditional story of the Virgin's image and the shepherd Gil Cordero belongs to this wider Guadalupe tradition, but it should be presented as legend rather than as a documentary origin for the station name.

For transport history, the station belongs to the late-1990s expansion of Line 1 into Villa de Vallecas. A 2025 Madrid metro chronology lists the Miguel Hernández-Congosto extension, including Sierra de Guadalupe, Villa de Vallecas and Congosto, as inaugurated on 3 March 1999. Community of Madrid project documentation also notes that the Cercanías station was moved to create the interchange with Metro at Sierra de Guadalupe.

The etymology is therefore strong at the station level but more nuanced at the word level. The immediate name is a Madrid street and mountain-place name; the older word **Guadalupe** is debated by toponymists, with Arabic *wadi* 'river' widely accepted as the first element but competing explanations for the second. For a public label, the safest wording is that the station is named after the nearby street, which in turn refers to the Extremaduran Sierra de Guadalupe/Villuercas.
