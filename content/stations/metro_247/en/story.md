Congosto station is named first of all for its immediate urban setting. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists its entrances on Calle del Congosto and Plaza de Congosto, and the official Madrid historical Callejero records Calle del Congosto and Camino del Congosto in Villa de Vallecas from 28 May 1971. The name therefore reaches the Metro through a local street and area name rather than through a person, institution, or event.

Behind that local name is an old geographical word. In Spanish, a congosto is a narrow defile or gorge between mountains. The Real Academia Española gives the word as coming from Latin coangustus, and Toponomasticon Hispaniae places related forms such as CŎNGǓSTU and CŎNGǓSTA in the semantic field of depressions or narrow landforms.

This does not mean that the Metro station stands beside a dramatic canyon. The stronger evidence found for Madrid shows Calle del Congosto, Plaza de Congosto, and the Colonia Congosto area, while the natural-landscape meaning belongs to the wider history of the word and of similar place names elsewhere in Spain. Toponomasticon Hispaniae is especially useful here because it explains how congosto and congosta commonly became place names for constricted valleys, river gorges, and narrow passages.

The station also records a modern chapter in Vallecas' growth. The Congosto area lies south of the historic core of Villa de Vallecas, near housing developments described in urban and cultural studies as part of the peripheral expansion of Madrid in the late twentieth century. One academic study of Vallecas in cinema identifies Calle Congosto and the Colonia Congosto area as a setting in Villa de Vallecas during the transition-era urban periphery.

The station opened with the Line 1 extension from Miguel Hernández to Congosto in 1999, after the official inauguration campaign dated 3 March 1999 and public service reported the following day. It later ceased to be the southern end of Line 1 when the line was extended to Valdecarros in May 2007. Older transport usage also preserves the name Barrio de Vilano for the area, so the best display wording is: named after Calle del Congosto and the local Congosto area, whose name is a Spanish geographical term for a narrow gorge.
