Legazpi station is named after the plaza above it, Plaza de Legazpi. Madrid's municipal street database records the plaza as an official, current public way with an existence date of 16 July 1926, and the station record itself points to Plaza de Legazpi as its location. The person behind the name is Miguel López de Legazpi, born in Zumarraga and deceased in Manila in 1572, remembered as the head of the Spanish expedition that settled Cebu and took Manila in 1571. The surrounding urban setting gives the name extra weight: the plaza sits beside the former municipal slaughterhouse and livestock market, now Matadero Madrid, and became a major southern interchange when Metro line 3 reached Legazpi in 1951 and line 6 arrived in 1981.
