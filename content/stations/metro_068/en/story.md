The station name is direct and well supported: **Legazpi** comes from Plaza de Legazpi, the square above the Metro station. Madrid's municipal street database lists Plaza de Legazpi as an official, current public way and gives it an existence date of 16 July 1926. The same database has a separate record for the Metro station, also called Legazpi, with its starting point at Plaza de Legazpi and lines 3 and 6 noted in the observations.

The plaza commemorates Miguel López de Legazpi, a Basque figure from Zumarraga who died in Manila on 20 August 1572. The Real Academia de la Historia includes him among the biographical names used in the Metro network and links the station name to his biography. Legazpi is remembered as the leader of the expedition sent from New Spain that established a durable Spanish colonial foothold in the Philippine archipelago.

The key chronology belongs to the sixteenth century and to Madrid's twentieth-century expansion. Legazpi's expedition reached the Philippines in 1565, founded the Spanish settlement at Cebu, and later established Manila as the colonial capital in 1571. The Pacific return route that made the Manila-Acapulco system possible was achieved within that expedition, but it is more precisely credited to Andrés de Urdaneta and the tornaviaje, so the station story should not imply that Legazpi alone created the Manila Galleon route.

The Madrid place name also has a strongly local layer. Plaza de Legazpi grew beside the former Matadero y Mercado Municipal de Ganados, a large industrial complex begun in 1911 and inaugurated in 1924, now transformed into Matadero Madrid, a contemporary creation centre. That makes the station name both a biographical commemoration and a marker of Arganzuela's old southern industrial edge near the Manzanares.

For Metro history, Legazpi was not part of the 1936 opening of line 3. The first line 3 section opened in 1936 between Sol and Embajadores; the line then continued south to Delicias in 1949 and to Legazpi in 1951. Line 6 reached Legazpi with the Pacifico-Oporto section in 1981, turning the name from a terminal stop beside the river into one of the important southern interchange names in Madrid's network.
