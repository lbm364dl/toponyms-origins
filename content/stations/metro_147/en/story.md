Valdezarza station is named after the neighbourhood around it, in the north-west of Madrid's Moncloa-Aravaca district. When the Line 7 extension opened on 12 February 1999, regional transport documentation described the work as connecting Metro with the peripheral neighbourhood of Valdezarza and listed Valdezarza as one of the new stations on the Canal-Valdezarza section.

The older place-name is probably descriptive. In Spanish, val is an apocopated form of valle, and valle comes from Latin vallis, meaning a valley, hollow, or river basin. Zarza means bramble or thorny shrub; the RAE derives it from older sarza, a pre-Roman word, and defines the plant as a thorny rosaceous shrub whose fruit is the blackberry.

Read together, Valdezarza is best understood as 'val de zarza': a bramble valley, bramble hollow, or place where brambles were notable enough to name the land. This kind of practical landscape naming is common in Castilian toponymy, especially around former rural edges of towns, where vegetation, slopes, streams, pastures, and small valleys often became enduring names.

The modern urban setting is much newer than the word suggests. Madrid City Council's district history describes Moncloa-Aravaca as an area shaped by former Crown and National Heritage lands, with much of the district still wooded or open until the early twentieth century and later transformed by residential growth. It specifically names Valdezarza among the large housing operations carried out from around 1970 on the edges of Tetuán, while recent municipal regeneration documents describe the Colonia de Valdezarza as a dense residential area with buildings now more than sixty years old.

The evidence is strong for the station's immediate origin in the neighbourhood name, but weaker for the exact medieval or early-modern history of the toponym itself. No official naming decree or inspected local toponymy monograph was found that explicitly says 'Valdezarza means bramble valley'. The linguistic pieces support that interpretation very well, but the safest public wording is 'probably from val de zarza' rather than an absolute claim.
