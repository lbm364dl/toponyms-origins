Julián Besteiro station takes its name from Julián Besteiro Fernández (Madrid, 1870-Carmona, 1940), a university professor, socialist politician and trade-union leader. In the transport landscape the name appears in Leganés on Line 12, MetroSur, with access on Avenida Rey Juan Carlos I and near the Centro Cívico Julián Besteiro, so the station name fits a wider local commemoration of the same figure.

Besteiro belonged to the educated reformist world of late nineteenth-century Madrid. The Real Academia de la Historia describes his formation at the Institución Libre de Enseñanza, a key liberal educational project associated with Francisco Giner de los Ríos, and his later career as a philosopher, professor, deputy, union leader and president of the Congress. That intellectual background matters: the station does not remember a local landform or neighborhood nickname, but a public figure whose reputation joined education, socialism and parliamentary politics.

His best documented national role came during the Second Republic. The Congress of Deputies records that he was elected interim president of the Cortes on 14 July 1931 and definitive president from 27 July 1931 to 9 October 1933. He also held leading posts in the PSOE and UGT, although his relationship with both organizations was marked by disagreements over strategy and by his opposition to the radicalization of socialist politics in the mid-1930s.

The station itself belongs to a much later chapter: MetroSur, the circular Line 12 that links Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe and Leganés. The Community of Madrid describes Line 12 as a more-than-40-kilometre ring with 28 stations, designed to connect the main municipalities of the southern metropolitan area and to work as an internal metro for them. Julián Besteiro is one of the Leganés stops and the CRTM station page confirms its Line 12 service, fare zone B1 and accesses on Avenida Rey Juan Carlos I.

The main point is secure: the name commemorates Julián Besteiro Fernández. A small nuance is worth preserving for public use: some station summaries say simply that he was president of the Cortes in 1931-1933, but the Congress gives the exact terms and distinguishes the interim and definitive presidencies. Another nuance is local: the strongest transport sources confirm the station and its location, while municipal sources confirm the nearby Centro Cívico Julián Besteiro; they do not by themselves publish a formal naming act for the metro station.
