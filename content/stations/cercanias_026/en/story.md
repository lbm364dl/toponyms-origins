Aranjuez station is named directly after the municipality of Aranjuez, the Real Sitio y Villa at the southern end of the Madrid region. For passengers on Cercanías line C-3, the name is first of all geographic: it identifies the town that grew around the royal palace, gardens, irrigated orchards and the meeting landscape of the Tagus and Jarama rivers.

The older name behind the station is more difficult. Medieval documentation records forms such as Arauz or Aranz in the twelfth century, Aranzuel or Aranzuech in the thirteenth, and later Aranzueque, Aranzugue, Aranzuet or Arançuex before the modern form Aranjuez became established in the fifteenth century. Those forms make the name much older than the railway and older than the planned Bourbon townscape.

The best-known scholarly explanation connects the early base Aranz with Basque aranz or arantza, meaning a thorn bush, hawthorn or related spiny shrub. Rafael Lapesa included Aranjuez among place-names that could be related to that Basque element, despite its distance from the historical Basque-speaking area. Other researchers have been more cautious, suggesting that *aranz may be a broader pre-Roman element or perhaps connected with river and valley names rather than a straightforward plant name.

Aranjuez became nationally prominent for reasons quite separate from the original toponym. UNESCO describes the Cultural Landscape of Aranjuez as a shaped royal and agricultural landscape structured by the Tagus and Jarama, and traces its transformation as a Royal Site to the reign of Philip II in the sixteenth century. Patrimonio Nacional similarly links the Royal Palace to Felipe II's decision to replace the old residence of the masters of Santiago with a new palace from 1564 onward.

The railway added another layer to the name. The Madrid-Aranjuez line opened on 9 February 1851, making Aranjuez the destination of Madrid's first railway and the second railway on the Spanish peninsula after Barcelona-Mataró. The present neomudéjar station was built later, with works beginning in 1922 and ending in 1927, replacing an earlier station near the Royal Palace; the name, however, remained the town's. The station-name origin is therefore secure, while the deeper etymology of Aranjuez should be presented as probable rather than fully proven.
