Aranjuez station takes its name from Aranjuez itself, the historic royal town at the southern edge of the Madrid region. The town-name is documented in medieval forms such as Arauz or Aranz, then Aranzuel and Aranzuech, before stabilizing as Aranjuez by the fifteenth century. Rafael Lapesa related it to Basque aranz, 'thorn' or 'hawthorn', a plausible explanation but not the only one. Older proposals derived the name from Latin Ara Iovis, 'altar of Jupiter', or from Arabic; more cautious recent discussion treats *aranz as possibly Basque, pre-Roman, or even hydronymic. The station also has major railway significance: the Madrid-Aranjuez line opened on 9 February 1851 as Madrid's first railway and the second on the Spanish peninsula.
