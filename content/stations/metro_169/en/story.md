The Metro station name **Lacoma** comes from the local neighbourhood name, especially the **Colonia Lacoma** area of Peñagrande. That colony is linked to Margarita Gonzalez Lacoma, a Madrid businesswoman whose surname became attached to housing, streets and, eventually, a Line 7 station.

Margarita Lacoma was first prominent in fashion. Madrid's built-heritage catalogue for the Gran Via records Casa Lacoma as the fashion salon of Margarita Lacoma, opened in 1925 in the Seguros La Estrella building on Gran Via. This places her in the luxury-commercial world of the new avenue before her later association with the north-western suburbs.

The urban link comes after the war, when Margarita Gonzalez Lacoma and Pilar Cudos Velasco appear in local historical accounts as promoters of Marcudos, S.L. Historias Matritenses dates that company to 1949 and says the two women had been buying land from 1947 in what became the Lacoma area, with the aim of building dwellings for rent or sale under the subsidised-housing legislation of the period. Madrid City Council's regeneration material confirms that Colonia Lacoma was built in the 1950s and describes it as the first urban nucleus of Peñagrande.

When Line 7 was extended north-west, the station took the already established local name rather than a full personal name. Memoria de Madrid documents the inauguration act on **29 March 1999** and identifies the access to Lacoma station during the opening of the Valdezarza-Pitis section. El Pais reported the same opening the following day, listing Lacoma among the five stations opened to the public on the new stretch.

The evidence is strong for the sequence surname -> housing colony/neighbourhood -> Metro station. The weaker point is biographical precision: some modern accounts state or imply a specific birthplace, while others describe Margarita Gonzalez Lacoma's origins as uncertain or linked to Valladolid and Santander. For public display, it is safest to emphasize the well-supported Madrid facts: Casa Lacoma on Gran Via, the Marcudos housing venture, Colonia Lacoma in Peñagrande, and the 1999 Metro opening.
