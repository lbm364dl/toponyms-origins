Rivas Vaciamadrid station is named after the municipality it serves, Rivas-Vaciamadrid, on Madrid Metro Line 9. The transport evidence is straightforward: the Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists the station as Rivas Vaciamadrid on Line 9, and Metro chronology records the Puerta de Arganda-Arganda del Rey extension, including Rivas Vaciamadrid, as inaugurated on 7 April 1999.

The municipal name is a compound of two older place names. Rivas, historically also written Ribas and later Ribas/Rivas de Jarama, is explained in local official history as a Christian enclave colonised in the eleventh century by the captain Guillermo Rivas or Ribas under Alfonso VI. Vaciamadrid was the other historic name, attached first to a territory and later to a small settlement near the strategic route between Madrid and Valencia, close to the Manzanares and Jarama river landscape.

Vaciamadrid is the most interesting and most contested part of the name. Jaime Oliver Asín's 1948 study of Madrid toponymy says that fifteenth-century documents used forms such as Masalmadrit and Hafalmadrit/Ahazalmadrit. In his interpretation, Masalmadrit reflected Manzil Mayrit, the 'parador of Madrid', while the form that prevailed came from Fahs Mayrit, the communal meadow or field of Madrid; once the Arabic meaning was no longer understood, speakers reshaped the strange sound into a folk etymology resembling 'va hacia Madrid'.

Current local summaries simplify that story in different ways. The city council's general history page says Vaciamadrid 'seems to descend' from Manzil Mayrit, the Mayrit stopping place for travellers, while the Oliver Asín argument gives Fahs Mayrit as the route to the modern name and reserves Manzil Mayrit for the inn or parador. For public wording, the safest wording is therefore: Arabic origin is well supported, but the precise evolution should be presented as a scholarly interpretation rather than an uncontested fact.

The modern municipality was also shaped by twentieth-century rupture. Rivas incorporated Vaciamadrid in 1845, and the old settlement area was devastated during the Civil War around the Battle of the Jarama. Postwar reconstruction by Regiones Devastadas began with a new nucleus in 1954, and municipal accounts date the birth of present-day Rivas Vaciamadrid as a town to 23 July 1959, when the school, doctor's house, town hall, church and first houses were inaugurated.
