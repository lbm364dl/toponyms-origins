The station opened on Line 9 on 7 April 1999 and takes its name from the municipality of Rivas-Vaciamadrid. That municipal name preserves two older places: Ribas or Rivas de Jarama, linked in local official history to a Christian settlement associated with Guillermo Rivas/Ribas, and Vaciamadrid, a former territory and small settlement near the Manzanares-Jarama confluence. The strongest inspected scholarly source is Jaime Oliver Asín's 1948 study, which argues that Vaciamadrid developed from Arabic Fahs Mayrit, 'communal meadow/field of Madrid', through medieval forms such as Hafalmadrit and later folk reinterpretation as 'va hacia Madrid'. Current municipal wording instead says Vaciamadrid probably descends from Manzil Mayrit, 'parador of Mayrit', so the public explanation should acknowledge that the Arabic origin is well supported but the exact path is contested. The Civil War destroyed the old settlement area, and the postwar rebuilt nucleus was founded in 1954 and inaugurated as the modern town on 23 July 1959.
