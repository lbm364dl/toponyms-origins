Nueva Numancia station is named for the old local toponym Nueva Numancia in Puente de Vallecas, not directly for a person. Metro’s public-transport listing places the station on Line 1 with accesses on Avenida de la Albufera, and a Metro archive photograph records that the station opened on 2 July 1962 as part of the Line 1 extension toward Vallecas.

The neighbourhood name is older than the Metro by almost a century. A BNE digitized issue of the Diario oficial de avisos de Madrid from 8 November 1866 already refers to people who had lived in the barrio of Nueva Numancia, within the jurisdiction of Vallecas. Local historical notes also point to works and new buildings in the area during the 1860s, when cheap peripheral land and road connections were drawing settlement beyond the old bridge over the Abroñigal.

The famous 1873 episode is real, but it did not create the name. In the Boletín oficial de la provincia de Madrid of 14 June 1873, Cayetano Agustí y Giner and other residents of the hamlet near the Puente de Vallecas asked to form a separate municipality with the name Nueva Numancia. The provincial commission rejected the request in the issue of 15 September 1873, stating that the settlement did not have the required 2,000 inhabitants.

The deeper allusion is probably to ancient Numancia, the Celtiberian city near present-day Garray in Soria. Official museum material from Castilla y León describes Numancia’s long resistance and its conquest by Scipio in 133 BC; Spanish cultural memory later turned the city into a shorthand for heroic endurance, the origin of the phrase resistencia numantina. That meaning fits well with a republican-era attempt to claim civic independence, but the name was already circulating before that petition.

There is one useful caution: the exact reason why the 1860s developers or residents chose Nueva Numancia has not been proved from a naming decree. A recent Vallecas local-history article says the origin is unknown and suggests that the name might have referred either to the ancient city or, perhaps more likely, to the frigate Numancia, launched in 1864 and prominent in the Pacific squadron shortly before the barrio name appeared. For public display, the safest wording is that the station is named after the historic Nueva Numancia neighbourhood, whose name probably evokes Numancia, while noting that the original naming motive remains partly open.
