San Cristóbal de los Ángeles station is best explained as a place-name: it serves and is named for the San Cristóbal de los Ángeles neighbourhood in Madrid's Villaverde district. Municipal sources describe the neighbourhood as a late-1950s/1960s social-housing development built by the Gerencia de Poblados Dirigidos and the Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda. The religious layer of the name is credible: San Cristóbal is the traditional patron of travellers, while 'de los Ángeles' points to the nearby Cerro de los Ángeles in Getafe, officially described by Getafe as named after the Ermita/Virgen de los Ángeles. The station lies on the historic Madrid-Aranjuez railway corridor opened in 1851, but the current neighbourhood name cannot itself date from that first railway opening.
