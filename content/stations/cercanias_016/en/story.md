The most direct origin of the station name is local and urban: **San Cristóbal de los Ángeles** is the Cercanías stop for the neighbourhood of the same name in Villaverde, in southern Madrid. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid lists the station with C-3 service and accesses on Calle de Paterna, while Renfe's station sheet records it as `SAN CRISTOBAL DE LOS ANGELES`, code 60107, in the Cercanías Madrid area.

Behind the neighbourhood name is a religious and geographical combination. **San Cristóbal** refers to Saint Christopher, a saint traditionally invoked by travellers, pilgrims and motorists. **De los Ángeles** is best read in relation to the nearby **Cerro de los Ángeles** in Getafe; Getafe's municipal page says the hill takes its name from the Ermita de la Virgen de los Ángeles, where the local patroness is venerated.

The neighbourhood itself is not medieval. Madrid municipal material dates its origin to a social-housing operation by the Gerencia de Poblados Dirigidos, working with the Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda, with 6,077 subsidised homes built in successive phases from 1959. The same sources place the development on more than 43 hectares of land formerly occupied by the La Norah brickworks, whose surviving chimney became a neighbourhood landmark.

The railway context is older than the neighbourhood. This stop sits on the Madrid-Aranjuez corridor, one of the foundational railway routes in Spain: official regional and Aranjuez tourism sources identify the Madrid-Aranjuez railway as opened in 1851, the second line in Spain after Barcelona-Mataró and the first railway to leave Madrid. That history is relevant to the place, but it should not be used as proof that the current station name existed in 1851.

The best-supported public explanation is therefore layered: the station is named after the modern neighbourhood; the neighbourhood name carries Catholic devotional language and points south-east toward Getafe's Cerro de los Ángeles. Secondary sources report that the station was formerly called **Los Ángeles**, but I did not find an authoritative date for the renaming to San Cristóbal de los Ángeles. The 'centre of the Iberian Peninsula' claim attached to the Cerro is traditional and repeated officially by Getafe, but it should be presented cautiously because it is a symbolic/geographical claim with competing local interpretations.
