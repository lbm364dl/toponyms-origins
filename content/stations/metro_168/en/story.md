Arroyofresno is named after Arroyo del Fresno, the residential area around the station and the older watercourse that gave that area its name. The official one-word station spelling, **Arroyofresno**, compresses the place-name, but the underlying toponym is the transparent Spanish phrase **Arroyo del Fresno**.

The name is descriptive. In Spanish, **arroyo** means a small stream or brook, while **fresno** means an ash tree. That makes “Ash-tree Stream” a fair English rendering of the words, but it should be read as a lexical explanation of the toponym rather than proof that a documented line of ash trees once stood along the stream.

The watercourse itself is not just a folk explanation. Spain’s official geographic names data records **Arroyo del Fresno** as a natural watercourse near the station and also records **Arroyo del Fresno** as a populated-place/neighbourhood name. A planning document for the area places the Arroyo del Fresno in the Manzanares drainage system and says that the nearby Arroyo del Monte Carmelo forms the origin of the Arroyo del Fresno.

The transport history adds a twist. The station was part of the 1999 Line 7 extension toward Pitis, but because the surrounding development had not yet consolidated and demand was low, the completed structure was left out of service. It finally opened to passengers on **23 March 2019**, becoming Metro station number 302; the official transport pages list it on Line 7 with access from Calle de Federica Montseny and Calle Arroyo del Monte.

Earlier references and reporting show the planned or closed station as **Arroyo del Fresno** or **Arroyo Fresno**, while the public station now uses **Arroyofresno**. The best-supported origin is therefore clear: Metro’s modern name refers to the local Arroyo del Fresno toponym, ultimately tied to the stream. The only weaker part of the old claim is the more pictorial statement about ash trees lining the stream, which remains an inference from the name rather than a directly sourced historical fact.
