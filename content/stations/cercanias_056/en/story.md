The Cercanías station called **Leganés** takes its name directly from the municipality in which it stands. Adif identifies the station as Leganés, code 35001, on commercial line C5, and the Madrid transport consortium lists it as a Cercanías station with interchange to Metro line 12 and local buses.

Behind the transport name is one of the best-known local etymological traditions in southern Madrid. In the sixteenth-century Relaciones Topográficas, the people answering for Leganés explained that the place had been founded where there was a lagoon producing much **légano**, a word related to **légamo**, meaning sticky mud, silt, or clayey soil. According to that account, the older name was Leganar or Legamar and later became Leganés through phonetic change.

The broader history fits the landscape of water, fields, and nearby older settlements. The municipal history page places the medieval settlement around 1280, when people from Butarque and other nearby places moved to a higher, cleaner site with water nearby. It also describes the area as tied to streams, old hamlets, farming, and the later agricultural identity that made Leganés famous as a market-garden town.

For the station, the name is therefore not a dedication to a person but a place-name carried into the railway system. The present railway stop sits in the urban centre, at Calle Virgen del Camino, and today functions as a C-5 station and interchange. The name also coexists with the Metro naming convention: the connected Metro station is commonly treated as Leganés Central, while the Cercanías station keeps the simple municipal name.

The safest public explanation is that Leganés is **traditionally** linked to mud or silt from a former lagoon, not that it is definitively from a Latin word 'leganos'. Toponomasticon Hispaniae calls the toponym difficult and notes that the local sixteenth-century explanation is valuable but not conclusive. It also raises competing possibilities, especially a connection with **lebanés**, a person from Liébana, or more remotely with the Latin personal name **Libanius**.
