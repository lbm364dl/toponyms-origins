Ventura Rodríguez station takes its name from the nearby Calle de Ventura Rodríguez, where Line 3 runs under the Princesa corridor. The street and station commemorate Buenaventura Rodríguez Tizón, usually known simply as Ventura Rodríguez, an eighteenth-century architect born in Ciempozuelos in 1717 and dead in Madrid in 1785.

Rodríguez's name is especially visible in Madrid because of his work for the city rather than because of a single building beside the station. The City Council describes him as maestro mayor of the Villa and of its waterworks and fountains, a post that made him central to Madrid's urban works after 1764. His drawings and projects are preserved in municipal collections, which helps explain why his memory has remained strong in the city's public space.

His best-known Madrid legacy is the fountain programme for the Salón del Prado. The present Paseo del Prado area was transformed in the reign of Carlos III from a semi-rural edge into a major ceremonial and recreational axis. In that programme, Ventura Rodríguez prepared the designs for the mythological fountains of Cibeles, Neptuno and Apolo, while sculptors such as Francisco Gutiérrez, Roberto Michel, Miguel Ximénez and others executed the stone figures.

The Metro station belongs to the history of Line 3. Regional transport history records the first Line 3 section, Embajadores-Lavapiés-Sol, opening in 1936, and the Sol-Argüelles extension in July 1941. Ventura Rodríguez is one of the stations on that extension, located between Plaza de España and Argüelles, in a stretch that tied the old centre to the Princesa and Argüelles area.

The name is therefore secure, but two details need care. It is better to say that the station is named for the architect via the street, rather than implying that Metro independently chose the name from his biography. It is also more precise to describe Rodríguez as designer and municipal architect: under Carlos III he was displaced from the Royal Palace works in favor of the court's preferred architects, even while he later held major municipal, academic and Council of Castile responsibilities.
