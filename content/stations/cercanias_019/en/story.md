Getafe Centro is a place-name station. Its name means the central station of Getafe, the large municipality south of Madrid served by Cercanías line C-4 and by MetroSur at the adjoining Metro station. At the railway level, the origin is therefore not a person, event, or saint, but the city itself and the station's role in the urban centre.

The deeper story is the name Getafe. In the 1575 Relaciones Topográficas ordered under Philip II, local witnesses said the town was then called Xetafe and admitted that they did not know the reason with certainty. They repeated, however, a local explanation: in Arabic, they had been told, Jata meant something long, and the name suited a settlement that had grown lengthwise along the royal road from Madrid to Toledo.

That 1575 account also connects Getafe with an older place called Alarnes. According to the witnesses, the earlier site was humid and unhealthy, so residents gradually moved toward the road, first building houses like inns and then forming a longer linear settlement. The story is valuable because it records how sixteenth-century Getafe understood its own origin, even if it cannot by itself prove the linguistic derivation.

Modern scholarship is careful. Jairo J. García Sánchez, writing for the Centro Virtual Cervantes, says that Getafe has serious etymological problems and that little can be said about it without doubt. He notes that an Arabic origin is presumed from the look and configuration of the name, and that a meaning connected with length has been proposed, but he warns that the medieval attestations do not allow the matter to go further for now.

The station has also had a transport-name history of its own. The railway stop was known as Getafe-Badajoz until 1981 and then as Getafe Centro; the Metro station opened in 2003 as Getafe Central, reflecting the same central interchange but using the Metro naming form. The best public reading is therefore double: the station name is securely named for central Getafe, while the ancient origin of Getafe remains probable but unresolved.
