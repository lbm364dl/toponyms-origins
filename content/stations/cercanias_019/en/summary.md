The station name is straightforward at transport level: Getafe Centro identifies the central Cercanías station in Getafe and distinguishes it from other Getafe stops. The more interesting question is the city name behind it. In Philip II's 1575 Relaciones Topográficas, local witnesses said the town was then called Xetafe and repeated a tradition that Arabic Jata meant a long thing, fitting a settlement stretched along the Madrid-Toledo road after people moved from Alarnes. Modern toponymist Jairo J. García Sánchez accepts only a cautious position: an Arabic origin is suspected from the form of the name, but the medieval evidence does not yet prove the proposed etymology.
