Cuzco station is named for the place directly above it: Plaza de Cuzco, a roundabout on Paseo de la Castellana at Avenida de Alberto Alcocer. The official Madrid street list places Plaza de Cuzco in Chamartín and Tetuán, and the transport authority lists the Metro station's accesses at Paseo de la Castellana 125 and 162.

The square's name commemorates Cusco, also traditionally written Cuzco in Spanish outside Peru. The Peruvian city was the capital of Tawantinsuyu, the Inca Empire, and UNESCO describes it as a city where the Inca capital and the colonial city remain layered together. Its Quechua name is commonly given as Qosqo or Qusqu; reference works often explain it as meaning 'navel' or 'centre,' reflecting the city's symbolic role in the Inca world.

The Madrid setting is also part of the story. The Plaza de Cuzco area belongs to the northward extension of the Castellana, planned after the old Hipódromo de la Castellana disappeared from this part of the city in the early 1930s. The current square forms part of a sequence of large junctions on the modern business axis north of Nuevos Ministerios.

Metro arrived later. Cuzco opened on 10 June 1982 in the Fuencarral-Nuevos Ministerios section of the former Line 8, shortly before the 1982 FIFA World Cup in Spain. In 1998 that northern Metro route was absorbed into today's Line 10, which is why Cuzco now sits between Plaza de Castilla and Santiago Bernabéu on Line 10.

The main etymology is secure: station, square, and Peruvian city form a clear chain. The more delicate point is the exact date when Madrid assigned the square's name; 21 October 1953 is repeated in secondary sources, but the original municipal act should be consulted before treating that date as fully archival.
