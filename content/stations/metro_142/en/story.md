Vinateros is first of all a station named after its street. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes places the station entrances on Calle del Camino de los Vinateros and confirms its service on Metro line 9, with the station lying in the Moratalaz district. The metro name therefore preserves the road name rather than commemorating an individual person.

The older word behind the name is straightforward. The RAE defines vinatero as someone connected with wine production or trade, and local tradition reads Camino de los Vinateros as the road of the wine people: producers, carriers, or merchants moving wine toward Madrid. That meaning makes the station name an occupational toponym, even though its immediate source is a street.

The road itself is not just a modern label. Research on Moratalaz based on nineteenth-century plans identifies the Camino de Vinateros among the routes crossing the area, and notes that the 1862 IGN plan shows Vinateros as both a road and a place-name in the northern part of what is now Moratalaz. That matters because much of modern Moratalaz was urbanized only in the twentieth century; the station name carries a much older rural geography into the metro map.

The wine connection is also geographically plausible. The official DOP Vinos de Madrid pliego lists the Arganda subzone in the southeast and records the long history of wine in the Madrid region, with official recognition of the modern denomination in 1990. A wine-history article on Camino de los Vinateros specifically says that wine entered Madrid along this old road from the Arganda area and also from La Mancha, first by animals and carts and later in relation to the well-known Arganda railway.

The strongest supported statement is that the station is named from Calle/Camino de los Vinateros, an older Moratalaz road/toponym whose wording means wine producers or traders. The more colorful claim about exact wine routes and quantities should be presented as well-supported local tradition, not as a fully proven official naming record. The line 9 section serving Moratalaz was inaugurated on 30 January 1980 and public service is commonly dated to 31 January 1980, so the metro name belongs to the first generation of line 9 stations in the district.
