Alameda de Osuna is a place-name station: it is named after the surrounding neighbourhood in Madrid's Barajas district. The deeper origin is historical, because this area became known as the Alameda de Osuna around the aristocratic estate of the dukes of Osuna and the nearby El Capricho garden. Madrid City Council links the name to the palatial and leisure landscape that grew around the Castle of La Alameda, the dukes' palace and El Capricho. María Josefa Alonso Pimentel, Countess-Duchess of Benavente and Duchess consort of Osuna, was the central patron behind El Capricho, buying land between 1783 and 1803 and shaping one of Madrid's great Enlightenment gardens. The metro name was in use when the Line 5 extension from Canillejas to Alameda de Osuna opened on 24 November 2006.
