The station name is best explained from the landscape beside it. Parque Polvoranca is a Cercanías C-5 stop in Leganés, and Renfe describes it as a station opened in 2004 to serve the municipality's new urban expansion. CRTM also lists its access as "Parque Polvoranca", confirming that the transport name is tied to the local park and surrounding urban area.

The park behind the station is the Parque de Polvoranca, or Parque Forestal Polvoranca in regional environmental material. The Community of Madrid describes it as a 153-hectare periurban forest park in Leganés, the largest of the periurban parks created by the regional government. It includes a large artificial lagoon, wooded and recreational areas, environmental education facilities, and the historical ruins of the old settlement.

That older settlement is the reason the word Polvoranca survived into the modern station name. The park occupies land associated with the former village or villa of Polvoranca, whose visible memory is the ruin of the church of San Pedro Apóstol. Nineteenth-century evidence is important here: local historical summaries note that Madoz's 1849 Diccionario already described Polvoranca as a villa attached to the municipality of Leganés, after earlier records treated it as a separate place.

The deeper origin of the word Polvoranca is less secure. A frequently repeated popular explanation derives it from the Roman personal name Laurianus, through Loranca or Loranque, then adds a medieval pol- element linked with settlement and finally a spelling change from b to v. The stronger scholarly source found for this research is more cautious: it says the Laurianus explanation may apply to Loranca-type names, but not to Polvoranca, and only suggests that pol- and a possible Mozarabic -que/-anque relationship may be relevant.

For public display, the safest etymology is therefore layered: the station is named after the park; the park is named after the old Polvoranca place-name; the old place-name is historically attested but linguistically uncertain. The current row should keep the place-based naming, but soften or remove the precise Laurianus-to-Polvoranca sequence unless a more direct philological source is inspected.
