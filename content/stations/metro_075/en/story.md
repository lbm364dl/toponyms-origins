San Cipriano station is named directly after Calle de San Cipriano in Vicálvaro. The strongest evidence is not a later guidebook but the Community of Madrid’s own description of the Line 9 extension: the accesses to the station are on Calle de San Cipriano, and that street gives the station its name.

The name behind the street is religious. In ordinary Spanish usage, “San Cipriano” most often points to Saint Cyprian of Carthage, a third-century North African bishop, Christian writer, and martyr. He was bishop of Carthage during the persecutions of Decius and Valerian and died as a martyr in 258.

The Metro name belongs to the modern transport history of Vicálvaro. The Pavones-Puerta de Arganda extension of Line 9 entered service on 1 December 1998, adding Valdebernardo, Vicálvaro, San Cipriano, and Puerta de Arganda. The regional works description gives a useful physical detail: San Cipriano lies only 397 metres after Vicálvaro station, reached on a steep section designed to bring the station to the required level.

The surrounding place is older than the Metro. Madrid’s official street directory places Calle de San Cipriano in Vicálvaro, from Calle de Ambroz to Calle del Jardín de la Duquesa, and the Facundo Cañada map of 1900 is a key source for the old urban forms of Vicálvaro and nearby towns. Vicálvaro itself was still a separate municipality until its incorporation into Madrid in the mid-twentieth century.

The safest public explanation is therefore two-layered: the station is certainly named after Calle de San Cipriano; the street probably preserves a saint’s dedication, most plausibly Saint Cyprian of Carthage. What remains unverified is the exact municipal act or older local reason for choosing this saint for the Vicálvaro street.
