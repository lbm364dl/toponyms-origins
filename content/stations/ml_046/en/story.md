Parque Parla Este is a straightforward descriptive name: it identifies the tram stop by the large park on the edge of the Parla Este development. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid lists the stop as Parque Parla Este and places its surface access at Calle del Paraguay, 16, in Parla. The official network diagram also includes Parque Parla Este among the stops of the circular Parla Tram line.

The place behind the name is the large green area now known as Parque del Universo. Municipal transparency records list Parque del Universo as public open-space parcels in the PAU-4 Bis Residencial Este, transferred on 17 June 2004. Contemporary local reporting says the park had been known as Parque de Parla-Este before the municipal plenary approved the new name Parque del Universo in 2008.

That change explains why the tram stop and the park name do not perfectly match today. The Parla Tram began service in 2007, and CRTM's 2010 light-rail publication gives 5 June 2007 as the opening date for Tranvia de Parla. The stop name therefore reflects the practical geography of the new eastern development at the time the tram opened, while the park soon acquired a more thematic civic name.

The astronomical context is real but should be stated carefully. Parla Este is strongly associated with street names such as Avenida del Sistema Solar, Avenida de las Galaxias, Avenida de los Planetas, Avenida de las Estrellas and streets named for planets, stars, constellations and zodiac signs. Official neighbourhood-boundary material, however, also mentions non-astronomical names such as Avenida de America, Avenida de las Comunidades Europeas, Calle Paris and the M-408, so the safest wording is that much of the district's street-naming follows an astronomy theme, not that every street does.

The etymology is therefore well supported as descriptive, but one detail remains slightly indirect: I found no formal station-naming resolution saying 'the stop is named after the park.' The evidence is the official stop name, its documented location by the park, the municipal records for the park area and the 2008 record of the park's renaming. The strongest interpretation is that Parque Parla Este preserves the older descriptive name of the park and development rather than naming the station after a person, event or astronomical object.
