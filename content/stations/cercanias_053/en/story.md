The station name **Las Retamas** is best read first as a local place-name. Renfe describes the stop as being on an embankment in Avenida de Mostoles, at the intersection with Avenida de las Retamas, beside Parque Oeste in Alcorcon. That location makes the avenue and its surrounding Las Retamas/Prado de Santo Domingo area the immediate source of the railway name.

Behind the street-name is the plant word **retama**. In Spanish it refers to broom shrubs: tough, much-branched Mediterranean plants, often with yellow flowers and sparse leaves. The RAE traces the word to Andalusi Arabic *ratama*, from classical Arabic *ratamah*, so the station preserves a small botanical Arabism in the suburban map of southwest Madrid.

The transport chronology is slightly more complicated than a simple opening year. Contemporary press reported that the Minister of Public Works, the regional president and the mayor of Alcorcon inaugurated the Las Retamas Cercanias stop on 31 July 2002. Renfe's own location page, however, says the station opened to the public in 2003 on line C-5, so both dates should be kept visible until an Adif or Renfe commissioning file settles the distinction between inauguration and public service.

Las Retamas also belongs to a moment of rapid urban growth. The stop was built to serve Parque Oeste, new housing, the Universidad Rey Juan Carlos area and nearby commercial zones. Alcorcon's municipal archive notes that the projects for the station works date from 1999 and 2000 and that it became the municipality's third railway station, after San Jose de Valderas and Alcorcon.

The botanical meaning is solid, but one attractive part of the older claim needs caution. It is plausible that local street-naming drew on retama as a familiar dryland shrub of the Madrid landscape, and the Community of Madrid identifies *Retama sphaerocarpa* as a shrub of Mediterranean habitats, common in much of the Iberian Peninsula. What is not yet directly proven is that the station name was officially chosen because retamas grew abundantly on this exact plot between Alcorcon and Mostoles.
