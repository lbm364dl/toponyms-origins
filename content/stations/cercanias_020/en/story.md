The name **Getafe Sector 3** is straightforward but revealing. The Cercanías station uses the name of the nearby Sector III neighbourhood of Getafe, one of the large residential districts built on the western side of the A-42, away from the older town centre.

The neighbourhood name is not an old village, saint, estate or landscape word. A local historical account explains that it remained known as Sector III because of the technical designation used in the General Urban Plan. In other words, a planning label became a lived toponym.

Sector III was urbanised and built mainly between 1979 and 1988. It was conceived as a large residential area of mostly two-storey terraced houses, organised in developments and served over time by wide avenues, shopping areas, sports facilities, civic buildings and green spaces. Getafe's municipal page describes it today as the town's largest neighbourhood and one of its most important, with about 29,200 residents recorded on 1 January 2019.

The rail stop came later. Contemporary reporting in *El País* states that the Sector III station entered service on 28 May 1995, after delays, and that it served the Sector III neighbourhood from the Madrid-Parla line. Its physical position is slightly awkward for the name: the station is by the railway corridor and the road to the municipal cemetery, across the Toledo motorway corridor from much of the neighbourhood it names.

The existing wording's broader story about Cerro Buenavista and Civil War remains should be handled with care. Municipal plenary documentation from 2023 supports that there are bunkers of the Cerro de los Ángeles and Sector III whose inclusion in Getafe's protected-assets catalogue and possible regional heritage protection were requested. However, the available sources did not include an authoritative source for the specific claim that bulldozers nearly destroyed them in 1985 and that the mayor ordered their preservation, so that detail should be marked as unverified unless a local archive or planning file confirms it.
