Villaverde Bajo station is named after the local place name Villaverde Bajo, historically associated with the lower, railway-side sector of Villaverde. Today the name survives as a familiar neighbourhood name around the station, although Madrid's formal administrative barrio in this area is Los Rosales.

The deeper place name is Villaverde. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats it as a transparent Latin-Romance formation from villa, a settlement or village, and viridis, green. The most plausible reading is a 'green settlement', referring to vegetation, meadows, or productive agricultural land, though the same source also notes that green could have carried an auspicious meaning in medieval agrarian culture.

The railway name has its own chronology. Railway historian Juan Pedro Esteve Garcia documents that the site was known as the Villaverde-Alicante siding after it opened on 1 June 1893, about two kilometres from the old village of Villaverde. A Royal Order of 19 July 1906 gave it the current railway name Villaverde Bajo, and it did not become a full station until 1 June 1931, when a new passenger building was already in place.

That railway setting explains why the name matters beyond simple geography. Villaverde Bajo became part of the southern rail system where routes toward Badajoz, Andalucia, Albacete and Cuenca shared the approach to Atocha, and MZA developed workshops, yards, housing and service buildings in the area. The surviving employee housing pavilions near Calle Eduardo Maristany are reminders of that railway-industrial landscape.

The current etymology is therefore well supported as a place name, but the inherited historical summary should be cleaned up. The 1861 opening date is not the best-supported date for this station site; stronger railway evidence points to the Villaverde-Alicante siding in 1893, the official renaming in 1906, and station status in 1931. Villaverde itself remained outside Madrid as an independent municipality until the 1954 annexation.
