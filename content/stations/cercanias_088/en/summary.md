The station name comes from Villaverde Bajo, the lower or railway-side part of the old Villaverde area. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains Villaverde as a transparent Romance place name from Latin villa and viridis: a 'green villa' or settlement, probably referring to vegetation or fertile land, with a possible auspicious sense of green as prosperity. In railway history, the site was first documented as the Villaverde-Alicante siding, opened in 1893, and became Villaverde Bajo by Royal Order on 19 July 1906. It was raised to station category on 1 June 1931, with a new passenger building and a larger MZA railway complex. Villaverde remained an independent municipality until its annexation to Madrid in 1954.
