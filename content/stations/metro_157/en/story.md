Marqués de la Valdavia station is a MetroNorte stop on Line 10 in Alcobendas. Its immediate source is not a square in Madrid, but the local Calle Marqués de la Valdavia, one of the streets that frame the station area near Parque de Cataluña and Paseo de la Chopera.

The Alcobendas street name is documented in a municipal street-history publication. It says that on 15 February 1958 the town council named the former Carretera del Plantío after Mariano Ossorio Arévalo, III Marquess of Valdavia, as thanks for the interest he had shown in Alcobendas.

Ossorio Arévalo belonged to a Palencia-linked political family and inherited a noble title whose name came from La Valdavia. He was active in politics before and after the Civil War, later serving as president of the Provincial Deputation of Madrid from 1947 to 1965, as well as holding Franco-period offices including procurador in the Cortes and delegate of Ex-Captives.

The title behind the street name reaches further back than the man honored by the street. Archival authority records place the concession of the Marquessate of Valdavia in 1883 to Josefa Lamadrid Cosío, while the Community of Madrid archives describe it as a reward from Alfonso XII for services connected with agriculture, charity, and public wealth. The geographical word Valdavia points to a river valley and comarca in Palencia, so a Madrid Metro stop preserves a layered Palencian toponym through a noble title and then through an Alcobendas street.

The station name was not uncontested. In February 2007, before MetroNorte opened, Alcobendas mayor José Caballero argued that the station should be called Parque de Cataluña because that park was its physical location; the regional government replied that the names had been agreed with local neighborhood associations and chosen to help orientation. The current evidence strongly supports the station-street-III Marquess chain, while the older previous wording's reference to Plaza Duque de Pastrana and the II Marquess should be corrected.
