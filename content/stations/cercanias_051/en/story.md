The station name is straightforward at transport level: **Alcorcón** is the Cercanías station serving the municipality of Alcorcón, southwest of Madrid, on line C-5. Official transport sources list the stop as Alcorcón, with C-5 service and interchange with Metro line 12 at the adjacent Alcorcón Central complex.

The older question is the origin of the municipal name. In his CVC article *La Comunidad de Madrid y sus topónimos (II)*, Jairo J. García Sánchez treats Alcorcón as a disputed name and proposes that it may have formed from *corcu/qorq, a Romance or Mozarabic development connected with Latin **quercus**, meaning 'holm oak' or 'oak'. Under that reading, the initial **al-** is not simply proof of an Arabic etymon, but an Arabic superstrate influence added to a non-Arabic base.

That interpretation fits a broader pattern of Iberian place names based on oaks and holm oaks. García Sánchez compares the case with **alcornoque**, a word that the RAE derives from Mozarabic *alqurnúq, ultimately from Latin **quernus**, 'of holm oak', plus a Hispanic suffix. In public terms, the likely sense is not a modern street-name dedication, but an old landscape name evoking oaks or an oak-bearing place.

There is also a strong local tradition for an Arabic explanation. The Alcorcón town council page says several writers, including Lorenzana, Madoz and Rosell, derived the name from **al-gor**, **alcor** or **al-kur**, understood as 'hill, knoll or pass', because the settlement stood on an elevated site. The modern Spanish word **alcor** does mean 'hill or pass' in the RAE dictionary, although its recorded etymology is from Hispanic Arabic *alqúll* and ultimately Latin *collis*, so it does not by itself prove that Alcorcón comes from that form.

The earliest secure references do not settle the linguistic question. The town council dates the first medieval mention to **28 July 1208**, as **Cañada de Alcorcón**, in boundary documentation linked to Madrid and Segovia; the name appears again in 1222. For display purposes, the safest wording is that the station is named after the municipality, whose name is probably a hybrid oak-related toponym, while acknowledging the older local hill-name theory.
