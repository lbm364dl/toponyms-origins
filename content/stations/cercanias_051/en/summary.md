The Cercanías station name is directly geographic: it is the station of Alcorcón, on line C-5. For the municipality's name, Jairo J. García Sánchez argues in the Centro Virtual Cervantes that Alcorcón may come from a base *corcu/qorq derived from Latin quercus, 'holm oak, oak', later influenced by Arabic through the apparent al- prefix. This would make the place name a hybrid rather than a straightforward Arabic formation. The town council preserves another long-standing explanation, from al-gor, alcor or al-kur, meaning a hill or knoll, tied to Alcorcón's elevated site. Because both explanations circulate and the oldest documentation only records the name, not its origin, the best degree of confidence is probable rather than fully verified.
