Reyes Católicos station takes its name from the Catholic Monarchs: Queen Isabella I of Castile and King Ferdinand II of Aragon. In everyday urban terms, the immediate source is the local Avenida de los Reyes Católicos, the avenue around which the station area is identified in San Sebastián de los Reyes.

The deeper meaning of the name is unusually local. San Sebastián de los Reyes is one of the Madrid-area places whose own foundation story is tied to Isabella and Ferdinand. Municipal information describes the town’s 2 May festivities as the anniversary of its foundation in 1492, and notes that the church of San Sebastián Mártir stands over an earlier hermitage already present when the municipality was founded under the Catholic Monarchs.

The traditional account says that families from nearby Alcobendas settled around the hermitage of Saint Sebastian on land under Madrid’s jurisdiction, seeking the protection of royal rather than seigneurial authority. A regional archival publication summarizes the foundation as taking place by a royal charter of the Catholic Monarchs dated 2 May 1492, under the protection of the Council of Madrid. Later local and scholarly accounts develop the same picture: a small late-medieval settlement became a legally recognized community through royal intervention.

The monarchs behind the name were not a single abstract symbol. Isabella was born in Madrigal de las Altas Torres in 1451 and died in Medina del Campo in 1504; Ferdinand was born in Sos in 1452 and died in Madrigalejo in 1516. Their marriage in 1469 and joint rule joined the dynastic interests of Castile and Aragon, while each crown retained its own political structures. Their reign also belongs to a highly charged year in Spanish history: Granada fell in January 1492, the expulsion decree against the Jews was issued in March, and Columbus sailed west later that year.

The station opened on 26 April 2007 as part of MetroNorte, the northern extension of Line 10 serving Alcobendas and San Sebastián de los Reyes. No former station name has been found. The etymology is therefore secure, but the most precise wording is that the station is named through the local avenue for the Catholic Monarchs, with added significance because those monarchs are central to the town’s own foundation narrative.
