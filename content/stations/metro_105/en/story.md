Alonso Cano station is a Chamberí stop on Metro line 7, opened with the Gregorio Marañón-Canal extension on 16 October 1998. The Community of Madrid's infrastructure page states that the station is on Calle de José Abascal and that its accesses sit on either side of Calle de Alonso Cano, the street that gives the station its name.

That street preserves the name of Alonso Cano (Granada, 1601-1667), one of the great all-round artists of Spain's Baroque age. The Museo del Prado describes him as a polymathic Spanish artist who practiced painting, sculpture and architecture; the University of Granada similarly presents him as a defining figure of the Granadan Baroque and a master across architectural design, sculpture, painting and drawing.

Cano's artistic formation links Madrid's station name to the wider Golden Age network of Seville, Madrid and Granada. His family moved to Seville in 1614, and in 1616 he began training with Francisco Pacheco, the painter and theorist whose workshop also brought him into contact with Diego Velázquez. By the late 1630s Cano was working in Madrid under court patronage, including royal commissions for the Alcázar.

The darker episode in Cano's biography should be handled carefully. The Prado notes that he seems to have left Madrid for Valencia in 1644-1645 after the violent death of his second wife, María Magdalena de Uceda, and that he was interrogated under torture but absolved of complicity in the murder. The more colorful story that royal orders spared his painting hand appears in older anecdotal literature, but it is not the best-supported way to explain a station name.

Cano later returned to Granada, where his work became central to the cathedral: he painted major Marian works, held the cathedral prebend of racionero, was ordained priest in 1658, and shortly before his death in 1667 was appointed master of works for the cathedral facade. For the station, the evidence is straightforward: Metro did not invent a separate commemorative name, but adopted the name of the street, which in turn commemorates the artist.
