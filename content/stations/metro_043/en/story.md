Ríos Rosas station is named for the urban place around it: the Metro stop lies on Calle de Santa Engracia at the height of Calle de Ríos Rosas. That street, and later the Chamberí neighbourhood that bears the same name, preserve the public memory of Antonio Sánchez del Río y López de la Rosa, better known in public life as Antonio de los Ríos Rosas or Antonio de los Ríos y Rosas.

Ríos Rosas was born in Ronda, Málaga, on 16 March 1812 and died in Madrid on 3 November 1873. He trained in law at the University of Granada and became one of the major parliamentary figures of liberal Spain in the reigns and regimes of the mid-19th century. His public career included seats in the Cortes, the Ministry of Gobernación, diplomatic service in Lisbon and before the Holy See, and the presidency of the Congress of Deputies in more than one legislature.

The name also belongs to the history of Madrid's expansion north of the old city. Calle de Ríos Rosas was a modern 19th-century street in the developing Chamberí area, linking the Bravo Murillo and Castellana sides of the district. By the time the Metro arrived, the name was already an established local reference, so the station took the name of the nearby street rather than inventing a new dedication.

Transport history gives the name extra weight. Ríos Rosas was one of the original stations of Line 1, the first Metro de Madrid line, officially inaugurated by King Alfonso XIII on 17 October 1919 between Sol and Cuatro Caminos. Metro's own historical material places the station near the Canal de Isabel II facilities and the School of Mining Engineers, two landmarks that help explain the urban identity of this part of Chamberí.

The etymology is therefore well supported, with one nuance: the station does not appear to have been named directly by a biographical act for the politician, but through the already existing street/toponym. The current claim is right about the person and the 1919 opening, while the most precise wording should use the full historical name Antonio Sánchez del Río y López de la Rosa and mention his better-known public form, Ríos Rosas or Ríos y Rosas.
