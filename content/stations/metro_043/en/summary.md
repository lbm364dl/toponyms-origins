The station is named for Calle de Ríos Rosas, at Santa Engracia, and ultimately for Antonio Sánchez del Río y López de la Rosa, better known as Antonio de los Ríos Rosas or Ríos y Rosas. He was born in Ronda on 16 March 1812 and died in Madrid on 3 November 1873. Authoritative biographical sources identify him as a lawyer, parliamentarian, minister, diplomat, president of the Congress of Deputies in several legislatures, member of the Real Academia Española, and founder member of the Real Academia de Ciencias Morales y Políticas. The station itself belongs to the first Metro de Madrid line, opened by Alfonso XIII on 17 October 1919 between Sol and Cuatro Caminos. The strongest correction to the current row is not the person identified, but the route of the name: it is a station named after an adjacent street and district toponym, which honour the politician.
