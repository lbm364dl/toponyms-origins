San Fernando de Henares station is directly named after the municipality it serves. The town began as the Real Sitio de San Fernando, an eighteenth-century royal industrial settlement built around a cloth factory. Felipe V ordered the purchase of Torrejón de la Ribera by royal decree on 29 June 1746, and Fernando VI took possession and drove the project forward soon afterward. The “San Fernando” element points to the saintly royal namesake, Ferdinand III of Castile, while “de Henares” was fixed in Spain's 1916 municipal nomenclature reform. Official transport sources use both San Fernando and San Fernando de Henares for the Cercanias stop, so the public story should distinguish the station name from the older municipal toponym.
