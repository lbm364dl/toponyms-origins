The Cercanias station name is best understood first as a place name: it identifies the railway stop serving San Fernando de Henares, on the eastern side of the Madrid metropolitan area. Adif and Renfe list the station as San Fernando de Henares, while the regional transport consortium presents the Cercanias stop as San Fernando and places its access at Calle Estacion de Ferrocarril, 6, in Coslada. That mixed usage reflects the station's edge-of-town position more than a different etymology.

Behind the station name is the eighteenth-century Royal Site of San Fernando. The older place acquired by the Crown was Torrejón de la Ribera, bought by order of Felipe V on 29 June 1746 for a royal superfine cloth factory. After Felipe V died, Fernando VI took possession of the place and the new industrial settlement developed under his reign.

The “San Fernando” part ultimately evokes Saint Ferdinand, normally identified with Ferdinand III of Castile and Leon, the medieval king known as “el Santo.” A bibliographic authority record of Spain's Biblioteca Virtual del Patrimonio Bibliografico gives him as Fernando III, king of Castile, saint, 1201-1252, and notes his canonization in 1671. In this station name, however, the saint is not the immediate object being named: the railway station is named for the municipality, and the municipality's royal name links the Bourbon founder Fernando VI with his saintly namesake.

The second half of the name, “de Henares,” belongs to a later layer of official toponymy. In the national municipal-name reform published in the Gaceta de Madrid on 2 July 1916, “San Fernando, partido de Alcala de Henares” was ordered to be called “San Fernando de Henares.” Some local accounts say the town had also been known as San Fernando de Jarama, which is geographically understandable because the Jarama is the river closest to the historic town centre, but the 1916 official list records the pre-change municipal name simply as San Fernando.

For public wording, the safest wording is therefore: the station is named after the municipality of San Fernando de Henares; the municipality's name comes from the Royal Site of San Fernando created around Fernando VI's cloth-factory project, and ultimately from Saint Ferdinand III, with “Henares” added as the official fluvial qualifier in 1916. The station should also be distinguished from the Metro Line 7 station called San Fernando in the town centre, although official transport pages can use the shortened name San Fernando for the Cercanias stop as well.
