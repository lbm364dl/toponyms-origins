Canillejas station is named for the Madrid barrio around it, but that barrio preserves a much older place-name. Before it was part of the capital, Canillejas was a small settlement and later an independent municipality on Madrid's eastern edge, near the historic road from Madrid toward Alcala de Henares and Aragon.

The usual explanation of the name links it with **canilla** or **canillas**: words connected with a small pipe, spout, tap or conduit. That interpretation fits the landscape well. Madrid's historic water system, the **viajes de agua**, collected underground water in the north and northeast of the city, including the areas around Canillas and Canillejas, and carried it toward the old town through galleries and channels.

The historical record is clearer for the place than for the exact linguistic origin. A 2019 heritage file for Santa Maria la Blanca cites the 1579 *Relaciones Topograficas* of Philip II, where Canillejas appears as a place under Madrid's jurisdiction, in the archbishopric of Toledo, about a league and a half from Madrid. Later sources describe its church, fields, gardens and springs, showing a rural settlement long before the metro arrived.

Canillejas lost its municipal independence during Madrid's postwar expansion. A decree dated 24 June 1949 approved the total annexation of the municipal district and town council of Canillejas to Madrid; archival inventories record the effective annexation as 30 March 1950. The name later survived as a barrio and, in 2012, was restored to the district name San Blas-Canillejas after a municipal vote recognizing the historical municipality.

Metro gave the name a new transport role when Line 5 was extended eastward from Ciudad Lineal to Canillejas. The inauguration record dated 18 January 1980 lists the new stretch with three stations: Suanzes, Torre Arias and Canillejas. The station name is therefore secure as a place-name; the water-conduit etymology is probable and well contextualized, but it should be presented as a traditional or likely explanation unless a specialist toponymic source is later found.
