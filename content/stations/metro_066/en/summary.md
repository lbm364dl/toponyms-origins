Canillejas is a place-name before it is a metro name. Metro Line 5 reached this point in January 1980 and named the station after the surrounding barrio, heir to the former municipality of Canillejas. The strongest historical context is the old settlement on the road to Aragon and Alcala de Henares, documented in early modern sources and annexed to Madrid by the mid-20th century. The usual etymology connects Canillejas with canilla or canillas, small pipes, spouts or conduits; official Madrid material confirms that the Canillas-Canillejas area lay near the catchment zones of the historic viajes de agua, although that does not by itself prove the toponym. The best public wording should therefore present the water etymology as probable, not as absolutely settled. The official Callejero adds local-road evidence for the inherited place-name, including Camino de Canillas a Canillejas in the 1950s and later Avenida de Canillejas a Vicálvaro and Plaza de la Villa de Canillejas rows.
