Leganés Central is a deliberately practical Metro name. It points passengers to the central rail interchange of Leganés: Comunidad de Madrid places the Line 12 station on Calle Virgen del Camino, beside the Renfe station, where it connects with Cercanías line C-5.

The second part of the name, Central, is therefore not an ancient place-name but a transport description. It distinguishes this stop from the other MetroSur stations in Leganés and signals that this is the town’s main rail connection, tied to the older railway station that Renfe records officially as Leganés.

Behind the station name is the municipal name Leganés. Local historical tradition, repeated by the town council, says that settlers from places such as Butarque and Overa moved in the late thirteenth century to a higher and healthier site near water. In that account, the early settlement was associated with Legamar or Leganar and with wet clay, mud, or silt.

The key early explanation comes through the sixteenth-century Relaciones Topográficas tradition. Comunidad de Madrid reproduces the statement that the place was called Leganar because there was a lagoon where much légamo formed, and that Leganés later came from Leganar by corruption of the word. The RAE defines légamo as sticky mud or silt and also as the clayey part of farmland, which fits the landscape-based explanation.

The caution is important. Toponomasticon Hispaniae, in a specialist entry by E. Nieto Ballester, calls Leganés a difficult toponym: the légamo/légano explanation is valuable because it is old and local, but the linguistic step from those words to Leganés is not fully clear. Other possibilities, including a link through Liébana/lebanés or the Latin personal name Libanius, are considered less or more plausible in different ways. For public wording, the best wording is that the station is named for central Leganés, while the town name is traditionally and plausibly, but not conclusively, connected with mud or silt.
