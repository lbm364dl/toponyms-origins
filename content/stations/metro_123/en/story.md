The direct origin of the station name is local and practical: Pío XII station is named for Avenida de Pío XII, the avenue under and beside which the station sits in Chamartín. The current CRTM station page lists the Line 9 stop as Pío XII and places its accesses at Avenida de Pío XII numbers 4 and 16.

The older name behind the avenue is Pope Pius XII, born Eugenio Maria Giuseppe Pacelli in Rome on 2 March 1876. He was elected pope on 2 March 1939, took the name Pius XII, and led the Roman Catholic Church until his death at Castel Gandolfo on 9 October 1958. In Spanish street form, the papal name becomes simply Pío XII.

That biography gives the station name a heavy twentieth-century background. Pius XII's pontificate began months before the outbreak of World War II and continued through the early Cold War. His wartime record remains debated: admirers emphasize Vatican relief work and protection efforts, while critics focus on his cautious public language and alleged silence in the face of genocide.

Madrid's avenue also belongs to a Spanish political and religious setting. The road name was already in use by 31 December 1950, when EMT records a bus route between Cibeles and Avda. Pio XII. During Pius XII's pontificate, Franco's Spain and the Holy See signed the 1953 Concordat, a major agreement that recognized Catholicism as the sole religion of the Spanish nation and formalized close Church-state relations.

For the Metro station, the evidence is strongest for an indirect naming chain: pope to avenue, avenue to station. A contemporary El País report says the Plaza de Castilla-Avenida de América section of Line 9 entered service on 30 December 1983 and included Pío XII among its six stations. No earlier station name or later renaming was found.
