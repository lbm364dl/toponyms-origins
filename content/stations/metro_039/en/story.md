The Metro station is named for the nearby Calle de O'Donnell, close to the junction with Calle del Doctor Esquerdo and the Royal Mint area. When Line 6 opened its first Pacífico-Cuatro Caminos section in 1979, O'Donnell was one of the ten stations in that new circular route.

Behind the street name stands [Leopoldo O'Donnell y Joris](https://historia-hispanica.rah.es/biografias/32598-leopoldo-odonnell-y-joris), born in Santa Cruz de Tenerife in 1809 and dead in Biarritz in 1867. The Real Academia de la Historia identifies him as 1st Duke of Tetuán, Count of Lucena, military officer, founder and head of the Liberal Union, and president of the Council of Ministers.

O'Donnell's name became especially visible after the Spanish-Moroccan War of 1859-1860. Official Gaceta material from March 1860 styles him as Duke of Tetuán, Count of Lucena, and commander-in-chief of the Spanish army in Africa during the preliminary peace arrangements with Morocco.

The street belongs to the Madrid that expanded eastward after the old city enclosure was removed. That enclosure is better described as the Cerca de Felipe IV, an early-modern boundary and fiscal barrier, rather than as a medieval wall. The present official street listing places Calle de O'Donnell in the Retiro and Salamanca districts, matching the station's position between those urban areas.

The etymology is therefore well supported, but with one important nuance: the station was not named directly by Metro after the statesman in isolation; it inherited the already established street name. No former station name was found, and the current name appears to have been in use from the station's opening on 11 October 1979.
