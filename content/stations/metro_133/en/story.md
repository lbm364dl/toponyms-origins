El Carmen station preserves the name of a vanished religious building: the Capilla del Carmen. The official history of the nearby Parroquia Espíritu Santo says explicitly that Metro El Carmen took its name from that chapel, which stood at today’s Calle de Alcalá 246, by the station area.

The chapel belonged to a much older edge-of-city landscape around the Puente de Ventas. This was the meeting point of the road to Alcalá, later the Carretera de Aragón and today Calle de Alcalá, with the Abroñigal stream, now buried under the M-30. West of the stream lay Madrid; east of it was then Vicálvaro, where a poor and fast-growing settlement developed in the early twentieth century.

The Capilla del Carmen opened for public worship in 1928 at Carretera de Aragón 40. The parish history links it to the Congregación de las Damas Apostólicas del Corazón de Jesús and describes it as both a chapel and a school, created to serve poor families, children, and marginal neighbourhoods around Las Ventas. Its dedication points to Our Lady of Mount Carmel, the Virgen del Carmen, but the immediate source of the station name is the local chapel itself.

After the Spanish Civil War, the chapel became more than a dependent place of worship. On 29 June 1940 the Parroquia Espíritu Santo was canonically erected there, recovering the older name of the Ventas del Espíritu Santo area while using the former Capilla del Carmen as its first seat. As the district urbanised and grew, the small building became insufficient; the present parish church on Calle Ricardo Ortiz was inaugurated and consecrated in 1968.

Metro arrived in 1964, with El Carmen opened on the Ventas-Ciudad Lineal section, initially worked as part of line 2 and later integrated into line 5 on 20 July 1970. The chapel has disappeared, and the parish itself moved away from Calle de Alcalá, but the station kept the older local name. The evidence is strong for the chapel as the direct namesake; the broader religious figure behind it is the Virgen del Carmen.
