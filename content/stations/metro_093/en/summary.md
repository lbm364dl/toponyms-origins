Canal station is named after the Canal de Isabel II, whose historic reservoirs, exhibition spaces and sports facilities occupy the surrounding area of Chamberí. The name is not a generic canal but the proper name of Madrid's great nineteenth-century water-supply enterprise. A royal decree of 18 June 1851 ordered the works to bring healthy water to Madrid by a canal from the Lozoya River and specified the name Canal de Isabel II. The water reached Madrid on 24 June 1858, a major urban milestone for a city long constrained by scarce and uneven water supply. The nearby Third Reservoir adds important local context, although its construction history began before the often-cited 1907-1915 final phase.
