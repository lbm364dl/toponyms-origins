Getafe Industrial is named for the industrial landscape around the station. It is not a commemorative name or a personal dedication: it describes the part of Getafe that the stop serves, where rail access, factories, industrial estates and the Madrid-Andalusia road corridor shaped the eastern edge of the municipality.

The station is much older than the modern Cercanias label. It stands on the Madrid-Aranjuez railway, opened in 1851, one of the earliest railway lines in peninsular Spain. Local railway history records that the same station was first known simply as Getafe, later as Getafe-Alicante, and eventually as Getafe Industrial.

The intermediate name, Getafe-Alicante, made sense in railway terms. From Getafe the line formed part of the great Madrid-Alicante axis, and the qualifier also helped distinguish it from Getafe's other station on the Madrid-Badajoz route, later Getafe Central. A 1970s railway archive record still describes the site through the Madrid-Alicante line and notes its present identification as Getafe-Industrial.

The industrial meaning is not just generic. Around the station were factories such as Uralita and, nearby, the aviation complex that grew from the Getafe airfield and the CASA works. Getafe's aviation history began with the Paris-Madrid air race reaching the Dehesa de Santa Quiteria in 1911; the military status of the aerodrome was decided in 1920, and CASA built its first Getafe works in the 1920s. Today Airbus Defence and Space keeps that aerospace thread visible in Getafe, with Eurofighter final assembly, A330 MRTT conversion, A400M maintenance and space-related work.

The best-supported reading is therefore descriptive: the station is named for Getafe's industrial area, not directly for the air base or for CASA/Airbus. The exact administrative date when Getafe-Alicante became Getafe Industrial was not found in an inspected naming order; CRTM's historical railway plan says the station was Getafe in 1851, became Getafe-Alicante in 1879, and was later renamed Getafe Industrial. Timetable evidence still shows Getafe Alicante in September 1985, so the precise change should remain open until a Renfe/Adif circular or official timetable transition is checked.
