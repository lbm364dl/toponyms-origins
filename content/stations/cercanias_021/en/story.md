The station name comes from two very local references: Las Margaritas, the Getafe neighbourhood beside the railway, and the nearby Getafe campus of Universidad Carlos III de Madrid. In public transport use the compound name has served to tell passengers both the residential area and the university destination.

Las Margaritas is not a medieval or personal name in this context. It is a modern neighbourhood name whose urban identity is strongly floral. Official regional documentation for regeneration works in the area lists many streets with flower names, including Adelfa, Alhelí, Amapola, Azahar, Azucena, Camelias, Clavel, Crisantemo, Flor de Lis, Geranio, Gladiolo, Hortensia, Jazmín, Lila, Loto, Nardos, Pensamiento and Rosa. That pattern supports the reading of margaritas as daisies, although the same documents also show that not every street in the area follows the flower theme.

The transport chronology begins before the university label. A contemporary El País notice reported that the new Renfe halt of Las Margaritas would begin service on 23 January 1987, with Madrid-Parla commuter trains stopping there. Railway lists also place the halt at kilometre point 12/855 on the Madrid-Badajoz route and give the same 23 January 1987 service date.

The second half of the name reflects Getafe's later transformation into a university district. Universidad Carlos III de Madrid was created by Law 9/1989 of 5 May 1989, and the law placed the Faculty of Social and Legal Sciences on the Getafe campus. The university's own travel information identifies Las Margaritas Universidad as the train stop for the Getafe campus, a short walk from Calle Madrid 126.

The most important caution is current usage. The Madrid transport consortium still lists the station as Las Margaritas - Universidad, and Getafe's municipal neighbourhood page also uses Las Margaritas Universidad. By contrast, Adif's station page and Renfe's station sheet identify code 37010 simply as Las Margaritas. The old compound name is therefore etymologically clear, but the record should flag the current official naming mismatch and treat the reported 28 May 1995 renaming date as secondary until confirmed in a primary railway or municipal record.
