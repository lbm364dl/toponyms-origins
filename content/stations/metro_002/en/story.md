The station name comes directly from the Gran Vía above it. Metro de Madrid places the station at the meeting point of Calle Montera and Gran Vía, around the Red de San Luis, a central junction where Montera, Fuencarral and Hortaleza converge. In everyday terms, the name is literal: this is the Metro station for Madrid's Gran Vía.

The older story is the making of the avenue itself. Madrid's historic centre had narrow, congested streets, and 19th-century reformers wanted a broad east-west route that could link Calle de Alcalá with the western side of the city without forcing all movement through Puerta del Sol. An early proposal by architect Carlos Velasco was presented in 1886, but the project that shaped the modern avenue was later developed by Francisco Andrés Octavio and José López Salaberry.

Work began ceremonially on 4 April 1910, when Alfonso XIII opened the demolitions at the rectory of the church of San José. The official Madrid educational guide describes a route of about 1,316 metres divided into three sections, with widths of 25 to 35 metres. Its construction removed, cut or transformed older streets and buildings, replacing part of the old centre with a modern commercial and architectural showcase.

The Metro arrived while the new avenue was still being built. Metro de Madrid's historical file records the Line 1 station opening on 17 October 1919, as part of the first Cuatro Caminos-Sol section; the project documentation also used Red de San Luis, the traditional name of the spot. Because the station was deep, Antonio Palacios designed a notable entrance pavilion with a lift, later dismantled and then recreated in the 2021 refurbishment.

The name also reflects Madrid's political history. In 1940 the station was renamed José Antonio, following the Franco-era naming of the avenue after José Antonio Primo de Rivera. Madrid's democratic city council changed the street name back to Gran Vía by municipal agreement on 25 January 1980, and the Metro station recovered its original name on 27 June 1983. The origin is therefore secure, but the exact chronology should keep the street and station renamings separate.
