Paco de Lucía station takes its name directly from the flamenco guitarist Paco de Lucía, born Francisco Sánchez Gómez in Algeciras in 1947 and deceased in Playa del Carmen, Mexico, on 25 February 2014. The Madrid regional government announced the tribute two days later, choosing his name for the new Line 9 station then being completed in the Mirasierra area.

The name was not an old local place-name. During planning and works, the station was commonly identified with Calle Costa Brava, where it was being built, and contemporary reports describe it as the future Costa Brava station. The change to Paco de Lucía was explicitly commemorative: a posthumous recognition of a Spanish musician whose work helped carry flamenco far beyond its traditional circuits.

Paco de Lucía’s artistic name also carries its own small toponymic story. He was Francisco, or Paco, the son of Lucía Gomes Gonçalves, his Portuguese mother; in Andalusian social usage, identifying a child through the mother’s name could distinguish one Paco from another. Under that name he became a central figure in modern flamenco, from the success of ‘Entre dos aguas’ to collaborations that opened the guitar to jazz and other musical languages.

The station opened on 25 March 2015, extending Line 9 by about 1.4 kilometres from Mirasierra and making Paco de Lucía the line’s northern terminus. The transport context later became intermodal when the adjoining Cercanías station Mirasierra-Paco de Lucía opened above it, linking the Metro stop with suburban rail services.

The tribute is unusually visible inside the station. CRTM’s 2015 annual report describes it as the first Metro station in Spain decorated with urban art, including a 300-square-metre mural of Paco de Lucía by Okuda and Rosh333, with Antonyo Marest and Madrid Street Art Project. The evidence for the naming is therefore strong: the name was chosen in 2014 as a public homage to the musician, not inherited from the street or neighbourhood.
