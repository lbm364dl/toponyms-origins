Somosaguas Centro is a practical transport name: it identifies the ML2 stop serving the central part of the Somosaguas area of Pozuelo de Alarcón. The Community of Madrid places the station on the M-502 corridor, in the southern part of Parque Empresarial La Finca and connected by footbridge with the Zoco de Pozuelo.

The older name behind the station is Somosaguas. Its first element belongs to the old Romance family of somo, a word that the RAE derives from Latin summum and defines as an obsolete word for a summit or the highest point of mountains. A linguistic article in the Boletín de la Real Academia Española treats the Madrid names Somosierra and Somosaguas as examples of this family and notes a medieval form somas aquas in the Fuero de Madrid.

That makes the usual explanation, 'high waters' or 'waters at the high place', more than a modern guess, although the exact medieval landscape reference cannot be pinned to one surviving spring. Pozuelo itself has long been associated with wells and springs, and local history records a lost Fuente de Somosaguas near the old road from Pozuelo to Húmera, with ferruginous water and traces of copper minerals.

Somosaguas also appears in the medieval history of Pozuelo. The town council's history page says that early Pozuelo included the villages of Pozuelo and Húmera and the hamlets of San Juan de Somosaguas and San Pedro de Meaque; the latter two were depopulated during the fratricidal struggles between Pedro I and Enrique II in the fourteenth century. In modern times the name passed into residential, university, business and transport geography.

The station name itself dates from the opening of ML2 on 27 July 2007. There is no strong evidence of a previous station name. The etymology is best treated as verified for the broad origin, while avoiding unrelated crime-story emphasis: the 1980 Urquijo murders occurred in Somosaguas and are part of the area's media memory, but they did not name the station.
