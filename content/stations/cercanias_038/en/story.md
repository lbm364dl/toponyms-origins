The Cercanías station name is direct: Torrejón de Ardoz is the railway stop for the municipality of the same name, east of Madrid in the Henares corridor. The transport sources identify the station with that municipal name, so the station itself does not appear to preserve a separate dedication or commemorative naming story.

The place name has two parts. Torrejón belongs to a large Spanish family of tower-based toponyms, but the most careful toponymic explanation is not just 'tower plus suffix'. Toponomasticon Hispaniae describes it as a diminutive formation developed from torreja, itself linked to Latin turris through Romance turricula, with later -ón in a form that no longer works simply as an augmentative in modern Spanish.

Local historical writing gives the name a physical setting: an early settlement around a defensive tower in the frontier landscape connected with the Castilian conquest of Alcalá and its territory. The municipal history Torrejón de Ardoz: una historia viva says the exact foundation date of the town is uncertain, but places the tower tradition in the line of fortifications separating Christian and Muslim domains in the eleventh-century frontier zone. Toponomasticon Hispaniae also links the primitive village to a defensive tower and to its dependence on Alcalá.

Ardoz is the water-name in the compound. The stream called Arroyo Ardoz ran through the municipal term and gave the settlement its distinguishing second element, in the same general way that many Spanish towns are identified by nearby rivers or streams. Unlike Torrejón, however, Ardoz is not securely explained: Toponomasticon Hispaniae calls it an opaque hydronym and only notes possible parallels such as Arduix and Ardós.

The documentary record supports the antiquity of the compound rather than a modern railway coinage. Toponomasticon Hispaniae cites Torrejón de Hardoz in 1491 and Torrejón de Ardoz in 1528, and the municipal history records that the locality received its Carta de Villazgo on 6 September 1554. The best-supported public explanation is therefore: the station is named after the municipality; the municipality's name joins a tower-related medieval settlement name with the Arroyo Ardoz, whose own origin remains unresolved.
