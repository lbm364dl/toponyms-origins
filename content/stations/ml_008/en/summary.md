Palas de Rey is a surface stop on ML1, beside Calle de Palas de Rey in the Las Tablas area of Madrid. The street name refers to Palas de Rei, a municipality in Lugo on the French Way to Santiago de Compostela. Medieval sources and heritage studies connect the place with forms such as Pallatium Regis or Palatinum Regis, meaning a royal palace or palace of the king. Local tradition links that palace to the Visigothic king Witiza, but Galician toponymy specialists warn that the element Palas is not securely explained by Latin palatium. The station-name origin is therefore secure as a Madrid street/place name, while the older Galician etymology is best presented as partly traditional and partly debated. The official Callejero resolves the street-date part of the record: Calle de Palas de Rey is recorded from 25 September 1998, nearly nine years before the ML1 stop opened.
