El Casar station takes its name from the El Casar area of Getafe Norte and from the nearby Avenida del Casar. In transport terms the name is a local place-name: the CRTM places the access at Avenida del Casar, and Getafe's municipal description identifies El Casar as one of the MetroSur stations serving Getafe Norte.

Behind that modern urban name is an older Spanish word. Casar is a noun derived from casa, "house", and the Real Academia Espanola defines it as a group of houses that does not amount to a town, with an obsolete sense referring to a ruined settlement or old building remains. Toponomasticon Hispaniae gives the same explanation for the place-name El Casar: casa plus the collective-abundancial suffix -ar, comparable in formation to villar.

The modern district context is much more recent. Getafe's own history page says that the city expanded from an agricultural town into a large industrial and residential municipality during the twentieth century, and that Getafe Norte was built at the end of the 1990s. The name El Casar was already part of the planning language before the neighbourhood was fully built: a 1991 BOE notice records the urbanisation works for Sector UP-I "El Casar" in Getafe.

The station's transport history mirrors that urban growth. MetroSur, Line 12, entered service on 11 April 2003, creating a circular metro link among the main southern municipalities and including El Casar as one of Getafe's stations. The interchange later gained strategic weight when Madrid extended Line 3 from Villaverde Alto to El Casar, connecting Line 12, Line 3, Cercanias C-3 and several bus routes in one node.

The etymology is therefore well supported, but with one useful precision: the station is directly named after a local place and street, not directly coined from a dictionary word for the railway. The dictionary and toponymic sources explain why that local place was called El Casar, while municipal and transport sources confirm the Getafe Norte setting and the station's role over time.
