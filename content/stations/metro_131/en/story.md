The Metro station is named after Ciudad Lineal, the Madrid place-name around the eastern end of Calle de Alcalá and Calle de Arturo Soria. In public transport terms, it marks a long-standing interchange area rather than a name invented for the Metro: today's CRTM station page places Ciudad Lineal on Line 5 and lists accesses at Calle de Alcalá, Calle de Arturo Soria and Hermanos García Noblejas.

Behind the place-name is Arturo Soria y Mata's famous urban idea. In 1892 Soria published his Proyecto de Ciudad Lineal alrededor de Madrid, proposing a new kind of city arranged along a transport corridor instead of expanding by compact blocks around a centre. The Madrid City Council's district history identifies that 1892 project as the origin of the district name.

The project became physical in the 1890s. The Compañía Madrileña de Urbanización was constituted in 1894, and the first stone of the Ciudad Lineal was laid on 16 July 1894 in what was then open land near the old municipalities east and northeast of Madrid. Municipal heritage material describes the scheme as hygienist, anti-speculative, tied to nature and based on public transport and social mixture.

Soria's ambition was much larger than what was built. Madrid's tourism office describes a planned linear ring of about 53 kilometres, from Fuencarral toward Pozuelo de Alarcón, but says that only the first section between Chamartín de la Rosa and the old carretera de Aragón was urbanized between 1892 and 1911. The surviving Calle de Arturo Soria, today about six kilometres long according to that source, preserves the memory of the intended axis, although the original tram and many early buildings have disappeared.

For the station itself, the name was already in use when the Metro arrived. The Ventas-Ciudad Lineal section opened on 28 May 1964 as an extension then associated with Line 2, and the route was later incorporated into Line 5. The existing wording's basic etymology is therefore verified, but it should avoid implying that the station was named after the present administrative district alone; the safer wording is that it is named for the established Ciudad Lineal place-name, ultimately derived from Arturo Soria's urban project.
