San Fermín-Orcasur is not the name of a single old village, parish, or estate. It is a compound Metro name, joining two neighbouring places in Usera: San Fermín and Orcasur. The station was opened on 21 April 2007 as part of the southern extension of Line 3, built to improve access for several southern Madrid neighbourhoods.

The San Fermín half of the name goes back to the Colonia San Fermín. Local accounts connect that name with Federico Mayo Gayarre, the Pamplona-born first director of the Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda, who directed postwar housing policy under the Franco regime. The colony's Navarrese references are still visible in nearby street names such as Estafeta, Elizondo, Oteiza and Lecumberri.

The saint behind that local naming is San Fermín, strongly associated with Pamplona and Navarra. A useful correction is that San Fermín is not simply 'the patron of Navarra' in isolation: official Navarrese sources describe San Fermín and San Francisco Javier as co-patrons of the old Kingdom of Navarra from 1657. In Madrid, the religious name arrived through a twentieth-century housing and street-naming decision, not through a medieval shrine.

The Orcasur half is more urbanistic than devotional. COAM's architectural guide describes Orcasur as the result of the remodelling of three Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda settlements: the Poblado Mínimo de Orcasitas, the Poblado de Absorción de Orcasitas and the Poblado Agrícola. That history explains why Orcasur is linked to Orcasitas even when the modern administrative barrio has its own identity.

The deeper origin of Orcasitas is less securely documented online. Isabel Gea's Madrid toponymy tradition, repeated in local blogs, says Orcasitas came from Pedro Orcasitas, owner of much of the land; other sources connect the Horcasitas/Orcasitas family with Arcentales in Biscay. Those surname details are plausible but should be checked against Gea's printed work and land records before being displayed as certain.
