The station name is a two-part place name. **Móstoles** identifies the municipality it serves, while **El Soto** points to the western neighbourhood and green area around Calle Granada, where Renfe locates the station. Renfe's own station information says Móstoles-El Soto operates on Cercanias line C-5 and stands in the El Soto neighbourhood of Móstoles.

The older word behind the second half is simple but very physical. The Real Academia Española gives **soto** as a place populated by trees and shrubs, especially in riverbanks or floodplains, and traces it to Latin **saltus**, meaning 'wood' or 'forest'. That makes El Soto a landscape name before it is a transport name: it evokes a wooded edge, not a person or an event.

In Móstoles, that meaning is reinforced by local geography. The municipality has an **Arroyo del Soto**, and the town hall lists a **Via Verde Arroyo del Soto** along the Camino del Soto de San Marcos, between Parque Natural de El Soto and Colonia Parque Guadarrama. A municipal environmental study describes the Arroyo del Soto as draining much of northern Móstoles and running toward its confluence with the Guadarrama, with a canalized urban stretch that functions as a green corridor ending at the Parque Natural del Soto.

The nearby municipal park keeps the same toponym alive in everyday use. The Ayuntamiento de Móstoles lists **Parque Natural El Soto** in the western district, classifies it as a singular park, and gives its current surface as 387,716 square metres. The station therefore names a transport terminus after a recognisable local landscape: the municipality plus the wooded Soto area around the arroyo and park.

There is also a useful correction to the station's own naming history. Renfe states that the station opened in 1984 under the name **Villaviciosa de Odón**, despite the distance from that municipality, and only later became known by the current Móstoles-El Soto name. Móstoles' famous 2 May 1808 history is important for the municipality, but it is not the direct origin of this station name; it is better treated as background to Móstoles, not as the reason for 'El Soto'.
