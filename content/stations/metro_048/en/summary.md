The Metro station was opened on 6 March 1929 as the northern terminus of Line 1's Cuatro Caminos-Tetuán extension. Its name comes from the surrounding district, historically known as Tetuán de las Victorias. Madrid municipal sources connect that barrio name with the Spanish victory in the War of Africa and the taking of Tétouan in Morocco in 1860, after troops under General Leopoldo O'Donnell returned and camped in the Dehesa de Amaniel. The most careful version is that the military episode gave the place its commemorative name, while the lasting settlement was built up by traders, visitors, workers, and migrants along the old carretera de Francia, now Bravo Murillo.
