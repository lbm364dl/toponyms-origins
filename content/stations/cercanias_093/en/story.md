The station name comes from Villalba, the railway-side name used for the station district of the municipality of Collado Villalba. Today the transport system often gives the fuller railway name as Villalba de Guadarrama, while the Cercanias service and everyday use commonly shorten it to Villalba.

Behind that short station name is the older municipal place name Collado Villalba. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats it as a clear Castilian compound: collado is a low rise or a pass through hills, and Villalba is made from villa and alba. The specialist note is important: alba is not necessarily a literal observation that the settlement was white, but may have carried a favourable sense such as clean, pure or prosperous.

The place also fits its geography. Collado Villalba lies on the southern side of the Sierra de Guadarrama, in a broad valley around the Guadarrama River and surrounded by mountain ranges and hills. Historical documentation collected by Toponomasticon records forms such as Collado Villalba in 1517, Collado de Villalba in the seventeenth century, and later official forms, showing that the name is not a modern railway invention.

The railway did, however, transform which part of the name people encountered most often. Planning-history documentation for Collado Villalba notes the sale of land at Navaluenga to the railway company in 1860 and describes how the railway, built away from the old village, produced rapid growth around the station. That new southern nucleus became locally known as La Estación, and it is the reason a station called simply Villalba can stand for a much older compound toponym.

For public display, the safest explanation is therefore: named for Villalba, the station district and railway form of Collado Villalba, whose deeper etymology is collado plus villa alba. The junction role is real, with Villalba de Guadarrama appearing in current CRTM and Adif sources for the C-8 and C-10 area, but the exact historical opening/name assignment should be checked in railway-company records if a precise naming date is required.
