Almendrales station takes its name from the Almendrales neighbourhood of Usera, beside Avenida de Córdoba. The Community of Madrid describes the 2007 Line 3 extension as serving the barrios of Almendrales and Las Carolinas in this part of Usera, so the naming chain from station to neighbourhood is clear.

The word behind the name is plain Spanish. The Real Academia Española defines **almendral** as a place populated with almond trees, and in the plural **almendrales** naturally means almond groves or almond-tree areas. That makes the local explanation attractive: before the dense southern expansion of Madrid, this edge of Usera included rural slopes, hollows and planted land.

The modern urban history of the name is layered. Madrid Film Office, a municipal filming service, places the Colonia de los Almendrales between Avenida de Córdoba, Hospital 12 de Octubre and Cerro de Usera, and says it began in 1941, with an early large housing promotion in 1941-1944. A 1954 BOE notice also mentions schools of the Mater Purissima patronage in the Colonia de los Almendrales, showing that the colony name was already official or semi-official by the mid-1950s.

Later, the name was reinforced by public housing and neighbourhood redevelopment. The COAM architectural guide records the Poblado de Almendrales, with housing for the Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda designed in 1959 and built in 1959-1966 by José Antonio Corrales, José María García de Paredes, Ramón Vázquez Molezún and Javier Carvajal. Local histories also distinguish Almendrales Altos and Almendrales Bajos, the latter associated with informal low houses built by migrant families on cheap rural plots.

The safest public reading is therefore: the station is named for the neighbourhood, and the neighbourhood name probably preserves a descriptive rural toponym connected with almond trees. The specific story that an owner sold almond-planted plots around 1954 is plausible but currently rests on secondary local-history writing. I found no stronger source for the exact phrase 'Cerro de los Almendros', so that wording should be treated as unverified rather than central.
