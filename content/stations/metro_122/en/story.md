Ciudad Universitaria station takes its name from the place it serves: Madrid's Ciudad Universitaria, the large academic campus and neighbourhood in the district of Moncloa-Aravaca. The name is descriptive, meaning 'University City', and points to a planned concentration of university buildings rather than to a saint, ruler, or individual benefactor.

The deeper origin of the name lies in a 20th-century urban project. Madrid's university faculties had long been scattered through the old city, and the new campus was conceived as a modern solution: a spacious university precinct in the north-west, on land associated with La Moncloa. The Universidad Complutense identifies the formal beginning with the royal decree of 17 May 1927, when Alfonso XIII created the Junta Constructora de la Ciudad Universitaria.

The project was not only educational but also architectural and political. It was promoted during Alfonso XIII's reign and the Primo de Rivera dictatorship, drew on international campus models, and placed technical direction in the hands of architect Modesto López Otero. Construction began in 1929, and before the Civil War important groups of buildings had already been planned or built, including medical, science, humanities, and hospital facilities.

The campus then became one of the most historically charged landscapes in Madrid. It was badly damaged during the Civil War and later rebuilt and expanded. Today the area is associated especially with the Universidad Complutense de Madrid and the Universidad Politécnica de Madrid, with UNED and other institutions nearby; it is also an official Madrid neighbourhood and has been protected as a historic ensemble since 1999.

The metro name is therefore well supported: it is a direct place-name borrowed from the surrounding campus and neighbourhood. The station opened on 13 January 1987, when Line 6 was extended from Cuatro Caminos to Ciudad Universitaria, and for eight years it served as the end of the line before the 1995 extension helped complete Line 6 as a circular route.
