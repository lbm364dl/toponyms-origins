Montecarmelo station takes its immediate name from the Montecarmelo urban area in the district of Fuencarral-El Pardo. The station was part of MetroNorte, the northern extension of Line 10 built to connect Tres Olivos, the new northern Madrid neighbourhoods, Alcobendas and San Sebastián de los Reyes. In public-facing terms, the name is first a place name: the station is called after the area it serves.

That area was not named from a person, but from an older local toponym, usually written Monte Carmelo or Montecarmelo. Municipal mapping shows the surrounding area as Barrio del Monte Carmelo, and Madrid planning documents identify the development as UZI.0.07 Montecarmelo (PP II.2). Several secondary accounts explain the name through a nearby stream called Monte Carmelo or Montecarmelo, but the available sources did not include an official source that conclusively documents the hydronym as the legal origin of the PAU name.

The words Monte Carmelo point to a much older religious geography. Mount Carmel, in the Holy Land, is central to Carmelite tradition: the Carmelite province in Spain describes the order’s origin on Monte Carmelo more than eight centuries ago, and the name spread widely through Carmelite devotion and the cult of Our Lady of Mount Carmel. In Madrid, this religious layer fits the wider local landscape, where many Montecarmelo streets carry monastery names.

A nearby historical anchor is the Sanctuary of Nuestra Señora de Valverde, north of Montecarmelo. The 2021 decree declaring it a Bien de Interés Cultural says the sanctuary stands near the old road from Madrid to Colmenar Viejo and records a local legend placing the Virgin’s apparition to shepherds in 1242. This is valuable context for the area’s religious and rural past, but it should be presented carefully: the Valverde legend explains the sanctuary, not directly the Metro station name.

The safest etymology is therefore layered. Montecarmelo station is named after the modern Montecarmelo development and neighbourhood; that development preserves the older Monte Carmelo place name; and the older name probably belongs to the family of Carmelite and Mount Carmel religious toponyms. The direct PAU-to-stream-to-Israel chain remains plausible, but it needs a stronger municipal, cartographic or archival source before being marked fully verified.
