The station name comes directly from Plaza de Alonso Martínez, the major crossroads above the Metro stop. Metro de Madrid's historical station sheet places the line 4 station under the square and explains that the square took its current name in honor of Manuel Alonso Martínez.

Manuel Alonso Martínez was born in Burgos on 1 January 1827 and died in Madrid on 13 January 1891. He was a jurist and politician, trained in Philosophy and Law at Madrid's Universidad Central, and held several ministerial offices, including Public Works, Finance, and Grace and Justice.

His public career made the name a natural fit for a prominent Madrid plaza. The Congress of Deputies records him as a deputy for several constituencies and as president of the chamber in the 1889-1890 legislature. His most durable legal association is the codification work that led to the Spanish Civil Code, published by Royal Decree of 24 July 1889.

The place-name also preserves an older layer of Madrid geography. The square was previously known as the Glorieta de Santa Bárbara, connected with the old Santa Bárbara gate or portillo in the city boundary. Metro's own history note says the square became Alonso Martínez in 1891 and links the tribute partly to his influence in the development of the Canal de Isabel II.

The station itself opened on line 4 on 24 March 1944, later becoming an interchange with line 5 in 1970 and line 10 in 1981. The etymology is therefore well supported, but the biographical wording should be kept precise: Alonso Martínez was central to the Civil Code process, yet the code was prepared through a legal and commission-based process under the Law of Bases, which listed 27 bases, not 26.
