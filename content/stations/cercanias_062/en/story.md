The station’s name is practical and very local. **El Barrial** is the older place-name; **Centro Comercial Pozuelo** was added to point passengers toward the large shopping centre next to the stop, historically identified with Hipercor Pozuelo.

The word **barrial** comes from **barro**, mud or clay. In the RAE dictionary, barrial is a kind of ground that is clayey or muddy, so the place-name probably began as a description of the terrain before it became a transport name.

The modern railway name belongs to the 1990s transformation of Madrid’s western edge. El País reported that Hipercor Pozuelo opened on 15 September 1994 by the A-6, near the future M-40 connection, and that El Corte Inglés, the regional transport authority and Renfe had agreed extra time to finish a Cercanías stop at the El Barrial junction.

Today the station functions as a borderland stop: operator data places it at Calle Rafael Botí / Vía Ferrocarril and transport maps list it on the C-7 and C-10 corridor between Pozuelo and Majadahonda. Municipal sources from Pozuelo also treat El Barrial as a working access point for nearby employment areas such as Cerro de los Gamos and for the Hipercor shopping area.

The broad etymology is secure, but one detail should be handled carefully. Secondary rail sources say the stop opened as **El Barrial** on 23 December 1994 and later took the fuller commercial name, often reported as two years later; I found strong official confirmation of the current name, but not an official naming order giving the exact renaming date.
