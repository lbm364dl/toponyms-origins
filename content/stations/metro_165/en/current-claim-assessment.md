The core claim is correct: the station name comes from the nearby historic garden Parque de El Capricho. Several details need tightening: official sources say the duchess bought the estate and adjoining lands between 1783 and 1803, the Goya witchcraft canvases were sold to the Dukes of Osuna in June 1798 rather than securely documented here as a personal commission by the duchess alone, and the park opens on weekends and public holidays, not only weekends.
