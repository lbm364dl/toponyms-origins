El Capricho station is named after the nearby Parque de El Capricho, one of Madrid’s most distinctive historic gardens. The clearest transport source is the Community of Madrid’s account of the Line 5 extension: it says that the station, located near the former Vía de la Gasolina and Calle Pinos de Osuna, received its name because of its proximity to the historic park of the same name.

The place behind the station name was the personal creation of María Josefa Alonso Pimentel, XII Countess-Duchess of Benavente and IX Duchess consort of Osuna. Madrid City Council explains that she bought the estate and adjoining land between 1783 and 1803 and gave it the name El Capricho. In this setting, capricho means more than a passing whim: it describes a highly personal pleasure estate, carefully staged for beauty, play, love, festivity and theatrical surprise.

The garden’s design helps explain why the name endured. El Capricho combines a French parterre, an English landscape garden and an older Italian giardino, and it is filled with small architectural fantasies also called caprichos: the apiary, the hermitage, the fort, the dance casino, the landing stage and the Casa de la Vieja. The estate was part of the enlightened aristocratic culture of late eighteenth-century Madrid, visited and imagined as a world apart from the city.

Its cultural associations are unusually rich for a Metro station name. Goya’s witchcraft series was linked to the Osuna country house at La Alameda: the Prado records that Vuelo de brujas belonged to a group of six canvases sold in June 1798 to the Dukes of Osuna for that residence, while the Museo Lázaro Galdiano identifies El Aquelarre as part of the group made for the dukes’ estate. The park also contains the Civil War bunker known as Posición Jaca, used by the headquarters of the Republican Army of the Centre and built about 1937 at roughly 15 metres below ground.

The station itself opened on 24 November 2006, with Alameda de Osuna, when Line 5 was extended beyond Canillejas. A projected or provisional name, Parque de la Luz, appears in non-official contemporary and derived sources, but the stronger official evidence supports El Capricho as the opened and current name. The etymology is therefore secure: the Metro name is a place-name borrowed from the historic garden, whose own name was deliberately chosen by the Duchess of Osuna.
