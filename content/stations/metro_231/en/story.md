The name **Fuenlabrada Central** is first of all practical: it identifies the Line 12 MetroSur station serving the central railway interchange of Fuenlabrada. The Community of Madrid places the Metro station beside the Renfe Cercanías station, with access from Paseo de Roma and Paseo del Ferrocarril, so the word *Central* distinguishes this stop from the other MetroSur stations in the municipality.

Behind the station name is the older place-name **Fuenlabrada**. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats it as a clear Castilian compound of *fuente* and *labrada*: literally a worked, carved, or shaped fountain. In this context, *labrada* is best understood not as 'ploughed' but as 'worked' in stone, a meaning preserved in the vocabulary of masons and stonecutters.

The classic documentary explanation comes from the 1576 Relaciones Topográficas of Philip II. In the local response, the village was said to be called Fuenlabrada because near it there was an old fountain made of worked stone and lime-and-stone masonry, and it was the local opinion that it had been made by Moors. The same tradition also says that the settlement was formed by people from nearby Loranca and Fregacedos, two abandoned villages.

The modern station belongs to the 2003 MetroSur project, a circular Line 12 built to link Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe, and Leganés without forcing every journey through central Madrid. Fuenlabrada Central is one of the line's interchange points with the Cercanías network, which is why the name points both to the town and to the station's transport function.

The etymology is strong, but one detail should be handled carefully. The 'carved fountain' explanation is supported by both early documentation and modern toponymic scholarship; the statement that the fountain was Moorish is better presented as a historical local belief recorded in the 16th century. The Metro station itself appears to have opened with its current name on 11 April 2003, while the adjacent railway station is officially Fuenlabrada.
