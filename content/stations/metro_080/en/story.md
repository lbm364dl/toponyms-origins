Puerta de Arganda is not named after a lost city gate or a monument in Vicálvaro. Its name is functional and geographic: it marks the point in Madrid where Line 9 becomes the route out toward Rivas-Vaciamadrid and Arganda del Rey. The station is also the connection with the Vicálvaro Cercanías railway station, so it became a real transport doorway between the Madrid urban network and the southeast corridor.

The place behind the name is Arganda del Rey, a municipality about 28 kilometres southeast of central Madrid. The word Arganda is much older than the metro. Jairo J. García Sánchez, writing for the Centro Virtual Cervantes, treats it as a Celtic name containing the Indo-European base *arg-, meaning 'clear', 'white' or 'bright', comparable with Latin argentum, 'silver'. That interpretation fits a wider family of place and water names where brightness or clear water may have motivated the original naming.

The station opened on 1 December 1998 as part of the Pavones-Puerta de Arganda extension of Line 9. The Community of Madrid describes that first section as a 4.65-kilometre extension serving Vicálvaro, with four new stations: Valdebernardo, Vicálvaro, San Cipriano and Puerta de Arganda. A second project, put into service on 7 April 1999, carried the line onward from Puerta de Arganda to Rivas and Arganda del Rey under the TFM concession.

That chronology explains why the word Puerta is more than a metaphor. The Community of Madrid notes that Puerta de Arganda is where the TFM fare begins and where successive zones B1, B2 and B3 start. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes page still lists Puerta de Arganda as a Line 9 station in zone A, with connections to Cercanías C-2, C-7 and C-8 and several bus routes.

There is one useful correction to the usual short version of the story. The royal identity of Arganda is not merely a modern branding choice: the local history points back to 1583, when the inhabitants paid heavily to leave the jurisdiction of the Archbishop of Toledo and become a royal town under Felipe II. INE census metadata also shows that older censuses used the name Arganda, so the modern official name Arganda del Rey needs to be handled as both an administrative form and a historical memory.
