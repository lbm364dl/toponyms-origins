The station name is local before it is royal: María Tudor station is on Calle de María Tudor, and the Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists its access at Calle de María Tudor, 15. In Madrid's light-rail geography, this is the Sanchinarro and Las Tablas branch of ML1, opened to serve new northern residential areas in the 2000s.

The woman behind the street name is María I of England, better known in Spanish as María Tudor. She was born in 1516 to Henry VIII and Catherine of Aragon, which gave her a direct family link to Spain: Catherine was the daughter of Isabel I of Castile and Fernando II of Aragon. Official British royal history identifies Mary as England's first queen regnant, a queen ruling in her own right rather than through a husband.

That Spanish connection became political in 1554, when Mary married Prince Philip, the future Felipe II of Spain. The Museo del Prado's portrait entry for Antonio Moro's 1554 image of María Tudor places her squarely in this Habsburg-Tudor context: queen of England, daughter of Henry VIII and Catherine of Aragon, and second wife of Philip II. For Spain, the marriage promised an alliance with England; for many English subjects, it raised fears of foreign influence.

The legal arrangements matter. Philip was styled as king during the marriage and the couple could be presented jointly, but the English settlement protected the realm's laws, offices and language of government, and did not give Philip a lasting personal right to the English crown if Mary died without an heir. That is a better summary than simply saying he was barred from being crowned: the point was that his authority was conditional, marital and politically constrained.

There is one local nuance for the record. The previous wording places the station in Fuencarral-El Pardo, but official municipal street listings and nearby municipal facilities place Calle de María Tudor in Hortaleza, in the Valdefuentes/Sanchinarro area. The station name itself is secure, and no former station name was found: it appears to have opened as María Tudor with ML1 on 24 May 2007.
