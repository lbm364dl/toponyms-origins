The Madrid Metro station is named in the most immediate sense after Avenida de Guadalajara. Memoria de Madrid, using material from Metro de Madrid's photographic archive, states that the station is in San Blas-Canillejas and sits under the meeting point of Avenida de Guadalajara and Avenida de Canillejas a Vicálvaro, 'from which it takes its name'.

That street-name carries a much older toponym into eastern Madrid. Guadalajara is the capital city of the province of Guadalajara, northeast of Madrid on the Henares River. Its name is not a modern commemorative invention but the Romance continuation of an Arabic place-name recorded in forms such as Wad al-Hayara, Wadi al-Hiyara or Wadi al-Hijara.

The exact spelling varies because medieval Arabic names pass through Latin, Romance and modern scholarly transcription, but the meaning is consistently explained around stone: 'river of stones' or 'valley of stones'. The Spanish Ministry of Culture's historical toponymy thesaurus says the surviving Arabic toponym was Wadi-I-Hiyara and notes that the present form Guadalajara already appears in documentation by 1341. Guadalajara's municipal tourism history also links Wad al-Hayara with the older pre-Roman name Arriaca.

The Metro name belongs to the 2011 extension of Line 2 from La Elipa to Las Rosas. The Community of Madrid's project page describes Avenida de Guadalajara as one of the new stations, a simple two-level station located on the avenue of the same name. The station opened with the rest of that extension on 16 March 2011, placing a medieval Castilian-Arabic place-name into Madrid's contemporary transport map.

The existing record's main idea is therefore sound, but the public explanation should keep the chain clear: station to avenue, avenue to the city of Guadalajara, and only then to the Arabic toponym. The claim that the avenue is the major road leading toward Guadalajara is plausible as a geographic intuition but was not supported by the strongest sources found; Madrid's main road toward Guadalajara is the A-2 corridor, not this local avenue. Details about the Palacio del Infantado are historically relevant to Guadalajara but are secondary to the station-name origin.
