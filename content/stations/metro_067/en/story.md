Torre Arias station takes its name from the Quinta de Torre Arias, the historic estate and public park that lies beside Calle de Alcalá in the district of San Blas-Canillejas. The station opened on 18 January 1980 as part of the Line 5 extension from Ciudad Lineal to Canillejas, and the name points passengers toward one of the old aristocratic estates that once marked the eastern approach to Madrid.

Before it was called Torre Arias, the estate was known as the Quinta de Canillejas. The protected heritage file says its first country house is believed to have been built around 1580 by García Alvarado Velasco, first Count of Villamor, who assembled the property by adding lands from several owners. That early quinta had a palace-house, vegetable garden, dovecote and at least one tower-like corner, a layout typical of fortified Castilian country houses of the period.

The estate changed repeatedly over the centuries. Between 1675 and 1682 the Count of Frigiliana enlarged and improved it, and by 1698 its area had roughly doubled. During the War of the Spanish Succession it was confiscated by troops of the Archduke of Austria for use as his residence. Later owners, including the Duchess of Osuna and the Marquis of Bedmar, reshaped it as a Baroque agricultural estate and then as a 19th-century landscaped quinta with gardens, waterworks, stables and farm buildings.

The name Torre Arias arrived only at the end of the 19th century. In 1895 María de los Dolores de Salabert Arteaga inherited the estate from her mother; she was married to Ildefonso Pérez de Guzmán el Bueno, VI Count of Torre Arias. The official heritage declaration states that this title gave the Quinta the name by which it is known today.

The last private owner, Tatiana Pérez de Guzmán el Bueno, died on 1 October 2012. A 1986 agreement with Madrid City Council then took effect, making the estate municipal property with the condition that it be used as a public park. The Quinta and palace were declared a Bien de Interés Cultural, in the category of historic complex, by Decree 59/2022 of 13 July 2022. The station name is therefore secure, but the older estate chronology should be stated carefully: Torre Arias is a modern name for a much older Quinta de Canillejas.
