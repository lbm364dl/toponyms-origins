The name **Alcalá de Henares Universidad** is straightforward but historically dense: it identifies the Cercanías stop with the Universidad de Alcalá. Official railway and transport sources place the station on the Carretera del Campus and list it as a Cercanías Madrid station served by C-2 and C-8, so the immediate naming logic is local access to the university campus area.

That campus name carries several centuries of history. Alcalá had a royal authorization for a Studium Generale from 1293, but the institution that made the city famous was Cisneros’s university project of 1499. The Universidad de Alcalá describes it as a new educational project shaped by Renaissance humanism and by the university models of Paris, Salamanca, Bologna, and Louvain.

The founding act is usually associated with April 1499, when Pope Alexander VI granted the bull authorizing the university project in Alcalá. The old Colegio Mayor de San Ildefonso became the heart of that academic city, and UNESCO later recognized Alcalá as the world’s first planned university city. This is the deeper historical reason why a modern suburban station can carry the word “Universidad” with more weight than a simple campus label.

One of the strongest cultural links is the **Biblia Políglota Complutense**, printed in Alcalá by Arnao Guillén de Brocar between 1514 and 1517 under Cisneros’s patronage. The Biblioteca Nacional de España describes it as the first complete Bible edition in several languages, with Latin, Greek, Hebrew, and Aramaic texts. This is better phrased as the first complete polyglot Bible, rather than simply the first multilingual Bible.

There is also a break and recovery in the story. The old University of Alcalá was moved to Madrid in the nineteenth century and is part of the institutional background of today’s Universidad Complutense de Madrid. The present Universidad de Alcalá was created in 1977 as a new public university with its seat in Alcalá de Henares; the station name therefore points both to the nearby modern campus and to a restored university identity with much older roots.
