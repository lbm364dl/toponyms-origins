The station name is a personal commemoration. Joaquín Vilumbrales honours Joaquín Vilumbrales López (1941-1999), remembered locally as both an educator and the short-serving mayor of Alcorcón.

Before entering the mayor's office, Vilumbrales was closely tied to the area around the station. Alcorcón City Council describes him as a professor and director of the Instituto de Enseñanza Secundaria Los Castillos, the same neighbourhood name that was originally expected to identify the station.

His political career made the name especially visible in Alcorcón at the turn of the century. In 1995, El País described him as a physics and chemistry teacher at Los Castillos and a long-serving councillor, then the PP candidate for mayor. In the 1999 municipal election his party won 14 councillors in Alcorcón, enough for a majority, but Vilumbrales died on 9 September 1999 after only a few months in office.

The transport name reflects a deliberate change from a place-name to a memorial name. A 2003 El País report on Alcorcón street names says one of the Line 10 stations in the municipality was called Joaquín Vilumbrales, instead of the initially planned Los Castillos, at the proposal of Pablo Zúñiga. That makes this station different from many suburban Metro names that simply repeat the district, street, or development around them.

The station entered service in the large southern expansion of Madrid Metro. CRTM's 2004 annual report states that the Line 10 extension from Colonia Jardín to Puerta del Sur opened on 11 April 2003 and that the MetroSur fare area included Joaquín Vilumbrales and Puerta del Sur on Line 10. The broad origin of the name is verified, although the exact decree or file that formally assigned the station name remains a useful archival item to consult.
