Cuatro Vientos station is named for the surrounding Cuatro Vientos place-name, today closely associated with the Madrid-Cuatro Vientos aerodrome and air base. The Madrid regional government has even described the Metro station's identity as linked to the airport, noting that the airport gave its name to the Line 10 station when that Metro stop opened in 2003.

The name is older than the modern transport interchange. In the BOE file for the Torre de Señales, the state records that on 11 January 1911 a strip of land was selected in the paramo known as **'A los Cuatro Vientos'** for the first military aerodrome in Spain. That wording is important: it shows that the place-name existed before the aviation school was built there.

Literally, *a los cuatro vientos* means 'to the four winds' or 'in all directions'. The RAE defines the expression as 'in all directions, everywhere', and it fits the image of an open, exposed plain on the western edge of Madrid. The often repeated explanation that the land was a windswept plateau is plausible, but the exact statement that winds blew from all four cardinal directions should be treated as interpretation rather than a fully documented historical fact.

The aerodrome then transformed the old place-name into a national aviation landmark. Aena's history records that a military commission proposed the Cuatro Vientos land on 11 January 1911, that troops arrived in February, and that the first aircraft from Ciudad Lineal landed there on 12 March. Later came buildings, laboratories, training schools, and the Torre de Señales, now protected as a Bien de Interes Cultural.

The rail history has its own chronology. The Cercanias halt at Cuatro Vientos was part of the Aluche-Mostoles railway opened in 1976, later absorbed into line C-5. The Metro Line 10 station opened on 11 April 2003 below or beside the commuter station, making Cuatro Vientos an interchange whose name preserves both an older landscape expression and a century of aviation history.
