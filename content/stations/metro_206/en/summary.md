The station name refers directly to Cuatro Vientos, the Madrid area best known for its aerodrome and air base. The strongest source found is the BOE file for the protected Torre de Señales, which states that on 11 January 1911 a strip of land was selected in the paramo known as 'A los Cuatro Vientos' for Spain's first military aerodrome. Aena confirms the same 1911 military commission and the early role of the site in pilot training. The name therefore predates the aerodrome as a local place-name, although the aerodrome later made it famous. The common explanation of a windswept open plain is linguistically plausible, but should be presented as an interpretation unless a local toponymic source is found. The official Callejero adds mid-twentieth-century road evidence, with Carretera de Carabanchel Alto a Cuatro Vientos recorded from 1 January 1949 and later Cuatro Vientos/Aviación successor rows.
