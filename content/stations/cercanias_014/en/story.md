Orcasitas station is named for the neighbourhood around it, on the boundary between Orcasitas and Orcasur in Madrid's Usera district. Adif and the regional transport consortium list Orcasitas as a Cercanías station on line C-5, with access from Calle de Campotejar and Calle del Censo. The railway name follows the urban place-name already established in the district.

The older place-name points to the Orcasitas family. In a study of Madrid's municipal names, Luis Miguel Aparisi Laporta identifies Barrio de Orcasitas as a reference to Pedro Bernardo Orcasitas Ruiz, owner of the land. The same entry records him as a councillor and acting mayor in the Ayuntamiento Popular during 1873-1874, a fact also reflected in the Madrid City Council's list of mayors.

Other sources describe the name at family level rather than as a single-person dedication. The Colegio Oficial de Arquitectos de Madrid's guide says that the Orcasitas family, majority owner of the land, gave its name to an area where migrant labourers settled between 1940 and 1960. The neighbourhood association Guetaria preserves a similar account and adds that María Orcasitas is the last known landowner in the area with that surname.

That land later became one of Madrid's best-known stories of neighbourhood mobilisation. Municipal material on the Meseta de Orcasitas explains that by 1956 the peripheral settlement of small houses was already largely formed, that planning disputes in the 1960s and 1970s led to a major residents' movement, and that the Supreme Court judgment of June 1977 confirmed the binding character of the planning memorandum that required rehousing in the same area. The new Orcasitas became a symbol of participatory urban redevelopment, with street names such as Memoria Vinculante and Participación preserving that history.

The strongest conclusion is therefore: the station is named after the neighbourhood, and the neighbourhood name is best explained by the Orcasitas landowning family, with Pedro Bernardo Orcasitas Ruiz the named figure in Madrid toponymic scholarship. The current dataset should not present him as born in 1878, because the documented political figure was already acting mayor in 1873. It should also avoid presenting the surname's Basque etymology as settled; Auñamendi identifies Horcasitas/Orcasitas as a Basque surname associated with Arcentales and Soba, but calls its etymology problematic.
