Cartagena station is a street-name station. Its name comes from Calle de Cartagena, the road that meets Avenida de América above the station and gives its name to the vestibule listed by the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid.

Behind the Madrid street is Cartagena in Murcia, one of Spain’s historic Mediterranean ports. The name reaches back to the Carthaginian city Qart Hadasht, commonly explained as “New City,” founded around 227 BCE under Hasdrubal. After the Roman capture of the city in 209 BCE, it became known as Carthago Nova.

That ancient name mattered because Cartagena was not a minor coastal settlement. Its harbour, nearby mining district and defensive position made it a strategic base for Carthage and later for Rome. Public material from Cartagena’s own municipal and archaeological institutions still presents Qart Hadasht and Carthago Nova as central layers in the city’s identity.

In Madrid, the station belongs to the Line 7 expansion toward Avenida de América, opened in 1975. The official Madrid street directory places Calle de Cartagena in the districts of Chamartín and Salamanca, and the station lies at the edge of Prosperidad and La Guindalera rather than in Ciudad Lineal.

The etymology is therefore secure at the station level: Metro used the neighbouring street name, and the street name points to the Murcian city. What is less directly documented in the available sources is the original municipal act assigning the Madrid street name, so the link from the street to Cartagena is best treated as strongly supported by conventional street-naming evidence rather than by a located naming decree.
