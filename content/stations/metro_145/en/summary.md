Cartagena station takes its name from the adjacent Calle de Cartagena, not directly from a local Madrid neighbourhood. The street commemorates Cartagena in Murcia, a Mediterranean port founded by the Carthaginians as Qart Hadasht, usually translated as “New City,” around 227 BCE. After the Roman conquest in 209 BCE, the city became Carthago Nova, a name remembered in many historical accounts of Roman Hispania. The Madrid station opened on Line 7 in 1975, at Avenida de América and Calle de Cartagena, on the edge between Prosperidad and La Guindalera.
