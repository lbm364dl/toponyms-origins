The name Cuatro Caminos means “Four Roads” and comes from the Glorieta de Cuatro Caminos, the long-standing crossroads above the station. Metro de Madrid's own historical material identifies the roads as Bravo Murillo, Santa Engracia, Reina Victoria and Raimundo Fernández Villaverde, while María Isabel Gea's dictionary tradition explains the older name through the road to France, the Aceiteros route and Santa Engracia, noting that the later street layout changed. The station opened on 17 October 1919 as the northern terminus of Spain's first metro line, Sol-Cuatro Caminos. Line 2 reached Cuatro Caminos on 10 September 1929, and line 6 made it a three-line interchange in 1979. The name is therefore descriptive, but it is also tied to the growth of a major working-class and transport district on Madrid's early twentieth-century edge.
