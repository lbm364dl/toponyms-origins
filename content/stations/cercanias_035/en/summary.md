The station's immediate name source is geographic: it serves the Santa Eugenia neighbourhood of Villa de Vallecas and stands on Avenida de Santa Eugenia. Madrid municipal sources place the creation of the private Ciudad Residencial Santa Eugenia in 1971, next to Cerro Almodóvar, with broad avenues, gardens and high-rise blocks. A local Vallecas article, based on neighbourhood memory, says the Burgos-born promoters of Pistas y Obras named the owners' community after their mother, Eugenia. However, the Madrid municipal publication on women in the street map records Avenida de Santa Eugenia in 1972 and explains the name by reference to saints named Eugenia. The safest public explanation is therefore layered: station after neighbourhood/avenue, neighbourhood after the residential development, with the ultimate origin reported but not fully resolved. The historical-vials CSV sharpens the official street chronology: both Calle de Santa Eugenia and Avenida de Santa Eugenia enter the Callejero on 28 June 1972, with the avenue remaining current.
