Santa Eugenia station takes its name first from its setting: the Santa Eugenia neighbourhood of Villa de Vallecas and Avenida de Santa Eugenia, where the railway stop is located. Renfe describes the station as opened to the public in 1989, built because of the development of the surrounding neighbourhood and now served by lines C-2, C-7 and C-8.

The older name behind the station is the Ciudad Residencial Santa Eugenia. Madrid City Council material says this private residential development was created in 1971 beside Cerro Almodóvar, separated from the historic centre of Vallecas by about three kilometres. It was conceived as a large suburban residential area with high brick blocks, broad avenues, gardens and services for the new middle-class population of the period.

The most attractive local explanation is personal. A Vallecas VA article, drawing on conversations with neighbourhood activists from Asociación de Vecinos La Colmena, says that the Burgos-born brothers Roberto, Carmelo and Alejandro, linked to the construction company Pistas y Obras and backed by Banco Rural del Mediterráneo, founded the community with their mother's name: Eugenia. The same source connects the many Burgos-town street names in the area with the promoters' provincial origin.

There is, however, an important complication. A Madrid municipal publication on women in the city's street map lists Avenida de Santa Eugenia as a 1972 street name in Villa de Vallecas and explains Santa Eugenia through the Christian saints who bore that name, not through the developers' mother. That does not necessarily disprove the local account: the religious prefix 'Santa' could have made a family homage acceptable as an urban place name, and later street-name documentation may have normalized it as a saint's name.

For public wording, the best-supported wording is therefore cautious. The station is securely named after the Santa Eugenia neighbourhood and avenue; the residential development and Pistas y Obras are well documented; the mother-of-the-promoters story is plausible and locally specific, but not yet confirmed by a primary founding document. A sale deed cited in the BOE and contemporary El Pais reports show Pistas y Obras operating in the Ciudad Residencial Santa Eugenia in the 1970s, reinforcing the chronology even if they do not settle the ultimate dedication.
