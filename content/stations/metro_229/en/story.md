The name **Hospital de Fuenlabrada** is descriptive. It points passengers to the public Hospital Universitario de Fuenlabrada, whose building stands beside the station area on Camino del Molino, near the Rey Juan Carlos University campus.

The institution behind the name was created to serve Fuenlabrada and nearby municipalities in the south of the Madrid region. Official hospital history says Fuenlabrada had previously been attended from Hospital Severo Ochoa in Leganés and describes the city as the only large Spanish city without its own hospital at the time.

The administrative and building chronology is unusually close to the station's own opening. INSALUD decided in 1998 to create the hospital; construction began in 1999 and finished in 2003. The Comunidad de Madrid created the public entity Hospital de Fuenlabrada through Law 13/2002 of 20 December, with statutes approved by decree on 26 December 2002.

Metro arrived first. MetroSur, Line 12, was put into service on 11 April 2003, and the Comunidad de Madrid's Line 12 documentation places Hospital de Fuenlabrada station on Camino del Molino. The hospital began activity in January 2004 with outpatient consultations, opened emergency services in June 2004, and was functioning fully during 2005.

There is no evidence that the station commemorates a person, saint, event, or older placename. The only nuance is the wording: public transport uses the shorter station name Hospital de Fuenlabrada, while the health institution is now presented officially as Hospital Universitario de Fuenlabrada.
