The station name **Aviación Española** means “Spanish Aviation.” For public etymology, the strongest direct explanation is not a single flight or a person, but the aviation identity of the surrounding area: former military lands at Campamento, Avenida de la Aviación, and the nearby Cuatro Vientos airfield. When the station opened in 2006, El País described the name as a tribute to the Aviation barracks of Campamento.

Those barracks belonged to a wider military and aeronautical landscape on Madrid's south-western edge. The area around Campamento and Cuatro Vientos was long marked by military installations, later slated for urban redevelopment under Operación Campamento. The station was built to serve existing neighbourhoods such as Las Águilas and Parque Europa as well as that planned development.

The deeper historical background is Cuatro Vientos. Aena's airport history records that on 11 January 1911 a military commission proposed acquiring land at Cuatro Vientos for a pilots' school, and the first aircraft and troops arrived soon afterward. Construction of the aerodrome buildings began in 1911, and in 1914 work began on what Aena describes as Spain's oldest control tower.

Cuatro Vientos also became a technical and educational aviation centre. Aena notes the Aerodynamics Laboratory, the wind tunnel that entered service in 1926, mechanics and classification schools, and the later Advanced Aerotechnical School. The Comunidad de Madrid's 2022 mural at Cuatro Vientos station presents this wider story to Metro passengers, including early flights, the wind tunnel associated with Emilio Herrera, and the 1935 stratonautical suit tradition.

The present station opened on 22 December 2006, after earlier reporting had referred to the project as “Avenida de la Aviación Española.” Its final public name shortened that idea to **Aviación Española**. The airport connection is real as context, but the best-supported wording should say that the name evokes the local aviation and military-aeronautical heritage, especially the former Aviation barracks and the Cuatro Vientos district, rather than claiming it was named solely after the airport.
