Hospital del Henares is named directly after the Hospital Universitario del Henares, the public hospital immediately beside the station in Coslada. The station is not named for a person, saint, battle or old village, but for the public service destination it was built to serve.

The hospital's own name belongs to a larger Madrid and Guadalajara landscape: the Henares. That name is carried by the river, by nearby municipalities such as San Fernando de Henares and Alcalá de Henares, and by the wider Corredor del Henares, the industrial and residential axis east of Madrid.

The deeper etymology is agricultural. Toponomasticon Hispaniae identifies Henares as the plural of henar, meaning a place populated with hay; the word is ultimately Latin in origin, while the collective place-name form is Romance. In other words, the river name points to hay fields or hay meadows once associated with its course.

The transport chronology is slightly more complicated than a simple simultaneous opening. Contemporary press reported that the new station was visited or inaugurated on 7 February 2008 and that the hospital would have Metro access from Monday 11 February. The Community of Madrid's hospital page says the hospital opened its doors on 11 February 2008 to serve residents of Coslada, San Fernando de Henares, Mejorada del Campo, Loeches and Velilla de San Antonio.

A useful older layer is the Arabic-derived name Guadalajara, from wadi al-hijara or a related form meaning 'river of stones'. That name is strongly associated with the city of Guadalajara and, in some accounts, with the Henares river valley before the modern river name prevailed. For this station, however, the direct naming chain is clear: station -> hospital -> Henares river/corridor -> hay-field toponym.
