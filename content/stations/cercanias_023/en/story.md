Pinto station has a direct local name: it is the Cercanías C-3 station serving the municipality of Pinto, south of Madrid. Renfe's station sheet records the stop as PINTO, places it in the municipality of Pinto, and links it to the C-3 service, while the regional transport consortium presents the same stop with access from Calle del Ferrocarril. For the railway, therefore, there is little ambiguity in the immediate naming chain. The station takes the name of the town in which it stands.

The older and more interesting question is what the town name Pinto means. A common local tradition connects it with *punctum*, "point", often folded into the well-known idea that Pinto marks the center of the Iberian Peninsula or a significant crossing point. That tradition is part of the town's historical self-presentation and appears in municipal and journalistic accounts. It is useful cultural context, but it is not the strongest current etymological explanation.

The best scholarly reading points instead to a Latin or Romance color adjective: *pincta*, *pinctu*, or *pinctus*, with the sense of "painted", "colored", or "of a certain color". Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats Pinto as a Latin-derived place name with a color value, and Jairo J. Garcia Sanchez gives the same broad explanation. Under that interpretation, Pinto may preserve an adjective that once described some local feature: perhaps land, stone, water, a slope, or another landscape noun that later disappeared from the place name.

That lost noun is the main uncertainty. The sources do not let us reconstruct with confidence exactly what was "colored" or "painted" in the original phrase. This is why the origin can be presented as well supported in broad terms, but not complete in every detail. The station name is secure because it comes from the municipality; the municipality's deeper name is best explained by the color adjective, with the precise original object left open.

For the site, Pinto should therefore be told as a layered name. The modern transport name is straightforward and documented by Renfe and CRTM. The municipal legend of a central "point" should be mentioned as a tradition, not as the preferred derivation. The stronger etymology is the older color-based one, a small survival of a descriptive landscape expression that became the name of the town and, much later, of its railway station.
