The Cercanías station is named directly for Aravaca, today a neighbourhood in Madrid's Moncloa-Aravaca district and formerly an independent municipality. Official transport sources identify the station as Aravaca and place it on lines C-7 and C-10. The place-name is old in Madrid's territorial history, appearing in medieval references to the Sexmo de Aravaca. Joaquín Caridad Arias argues that Aravaca is an ethnic name connected with the Celtiberian Arevaci/Aravaci, but Emilio Nieto Ballester has warned that such Celtic explanations need caution and that Aravaca should be studied within a wider series of similar names. The safest public explanation is therefore: named after the place Aravaca; deeper etymology probable but contested.
