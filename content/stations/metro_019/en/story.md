The metro station takes its name from Eugenia de Montijo, the public name by which Eugenia María Guzmán y Portocarrero is best known. The Real Academia de la Historia and Metro de Madrid's joint biographical project identify the station with her, and the City of Madrid's street-name material records Calle de Eugenia de Montijo in Carabanchel from 1945.

Eugenia was born in Granada on 5 May 1826 and died in Madrid on 11 July 1920. In 1853 she married Napoleon III and became Empress of the French, a role that made her one of the most visible Spanish aristocrats in nineteenth-century Europe. The surname-like form "de Montijo" is the historical name by which she became famous, even though reference authorities note that her formal family names were Guzmán and Portocarrero.

The Carabanchel connection is not decorative. The Montijo family was associated with the old Quinta de Miranda in Carabanchel Alto, an estate already mentioned in sixteenth-century topographical sources and later absorbed into the world of the Montijo household. In the nineteenth century it was a place of gardens, social life, antiquarian curiosity and archaeological attention, especially after the discovery of the late-antique Roman mosaic of Carabanchel.

That cultural world helps explain why the name persisted in the district. The Museo de San Isidro's study of the Carabanchel mosaic describes María Manuela, the widowed Countess of Montijo and Eugenia's mother, as a woman with important cultural interests whose circle included Stendhal and Prosper Mérimée. Mérimée even advised her by letter on possible excavations around the mosaic, turning the family estate into a small node of European literary and antiquarian networks.

The present station is modern: it was inserted into Line 5 between Carabanchel and Aluche and opened on 27 October 1999. Much of the old estate disappeared in the twentieth century, including the palace, demolished around 1969; what remains in public memory is scattered through names such as Calle de Eugenia de Montijo, Parque Eugenia de Montijo, and the pond recorded by the City of Madrid as a municipal landscape feature. The etymology is therefore secure, but the best public explanation should connect the imperial biography with the very local Carabanchel estate that kept her name on the map.
