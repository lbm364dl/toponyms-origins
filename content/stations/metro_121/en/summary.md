Plaza de España station is named directly after the square above it. That square was planned after the demolition of the San Gil barracks, beside the earlier San Marcial parade ground, as a monumental western end to Gran Vía. A 1910 press notice already records a municipal proposal for a large square called Plaza de España on the barracks site, and a 1911 plan helped attach the name to the new urban space. The Cervantes monument, selected in the 1915-1916 competition process and partly inaugurated in 1929, made the plaza one of Madrid's most recognizable civic scenes. Metro reached the name in stages: Line 3 opened here in 1941, and the former Suburbano, now Line 10, opened from Plaza de España to Carabanchel in 1961.
