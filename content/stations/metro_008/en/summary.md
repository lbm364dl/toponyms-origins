The transport station is named for the Glorieta de Embajadores, where the old Calle de Embajadores crossed the former Felipe IV wall at the Portillo de Embajadores. Madrid street-name tradition traces the word back to a Campo de Embajadores, an area south of the old town where foreign envoys stayed apart from the population during an epidemic in the reign of Juan II of Castile. Later writers identify envoys linked with Tunis, Aragon, Navarre and France, and place them in nearby country houses such as San Pedro and Santiago el Verde. The story is well established in Madrid toponymy literature, but it should be presented as a historical tradition rather than as a fully documented primary-source fact. The official Callejero fixes the immediate urban setting more firmly: Calle de Embajadores is recorded from 11 January 1835, Portillo de Embajadores from 1916 to 1917, and Glorieta de Embajadores from 11 May 1917.
