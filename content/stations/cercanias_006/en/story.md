The station name comes from the place around it, not from a person. The official Madrid street directory records both Avenida de la Fuente de la Mora and Camino de la Fuente de la Mora in the district of Hortaleza, and the transport authority places the station accesses on those same streets. In that practical sense, the railway and light-rail stop inherited a local place-name already fixed in the urban map.

Behind the street name is an older rural landscape. A local historical account of Hortaleza's roads describes the Camino de la Fuente de la Mora as a branch from the Camino del Charco Pescador and notes that the name has survived as a street in the new Sanchinarro area. The first element, fuente, is clear Spanish: a spring, fountain, or water source, exactly the kind of feature that often named paths, fields, and boundaries in pre-urban Hortaleza.

The difficult part is Mora. It could tempt a romantic reading as a Moorish woman, and in wider Spanish toponymy some Mora names have been linked to mulberries, personal names, Moors, or stony ground. Jairo J. García Sánchez, discussing Valdemoro rather than this station, notes that mor- can be interpreted as a pre-Roman element meaning a heap of stones, but he also presents that kind of explanation as uncertain. For Fuente de la Mora in Hortaleza, the cautious public reading is therefore 'the spring called de la Mora', with the second element unresolved.

Transport history explains why this older name became prominent again. Metro Ligero ML1 opened in 2007 through the new northern developments, and the Cercanías stop opened to passengers in March 2011 beside the light-rail station. Contemporary reports say the Cercanías station had initially been planned as Manoteras, after the nearby business and neighbourhood area, but it opened as Fuente de la Mora, matching the existing interchange and the local street name.

One correction is important: the often-cited archaeological site Fuente de la Mora is not evidence for this station's name. The Comunidad de Madrid page identifies that Iron Age site as Cerro de Fuente de la Mora in Leganés, linked to works on the M-45 and the Arroyo Butarque archaeological zone, far from Hortaleza. It is a real and important site, but it should not be presented as a nearby archaeological context for this station.
