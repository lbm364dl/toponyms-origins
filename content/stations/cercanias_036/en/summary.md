The Cercanías station name is a straightforward place-name: it refers to Coslada, the municipality east of Madrid. The deeper origin of Coslada is not settled. Ramón Menéndez Pidal proposed that it contains Celtic coslo/cosla, 'hazel' or 'hazelnut', with a Romance suffix, giving the sense of a hazel grove. Later local and regional works record competing explanations based on the terrain, especially flint and gypsum quarries, and one regional publication even suggests a possible link with coladas or livestock droveways. The safest public explanation is therefore that the station is named after the municipality, whose name has several debated etymologies, with the Celtic hazel hypothesis the best-known but not definitive one.
