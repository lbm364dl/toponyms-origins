Coslada station takes its name from the municipality of Coslada. In transport terms this is a simple naming case: Renfe identifies Coslada as the Cercanías station in fare zone B1, served by lines C-2, C-7 and C-8, and connected with Metro line 7 at Coslada Central.

The harder question is what the older place-name Coslada means. Ramón Menéndez Pidal, in *Toponimia prerrománica hispana*, connected the name with a Celtic form *coslo* or *cosla*, meaning hazel or hazelnut. With a Romance suffix, that would make Coslada something like a hazel grove, an explanation repeated in later local accounts and in studies of Madrid toponymy.

That attractive explanation is not the only one. A regional historical guide describes the origin of the name as confused and records another proposal by Aurelio García López: *cos* as flint and *late* as abundant or extensive, linked to Coslada's gypsum and flint quarries. The same guide adds a cautious suggestion that the name might relate to *colada*, a livestock route, because important droveways crossed the term, including the Cañada Galiana.

The historical setting fits a modest medieval settlement on the eastern side of Madrid. Publications on the Henares valley and municipal archival history place Coslada in the medieval alfoz of Madrid and the sexmo of Vallecas; the clearest early references belong to the period of Fernando III or by 1273, depending on the source and document being cited. Later descriptions still portray it as a small agricultural place where stone extraction mattered.

For a station display, the strongest wording is therefore conservative. The station is certainly named after the municipality. The municipality's name is plausibly old and pre-modern, but its etymology remains debated: the Celtic hazel-grove reading is the best-known scholarly proposal, while the flint/quarry and livestock-route explanations preserve local geographical arguments. A derivation from Latin *cova ustulata*, 'burnt cave', is explicitly treated by Jairo Javier García Sánchez as no more than a hypothesis.
