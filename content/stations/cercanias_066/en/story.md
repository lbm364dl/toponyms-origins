The name **Pinar** is straightforward but very local. Renfe describes Pinar de Las Rozas as the station commonly known as Pinar and says its name comes from the nearby natural setting, beside the A-6 and away from the old town centre of Las Rozas. In ordinary Spanish, *pinar* means a place populated by pine trees, so the station name is a landscape name rather than a dedication to a person.

That landscape reference points to the Pinar or El Pinar area of Las Rozas. Public descriptions of the nearby Dehesa de Navalcarbon note its pine woodland and the local use of the expression Pinar de Las Rozas for a place marked by pine trees. The station name therefore works both as a practical geographical label and as a reminder of the wooded character that shaped this part of the municipality.

The railway history is older and more complex than a simple suburban stop. The Madrid-El Escorial section of the northern railway corridor opened in 1861, but Pinar was not one of the original passenger stations on that route. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes historical railway map records a 1961 'Estacion de Pinar de las Rozas' as a support station for the branch linking the Norte-Villalba line with the future Chamartin station, and other railway chronologies place the opening of the Pinar-Chamartin connection in the 1960s.

That junction role is central to the identity of the place. Pinar sits where the old Príncipe Pio-Villalba corridor, the Chamartin route and later Cercanias movements meet. The 1989 completion of the 'Triangulo de Pinar' allowed all the main movements between Príncipe Pio-Villalba, Villalba-Chamartin and Príncipe Pio-Chamartin; it is an important date for the junction, but not the safest date to give as the origin of the station name.

The best-supported explanation is therefore descriptive: Pinar is named for the pine landscape and local Pinar de Las Rozas area. There is no strong competing person-based or commemorative theory. The remaining nuance is chronological: the station's public-facing Cercanias role changed over time, while the railway place name seems to have been in use at least from the 1960s.
