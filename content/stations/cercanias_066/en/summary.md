Pinar is a descriptive station name: in Spanish, a pinar is a place populated by pine trees. Renfe's own station-location material says the station's name is due to the nearby natural setting, an area also associated with the Pinar or El Pinar of Las Rozas. The railway site became significant in the 1960s as a support point and junction for the link toward the future Chamartin station. In 1989 the completion of the 'Triangulo de Pinar' made the junction more flexible for Cercanias movements, but that should not be confused with the first existence of the station.
