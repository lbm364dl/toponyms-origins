Carpetana station was opened on Line 6 on 1 June 1983, on the Oporto-Laguna extension. Its name comes from Vía Carpetana, which Metro de Madrid identifies as an avenue named after the Carpetani, a pre-Roman people of central Iberia. The broader historical region, Carpetania, covered parts of present-day Madrid, Toledo, Guadalajara, Cuenca and Ciudad Real. The station is also notable for the Miocene fossil site discovered during accessibility and modernization works in 2008-2009, now presented in Metro's Yacimiento de Carpetana display. The name origin is well supported, but the Carpetani's exact ethnic-linguistic classification should be presented with caution rather than as simply Celtic.
