The metro station called **Jarama** is on Line 7 in San Fernando de Henares. Its access is listed by the regional transport consortium at Plaza de Guernica, and it belongs to the MetroEste branch that links Madrid with Coslada and San Fernando. The most direct explanation for the station name is geographical: it points to the Jarama River, the major river whose valley defines much of the eastern side of the Madrid region.

The river matters locally even though the station is not on the riverbank. San Fernando de Henares sits in a landscape shaped by the Jarama and Henares valleys, and older local history was even more Jarama-centered than the modern municipal name suggests. A Comunidad de Madrid publication notes that the town was known as **San Fernando de Jarama** until 1916, when it was renamed San Fernando de Henares; later local voices still argued for recovering the Jarama form because of tradition and proximity to the river.

The station opened on **5 May 2007**, when the eastern extension of Line 7 began operating for Coslada and San Fernando de Henares. Contemporary press described that day as the opening of seven of the eight MetroEste stations, with the later Hospital del Henares stop still waiting for the hospital to open. Technical documents for Line 7B also list Jarama among the San Fernando de Henares stations in the Barrio del Puerto-Henares section.

The name behind the name, however, is not simple. Toponomasticon Hispaniae records medieval forms such as **Xarama** and **Sarama**, showing that the name has deep documentary history and that its initial sound changed over time. The same specialist entry does not treat the old Indo-European river-root explanation as secure; instead it cautiously considers a connection with plant names such as **jaramago** and Galician-Portuguese **saramago**, possibly with later influence from the familiar Castilian word **jara**.

For public interpretation, the safest wording is therefore: the station is named for the Jarama River, while the origin of **Jarama** as a hydronym remains debated. The popular ideas that it means a Berber 'border river' or that it comes simply from **jara** should be presented as weaker or secondary explanations unless a stronger source is found. What is secure is the river's local importance: in San Fernando, Jarama is not only a station name but a reminder of the valley that preceded the metro.
