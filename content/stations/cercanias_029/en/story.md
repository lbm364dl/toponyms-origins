The station name is practical before it is poetic: it points to Cantoblanco, the area of northern Madrid where it stands, and to the university campus it serves. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid lists the stop as Cantoblanco Universidad, while Renfe station data has also used the form Universidad-Cantoblanco. Both versions combine the same two ideas: place and university.

Cantoblanco was not invented by the university. The name appears in older local geography: a mid-19th-century Charles Clifford photograph in the Biblioteca Nacional records the Almenara de Canto blanco on the Canal de Isabel II, and descriptions of the Canal Bajo also refer to the Arroyo de Canto Blanco and the Almenara de Canto Blanco near what is now the UAM area. The university inherited a pre-existing rural toponym and made it nationally familiar.

The university part of the name is firmly documented. Decreto-ley 5/1968, published in the BOE on 7 June 1968, created a new University of Madrid, later officially identified as the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid. UAM's own history says classes began in 1968, the Cantoblanco campus was inaugurated on 25 October 1971, and the campus gathered several faculties in a deliberately peripheral, green setting.

The railway arrived a few years after the campus. Vía Libre reported that on 3 December 1975 Renfe inaugurated the railway branch to the university installations at Canto Blanco, with a new station opposite the campus and a journey of under ten minutes from Chamartín. The article explicitly described the branch as a way to improve access for the roughly 16,000 students then using the university.

The tempting etymology is to read Cantoblanco as 'white stone' from Spanish canto and blanco. That is not impossible as a later popular understanding, but it is not the strongest scholarly explanation found. Joaquín Caridad Arias argued in Anales del Instituto de Estudios Madrileños that Cantoblanco likely does not refer to identifiable white stones and may instead be a tautological hybrid formed when an older Celtic-Latin element related to brightness or whiteness was re-explained in Romance. The station-name origin is therefore secure, but the ancient meaning of Cantoblanco should be presented with caution.
