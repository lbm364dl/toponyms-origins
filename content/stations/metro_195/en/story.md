La Rambla station takes its name from the Coslada place name La Rambla, not from a person. The station entrance listed by the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid is on Calle Honduras, and local municipal references place La Rambla as an urban axis with nearby civic, cultural, commercial and sports facilities rather than as a single memorial name.

The older word behind the name is rambla. The Real Academia Española gives its origin as Andalusi Arabic rámla, from classical Arabic ramlah, meaning a sandy place, and defines it first as the natural bed of rainwater when it falls heavily. Toponomasticon Hispaniae gives the same Arabic root and connects it with hydronymy and the Spanish and Catalan word rambla.

In Coslada, however, the evidence points most clearly to a modern urban toponym. Municipal material describes the Parque del Lago as being in Valleaguado and on the axis of the paseo de La Rambla, while another municipal publication describes La Rambla as an area developed from 2008 into a social and civic axis between Calle Honduras and Avenida de los Príncipes de España. This explains why the station name is locally intelligible even if the official transport page simply records the access as Calle Honduras.

That double meaning is important. A rambla can be a dry or intermittent channel shaped by runoff, but in Spanish urban vocabulary it can also be a broad, often tree-lined promenade. Coslada's La Rambla now functions in the second sense as a civic corridor: nearby references include the Centro Multifuncional La Rambla, the Centro de Mayores La Rambla, the sports pavilion, the shopping centre and the Parque del Lago.

The current dataset claim should therefore be tightened. The Arabic origin and general landscape meaning of rambla are well supported, and the station's relation to the local La Rambla area is very plausible. What remains unproven is the specific narrative that Coslada's name preserves the memory of seasonal watercourses draining toward the Henares; that may be a reasonable interpretation, but No direct municipal, archival or historical source confirming it for this exact place name.
