Pueblo Nuevo is best understood as a place-name: the Metro station was given the name of the barrio it serves in Ciudad Lineal. Local-history sources trace the name to a new settlement area near the old road to Aragón, with early references to 'Pueblo Nuevo de la Concepción' before the shorter form prevailed. The surrounding territory was once rural land and market-garden country outside Madrid, divided among former municipalities such as Canillas, Canillejas and Vicálvaro. The station opened on the Ventas-Ciudad Lineal metro extension in 1964 and later became an interchange with Line 7 in the 1970s. The broad etymology is strong, but the precise foundation story should be treated as probable rather than fully verified until primary nineteenth-century records are inspected.
