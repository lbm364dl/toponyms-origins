The station name is direct and local: **Pueblo Nuevo** is the Madrid neighbourhood around the station, in the district of Ciudad Lineal. In plain English the name means **'New Town'** or **'New Village'**, a descriptive place-name rather than the name of a person, saint or event.

The older story behind the name belongs to the suburban edge of Madrid. Local-history accounts describe a settlement near Calle de Alcalá and the old Aragón road that was once linked with the name **Pueblo Nuevo de la Concepción**. Over time the shorter form, Pueblo Nuevo, appears to have become the usual name, while nearby Concepción preserved another part of that older religious and neighbourhood geography.

Before it became part of the capital’s urban fabric, this was not central Madrid. Studies of Ciudad Lineal describe the present district as land once shared by surrounding municipalities and crossed by roads leading from Madrid toward Alcalá and Guadalajara. The landscape was agricultural for much of its history, with fields, vineyards, olive groves and later suburban building plots rather than a single planned city centre.

Transport helped fix the name in everyday use. The station opened on the Ventas-Ciudad Lineal metro extension in 1964, originally in the orbit of what later became Line 5, and the Line 7 platforms made Pueblo Nuevo an interchange in the 1970s. The present CRTM station page confirms the station’s role on lines 5 and 7 and its location on Calle de Alcalá, the same axis that shaped the older neighbourhood.

The essential etymology is therefore well supported: the Metro station is named after the neighbourhood called Pueblo Nuevo. The more detailed claim that the area began around 1860 as the Colonia Agrícola de la Purísima Concepción is plausible and repeated in local-history sources, but I did not find a primary nineteenth-century document proving that full chain. A safer public wording is that the name grew from a new suburban settlement associated with the Concepción area, later shortened and consolidated as Pueblo Nuevo.
