Majadahonda is a place-name before it is a station name. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains it as a transparent Castilian compound of majada and honda: a pastoral shelter or sheepfold set in a hollow or lower ground. The municipal history preserves the traditional account that Segovian shepherds settled there around the 13th century, although it also notes archaeological doubts about an even earlier occupation. For the railway, the present Cercanías station opened in 1989 and replaced the closed El Plantío stop, taking the municipality's established name.
