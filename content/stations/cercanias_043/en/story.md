Meco station is named after Meco, the municipality in the eastern part of the Community of Madrid where the station is located. Current transport sources list it simply as Meco: Adif identifies station 70104 as Meco, and the Madrid Regional Transport Consortium places it on Cercanias lines C-2 and C-8, with access at Travesia de la Calera in Meco.

The harder question is what the place-name Meco originally meant. A useful contemporary summary appeared in El Pais in 1995: specialists had considered three broad possibilities, a Celtic form miccon connected with sheep, a Roman Miaccum connected with a rural estate or roadside settlement, and an Arabic maksusk glossed as something like a bare hill. That range of theories fits the layered archaeology and landscape of the Henares countryside, but it also shows the problem: the same evidence can be made to support more than one story.

The Roman explanation is the most tempting because Miaccum is a known ancient name from the Antonine Itinerary. Older local writing sometimes connected Miaccum with Meco, but more recent archaeological discussion has shifted the strongest case away from Meco. The Community of Madrid's page for El Beneficio says that, after early twenty-first-century excavations, some researchers proposed that the Collado Mediano site was the Miaccum inn mentioned in the Itinerary; Jesus Jimenez Guijarro's archaeological publication presents El Beneficio as a Roman roadside establishment linked with route XXIV.

The Arabic and Celtic proposals remain part of the local tradition, but they are harder to verify from the available web sources. The Arabic explanation is attractive because the town stands on an exposed rise and because medieval Islamic occupation is part of the region's history, yet archaeological presence is not the same thing as a proven linguistic derivation. The Celtic/sheep explanation also fits a pastoral landscape, but it needs a stronger philological source before it can be treated as more than a hypothesis.

For public display, the secure etymology is therefore modest: the station is named for the municipality of Meco. The municipality's own name is best presented as disputed, not as a solved Celtic, Roman or Arabic derivation. One correction is important: the modern RAE entry for meco is a Spanish dictionary entry with American and regional senses, including a Mexican animal colour description; it does not by itself explain the Madrid place-name.
