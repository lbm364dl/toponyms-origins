El Carrascal station takes its public name from the part of Leganés where it stands. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid lists the station as El Carrascal, on Line 12, with accesses on Avenida Rey Juan Carlos I; the City Council of Leganés also identifies the MetroSur stop at that avenue. In practice, the station name points travellers to the El Carrascal neighbourhood and its nearby civic, residential and commercial area.

Behind that neighbourhood name is a plain Spanish landscape word. The Diccionario de la lengua española defines carrascal as a place or hill/wood populated by carrascas, and defines carrasca as a usually small encina, or a clump of it, from a pre-Roman root karr-. Toponymasticon Hispaniae likewise treats El Carrascal as a transparent and very frequent botanical place name, derived from carrasca with a collective or locative ending.

The modern urban district belongs to the great twentieth-century expansion of Leganés. A study of commerce and urban structure in Madrid's southern periphery states that the General Plan of Leganés was approved in January 1966, that Zarzaquemada developed under a 1968 plan, and that the El Carrascal residential estate was approved in 1974. That places the name within a planned urban-growth sequence rather than in a medieval foundation story.

MetroSur added a transport layer to this urban geography. The Community of Madrid describes Line 12 as a more-than-40-kilometre ring serving Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe and Leganés, and lists El Carrascal as one of Leganés's stations, under Avenida Rey Juan Carlos I in a consolidated urban zone. The line opened to public service on 11 April 2003, so the station name appears to have entered the Metro network with the opening of MetroSur.

The botanical origin is strong; the exact reason why this particular Leganés sector was historically called El Carrascal is less directly documented in the available sources. It may preserve an older rural land-name, but the available sources did not include a cadastral or municipal naming record for the Leganés toponym itself. For display purposes, the safest explanation is: the station is named after the Leganés neighbourhood, whose name ultimately means a place with carrascas.
