The station name comes from the local street name rather than directly from an artist or landscape. The stop stands on the ML1 light-rail route in Sanchinarro, and CRTM lists its access on Calle del Príncipe Carlos, with the vestibule named Álvarez de Villaamil. The nearby full street name is Calle del Alcalde Álvarez de Villaamil.

That longer street name is the key to the etymology. It points to Sergio Álvarez Rodríguez de Villaamil, also found in sources with the spelling Villamil, a Madrid doctor and politician remembered in the city’s list of mayors. Madrid City Council’s official mayoral list includes “Sergio Alvarez de Villaamil, 1935.”

His mayoralty belongs to the complicated municipal politics of the Second Republic. Secondary street-name sources describe him as entering Madrid City Council in the period of the 1934 managing commission, succeeding Rafael Salazar Alonso after the straperlo scandal, and holding the mayoralty until the February 1936 elections restored the earlier municipal corporation. Those details are useful context, but the strongest official point is simpler: Madrid records him among its twentieth-century mayors.

The transport name arrived with a very different phase of Madrid history. The ML1 line was put into service on 24 May 2007 to connect Pinar de Chamartín with Sanchinarro and Las Tablas, areas then being consolidated as new northern urban developments. The Comunidad de Madrid infrastructure page notes that the line’s opening involved nine stations, and CRTM’s current line page lists Álvarez de Villaamil as a surface, accessible stop in fare zone A.

The main caution is spelling and mistaken identity. Some transport material and encyclopedic pages have used “Álvarez de Villamil” with one “a,” while current CRTM station material uses “Álvarez de Villaamil.” More importantly, the station should not be attributed to Genaro or Jenaro Pérez Villaamil, the Romantic painter from Ferrol; Prado sources confirm that painter’s identity, but the street’s “Alcalde” title and Madrid mayoral evidence point instead to Sergio Álvarez Rodríguez de Villaamil.
