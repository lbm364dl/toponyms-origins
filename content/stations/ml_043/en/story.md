The name of this tram stop has two layers. The older part, **Bulevar Sur**, points to the southern boulevard of Parla, a central urban corridor served by the Tranvía de Parla. The added dedication, **Miguel Ángel Blanco**, commemorates Miguel Ángel Blanco Garrido, the young Ermua councillor whose kidnapping and murder by ETA in July 1997 became a landmark in Spain’s civic rejection of terrorism.

Miguel Ángel Blanco was born in Ermua in 1968 and was a councillor for the People’s Party in that Basque municipality. On 10 July 1997 ETA kidnapped him and issued a 48-hour ultimatum demanding that ETA prisoners be moved to prisons in the Basque Country. The demand was directed at the Spanish government, but the effect was national: vigils, silent protests, white hands, blue ribbons and mass demonstrations spread across Spain while the deadline approached.

When the ultimatum expired on 12 July 1997, Blanco was taken to a rural area near Lasarte and shot. He was found still alive and transferred to hospital, but died in the early hours of 13 July. The reaction to the crime came to be known as the **Spirit of Ermua**, a phrase used for the broad civic mobilisation that confronted ETA’s violence and changed the public atmosphere around terrorism in Spain.

The Parla name is not simply a general tribute added at random to a transport map. In January 2019 the municipal plenary approved assigning the name **Bulevar Miguel Ángel Blanco** to the section of the former Bulevar Sur between Travesía Severo Ochoa and the roundabout by the Police Station. Tranvía de Parla then announced on 22 March 2022 that the stop on that boulevard would also carry the name, producing the combined station name used in operator timetables: **Bulevar Sur - M.A. Blanco**.

There is a small naming wrinkle for public readers: some transport pages still list the stop simply as **Bulevar Sur**, while Tranvía de Parla’s own 2022 notice and current timetables include the Miguel Ángel Blanco dedication. The best-supported reading is therefore that the stop was originally Bulevar Sur and was later given a commemorative suffix following the municipal renaming of the boulevard.
