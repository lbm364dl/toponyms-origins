Campamento station is named after the Campamento area, not after a single building or person. In Spanish, *campamento* can mean a military encampment; in this part of Madrid the word became a durable place-name because of the Campamento Militar de Carabanchel.

The older setting was not originally a Madrid city neighbourhood. The land belonged to Carabanchel Alto, one of the historic Carabanchel municipalities west and southwest of the capital. Municipal planning documents for the protected Colonia Militar Campamento describe the area as part of Carabanchel Alto until the Carabancheles were incorporated into Madrid in the late 1940s.

The military association is nineteenth-century. Madrid City Council planning documentation states that the Campamento Militar de Carabanchel had formed in that century, and the Spanish Army identifies a barracks project for the Campamento de Carabanchel approved in 1886. Local histories add an earlier sequence: pastureland, a gunpowder store in the late eighteenth century, military manoeuvre grounds from 1856, and then barracks, homes and taverns growing into a named barrio.

That civilian growth matters for the station name. A local-history account from the Centros Municipales de Mayores Latina says that in 1885 the mayor of Carabanchel Alto appointed a regidor for the barrio de Campamento, explicitly linking the name to the military camps. Later, the area was reinforced by military colonies and installations around the Paseo and Carretera de Extremadura.

The transport history adds a small twist. The present station is on Metro line 5 and CRTM lists its accesses on Avenida del Padre Piquer, but the railway corridor is older than its current line assignment: the section now used by line 5 includes former Suburbano infrastructure that later changed operating lines. The name, however, has remained geographically consistent: it points to the nearby Campamento military landscape and the neighbourhood identity that grew from it.
