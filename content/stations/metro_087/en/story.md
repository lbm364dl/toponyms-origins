The Metro station is not named directly from a modern commemorative decision but from the place where it was built: Plaza de Antón Martín. Metro de Madrid’s historical station file places the station in that square and records its opening as part of the Sol-Atocha extension of Line 1 on 26 December 1921.

The older name behind the station belongs to Antón Martín, a 16th-century hospital brother from Cuenca, often identified in sources as Antón Martín de Dios or Antón Martín Aragón. He became the first companion and successor of San Juan de Dios, the founder of the hospital order devoted to caring for the sick and poor.

In 1552 Antón Martín founded the Hospital de Nuestra Señora del Amor de Dios on or beside Calle de Atocha, in the area now marked by the plaza and the parish of San Salvador y San Nicolás. The institution was also known popularly as the Hospital de Antón Martín or Hospital de San Juan de Dios. Madrid municipal sources preserve the traditional account that the hospital began with a small ward and that Martín died in 1553 after illness, while his foundation continued to grow.

That hospital explains why the name moved from a person to an urban landmark. Memoria de Madrid states that the former Plaza del Hospital received the name Antón Martín in the 17th century, and 19th-century Madrid chronicler Ramón de Mesonero Romanos already described the plazuela as named for the venerable brother who founded the hospital there in 1552. The plaza later became a busy crossing of Atocha, Magdalena, Santa Isabel, Amor de Dios, León and Moratín, so the Metro naturally adopted the established place-name.

The etymology is therefore secure, but a careful display should separate the station, the plaza and the man. The station name was fixed by its 1921 opening; the plaza name is much older. Exact biographical details vary between sources, especially the form of his surname and whether his death is given simply as 1553, Christmas Eve 1553, or 21 December 1553, so the strongest public wording is that he died in Madrid in December 1553.
