Colón station is named after Plaza de Colón, the major square beside the station. The square is explicitly dedicated to Christopher Columbus, whose Spanish name is Cristóbal Colón, so the station name is a short transport version of the surrounding urban toponym.

The public monument explains why the name is so visible in this part of Madrid. In the late nineteenth century, a commemorative monument was promoted for the square: Arturo Mélida designed and carved the neo-Gothic pedestal, and Jerónimo Suñol made the Carrara marble statue of Columbus. The monument was completed in 1885, although its official handover to the city was delayed until 12 October 1892, matching the fourth centenary of the 1492 voyage.

The metro station came later. Colón opened on 23 March 1944 as one of the eight stations of the first independent Line 4 section between Argüelles and Goya. That route followed streets including Génova and Goya and gave the new stop the name of the already established square above it.

The square itself has changed repeatedly. The former Casa de la Moneda site was converted into the Jardines del Descubrimiento, the monument was moved during the 1970s remodelling, underground facilities including the Centro Cultural de la Villa were planned below the square, and in 2009 the Columbus monument was returned to the centre of the roundabout.

The etymology is therefore secure, but a few details need care. The station is served by Metro Line 4; it is not a Line 8 station. Also, the monument should not be attributed only to Suñol: Suñol made the statue, while Mélida was responsible for the project and pedestal.
