Colón station takes its name from nearby Plaza de Colón, Madrid’s square dedicated to Christopher Columbus. The plaza’s monument was promoted in the late nineteenth century: Arturo Mélida designed and carved the neo-Gothic pedestal, while Jerónimo Suñol sculpted the Carrara marble figure of Columbus. The work was completed in 1885 and officially handed over to Madrid on 12 October 1892, the fourth centenary of Columbus’s first Atlantic crossing. The station opened on 23 March 1944 as part of the original Argüelles-Goya section of Metro Line 4. The surrounding square was substantially reshaped in the 1970s and again in 2009, but the name and dedication remained tied to Columbus.
