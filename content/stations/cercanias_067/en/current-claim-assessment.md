The existing explanation is substantially supported: the station takes its name from the Las Matas place-name; mata is a vegetation term with the expected Late Latin etymology; municipal planning material and local railway history support Matas Altas in 1755, the 1861 railway opening, the 1919 classification yard, and the 1922 railway workers' housing. The main nuance is that Las Matas was not simply born from the railway: an older Matas Altas landscape/toponym and road-building context preceded the railway settlement.
