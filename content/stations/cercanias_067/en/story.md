Las Matas station is named after the Las Matas area of Las Rozas de Madrid. The name is descriptive rather than commemorative: in Spanish, mata can mean a low woody plant, a small plant, a branch or clump of herbs, or even a patch of land covered by trees or shrubs. In a Madrid landscape of scrub, encinas, retamares and sandy soils, the name reads as a vegetation place-name: the place of the thickets.

The older form most often cited for the area is Matas Altas. Las Rozas municipal planning documentation says that this name appears on a 1755 map and links it with the route toward Segovia. That evidence matters because it shows that the place-name was older than the commuter station and older than the great railway works that later made Las Matas famous.

The railway changed the scale and identity of the place. The Madrid-El Escorial section of the northern railway opened to the public on 9 August 1861, and contemporary accounts of the route list Las Matas among the intermediate stops between Madrid and El Escorial. From then on, the old landscape name was fixed to a railway point on the line that would become the Madrid-Irun/Hendaye route.

Las Matas became much more than a stop in the early twentieth century. Municipal sources state that the works for the freight classification yard were completed in 1919 after beginning four years earlier, and that the company then decided to build 47 homes for railway employees; those houses began construction in 1922. The Las Rozas railway museum page describes today's Las Matas railway district as one of Spain's best-preserved railway settlements.

The name origin is therefore well supported, but the simplest version needs one correction. Las Matas was not a new name created by the railway: the railway inherited an older descriptive toponym, probably from Matas Altas, and then gave it urban and social permanence. The station name preserves both layers: the scrubland word mata and the railway community that grew around it.
