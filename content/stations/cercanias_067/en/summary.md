Las Matas is a descriptive place-name: a mata is a low woody plant, a clump of vegetation, or by extension a scrub-covered area. Municipal planning material records the older form Matas Altas on a 1755 map in the area of present Las Matas. The railway did not invent the name, but it transformed the place: the Madrid-El Escorial section opened to the public on 9 August 1861, and Las Matas became one of the intermediate railway points. Its later identity became strongly railway-based when the Las Matas classification yard was completed in 1919 and the company began building workers' housing in 1922.
