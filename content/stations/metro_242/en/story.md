San Nicasio station takes its name from the San Nicasio neighbourhood of Leganés. The regional transport authority places the station on Metro Line 12 and lists its accesses on Avenida del Mar Mediterráneo and Calle de la Encina, while the Comunidad de Madrid describes it as standing in the San Nicasio neighbourhood. At station level, the name is therefore inherited from the place around it.

Behind the neighbourhood name is a much older religious devotion. Leganés venerates San Nicasio as one of its patrons, and the local hermitage, now a parish church, became the visible landmark around which the name settled. The Diocese of Getafe's account of the brotherhood says the devotion began in the 16th century and cites the 1580 Relaciones Topográficas of Philip II, where residents said they kept the day of San Nicasio because earlier generations had vowed it during pestilence.

The present church building belongs to a later phase. Local and municipal sources describe the Ermita de San Nicasio as an 18th-century neoclassical temple associated with Ventura Rodríguez, built in the second half of that century and protected in the municipal catalogue. The Diocese gives 1775-1785 and says the Marqués de Leganés, Diego Messía de Guzmán, commissioned it from Ventura Rodríguez shortly before the architect's death; other local sources mention a project presented in 1772 and an earlier, more modest sanctuary on the same site.

That long religious history also shaped civic identity. The Ayuntamiento notes that the Cofradía de San Nicasio was constituted on 16 April 1600 and that its founding record said Leganés had already had San Nicasio as patron for about a century, seeking protection from plagues. Today the October festivities, the brotherhood, and the church keep the name active not only as a devotional label but also as a district identity.

There is one important correction. The existing record links the name to Saint Nicasius of Reims, a bishop martyred around the early 5th century, but the Leganés tradition documented by the Diocese and local historians identifies its patron as San Nicasio, bishop and martyr of Rouen, celebrated on 11 October. The station-name origin is secure; the exact hagiographical identity should be recorded according to the Leganés tradition rather than the Reims identification.
