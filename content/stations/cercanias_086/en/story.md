Vallecas station is named after Vallecas, the historic territory and former municipality whose name still identifies two Madrid districts: Puente de Vallecas and Villa de Vallecas. The Cercanias stop sits by Sierra de Guadalupe and serves lines C-2, C-7 and C-8, so the railway name functions as a gateway to a much older place-name rather than as a modern descriptive label.

The name is medieval. A text transmitted with the Fuero de Madrid of 1202 mentions Balecas, and later local history links that form with the carrascal or holm-oak grazing land of Balecas. That early evidence is especially useful because it shows that the name existed long before the modern neighbourhoods, the railway, or the twentieth-century expansion of Madrid.

The strongest etymological explanation found in this review is the one attributed to the Arabist Federico Corriente. In that reading, Vallecas would descend from an Andalusi Romance form often rendered ballaykas, meaning 'valley areas': a Romance base related to Latin vallis, 'valley', with an attributive suffix used in the bilingual linguistic world around medieval Madrid and Toledo. This is still an etymological hypothesis, but it fits the old Balecas form better than later anecdotal stories.

Vallecas also has famous popular etymologies. The best known tells of a Muslim settler named Kas, producing a supposed 'Valley of Kas'. Local writers trace versions of that story through nineteenth- and twentieth-century publications, but they also note that it rests on resemblance and legend rather than rigorous linguistic evidence. The older Vallis Egas, or 'valley of Egas', theory is another historical proposal, but it is usually treated today as less persuasive than the valley-areas explanation.

The transport name has its own chronology. A railway station called Vallecas existed from the nineteenth-century Madrid-Zaragoza railway era; the current Cercanias station was shifted and rebuilt around the 1999 Metro line 1 extension to create the interchange with Sierra de Guadalupe. Administratively, Vallecas remained outside Madrid until the annexation decree of 10 November 1950, implemented in December 1950, so the station name preserves the memory of a place that was once a municipality in its own right.
