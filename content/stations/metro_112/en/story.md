The metro name **Puerta del Ángel** comes first from the place around the station: the Puerta del Ángel neighbourhood, plaza and old approach to the Casa de Campo. The current station is under the Paseo de Extremadura, close to the Puente de Segovia, exactly in the area where that older toponym became fixed in Madrid’s urban geography.

Behind the modern place-name was a former gate of the Casa de Campo. Madrid municipal material on the historic Casa de Campo wall states that the Puerta del Ángel received its name from the disappeared Hermitage of the Santo Ángel de la Guarda, which stood in this area from the early modern period until the eighteenth century. The archaeological documentation for Madrid Río also connects the name with the Casa de Campo access and with the hermitage, and notes that the older route here was the road of Alcorcón or Extremadura.

The religious story attached to the name goes back to an image of the Holy Guardian Angel. Several sources say that the image had been associated with the medieval Puerta de Guadalajara in central Madrid and was later placed in a hermitage near the Segovia bridge. The hermitage was founded in 1605 by the porters or mace-bearers of Madrid’s municipal council, a group whose devotion to the Santo Ángel became part of the name’s transmission.

The chronology is not entirely tidy. Some sources place the destruction of the Puerta de Guadalajara in 1580, during celebrations over Felipe II’s annexation of Portugal; others give 1582, connected with later Portuguese-war celebrations. What is well supported is the broader sequence: an angel image linked to the Puerta de Guadalajara, a 1605 hermitage near the Puente de Segovia, a Casa de Campo gate called Puerta del Ángel, and then a neighbourhood, plaza and metro station carrying the same name.

For the transport history, Puerta del Ángel is a relatively modern station. The Comunidad de Madrid records the closure of Line 6 as a circular line in May 1995, with six new stations including Puerta del Ángel; Memoria de Madrid likewise describes the station as inaugurated in May 1995 in the Laguna-Ciudad Universitaria section. The name therefore preserves a much older local toponym, but the metro station itself dates from the 1995 completion of the circular Line 6.
