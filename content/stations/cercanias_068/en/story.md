Torrelodones station is named after the municipality of Torrelodones, in the northwest of the Community of Madrid. Transport sources identify the stop as Torrelodones and place it on the Cercanías C-8/C-10 corridor, while municipal information locates it in the Colonia area, around three kilometres from the historic centre.

Behind the municipal name is an older landscape name. Toponomasticon Hispaniae analyzes Torrelodones as a Castilian compound of **torre** and **lodones**: the second element is the plural of **lodón**, a tree now more often called **almez** in Spanish, the hackberry. The meaning is therefore not a personal surname but a descriptive place-name: the tower associated with the lodones.

The tower is the Torre de los Lodones, also called the Atalaya de Torrelodones, still one of the municipality's most visible symbols. The town council describes it as the oldest and most emblematic element of local heritage, and relates it to the Andalusi defensive system of the central frontier. Its exact original Arabic name is not known; the municipal history page stresses that the surviving name is Romance and therefore later than the Christian conquest of Toledo in 1085.

The chronology needs a little care. The current public claim says the name is first documented in 1287; the Torrelodones municipal history page also gives 1287 and lists variants such as Latorrelodones, Latorredelodones, La torre de los Lodones, Torre Lodones and Torrelodones. The consulted Toponomasticon entry, however, cites the medieval form **La Torre de Lodones** only around 1340, then later forms including **Torrelodones** in 1591 and **Torre di lodones** on a 1682 map.

There are other local explanations, including mud around the tower, legendary lords called Lodón, Odón or Ordón, and a learned Latin interpretation proposed by Coromines. The best-supported explanation is the simpler one: a Romance compound referring to a tower and to lodones trees. The station did not create the name; rather, the railway stop inherited a medieval place-name that had already condensed a watchtower, vegetation and frontier history into one word.
