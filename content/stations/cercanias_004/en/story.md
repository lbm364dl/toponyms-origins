The present station name has two origins. **Chamartín** comes from the north-Madrid district where the railway complex stands, while **Clara Campoamor** was added officially to honor the lawyer, writer and parliamentarian who became the great public defender of women’s suffrage in Spain’s Second Republic.

The older place name points back to **Chamartín de la Rosa**, once a separate municipality north of Madrid. The City of Madrid explains that this old territory roughly covered parts of today’s Chamartín and Tetuán, and that it was definitively incorporated into Madrid in 1948 after a 1947 decree. The station, opened in 1967 according to Renfe, therefore inherited a district name whose history is older than the railway itself.

For the word **Chamartín**, the strongest scholarly explanation is not the tavern legend of “echa, Martín” or a French “Chez Martin”, but a medieval personal-name formation. Toponomasticon Hispaniae, in a signed entry by E. Nieto Ballester for the Chamartín name family, explains Chamartín as a repopulation toponym made from two names: **Echa**, related to Basque *aita* in an old personal-name use, plus **Martín**, from Latin *Martinus*. That academic source explicitly connects this pattern with the well-known Madrid Chamartín, although its dated documentary examples are for the Ávila municipality of Chamartín.

The modern dedication is much firmer. Order TMA/1240/2020, published in the BOE on 23 December 2020, changed the railway station’s official name from **Estación de ferrocarril de Madrid-Chamartín** to **Estación de Madrid-Chamartín-Clara Campoamor**, with legal effects from 24 December 2020. The order links the change to the coming 90th anniversary of women’s suffrage in Spain and describes Campoamor as the promoter and parliamentary defender of that right.

Clara Campoamor was elected deputy for Madrid in 1931, at a time when women could be elected but could not yet vote. In the Cortes, she took part in the constitutional work that led to Article 36 and defended women’s political rights in the decisive debates of 30 September and 1 October 1931. For public wording, the name can therefore be presented as verified for Campoamor and probable for the older Chamartín etymology: the official dedication is certain, while the medieval meaning of Chamartín is best explained as scholarly but not Madrid-documentary proof from the cited 1247 form.
