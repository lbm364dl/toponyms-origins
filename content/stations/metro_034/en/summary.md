Quevedo is best explained as a station named after a place that was already named after a person: the Glorieta de Quevedo. Metro de Madrid's historical sheet states that the station name comes from that roundabout and that the square received the writer's name in the mid-nineteenth century. Francisco de Quevedo y Villegas was born in Madrid in 1580 and died in Villanueva de los Infantes in 1645, after a career that made him one of the major voices of the Spanish Golden Age. The station opened on 21 October 1925 on Line 2, initially as a terminus, and became an intermediate station when the line was extended to Cuatro Caminos in 1929.
