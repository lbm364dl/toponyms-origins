Quevedo station is named for the Glorieta de Quevedo, the roundabout under which Line 2 runs in Chamberí. The name therefore reaches the Metro through the city map: the square honored Francisco de Quevedo y Villegas before the station opened there in 1925.

Francisco de Quevedo was a Madrid-born writer of the Spanish Golden Age, born in 1580 and dead in Villanueva de los Infantes in 1645. He wrote poetry, satire, political prose and fiction, and his sharp conceptist style made his name one of the most recognizable in Spanish literary history.

The place had an older urban story before it became a Metro stop. Metro de Madrid's historical material describes the area as once near the Puerta de Fuencarral, also called the Puerta de los Pozos, on the edge of the built city and on roads leading toward Fuencarral and France. By the mid-nineteenth century the square had received Quevedo's name.

The station opened on 21 October 1925 as part of the Sol-Quevedo extension of Line 2. At first it was the end of the line; in 1929 the extension from Quevedo to Cuatro Caminos made it an intermediate station and improved the connection between Lines 1 and 2 and their depots.

The present square reinforces the literary dedication visually. Madrid's municipal heritage catalogue records the monument to Francisco de Quevedo by Agustín Querol, made in 1902 and moved in 1963 to the glorieta dedicated to the writer. The etymology is secure, but the best public version should foreground the place-name chain rather than rely on famous but less essential anecdotes about Quevedo's feuds.
