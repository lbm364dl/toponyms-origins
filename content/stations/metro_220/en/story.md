Abrantes station is named after the place where it sits: Avenida de Abrantes, in the Carabanchel district. The Community of Madrid describes the first Line 11 works as running parallel to Avenida de Abrantes, and the station opened with the Plaza Elíptica-Pan Bendito section on 16 November 1998.

The name had already become part of local geography before the Metro arrived. Madrid's official mapping identifies Abrantes as neighbourhood 117 of Carabanchel, and the avenue is one of its main reference axes. In everyday terms, the station name is therefore both a street name and a neighbourhood name.

The deeper origin is best read as a place-name. Abrantes is a Portuguese town and municipality on the Tagus, and the surrounding Madrid streets form a small Portuguese toponymic landscape: Oporto, Portalegre, Elvas, Cintra, Viseo and Estoril appear around the same area. That pattern makes a Portuguese geographical dedication more plausible than a direct dedication to one individual person.

There is also a noble Madrid connection to the word Abrantes. The Palacio de Abrantes on Calle Mayor took its name from the Dukes of Abrantes, who acquired the building in 1842 and reshaped it in the nineteenth century; it later became the Italian Cultural Institute. This is useful context, but it is not the same as proof that the Carabanchel avenue was named after the dukes.

For public display, the safest wording is that the station is named for Avenida de Abrantes and the Abrantes neighbourhood, ultimately linked to the Portuguese place-name Abrantes. The older dataset's person-type classification is too strong unless a municipal street-naming file is found that explicitly says the avenue honoured the Dukes of Abrantes.
