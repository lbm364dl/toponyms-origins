Azuqueca station is named for Azuqueca de Henares, the municipality in Guadalajara where the station stands. Official railway data list the station simply as AZUQUECA, in the municipality of Azuqueca de Henares, within the Cercanías Madrid area.

The oldest layer of the name is not railway-related. Toponomasticon Hispaniae, in an entry by J. A. Ranz Yubero with additions by E. Nieto Ballester, derives Azuqueca from Arabic sukayka, a diminutive associated with a road, little path, or alley. In that interpretation, the name describes the line of movement or narrow passage around which the first buildings gathered.

The full municipal name adds de Henares, referring to the River Henares. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains Henares as a Romance river name related to henar, a place with hay or hay meadows. The combination therefore joins two different naming layers: an Arabic settlement name and a later identifying reference to the river valley.

The place is historically tied to routes. The Toponomasticon entry notes earlier traces of settlement but treats the locality as probably founded in the Arabic period; it also records documentary forms such as Azuqueca in a papal bull of 6 June 1192, Açuqueca in 1528, and Azuqueca de Nares in the 1751 Catastro de Ensenada. The 1575 Relaciones Topográficas already describe Azuqueca as an aldeia under the jurisdiction of Guadalajara and give its local boundaries between the Henares side and nearby roads and ventas.

There is a competing Arabic explanation that links the name with a market or fair, but the strongest specialized source calls that less plausible than the 'small road/alley' interpretation. A later scholarly article by Ranz Yubero discusses additional possibilities involving waterworks, acequias, or small canals, but also acknowledges the phonetic difficulties. For public use, the safest wording is that Azuqueca is an Arabic-origin place name best supported as sukayka, a diminutive referring to a small way or passage, while noting that older literature proposed 'small market' or water-channel explanations.
