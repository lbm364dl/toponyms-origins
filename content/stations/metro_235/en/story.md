Getafe Central is named after Getafe, the municipality it serves, and after its central position in the town's transport network. The station opened with MetroSur, Line 12 of Metro de Madrid, on 11 April 2003. It is also tied to the Cercanías station known as Getafe Centro, making the name a practical description of a central interchange rather than a dedication to a person.

The older name behind the station is the place name Getafe. Local and municipal accounts connect it with the medieval forms Xatafi, Satafi or Xetafe, and with the settlement that grew along the royal road between Madrid and Toledo. In that story, the town took shape when inhabitants of Alarnes and nearby settlements moved to a healthier roadside site.

The best early testimony for the traditional explanation comes from the Relaciones Histórico-Geográficas ordered under Philip II. In the Getafe response, dated in the context of the 1575 questionnaire and copied as a 1576 local relation, the informants said they did not know the matter with certainty, but had heard that Arabic jata meant a long thing and that the name referred to houses strung out along the road. That is valuable evidence for the tradition, not proof that the Arabic etymology is linguistically correct.

Modern toponymic caution matters here. Jairo J. García Sánchez, writing for the Centro Virtual Cervantes, says Getafe presents many etymological problems: an Arabic origin is presumed from its shape and configuration, but the medieval documentation does not currently allow scholars to go further. This makes Getafe Central a clear station name but a layered place-name story.

There is also a railway naming nuance. Renfe's station page says the earlier railway station belonged to the former Madrid-Ciudad Real line and kept the name Getafe-Badajoz until the creation of Cercanías Madrid; the present building opened on the same day as Line 12. For public display, the strongest formulation is therefore: the station is named for central Getafe, while the city name's popular 'long street' Arabic explanation is traditional but uncertain.
