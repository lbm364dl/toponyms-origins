Colonia Jardín is a place-name station: it refers to the local residential enclave around Arroyo Meaques, not to a person. The best documented historical nucleus is the Colonia Militar Arroyo Meaques, built after the Civil War by the former Ministry of the Army to house officers, non-commissioned officers, and their families. Madrid City Council records describe it as a protected historic colony with 133 detached houses, most of them small houses with gardens. The station opened under this name on 22 October 2002 and later became the shared interchange for Line 10 and light metro lines ML2 and ML3. The broader garden-city interpretation is plausible as urban context, but the directly documented explanation is simpler: the station is named for the nearby colony-like, gardened residential area.
