Villaverde Bajo-Cruce is a deliberately local name. The Metro station is in the area long known as Villaverde Bajo, and the suffix **Cruce** points to **El Cruce**, the busy junction of Avenida de Andalucía with the road from Villaverde to Vallecas. When Line 3 was extended south in 2007, the station was placed there to serve Villaverde Bajo and the southern edge of Los Ángeles, with a surface bus interchange reinforcing the same name.

The deeper name is **Villaverde**. It is a transparent Spanish place-name: *villa* in the sense of a settlement or town, plus *verde*, 'green'. For the color word, Toponomasticon Hispaniae links Spanish *verde* to Latin *viridis*. That supports the broad 'green village/town' explanation, although the available sources did not include a medieval or official source proving that this Madrid place was historically written as the exact Latin phrase *villa viridis*.

The **Bajo** part belongs to the local geography and urban history of Villaverde. The old municipality was incorporated into Madrid by decree in 1954, after a long rural and industrial history on the southern edge of the capital. In common usage, Villaverde Alto refers to the old upland/historic core, while Villaverde Bajo refers to the lower/eastern area shaped especially by the railway and later housing growth.

The **Cruce** part is more than a road label. Villaverde Bajo sat beside one of Madrid's important railway junction landscapes, where lines and industrial branches connected the routes toward Extremadura, Andalucía and Levante. COAM's architectural guide describes Villaverde Bajo as a railway area near the bifurcation between the Extremadura and Andalucía-Levante lines, with an MZA halt opened in 1893 and a larger complex projected in 1924 and inaugurated in 1931.

One detail in the existing record needs correction: the early railway opening should not be given as 9 February 1861 for the Madrid-Aranjuez line. The standard chronology is 9 February 1851 for that line, while COAM's available source gives 1893 for the first notable MZA halt at Villaverde Bajo. The name origin itself is well supported, but the railway chronology should be handled as layered history rather than as one single opening date.
