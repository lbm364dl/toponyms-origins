The Metro station name combines two local references: Villaverde Bajo, the eastern/lower sector of the old municipality of Villaverde, and El Cruce, the crossing where Avenida de Andalucía meets the road from Villaverde to Vallecas. Regional transport documentation places the station precisely at that road junction and notes that it serves Villaverde Bajo and southern Los Ángeles. The older name Villaverde is best treated as a transparent Romance place-name made from villa and verde, with verde continuing Latin viridis; a specific historical form 'villa viridis' for this Madrid place was not found. The railway context is important but should be stated carefully: Villaverde Bajo became a major rail junction, with a notable MZA halt opened in 1893 and a larger station complex projected in 1924 and inaugurated in 1931.
