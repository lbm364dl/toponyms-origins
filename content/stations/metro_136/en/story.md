Las Musas station takes its name from the surrounding Las Musas place-name in eastern Madrid: Calle de las Musas and the Colonia Las Musas area in today’s San Blas-Canillejas district. The official Madrid street GIS lists Calle de las Musas as a current municipal street, and city planning material separately names Colonia Las Musas as an urban area. For the Metro station, the practical source of the name was therefore local geography, not a person or a transport company brand.

Behind that local name stand the Muses of classical mythology. In the tradition systematized by Hesiod, the Muses were nine sister goddesses, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, associated with poetry, music, history, dance, astronomy and other arts and forms of knowledge. The name gives this part of Madrid a consciously cultured, literary tone rather than describing a natural feature.

The surrounding street pattern supports that reading. Municipal rehabilitation maps for the area show Colonia Las Musas alongside street names such as Musas, Lucano, Pitágoras, La Ilíada and Troya. That cluster places the station name in a wider classical and literary naming landscape, where mythology, antiquity and letters were used to give a new residential area a recognizable identity.

The transport chronology is clear. Metro de Madrid archival material records that Line 7 opened on 17 July 1974 on the Las Musas-Pueblo Nuevo section, together with the Canillejas depot. Another Metro archival record describes Las Musas as the station at the end of the Pueblo Nuevo-Las Musas stretch, configured as the line’s terminus in July 1974. It remained the eastern end of the line until the later MetroEste extension changed the role of this part of Line 7.

The best-supported explanation is therefore: a Metro station named after the nearby Las Musas street and colony, whose name ultimately invokes the mythological Muses. One useful caution is that Las Musas is not one of Madrid’s official administrative neighbourhoods today; the station is in Rosas, within San Blas-Canillejas. No original naming decree for the station was found, but the convergence of official street data, municipal urban-area naming and Metro archival records makes the origin highly secure.
