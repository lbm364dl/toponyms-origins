The station name comes from the Paseo de las Delicias, the avenue under or beside which the modern transport stops sit. The older name was Paseo de las Delicias del Rio, because it ended at a place called Las Delicias near the Manzanares canal. In the late 18th century the promenade formed part of the planned southern approaches to Madrid, with tree-lined walks for pedestrians, coaches and carriages. The name later attached itself to the railway station of Madrid-Delicias, inaugurated on 30 March 1880, and then to the Metro and Cercanias stops in the same area.
