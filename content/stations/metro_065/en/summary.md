The Metro station is named after Barajas, the historic settlement at the north-eastern edge of Madrid. Barajas was once an independent municipality; a decree of 18 November 1949 approved its total annexation to Madrid, and in 1987 it became a separate municipal district. The station opened as the Line 8 terminus serving the old Barajas village area, near Avenida de Logroño and Plaza de Pajarones. The origin of the place-name itself is not settled: early sources record a story about a Moor called Baraxa or Bar Axa, but modern toponymists consider that a folk explanation. Comparative work gives stronger, though still debatable, support to a pre-Roman personal, tribal, or place-name base related to other Baraja/Baralla-type names.
