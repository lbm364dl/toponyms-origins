The direct origin of the station name is a place: Palos de la Frontera, a municipality in the province of Huelva, Andalusia. The Metro stop sits under Calle de Palos de la Frontera in Arganzuela, so its transport name follows the Madrid street name as well as the older historical town it commemorates.

Palos de la Frontera is famous above all for its old river port on an inlet of the Tinto River. From that Port of Palos, on 3 August 1492, Christopher Columbus sailed with the Pinzón brothers and the crews of the Santa María, Pinta and Niña. Local and regional sources identify the port as one of the key Columbian sites connected with the preparations and departure of the first voyage across the Atlantic.

The name also carries a toponymic correction. The Huelva town was historically Palos, and the town council adopted the full name Palos de la Frontera on 25 May 1642. Madrid, however, laid out a street called Palos de Moguer in the late nineteenth century, using a name that official Madrid documentation now describes as an erroneous, non-existent toponym created when early chroniclers confused Palos with nearby Moguer.

Metro inherited that street name when the station opened on Line 3 on 26 March 1949, during the Embajadores-Delicias extension. For decades the station was therefore Palos de Moguer, matching the old street sign rather than the official Huelva place name. The City of Madrid corrected the street name in 1979, and a 1986 agreement between Metro and the Palos de la Frontera town council led to the station's renaming.

The best-supported date for the Metro change is 30 May 1986: El País reported on 29 May that the station would take the new name the following day. The broader Madrid administrative barrio lagged behind both street and station, remaining officially Palos de Moguer until the 2022 municipal regulation changed it to Palos de la Frontera. The station name is therefore a small but clear example of a transport map preserving, and then correcting, a historical naming error.
