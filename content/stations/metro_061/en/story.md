Colombia station takes its name from its immediate street setting. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes places the station at Príncipe de Vergara, with accesses by Plaza de la República Dominicana and Calle de Colombia, and the official Madrid street directory records Calle de Colombia in the district of Chamartin. The most direct reading is therefore street first, country second: the metro name reflects Calle de Colombia, whose referent is the Republic of Colombia.

That street sits in one of Madrid's clearest Hispano-American naming clusters. In and around the Hispanoamerica neighborhood, Madrid's street map gathers names such as Chile, Puerto Rico, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, República Dominicana, Ecuador, Lima, Cuzco, La Habana and Colombia. The pattern is not accidental in everyday urban reading: this part of Chamartin turns the map of Spanish-speaking America into a local geography of streets and squares.

The transport chronology is more precise than the common shorthand suggests. Colombia did not open in 1982. It opened on 30 December 1983 on the Line 9b section between Plaza de Castilla and Avenida de América, with neighboring stations including Duque de Pastrana, Pío XII, Concha Espina and Cruz del Rayo. In 1986 that northern section was integrated into Line 9.

The Line 8 connection came later and changed the station's importance. On 21 May 2002, Metro opened the Nuevos Ministerios-Mar de Cristal section of Line 8 and remodeled Colombia as part of the airport-line connection through northern Madrid. Since then, Colombia has been both a neighborhood station and a strategic interchange between Line 8 and Line 9.

The etymology is highly secure, but the exact municipal act that assigned the street name was not located in the sources inspected. What is well supported is the chain of naming: the station is located by Calle de Colombia; the street belongs to a Hispano-American commemorative street-name environment; and Colombia is the South American republic whose name appears in that street.
