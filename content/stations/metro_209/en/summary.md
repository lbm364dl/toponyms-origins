Fuencarral is a place-name station: it carries the name of the historic village north of Madrid rather than a person. In the sixteenth-century Relaciones Topograficas, local informants explained the name through a fountain where cart-drivers watered their animals. A later academic reading adds an older form, Foncarral, and interprets the second element carra/carral as possibly connected with a stony road or ground. The former municipality was annexed to Madrid by a decree dated 10 November 1950, with the transfer commonly recorded as taking effect on 20 October 1951. The Metro station opened in 1982 on the former line 8, later incorporated into line 10. The official Callejero supplies a long city-road record for the same place-name, including Calle de Fuencarral from 11 January 1835 and Camino de Fuencarral from 1 January 1958.
