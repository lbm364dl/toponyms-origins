Opañel station carries the name of the neighbourhood around it in Carabanchel. That was not its first Metro name: when the Line 6 section through southern Madrid opened to the public in May 1981, this stop appeared as Elvas, after a nearby street name.

The change came quickly. Contemporary press reported that Elvas would become Opañel because changes to the original line route had left Calle Elvas away from the station, while Opañel better identified the surrounding area. The official change took effect at 6:00 on 27 June 1983, alongside the renaming of José Antonio as Gran Vía and General Mola as Príncipe de Vergara.

Behind the station name is an older Carabanchel place-name. Opañel is now one of the official barrios of the district, and the name is also preserved in Calle Arroyo de Opañel and in municipal facilities using that address. This strongly suggests that the neighbourhood name was tied to the remembered Arroyo de Opañel landscape, even though the stream itself has largely disappeared from the everyday urban surface.

There is a tempting deeper explanation: some Iberian place-names and river-names have been compared with a pre-Roman Indo-European root *up- or *op- meaning water or river. Francisco Villar's study is real and relevant to that wider hydronymic family, but it does not cite Opañel by name. For public interpretation, that idea should be presented as a linguistic possibility, not as a proven origin.

The best-supported station story is therefore practical and local: Metro replaced an inaccurate street-based name, Elvas, with the barrio name Opañel in 1983. The older arroyo connection is probable and useful context, while the prehistoric hydronym theory remains open for specialist review.
