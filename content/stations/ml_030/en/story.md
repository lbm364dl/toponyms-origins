Prado del Espino station takes its name from the place where it stands: the Prado del Espino area of Boadilla del Monte. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid lists the stop as a surface station at Calle Labradores, 1, and that location lies inside the business and industrial area known by the same name.

The words themselves point to a rural landscape. The Real Academia Espanola defines prado as land, often wet or irrigated, where grass is grown or left for grazing. Espino is a thorny rosaceous tree with white scented flowers and reddish fruit; in ordinary Spanish it includes the espino blanco or hawthorn-type tree also known as majuelo.

The toponym is not only a modern business-park label. Municipal planning documentation for the Sector S.U.R. 1 "Prado del Espino" describes the Barranco de Prado del Espino and says that this stream rises in Boadilla del Monte between the studied sector and the Poligono Industrial Prado del Espino before flowing toward the Arroyo del Nacedero. A 2024 official notice from the Confederacion Hidrografica del Tajo also names the Arroyo Prado del Espino as a regulated watercourse in Boadilla.

That hydrological evidence is useful because it shows Prado del Espino as a landscape name attached to a drainage line and surrounding ground, not merely a marketing name for offices. The present-day business park is a major employment area beside Santander's Ciudad Financiera, with municipal mobility documents describing it as an economic activity zone of roughly 150 companies and more than 2,500 workers. Its street names, such as Artesanos, Forjadores and Impresores, belong to the later urbanization of the area.

The safest explanation is therefore that the station was named after the existing Prado del Espino place-name, which literally means a meadow or pasture associated with espino. It is reasonable to connect espino with hawthorn or espino blanco, but the exact historical reason why this particular meadow or barranco acquired that plant name has not been directly documented in the sources inspected. The station name itself dates from the opening of Metro Ligero Oeste's ML3 service in July 2007.
