Tierra takes its name from Calle Planeta Tierra, the street that gives this part of Parla Este its immediate astronomical reference. The Regional Transport Consortium lists the stop as two tram platforms, Tierra Norte and Tierra Sur, on Avenida de las Estrellas and Avenida de los Planetas respectively, placing the station directly inside the planet-and-star grid of the neighborhood.

The name is not an old rural toponym or a commemorative dedication to a person. It belongs to a modern municipal naming system in which many Parla Este streets are organized around astronomy: Planeta Mercurio, Planeta Venus, Planeta Marte, Planeta Jupiter, Planeta Saturno, Planeta Urano and Planeta Neptuno appear alongside Calle Planeta Tierra. Nearby names also include stars such as Sirio, Vega, Altair, Antares and Polar, and constellations such as Orion, Andromeda, Pegaso, Casiopea, Osa Mayor and Osa Menor.

That street system belongs to the new eastern expansion of Parla. A 2001 BOE notice already refers to the urbanization works for the Plan Parcial area 4 bis, Residencial Este de Parla, and later transport sources describe the tram's second phase as the link between the established town and the new Parla Este district. In that context, Tierra is a transport name inherited from the planned street layout rather than a free-standing station name.

The stop also reflects the way the tram was fitted into Parla Este's avenues. CRTM's current station pages place Tierra Norte at Avenida de las Estrellas and Tierra Sur at Avenida de los Planetas, while the municipal street inventory identifies Calle Planeta Tierra within Residencial Este. The station is therefore better described as a dual stop named from the street it serves, not simply as a symbolic reference to Earth in isolation.

The evidence strongly supports the naming origin, but one detail in the existing claim should be softened. The idea that Tierra occupies the third orbital position, like Earth in the Solar System, is attractive and may work as interpretive context, but I did not find an official source stating that the tram stops or streets were deliberately ordered that way. The verified claim is narrower and stronger: the stop is named after Calle Planeta Tierra, within Parla Este's astronomy-themed urban nomenclature.
