The station name is direct and local: Avenida de Europa is named after Avenida de Europa in Pozuelo de Alarcón. The Community of Madrid describes the ML2 line as entering tunnel here to cross Vía de las Dos Castillas and Avenida de Europa, “which gives its name” to the underground station. The stop is placed at the junction of Avenida de Europa and Avenida de la Comunidad de Madrid, so the transport name follows the street name rather than a person or historic event.

Behind the present avenue is a large modern urban-development area formerly identified in planning documents as the Plan Parcial Ampliación Casa de Campo. Pozuelo’s municipal archive explains that this plan had an initial approval phase in 1975, was discussed repeatedly by municipal bodies in 1979, and saw its first constructions in 1985. The same archive notes that building references continued for years to use the old descriptor “Plan Parcial de Ampliación de la Casa de Campo,” even as street names gradually became the everyday way to locate addresses.

The development was controversial and ambitious. El País reported in April 1981 that Pozuelo approved the plan, involving the construction of about 7,500 homes along the new border line inside Pozuelo and the transfer of land connected with the Casa de Campo expansion. A later BOE notice from 2004 still identified a municipal parcel at Avenida de Europa 26 as part of the Plan Parcial Ampliación Casa de Campo, showing how the planning name and the street address coexisted for decades.

Today the name Avenida de Europa points less to the old planning file and more to a recognizable district. Pozuelo’s town hall describes Avenida de Europa as an area with many businesses in office buildings, professional premises and shops, and its mobility pages list Avenida de Europa as a regulated-parking zone. Around it, the European motif is easy to see in official street lists: Alemania, Bélgica, Dinamarca, Francia, Grecia, Inglaterra, Irlanda, Portugal, Rumanía, Suecia and Suiza all appear in the Avenida de Europa parking area, together with city names such as Atenas and Estocolmo.

The station opened with ML2 on 27 July 2007, when the Community of Madrid put the Colonia Jardín-Pozuelo de Alarcón light-rail line into service. Its naming is therefore well supported as a street-derived place name. What remains less firmly documented is the symbolic origin of the avenue’s own name: the European street cluster is real, but the claim that it was specifically chosen because of Spain’s 1986 entry into the European Economic Community should be treated as an attractive but currently unproven interpretation unless a municipal naming agreement or planning memorandum is found.
