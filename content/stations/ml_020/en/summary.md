Avenida de Europa is a place-name station: its name comes directly from the Pozuelo de Alarcón avenue above and beside the stop. The Community of Madrid states that the ML2 route crosses Avenida de Europa, which gives its name to the underground station, located below the junction of Avenida de Europa and Avenida de la Comunidad de Madrid. The surrounding area was historically part of the large Plan Parcial Ampliación Casa de Campo, whose approval and construction unfolded from the late 1970s and early 1980s onward. Municipal material now treats Avenida de Europa as a distinct zone and business area, with many offices, shops and hospitality venues. The European theme is visible in nearby streets such as Alemania, Bélgica, Dinamarca, Francia, Grecia, Inglaterra, Irlanda, Portugal, Rumanía, Suecia and Suiza, although the exact original reason for choosing the name Europa still needs an official naming record.
