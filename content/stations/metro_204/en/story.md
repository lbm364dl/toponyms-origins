Puerta del Sur is a modern descriptive name: literally, the Southern Gate. In transport terms, it names the place where Madrid Metro opens onto the southern metropolitan ring, linking Line 10 with Line 12, MetroSur.

The name does not appear to preserve an old rural toponym or honor a person. Its meaning is tied to the geography of the network: from central and northern Madrid, Line 10 reaches Alcorcón here, and from this station passengers enter the circular MetroSur system serving the large southern and south-western municipalities.

That context belongs to the major Metro expansion completed in 2003. MetroSur was conceived as a ring of more than 40 kilometres with 28 stations, connecting Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe and Leganés, while Line 10 was extended to meet it at Puerta del Sur. Contemporary technical accounts explicitly describe the station as the communication link between Madrid Metro and MetroSur.

The architecture reinforces that function. Puerta del Sur is a double station with the Line 10 and MetroSur platforms arranged on different levels and crossing each other; regional material describes it as a singular correspondence station within the MetroSur project. Its public art also plays with the southern theme: Jorge Bernabeu's work for the station is titled "Paisajes del Sur".

The broad origin is therefore secure, although the available sources did not include a formal naming resolution explaining the exact choice of words. The existing record should not say that the station was named after a Puerta del Sur commercial centre in Alcorcón: the evidence found points instead to the station's transport role, and later nearby institutions using the same name appear to post-date the Metro station.
