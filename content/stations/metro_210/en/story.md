Ronda de la Comunicación is not named after a historic figure, saint, battle, or old settlement. Its immediate source is the road called Ronda de la Comunicación, beside Telefónica's large headquarters campus in Las Tablas, in the district of Fuencarral-El Pardo. In plain terms, the station name is a transport stop taking the name of the street it serves.

The deeper layer is the corporate landscape around that road. Telefónica developed the campus as Distrito C, also understood as a communications district, and it later became known as Distrito Telefónica. The word "comunicación" is therefore not decorative: it points directly to the telecommunications company and to the idea of bringing the group's offices, workers, services, and networks into one campus.

The chronology belongs to the rapid northward expansion of Madrid's rail network in the 2000s. The Community of Madrid describes MetroNorte as the Line 10 extension linking Tres Olivos, Fuencarral, Las Tablas, Alcobendas, and San Sebastián de los Reyes, including Ronda de la Comunicación among its eleven new stations. Telefónica's 2006 annual reporting says that, by April 2007, the new underground station had opened and that the company had invested 14 million euros in it.

The station is also a small clue to the urban design of Distrito C. Architect Rafael de la Hoz's campus was planned around plazas, an atrium, landscaped areas, water, glass facades, and a strong pedestrian structure. Architectural descriptions of the project stress that the public metro station helped make the campus functional and more open to the surrounding city rather than merely an isolated office enclosure.

The evidence is strong for the basic origin: the Metro name follows the road name, and the road is inseparable from Telefónica's communications-themed campus. There is, however, a useful caution about details. Sources vary between 23 and 26 April 2007 for the opening/public opening date, and one Telefónica source used the variant "Ronda de Comunicaciones"; I found no evidence that this was an official former station name.
