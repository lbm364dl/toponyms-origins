Pozuelo is a place-name station: it takes its name from the municipality of Pozuelo de Alarcón. The older element, Pozuelo, is best explained as the Castilian diminutive of pozo, 'well', probably referring to the local presence of wells, springs, or water points. The town appears in a 1208 boundary document in the form Pozolos, and for centuries was known as Pozuelo de Aravaca because it belonged to the sexmo of Aravaca within Madrid's territory. In 1632 Gabriel de Ocaña y Alarcón acquired the former royal jurisdiction, and the place became Pozuelo de Alarcón. The present Cercanías station also reflects the railway history of the municipality: the Madrid-El Escorial section opened to the public on 9 August 1861, with Pozuelo as the first station after Madrid.
