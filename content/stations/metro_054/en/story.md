Vista Alegre station carries a neighbourhood name, but the older source of that name is the Finca or Quinta de Vista Alegre in Carabanchel. The station opened on Line 5 in 1968, in a district whose modern streets grew around a much older landscape of country houses, gardens and leisure estates.

The strongest documented origin is not a literal station-dedication act but the heritage history of the estate. According to the Community of Madrid and the 2018 BIC declaration, Pablo Cabrero Martínez and Josefa Martínez Artó acquired the property in 1823, turned it into a public pleasure estate and named it Vista Alegre. It opened in 1824 with gardens, baths, a casino and spaces for recreation.

The royal chapter began in 1832. The BIC decree states that the property was sold to King Fernando VII, who gave it to Queen María Cristina de Borbón. María Cristina enlarged the estate with many neighbouring parcels, enclosed it and developed it as the Real Posesión de Vista Alegre, with romantic landscaped gardens, palatial buildings and a navigable ría.

That setting explains why the name endured beyond the estate itself. Official heritage texts describe the Carabancheles as a favoured area for Madrid's nobility and upper bourgeoisie because of their closeness to the city, healthier climate and open views. Vista Alegre was later associated with Isabel II, the infanta Luisa Fernanda and José de Salamanca, and in the late nineteenth century it passed into public and charitable uses.

The current dataset claim is broadly right, but it compresses the chronology. Vista Alegre was already the name of the public recreational quinta before María Cristina's ownership, so the safest wording is that the station is named after the local area whose name ultimately comes from the Finca de Vista Alegre. The explanation that the name specifically refers to views over the Manzanares valley is plausible but less directly documented than the more general official reference to open, attractive views of the Madrid area.
