Iglesia is one of the original stations of Madrid Metro Line 1, officially inaugurated on 17 October 1919 and opened to the public on 31 October. Its name comes from the Church of Santa Teresa y Santa Isabel, a nineteenth-century parish built for the growing Chamberí area between 1842 and 1856. Metro de Madrid’s historical material shows that the station was designed under the project name Martínez Campos, after the surrounding glorieta and nearby paseo, but from its opening it was known as Iglesia. During the Civil War it was renamed Sorolla, then recovered the name Iglesia after the war. The current station name therefore preserves an older everyday landmark even though the square is now officially Glorieta del Pintor Sorolla.
