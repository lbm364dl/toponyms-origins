The Metro station Iglesia is named for the church that stands above it: the Parish Church of Santa Teresa y Santa Isabel, at today’s Glorieta del Pintor Sorolla in Chamberí. Metro de Madrid’s historical station sheet states that the stop was designed as Martínez Campos, but that from its 1919 inauguration it was known as Iglesia because of this church.

The church belongs to the nineteenth-century history of Chamberí, when the area was shifting from outer suburb to urban neighbourhood. Architectural records from the Fundación Arquitectura COAM give the original building dates as 1842 to 1856, and the parish itself links its foundation to local residents, workshops, factories, donations and the patronage associated with Queen Isabel II.

The first Metro line was inaugurated by Alfonso XIII on 17 October 1919 between Puerta del Sol and Cuatro Caminos, with Iglesia as one of the original intermediate stops. The Comunidad de Madrid still lists the early stop as “Martínez Campos (Glorieta de Iglesia),” a useful reminder that the railway inherited both formal street geography and popular place names.

The name also records a small piece of urban memory. The square is now officially Glorieta del Pintor Sorolla, and the station had a Civil War period in which it was renamed Sorolla. After the war, Metro restored the name Iglesia, so the transport name continued to point to the parish even as the surrounding square’s official name changed.

The etymology is therefore secure, but the chronology is more layered than a simple “named after a church” note suggests. Martínez Campos was the project or early formal reference, Iglesia was the name used from the station’s opening, Sorolla was the wartime replacement, and Iglesia returned after 1939.
