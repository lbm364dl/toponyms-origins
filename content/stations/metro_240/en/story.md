The name **Casa del Reloj** is local and literal. The Metro station is on Avenida de Gibraltar in Leganés, beside the municipal administrative building known as the Casa del Reloj. The Community of Madrid's MetroSur documentation gives the clearest etymology: the station is located next to the Town Hall building that gives the station its name.

The building behind the name is not an old village house or a medieval landmark. It is a late-twentieth-century municipal building used by the Ayuntamiento de Leganés for administrative services, citizen attention, tax information, registry functions, and related public services. Its name, “Clock House,” is a descriptive place-name tied to the visible clock feature by which the building is recognized.

The chronology is useful because it shows that the place-name predates the Metro station but is still modern. A municipal-archive description published in the municipal-archives conference proceedings states that the new administrative building, Casa del Reloj, was inaugurated in 1994 and that the Leganés municipal archive was installed in its basement. A 1999 BOE notice already refers to the “edificio Casa del Reloj” at Avenida de Gibraltar 2, confirming the name was in official administrative use before MetroSur.

MetroSur then turned the building name into a transport name. Line 12 opened as a circular metro line serving the southern metropolitan municipalities, including Leganés, and the Casa del Reloj station opened with the line on 11 April 2003. The station accesses and the CRTM area map place it directly at Avenida de Gibraltar, next to municipal and civic destinations such as La Cubierta de Leganés and nearby public services.

There is no strong evidence for an older, competing etymology. The best-supported explanation is straightforward: the station was named for the adjacent municipal building, and the building's own name is a descriptive reference to its clock. A minor caution is that some secondary sources describe the station as being next to a “Casa del Reloj” housing development, but the official Madrid source identifies the naming feature as the Ayuntamiento building.
