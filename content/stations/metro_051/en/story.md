Francos Rodríguez station is named for Calle de Francos Rodríguez, the street axis near which the Line 7 station was built. The name commemorates José Francos Rodríguez, a Madrid-born public figure whose career crossed medicine, journalism, literature and politics.

Francos Rodríguez was born in Madrid on 5 April 1862. He studied medicine and was connected with Carlos María Cortezo, but his public reputation came mainly through journalism and writing. The Real Academia Española notes that he wrote for many newspapers and directed La Justicia, El Globo and Heraldo de Madrid, one of the important Madrid dailies of the early twentieth century.

His political career also made him a natural name for Madrid's urban memory. The Real Academia de la Historia identifies him as mayor of Madrid from 1910 until March 1912, governor civil of Barcelona in 1913, minister of Public Instruction and Fine Arts in 1917, and minister of Grace and Justice in 1921. He also served in parliament for Puerto Rico, Almansa and Alicante, and later became a senator for life.

The station itself belongs to a much later transport story. The Community of Madrid records that the Canal-Valdezarza extension of Line 7 entered service on 12 February 1999 and created three new stations: Islas Filipinas, Francos Rodríguez and Valdezarza, plus the interchange at Guzmán el Bueno. At Francos Rodríguez, the accesses were placed on Avenida de Pablo Iglesias, Calle de Alejandro Rodríguez and Calle de Moguer, tying the station to the surrounding Bellas Vistas, Tetuán and Moncloa-Aravaca urban fabric.

The person behind the name is well supported, but a few details in popular summaries are easy to blur. The Madrid Press Association presents Francos as succeeding Miguel Moya as APM president in 1920 and says the Palacio de la Prensa was formally inaugurated in April 1930, although other accounts refer to installation or opening activity around 1928. His exact death date also appears inconsistently in authoritative-looking sources: RAH gives 11 July 1931, RAE gives 11 December 1931, while press-based accounts and later studies give 13 July 1931. These discrepancies do not affect the station-name origin, which is securely personal and commemorative.
