The name **Mirasierra-Paco de Lucía** joins two layers of local identity. **Mirasierra** identifies the Madrid neighbourhood where the station sits, while **Paco de Lucía** comes from the adjacent Line 9 Metro station, named in honor of the flamenco guitarist Francisco Sánchez Gómez.

Mirasierra began as a residential colony in northern Madrid. Madrid Film Office, using information from Fundación Arquitectura COAM, traces the origin of Colonia Mirasierra to a 1927 Sociedad de Casas Baratas project for a satellite garden settlement, later reshaped by the 1954 housing plan and by growth from the 1960s onward. The name is a transparent Spanish compound: mirar or mira plus sierra, commonly understood as a reference to views toward the mountain range north of Madrid.

The Paco de Lucía part is more precisely documented. On 27 February 2014, two days after the guitarist's death in Playa del Carmen, the Community of Madrid announced that the new Line 9 station being built by Calle Costa Brava in Mirasierra would bear his name. The official reason given in the announcement was both artistic and local: he had carried Spanish culture beyond Spain and had a family home in Mirasierra.

The transport chronology explains why the Cercanías name is compound. Metro opened Paco de Lucía on 25 March 2015 as the northern extension of Line 9 from Mirasierra. The railway station above it was inaugurated on 5 February 2018, designed as an intermodal link between Cercanías and Metro for Mirasierra, Montecarmelo and nearby northern neighbourhoods.

The earlier planning name **Costa Brava** is well attested in press and transport reporting because the station is located by Calle Costa Brava. For public etymology, the safest wording is that the station is named for both the Mirasierra place-name and Paco de Lucía; the tribute to the guitarist is verified by contemporary sources, while the exact origin of the word Mirasierra is linguistically clear but less directly tied to a single official naming act.
