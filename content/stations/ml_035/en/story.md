The station name comes from its immediate setting: Avenida del Siglo XXI in Boadilla del Monte. Metro Ligero Oeste identifies Siglo XXI as an ML3 station located on the avenue of the same name, while the Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists the station access at Avenida del Siglo XXI, 1.

Behind the stop name is not a person or an old rural place-name, but a modern urban label. 'Siglo XXI' means '21st century', and in Boadilla it names both a major avenue and the surrounding Siglo XXI sector, a newer residential area west of the historic town.

The chronology matters. The regional project description for the Colonia Jardín-Boadilla light rail line says the route ended in the new developments of Avenida Siglo XXI, beyond the M-50, next to Boadilla's transport interchange. Metro Ligero Oeste records 27 July 2007 as the inauguration date of its light-rail system, and the ML3 line ran from Colonia Jardín to Puerta de Boadilla, passing through Siglo XXI.

The name is also a small clue to Boadilla's recent urban geography. Older stops on this part of ML3 refer to the town centre, the fairground or nearby roads; Siglo XXI belongs to the later expansion axis where the light rail, residential blocks, local services and new streets were planned together.

The evidence is strong for the direct naming source: Avenida del Siglo XXI and the Siglo XXI sector. What remains less certain is the intention behind the street or sector name itself. It is reasonable to read it as a modern, future-facing name, but no municipal naming resolution or planning document inspected explicitly states that symbolism.
