The station name comes directly from Retamares, the local area on the M-511 in Pozuelo de Alarcón. Metro Ligero Oeste describes the stop as the Retamares station on ML3, next to the M-511 and closest to the General Cavalcanti barracks. The underlying toponym is probably botanical: the RAE defines retamar as a site populated by retamas, and retama itself is a shrub name of Hispano-Arabic origin. The nearby Base de Retamares later made the name nationally visible, especially after NATO left in 2013 and the EMAD concentrated major operational commands there from 2014.
