Retamares station is named for the Retamares area of Pozuelo de Alarcón. The transport evidence is direct: Metro Ligero Oeste places the ML3 stop next to the M-511 road and identifies it as the closest station to the General Cavalcanti barracks, while the Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists Retamares as a surface light-rail station on the Boadilla-Carabanchel road.

Behind that modern stop name is an older landscape word. The Spanish retamar means a site populated by retamas, and retama is the name of several broom-like shrubs common in Spain. The RAE gives retama an Arabic path through Hispano-Arabic ratáma and classical Arabic ratamah, so the place-name is best read as a botanical toponym: 'the retama grounds' or 'the broom-shrub area'.

The local geography supports that reading. Pozuelo de Alarcón's own description of its physical setting names the Arroyo de Retamares as one of the streams feeding the Arroyo Meaques in the south of the municipality. That shows Retamares was not merely a station label or a barracks nickname: it belongs to the local landscape vocabulary of the south-western edge of Pozuelo.

The name became much more visible because of the military complex beside the stop. The Base de Retamares was NATO's Allied Force Command Madrid headquarters from 1 September 1999 until 30 June 2013, according to the Ministry of Defence's Revista Española de Defensa. In 2014 the Estado Mayor de la Defensa took over the facilities and began moving major commands and units there, including the Mando de Operaciones, CIFAS, cyber and special-operations structures.

The current data row is therefore strong in its main direction but should be phrased more carefully. There is no need to make the suffix '-es' do the etymological work: retamar already carries the collective sense 'place of retamas', and Retamares is best treated as a pluralized local place-name. No formal naming resolution for the station was found, so the station-name link is probable rather than documented by a naming act.
