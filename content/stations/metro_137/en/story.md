San Blas station takes its visible name from the San Blas area of eastern Madrid. It is a Line 7 stop serving the district now called San Blas-Canillejas, and the transport name follows the local urban name rather than a street, building, or saint's shrine at the station itself.

The deeper origin is less straightforward. Local histories of the district say that the new neighbourhood was called San Blas as a tribute to Blas Pérez González, a jurist and Franco-era politician who served as Minister of Gobernación from 1942 to 1957. In that explanation, the religious-looking 'San' is not the primary origin but a way of turning the minister's given name, Blas, into a place name.

The chronology fits the development of the area. Canillejas was annexed to Madrid by decree in 1949, and the fields east of the city were transformed during the post-war housing emergency. The Gran San Blas operation began in the late 1950s under the Obra Sindical del Hogar and the official housing apparatus, creating one of Madrid's major working-class housing districts.

That urban history matters because the station arrived later, after the neighbourhood name was already established. The first section of Line 7 connected Las Musas and Pueblo Nuevo in 1974, bringing the metro into this eastern working-class district after years of weak transport links. The station name therefore preserves a mid-20th-century planning name rather than an older rural toponym.

The ministerial tribute should be presented with care. The best sources found for it are local secondary accounts that say the neighbourhood 'is said' to have received the name in honour of Blas Pérez, while more authoritative architectural and municipal sources confirm the creation of Gran San Blas but do not document the naming decision. For display, the station can confidently be described as named for the San Blas neighbourhood; the Blas Pérez González origin is probable, but still needs a primary municipal, housing-ministry, or Obra Sindical source for full verification.
