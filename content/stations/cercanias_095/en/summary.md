The transport name is straightforward: Zarzalejo station takes its name from the Madrid municipality of Zarzalejo. The older place-name is a phytonym, a name drawn from vegetation, and local historical writing says researchers agree it comes from zarza, 'bramble'. The DLE defines zarzal as a place populated by zarzas, and Toponomasticon Hispaniae uses Zarzalejo as an example of the common Spanish toponymic pattern formed with collective and diminutive suffixes. Some eighteenth-century documents reportedly used the form El Zarzalejo. A local legend about an apparition of the Virgin on a bramble survives, but it is a devotional explanation rather than the strongest etymology.
