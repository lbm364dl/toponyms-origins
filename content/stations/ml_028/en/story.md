Montepríncipe station is named for the place immediately around it: Avenida de Montepríncipe and the Montepríncipe residential development. Metro Ligero Oeste describes the stop as an ML3 underground or trench station at the southern end of the development, close to the CEU San Pablo campus and the UPM computer-science school.

Behind that modern suburban name is the older Monte de Boadilla, the wooded estate that shaped this part of the Madrid metropolitan fringe. Local history from the Montepríncipe community traces the estate from the marquises of Mirabal to the Infante Luis Antonio de Borbón, who bought the Señorío de Boadilla in 1761 and built the nearby palace between 1763 and 1765.

The estate later passed through the family of María Teresa de Borbón y Vallabriga, Manuel Godoy and their descendants. Carlota Luisa de Godoy y Borbón married Camilo Rúspoli, a prince of Cerveteri, and the Rúspoli family remained central to the later ownership history of Boadilla. According to the community history, Carlos Rúspoli y Caro sold part of the Monte de Boadilla in the 1960s for the Monte de las Encinas and Montepríncipe developments.

That makes the station name a compact record of landscape and aristocratic property history. The first element, monte, points naturally to the Monte de Boadilla woodland; the second, príncipe, is most plausibly connected with the princely titles in the Rúspoli line and the older royal-aristocratic history of the estate. The station itself opened with Metro Ligero Oeste in 2007, when ML3 linked Colonia Jardín with Puerta de Boadilla.

The direct transport-name evidence is solid: the station is named after the nearby Montepríncipe place-name. The deeper etymology is probable rather than fully proven, because the available online sources explain the estate history and the 1960s development but do not show an original naming document for the urbanization. One correction is important: although Montepríncipe is strongly associated with Boadilla del Monte, MLO places the station itself in Alcorcón, close to the Boadilla boundary.
