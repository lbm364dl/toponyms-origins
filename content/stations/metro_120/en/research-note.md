The previous wording's etymology is reliable, but its supporting evidence should be upgraded from Wikipedia to official municipal, heritage and transport documentation. The main nuance is that 'Casa de Campo' has two layers: the station is named for the adjacent park, and the park name comes from an actual sixteenth-century country house or villa of the Vargas family later incorporated into a royal estate. The station's transport chronology is modern: it was built in 2002 as part of the line 10/MetroSur reconfiguration and line 5 extension, not as part of the original 1961 Suburbano stations.
