The station name is direct and geographical: **Casa de Campo** refers to the Casa de Campo de Madrid, the large park and historic estate immediately beside the station. The stop serves one of the park's southern access areas, near Batán, the Zoo, the Parque de Atracciones and the Anillo Verde Ciclista.

The older name behind the station is not a generic modern phrase but a historic property. The Community of Madrid's heritage declaration explains that Francisco de Vargas, a royal councillor, built a villa or 'casa de campo' on family lands on the right bank of the Manzanares, near the mouth of the Meaques stream. That house, later known through the Vargas estate, gave the wider royal property its name.

Philip II began assembling the estate in the years around Madrid's rise as court city. The Vargas property, with its small palace and gardens, was incorporated in 1562, and the king commissioned Juan Bautista de Toledo to adapt the house and adjoining gardens as a royal residence. Over time the Casa de Campo became a royal hunting and recreation landscape, enlarged and reshaped by later monarchs.

Its public history changed abruptly in 1931. After the proclamation of the Second Republic, the Crown properties were seized by decree and the Casa de Campo and Campo del Moro were assigned to Madrid for public recreation and instruction; municipal materials date the first public opening to 1 May 1931. During the Civil War the park became part of the western front of Madrid, and official interpretive material notes that remains of wartime fortifications can still be found.

The metro station should not be treated as a 1961 station, even though the old Suburbano route through this area dates from that era. Casa de Campo station opened on 22 October 2002 as a new interchange, with line 5 extended to terminate there and line 10 continuing toward Colonia Jardín and later Puerta del Sur. The etymology is therefore secure, while some details in popular summaries need care: official municipal pages give the managed park area as 1,535.52 hectares, whereas the often repeated 1,722.6-hectare figure appears in broader descriptions that may include associated areas such as the Club de Campo.
