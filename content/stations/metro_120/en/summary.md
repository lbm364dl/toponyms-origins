Casa de Campo is a place-name station: it refers to the Casa de Campo de Madrid, the large historic park immediately beside the stop. The park's name ultimately comes from the Vargas family's rural residence, a sixteenth-century villa or country house on the right bank of the Manzanares. Philip II incorporated that estate into the royal domain in 1562 and had Juan Bautista de Toledo adapt the house and gardens as a royal residence and hunting landscape. The estate remained linked to the Crown until the Second Republic transferred it for public recreational use in 1931. The station itself is much newer: it opened on 22 October 2002 as a line 5 and line 10 interchange.
