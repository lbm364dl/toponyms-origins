Estrella Polar is a transport name taken from the street beside the stop: Calle Estrella Polar in Parla Este. On the tram map the stop appears as a paired north/south tram stop, Estrella Polar Norte and Estrella Polar Sur, between the Venus and Jaime I stops on the circular Tranvía de Parla route.

The street name belongs to Parla Este's deliberately astronomical street grid. Parla's municipal inventory lists Avenida de las Estrellas, Avenida de los Planetas, Avenida del Sistema Solar, Calle Estrella Polar, Calle Estrella Antares, Calle Estrella Sirio, Calle Estrella Vega, and several constellation and zodiac streets in the same Residencial Este area. In that setting, the station name is not a commemorative dedication to a person but a descriptive urban toponym derived from a modern theme.

The astronomical object behind the name is Polaris, the present North Star. NASA explains that Polaris lies close to the point where Earth's rotational axis points in the northern sky, the north celestial pole. For observers in the Northern Hemisphere it therefore stays in roughly the same part of the sky and marks the direction of geographic north.

That makes the name especially apt for a transit stop: the North Star is a traditional orientation marker, and this stop helps orient riders in the eastern expansion of Parla. The Parla tram itself began commercial operation in 2007 and was designed to connect the historic town, Parla Centro and Cercanías C-4, public facilities, and the new urban development of Parla Este.

The evidence is strongest for a street-derived station name within an official astronomy-themed street plan. A secondary source states directly that the station takes its name from Calle Estrella Polar; the municipal and transport sources independently confirm the street, the stop names, and the surrounding astronomical pattern. The broader claim that Parla Este is one of Spain's most coherent astronomical neighbourhoods should be treated as interpretive unless a planning document explicitly says so.
