The station name is direct and descriptive. **Polígono Industrial Ciudad de Parla** means the industrial estate called Ciudad de Parla, and the CRTM places the surface stop at Calle Berlín, 52, immediately serving that employment and service area.

In Spanish urban usage, a *polígono* is not only a geometric figure. The RAE also records it as a delimited urban unit for a specific purpose, with *polígono industrial* as a standard example. Here the station name is therefore not commemorative: it points passengers to a planned industrial district.

The industrial estate belongs to a wider story of Parla's transformation. The town had little more than a thousand residents at the beginning of the twentieth century and then experienced a major demographic expansion from the late 1960s onward. By the 2020s Parla had passed 130,000 inhabitants, making local jobs, local services and transport links increasingly important.

The tram chronology helps explain why this stop mattered. The section from Parque Parla Este to Polígono Industrial Ciudad de Parla opened for service on 8 September 2007, temporarily making this area one of the outer ends of the operating tram. The later section from Polígono Industrial Ciudad de Parla to Plaza de Toros opened on 21 May 2008, completing the circular route.

The broad origin of the name is secure, but some secondary details should be handled carefully. A 2011 report citing the Ministry of Fomento says SEPES had developed an industrial business park of about 30 hectares in Parla, while local commentary places the origin of the Ciudad de Parla estate around 1988. That means the station's immediate etymology is verified, but the precise development chronology of the estate needs a planning or SEPES file before being stated as certain.
