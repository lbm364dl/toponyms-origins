Valdebernardo station takes its name from the Valdebernardo area of Vicálvaro, not from a person directly chosen for the Metro. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists the station on Line 9 with accesses on Bulevar Indalecio Prieto, and Madrid’s municipal account of the 1998 extension places Valdebernardo among the stops opened when the Metro finally reached this part of the district.

The word itself is older than the station. “Val” is an apocopated form of “valle,” so Valdebernardo reads naturally as “Bernardo’s valley.” That makes the name a classic rural place-name: a landscape word plus a personal name, later inherited by a planned urban neighbourhood.

The name was already official in planning before the neighbourhood existed in its present form. In December 1989 the BOE published the definitive approval of PAU-4, explicitly called “Valdebernardo norte y sur,” and architectural sources describe Valdebernardo as the name of the land between Moratalaz and the old nucleus of Vicálvaro in the mid-1980s. The residential project was part of Madrid’s 1985 General Plan and became one of the notable eastern expansion operations of the 1990s.

Local historical association Vicus Albus also matters here. In 1990 and 1991 it prepared proposals on the urban toponymy of the Valdebernardo development, arguing that pre-existing rural names should guide the naming of the new streets. That context helps explain why the Metro name feels less like a commemorative label and more like the continuation of an older territorial name.

The uncertain part is the identity of Bernardo. Several secondary station pages say the name refers to Bernardo Gómez, from Barcelona, connected with the former ownership of the land, and they also mention that “Lucio de Mingo” was considered as a station name. I could not verify those details in an official Metro, municipal, cadastral, notarial, or archival source, so the safest public explanation is: Valdebernardo is a place-name meaning “Bernardo’s valley”; Bernardo Gómez remains an unconfirmed attribution.
