Barrio de la Concepción station is best understood as being named after the neighborhood it serves. That neighborhood name has older roots: local historical accounts place the mid-19th-century Colonia Agrícola La Purísima Concepción near the old road junction of the Camino de la Cuerda and the Carretera de Aragón. In the 20th century, the name was consolidated by the large private housing development promoted by José Banús between the late 1940s and the 1950s. The religious element is therefore real, but indirect: the metro station carries a neighborhood name that ultimately recalls the Catholic Marian title of the Immaculate Conception.
