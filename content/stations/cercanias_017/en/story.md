San Cristóbal Industrial is best understood as a practical railway name. The first half, San Cristóbal, points to the San Cristóbal de los Ángeles area of Villaverde; the second half, Industrial, distinguishes this stop as the one serving the industrial side of the district rather than the residential San Cristóbal de los Ángeles stop nearby.

Official transport data supports that reading. The CRTM gives the access as Calle Transversal Sexta, 32, Madrid, and Renfe's station file places San Cristóbal Industrial in the Polígono Industrial de Villaverde. The station is also served by line C-3 and by EMT route T41, a work-oriented bus link associated with the La Resina industrial estate.

The San Cristóbal name sits in a broader post-war urban story. San Cristóbal de los Ángeles was developed as a protected-housing neighbourhood in the Villaverde district, with municipal material describing it as a barrio born in the 1960s as a poblado dirigido. Architectural sources place the Poblado Dirigido de San Cristóbal de los Ángeles among Madrid's large state-directed housing operations, with thousands of subsidised homes built in phases from 1959.

The Industrial part is equally important. Villaverde became one of Madrid's major manufacturing landscapes in the mid twentieth century, with factories and estates tied to Boetticher y Navarro, Standard Eléctrica, Marconi, Barreiros and later automotive production. The former Boetticher factory, now La Nave, is a public reminder of that industrial past, and the Barreiros works in Villaverde became one of Spain's best-known motor-industry sites.

The main record correction is geographical: this station should not be described as named after a San Cristóbal industrial estate in Valdemoro. The evidence points to Madrid, specifically Villaverde, Colonia Marconi/La Resina and the San Cristóbal de los Ángeles area. A secondary caution is chronological: some secondary sources say the station was formerly called San Cristóbal and that San Cristóbal became San Cristóbal Industrial in the early 1990s, but the available sources did not include an authoritative naming resolution or opening notice.
