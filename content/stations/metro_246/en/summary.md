The station is named after Villa de Vallecas, the historic nucleus of the former municipality of Vallecas and today one of Madrid's districts. Vallecas was annexed to Madrid by decree in 1950, and in 1987 the old Vallecas district was divided into Puente de Vallecas and Villa de Vallecas. The name Vallecas is older: the Fuero de Madrid of 1202 records Balecas, though the reference appears to be to a carrascal or dehesa rather than necessarily to the later town. Several etymologies have been proposed, including Vallis Egas, the folk legend of an Arab named Kas, and Federico Corriente's Andalusi-Romance hypothesis ballaykas, 'valley areas'.
