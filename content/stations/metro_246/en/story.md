The Metro station is named for Villa de Vallecas, the historic town center and current Madrid district in which it stands. The Community of Madrid's documentation for the Line 1 extension places the station in the old urban fabric of Villa de Vallecas and records that the Miguel Hernández-Congosto extension entered service in March 1999.

Behind the district name is the older toponym Vallecas. Madrid City Council records that the former municipality of Vallecas was annexed to Madrid by decree of 10 November 1950, and that the 1987 municipal restructuring divided the earlier Vallecas district into Puente de Vallecas and Villa de Vallecas. The station therefore points to a place name with a municipal history before it became a Madrid district label.

The earliest commonly cited written form is Balecas in the Fuero de Madrid of 1202. Modern summaries often note that this medieval reference was not simply a modern-style settlement name: it referred to a carrascal or dehesa connected with the council's resources. That distinction matters because it separates the documentary age of the name from the later urban identity of the Villa.

The origin of Vallecas is disputed. One older explanation, associated with Juan Antonio Pellicer in 1803 and later repeated by other writers, derives it from Vallis Egas, 'valley of Egas'. A popular legend instead tells of a Muslim or Arab figure named Kas, giving a supposed Valle-Kas, but local historical writers explicitly treat that as folklore rather than defensible etymology.

The strongest modern linguistic hypothesis found in this review is Federico Corriente's proposal that Vallecas may derive from an Andalusi-Romance form ballaykas, roughly 'valley areas', built on Latin vallis with an Arabic or Andalusi suffix. That fits the medieval linguistic setting better than the folk story, but it remains a hypothesis rather than a fully proven origin. The nearby Cerro de la Gavia, with major Second Iron Age occupation from the 4th century BC to the 1st century AD, adds deep landscape history to the area, but it does not directly explain the station name.
