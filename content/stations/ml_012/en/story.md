The station name is local and literal: **Colonia de los Ángeles** serves the Colonia de los Ángeles of Pozuelo de Alarcón. CRTM places the stop on the Carretera de Carabanchel, the M-502, in Pozuelo; the name therefore points to the surrounding colony rather than to a person, a monument, or the Cerro de los Ángeles in Getafe.

The colony belongs to a distinctive chapter in Pozuelo's urban history. After the railway between Madrid and the northwest reached Pozuelo in 1861, the municipality became attractive to Madrid families looking for cleaner air, summer houses, and single-family dwellings. Contemporary local histories describe these houses as *hoteles*, the old Spanish term for detached villas, and the resulting settlements as *colonias de hotelitos*.

Pozuelo's municipal history gives the key chronology for this name. The Colonia de los Ángeles was founded and parcelled by its owner in 1926, and three years later the Sociedad Colonia de los Ángeles was created. It followed earlier colonies such as San José, founded in 1914, and developed in the same broader landscape of Húmera, Somosaguas, the Casa de Campo edge, and the old roads linking Pozuelo with Madrid.

The word *Ángeles* also has a living religious and neighbourhood association. The town council records that the local feasts in honour of Nuestra Señora de los Ángeles began in 1942, organized by roughly 35 colonist families, with Mass, procession, dances, games, and neighbourly celebrations. That tradition still ties the place-name to the parish and to the image of the Virgin, even though the colony name itself is older than the documented 1942 festivities.

The evidence is strong for the station's immediate origin: it was named for the nearby Colonia de los Ángeles, and Metro Ligero Oeste opened its light-rail network in July 2007. What remains less certain is the first reason why the 1926 promoter chose the name *Los Ángeles*. The current dataset should also remove the phrase that links the Pozuelo colony to the Cerro de los Ángeles, because official Getafe sources place that Cerro and its sanctuary in Getafe, on the opposite side of Madrid.
