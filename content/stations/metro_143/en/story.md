Artilleros is a transport name inherited from the street beside the station: Calle del Pico de los Artilleros. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists station accesses on that street, including the 'Pico de Artilleros' access, so the immediate naming link is local and topographical.

Behind the street name is a much older Moratalaz landscape. Before the apartment blocks, metro tunnels and the A-3 corridor, this part of eastern Madrid was crossed by arroyos, tracks, fields, brickworks and scattered estates. One of its nineteenth-century uses was military: an artillery firing and manoeuvre ground.

Ricardo Márquez Ruiz's local history of Moratalaz records that the artillery manoeuvre field was leased from the Conde de Polentino in November 1861. The same research notes that the road from Calle Granada in Pacífico had to be repaired in early 1886 because of the passage of troops and artillery, that the firing range was declared of public utility in 1907, and that the last manoeuvre found in the sources took place in June 1927.

This makes Artilleros a compact survival of a former military geography. The station opened with the first section of Metro line 9, between Sainz de Baranda and Pavones, on 31 January 1980, when Moratalaz was already a dense modern district. The metro name did not invent the memory; it carried an existing street name into the transport map.

The best-supported explanation is therefore not a dedication to individual artillerymen, nor to a regiment, but to the place-name created by the old artillery range. A useful correction is to use the full street form, Calle del Pico de los Artilleros, and to treat the 1861, 1907 and 1927 dates as evidence from local historical research rather than as Metro naming acts.
