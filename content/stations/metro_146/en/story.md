The Metro station **Antonio Machado** takes its name from the commemorative name already present in its surroundings: Calle de Antonio Machado, near the station accesses on Calle de Valderrodrigo. It is not an old landscape name or a former village name, but a public tribute to the poet Antonio Machado Ruiz.

Machado was born in Seville in 1875 and died in Collioure, France, on 22 February 1939. Reference sources describe him as a Spanish writer and poet, a late and highly representative member of the Generation of '98, with early work linked to Modernism and later poetry deeply marked by Castile, memory, time and Spain's moral landscape.

The station opened to the public on **29 March 1999**, when Line 7 was extended from Valdezarza toward Pitis. Contemporary and institutional accounts of the extension list Antonio Machado among the new stations serving the north-western edge of Madrid, especially Valdezarza, Peñagrande and nearby residential colonies.

The local setting strengthens the reading of the name. Madrid's municipal heritage catalogue places a 1985 monument to Antonio Machado in the area known as the **Ciudad de los Poetas**, facing Calle Antonio Machado. That monument, with a head by Pablo Serrano, shows that the poet's name was already a visible civic landmark before the Metro arrived.

The etymology is therefore secure, but the exact administrative act that chose the station name has not been located in the consulted online sources. The safest public wording is that the station bears the name of Antonio Machado through the nearby street and neighborhood commemorative landscape, and that it has used this name since its 1999 opening.
