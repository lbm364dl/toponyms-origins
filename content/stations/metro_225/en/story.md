Móstoles Central is named in the most direct possible way: it is the central transport station of Móstoles. The stop belongs to Metro de Madrid Line 12, MetroSur, and the municipality lists it as a station with interchange to Cercanías Line C-5. The word Central therefore distinguishes it from other Móstoles stations and points to its role as the main rail interchange for the city.

The older name behind the station is the municipality of Móstoles itself. That place-name is medieval and not fully resolved. One influential explanation connects it with Latin monasterium, or with a locative plural form *monisteriis, meaning something like 'at the monasteries'. If correct, the name would preserve a memory of a Christian monastery or basilica before Islamic rule.

The documentary record is clearer than the etymology. The Móstoles municipal history page and an academic article citing medieval Segovian cathedral documentation point to October 1144, when Alfonso VII's grant of Freguezedos located that place 'between the tower of Monsteles and the road from Madrid to Olmos'. A later 1208 boundary text also places Móstoles in the Segovian-Toledan frontier landscape. These forms, Monsteles and Mosteles, are important because they show the name in use centuries before the modern spelling.

The station also tells a transport story. MetroSur opened on 11 April 2003 as a circular line linking Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe and Leganés, with several interchanges to Cercanías. In Móstoles, the central stop made the older railway station part of the Metro network's southern ring and helped define the city's rail hub around Paseo de la Estación and nearby central streets.

The safest public explanation is therefore layered. The station is certainly named for Móstoles and its central interchange. The town name is probably connected with medieval Monsteles/Mosteles and may derive from a Latin monastery-related form, but the sources do not prove that conclusively. The Al-Idrisi form often cited as Mostel is better treated as a possible medieval attestation, not as proof of an Arabic origin; the popular 'must and olive oil' explanation is much weaker.
