The name **Islas Filipinas** points first to the nearby Madrid street landscape. The station, opened on Line 7 in 1999, lies under Calle de Cea Bermúdez near the intersection with today’s Avenida de Filipinas. Its wording keeps the fuller form, Islas Filipinas, which local street histories identify as the former or popular name associated with that avenue.

The Madrid street name has its own small urban history. According to the street-name tradition collected in María Isabel Gea’s *Diccionario Enciclopédico de Madrid*, the first opened stretch between Calle de Vallehermoso and Plaza de Juan Zorrilla was called Lozoya because it ran beside the Canal de Isabel II’s third reservoir, originally associated with the Lozoya River. When the rest of the road was opened and urbanized in the 1950s, it became Islas Filipinas; since 1977 it has officially been Avenida de Filipinas, although the older fuller name remained familiar enough for the Metro station.

Behind the Madrid name stands the Philippine archipelago. The usual historical explanation is that Ruy López de Villalobos’s 1542-1546 Pacific expedition applied the name Filipinas in 1543 in honor of Prince Felipe, the future Philip II of Spain. More careful sources note that the name was not necessarily applied to the entire present-day country at once: it first referred to islands encountered in the central Philippines and was later extended to the archipelago as Spanish rule consolidated.

The station area also contains a visible Filipino memorial. At the corner of Avenida de Filipinas and Calle Santander stands Madrid’s monument to José Rizal, donated by the Philippine government and installed in 1996 for the centenary of his execution. It is a replica of the Rizal monument in Manila and links this Chamberí corner not only to Spain’s colonial past, but also to Filipino national memory.

The etymology is therefore well supported, but the best wording is slightly more precise than simply “named after Avenida de Filipinas.” The station name refers to the Philippine Islands through a local toponym that had already existed in Madrid as Islas Filipinas and then as Avenida de Filipinas. No former name for the Metro station itself was found; the naming appears to date from its opening on 12 February 1999.
