The Metro station is named for the Philippine Islands, using the older or popular Madrid street form Islas Filipinas associated with today’s Avenida de Filipinas. Local street-name accounts say the road was developed in stages: an earlier stretch was called Lozoya because of its relation to the Canal de Isabel II, and the expanded street later took the name Islas Filipinas before becoming Avenida de Filipinas in 1977. The deeper origin is the Pacific archipelago: Spanish accounts link the name Filipinas to Ruy López de Villalobos’s 1543 expedition and to Prince Felipe, later Philip II. Nearby, the José Rizal monument on Avenida de Filipinas and Calle Santander adds a modern Filipino historical layer to the station’s surroundings.
