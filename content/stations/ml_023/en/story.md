The station name is direct and local: Ciudad de la Imagen is the audiovisual business park immediately around this part of Pozuelo de Alarcón. Metro Ligero Oeste describes the ML3 stop as a station beside calle Juan de Orduña, close to the ECAM film school and the Carrefour Ciudad de la Imagen centre.

The place name means 'City of the Image', but here 'image' is not a poetic reference to the landscape. It points to cinema, television, post-production, film education and audiovisual archives. Pozuelo's own municipal pages describe Ciudad de la Imagen as an office and leisure complex with an audiovisual theme, and the town history notes the concentration there of television, production companies and audiovisual training.

The project was already visible in the press in July 1990, when Comunidad de Madrid, Caja de Madrid and the Instituto de Crédito Oficial created the company to promote and manage what was officially called the Centro de Actividades Audiovisuales. Contemporary coverage described it as a Madrid 'cinecittà', planned on 859,000 square metres of public land in Pozuelo near Prado del Rey and the Casa de Campo, with production, commercial and support spaces.

That planned identity became unusually legible in the street grid and transport corridor. Nearby streets and stops carry cinema-related names, including Juan de Orduña, José Isbert and Ciudad del Cine. The station is therefore not named for an old rural toponym or a person, but for a late-20th-century urban and industrial project built around Madrid's audiovisual economy.

The evidence for the station's naming is strong, although some public summaries overstate one point. Filmoteca Española as an institution is not wholly 'based' at Ciudad de la Imagen; the facility there is its Centro de Conservación y Restauración de Fondos Fílmicos Carlos Saura, inaugurated in 2014 on parcels ceded in 1990. The station name itself appears to have been assigned with the opening of Metro Ligero Oeste on 27 July 2007.
