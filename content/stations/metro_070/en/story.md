Méndez Álvaro is not a landscape name or a neighborhood name in origin. The station is named for Calle de Méndez Álvaro, the major street running by the interchange; official transport sources place the station and its accesses on or next to that street. The street, in turn, honors Francisco Méndez Álvaro, one of the best-known Spanish medical public-health figures of the nineteenth century.

Francisco Méndez Álvaro was born in Pajares de Adaja, in Ávila, in 1806 and died in Madrid in 1883. Trained first under the complicated medical and surgical system of his time, he qualified in surgery and medicine and became known not only as a practitioner, but as a writer, translator and organiser of medical knowledge. His career connected medicine, journalism and politics at a moment when epidemics, urban mortality and public hygiene were central problems for Madrid and for Spain.

The strongest biographical sources describe him as a pioneer of Spanish hygienism and medical journalism. During the 1834 cholera epidemic his work in Brihuega earned him the Cruz de Epidemias; later he served in health administration, wrote on public hygiene, mortality, leprosy and vaccination, and participated in international sanitary conferences. He was associated with El Siglo Médico and other medical publications, helped build professional medical institutions, and presided over the Instituto Nacional de Vacunación under the Real Academia.

His public career also explains why Madrid remembered him in its street names. Méndez Álvaro was linked to the Partido Moderado, served as deputy, held senior posts in public instruction and sanitary administration, and is widely described as mayor of Madrid for about a month in 1843. The mayoral detail is repeated in medical-biographical sources, although one modern biography notes that the appointment is difficult to verify in some municipal chronicle material.

The transport name arrived later, through urban growth around Atocha and Arganzuela. Metro Line 6 opened the Pacífico-Oporto section, including Méndez Álvaro, on 7 May 1981; Cercanías and the Estación Sur de Autobuses later turned the site into a major interchange. The etymology is therefore well supported, but it should be stated as an indirect dedication: the station is named from the street, and the street commemorates Francisco Méndez Álvaro.
