Villaverde Alto station takes its name from the surrounding historic place, today officially called Villaverde Alto, Casco Histórico de Villaverde. It is not a commemorative name: it points to the old upper nucleus of Villaverde, the former municipality that gave its name to the present Madrid district.

Madrid City Council material describes Villaverde Alto as the oldest nucleus of Villaverde and, in its day, the origin of the municipality. When Villaverde was integrated into Madrid in 1954, this part became known as Villaverde Alto in contrast with Villaverde Bajo, the newer settlement on the other side of the old Andalucía road, beside the railway workshops.

The older word Villaverde looks transparent in modern Spanish, and the verde element is supported by Toponomasticon Hispaniae as part of the Latin viridis family, meaning green. Even so, Madrid toponymy is not quite that simple: one published account citing Luis Miguel Sánchez Molledo notes two explanations, either the greenery of the surroundings or a connection with the former Santiago el Verde near the Manzanares. The name appears in documentation from the fifteenth century, so the exact medieval motivation is not directly visible from the modern name alone.

The transport history reinforces the place-name rather than creating it. Villaverde Alto is an interchange with Cercanías lines C-4 and C-5, and Metro line 3 reached it in the major southern extension inaugurated on 21 April 2007. The railway helped pull industry toward Villaverde, and the district later became strongly associated with factories, including the Barreiros complex, but those industrial facts explain the area's twentieth-century identity rather than the station name's origin.

A useful way to read the name is therefore layered: the station is certainly named for Villaverde Alto; 'Alto' is best explained by its contrast with Villaverde Bajo after the 1954 annexation; and Villaverde itself is probably connected with verde/viridis, while an alternative Santiago el Verde tradition remains part of the local toponymic discussion. The administrative barrio was later renamed from San Andrés to Villaverde Alto, Casco Histórico de Villaverde by the Madrid City Council plenary agreement of 31 October 2017, confirming the historic place-name in official use.
