Colmenar Viejo station is named directly after the municipality of Colmenar Viejo, north of Madrid. Renfe's station data places it in that municipality and on the C4B Cercanías corridor, so the transport name is not a separate dedication: it simply identifies the railway stop serving the town.

The place-name itself is built from two very ordinary Spanish elements. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains Colmenar as a collective or locative word from colmena, meaning a place or concentration of beehives, while Viejo is the adjective 'old', ultimately from Latin vetulus. In plain English, the safest rendering is 'old apiary' or 'old place of beehives'.

A much-loved older explanation appears in the Relaciones Topográficas tradition. The answer given for Colmenar Viejo says that before the settlement grew, there was a beehive site and the house of an old man near the road from Alcalá to Segovia; travellers stayed there, more houses followed, and the place came to be called El Colmenar del Viejo. A recent local history volume notes that the questionnaire reached the town in December 1579 and that the answers were known on 6 January 1580.

That story matters because it is old and local, but it should not be repeated as the only proven etymology. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explicitly says the 'old man's apiary' account has the look of an anecdotal or popular explanation and that it is better, in principle, to understand viejo as an adjective applied directly to colmenar. The same source also notes that the word colmena has an uncertain deeper origin: Coromines suggested a Celtic *kolmena from kolmos 'straw', but with doubts, and a Latin or Romance explanation remains possible.

The documentary trail is older than the 16th-century tale. Toponomasticon Hispaniae cites a 1275 form, 'colmenar vieio', in a privilege of Alfonso X, followed by later medieval and early modern attestations such as Colmenar Viejo in 1485, 1495, 1515 and 1591. Some maps give shortened or variant forms, including Colmenar and Colmenarejo, but the recommended station display should keep the current municipal name and explain the origin as an 'old beehive place' with a traditional, not fully secure, old-beekeeper story.
