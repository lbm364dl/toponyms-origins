Parque Lisboa station is named for the surrounding Parque Lisboa, or Parque de Lisboa, area in Alcorcón. In transport use the official station name is shortened to **Parque Lisboa**, while the CRTM station page still identifies its ticket hall as **Parque de Lisboa**. The name therefore comes from a local neighborhood and place name, whose ultimate reference is Lisboa, the Portuguese name for Lisbon.

The older story behind the name is the urban growth of northern Alcorcón. Municipal planning documentation says Parque Lisboa began in 1969 between San José de Valderas and the old town center, in the same period as other large residential developments. An Alcorcón Municipal Archive note also shows the place name in use around the late 1960s: in 1968 a request was made to build the Club Deportivo Parque de Lisboa on land in the superblock of the San José de Valderas settlement.

That chronology matters because the name belongs to the city-building phase that changed Alcorcón after the 1950s and 1960s. The town had previously been associated with farming and pottery, but Madrid's metropolitan expansion pushed it toward a residential role. Parque Lisboa helped fill the space between the detached San José de Valderas development and the older urban core, later becoming one of the recognizable northern neighborhoods of Alcorcón.

The Lisbon reference is also visible in the nearby street landscape. The station sits by Avenida de Leganés and close to Avenida de Lisboa, and local streets include names such as Porto Cristo, Porto Colón, Porto Lagos and Cabo de San Vicente. This does not prove that the Metro name was chosen to celebrate Portugal directly; it shows that the station inherited a neighborhood identity already marked by Portuguese and Atlantic place names.

Metro arrived much later than the neighborhood. Line 12, MetroSur, opened its 28 stations on 11 April 2003 to connect Alcorcón, Leganés, Getafe, Fuenlabrada and Móstoles with each other and with the wider Madrid network. The best-supported reading is therefore straightforward: the station is named after the Parque Lisboa area of Alcorcón, ultimately after Lisbon, with the neighborhood's late-1960s development providing the historical context.
