Ventas station takes its name from the old eastern edge of Madrid known as Las Ventas, more fully Las Ventas del Espiritu Santo. The word venta here is not about selling tickets or goods in the modern everyday sense; it refers to a wayside inn or stopping place for travellers. The station opened on Line 2 in 1924 beside Calle de Alcala, at a point already identified with that historic place-name.

The older name belonged to the area around the Abroñigal stream, near today’s Puente de Ventas and M-30. Roads from Madrid toward Alcala de Henares, Aragon and Vicalvaro met in this zone, making it a natural stopping place for passengers, muleteers, carts and later day-trippers. Historical accounts identify the Venta del Espiritu Santo as an important establishment there from the seventeenth century, with other small inns, eating houses and leisure venues later clustering around it.

By the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Las Ventas del Espiritu Santo was a recognizable fringe landscape: part road junction, part popular recreation area, part rough urban edge. The shorter form Las Ventas survived as the neighbourhood name, while Espiritu Santo remained attached to nearby religious memory and later parish identity. This makes the metro name a transport label for a pre-existing toponym rather than a commemorative name invented by Metro.

The bullring is still essential to the modern identity of the station. The Comunidad de Madrid notes that the 1924 station even had an access planned for the exceptional crowds expected on bullfighting days because of its proximity to the Monumental. The Plaza de Toros de Las Ventas was designed in neo-Mudejar style by Jose Espeliu; after his death, Manuel Muñoz Monasterio completed the works, and the building was finished in 1931. Its first charity bullfight was held in 1931, while several official histories distinguish a later official opening in 1934 after problems with the surrounding urbanisation.

The best-supported origin is therefore the place-name Las Ventas del Espiritu Santo, shortened to Ventas. The bullring did not originate the name, because the metro station and the place-name predate its normal operation; instead, the bullring amplified and preserved the name. A remaining nuance is that accessible secondary sources cite the Archivo de Villa for the earliest documentation of the Venta del Espiritu Santo, but the underlying archival file should be inspected directly for a final documentary chain.
