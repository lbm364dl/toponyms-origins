The station name comes from El Batán, a Casa de Campo place name best explained by the former fulling mill beside the Meaques/Vadillo watercourse. In Spanish, a batán is a fulling mill: a machine whose wooden hammers beat woollen cloth to make it denser. Historical accounts of the Casa de Campo record a mill site that later became associated with the Venta del Batán. The modern Venta del Batán, opened in 1950 as bull pens for Madrid's San Isidro season, preserved and popularized the name near the station. Batán station opened in February 1961 on the former Suburbano line, later incorporated into Metro line 10.
