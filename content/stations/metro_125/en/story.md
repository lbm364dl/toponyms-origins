The name Herrera Oria refers to Ángel Herrera Oria, not to an older village or landscape feature. The Community of Madrid has explicitly described Ángel Herrera Oria as the figure who gives the Metro station its name, and in 2023 it installed biographical panels about him in the station. The surrounding street context reinforces the origin: one of the station accesses is beside Avenida del Cardenal Herrera Oria, the Madrid avenue that carries his ecclesiastical title and surname.

Ángel Herrera Oria was born in Santander on 19 December 1886 and died in Madrid on 28 July 1968. He trained as a jurist, entered the State Lawyers corps, and soon became one of the central lay Catholic organizers of early twentieth-century Spain. The Real Academia de la Historia identifies him as a journalist, lawyer, and cardinal, and links his public life to the Asociación Católica de Propagandistas, Acción Católica, and the diocese of Málaga.

His best-known lay work was in journalism and education. Herrera Oria directed El Debate from 1911 to 1933 and promoted the newspaper as a major Catholic daily. He also founded the Escuela de Periodismo de El Debate in 1926 and promoted educational and social institutions including CEU and the Instituto Social León XIII.

His life then took an unusual turn. In the 1930s he left much of his public lay activity for priestly formation in Switzerland and was ordained on 28 July 1940. In 1947 he was appointed Bishop of Málaga, where sources consistently stress his social and educational work, especially rural school-chapels and housing initiatives; in 1965 Pope Paul VI made him a cardinal.

The Metro station opened on 3 June 1983 as the northern terminus of an initially disconnected Line 9b section between Herrera Oria and Plaza de Castilla. It was integrated into the through Line 9 on 24 February 1986. No earlier station name has been found: the name appears to have been used from opening, while the older local road memory survives in the former popular name of the avenue, Carretera de la Playa.
