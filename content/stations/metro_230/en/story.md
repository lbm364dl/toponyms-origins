The station name is local and literal: **Parque Europa** refers to the nearby urban park in Fuenlabrada. The current CRTM station record places the Metro access on Calle de Francia, while regional MetroSur documentation locates the station under the axis of Calle Francia near its junction with Calle Holanda.

The park itself is usually called Parque Europa, although the form **Parque de Europa** also appears in Fuenlabrada municipal material and on transport mapping. That small variation is not a different origin; it is the same place name expressed with or without the preposition. The name points to Europe as a civic and urban theme rather than to a person, battle, saint or older rural toponym.

The surrounding neighbourhood helps explain why the name feels deliberate. CRTM's zonal map shows the park among streets named for European countries and places: Francia, Bélgica, Alemania, Holanda, Portugal, Italia, Suiza, Andorra, Luxemburgo, Austria, Checoslovaquia and Viena, together with Avenida de las Naciones. The station therefore belongs to a planned modern urban vocabulary in which the park and street grid share a continental naming pattern.

A local green-route description gives useful park details: it describes Parque Europa as a 17,454-square-metre green space facing the Fuente de las Escaleras, with landscaped island-like beds, lawn, children's play space and trees such as white poplars, elms and plane trees. The same source says the park was inaugurated on 4 May 1983 and explicitly notes that it gives its name to a Metro station. Because that source is not an official municipal park file, the date and size should be kept as well-supported secondary details rather than the foundation of the etymology.

The transport chronology is clearer. Parque Europa opened as part of MetroSur, the Line 12 ring connecting the major southern municipalities of the Madrid region. The most secure public-display wording is therefore that the station was named for the adjacent Parque Europa/Parque de Europa in Fuenlabrada, in an area whose street names reinforce the European theme.
