Guadalajara is a place-name station: it carries the name of the city and provincial capital served by the rail stop. Municipal tourism material says the early city was known as Madinat al-Faray and as Wad al-Hayara, an Arabic rendering of the pre-Roman name Arriaca. The name is traditionally explained as 'river of stones' or 'valley of stones', with the Henares landscape in the background. In 1085 Guadalajara passed into the kingdom of Castile under Alfonso VI, and later tradition associated that transfer with Alvar Fanez de Minaya. The present station name is therefore not commemorative of a person, but of a layered city toponym with pre-Roman, Arabic, medieval Castilian, and modern railway history.
