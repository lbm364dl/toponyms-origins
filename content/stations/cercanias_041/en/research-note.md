The previous wording's broad structure is usable, but it mixes three layers: the station name, the municipal toponym, and older urban names. For display, the cleanest wording is that the station is named after the city; the city name combines Arabic-derived Alcalá with the river-name Henares. Toponomasticon Hispaniae improves the previous wording substantially by replacing the uncertain Arabic explanation of Henares with a Romance/Latin-origin explanation from henar. A useful bibliographic source to consult physically or through a library is Leopoldo Torres Balbás, 'Complutum, Qal at Abd-al-Salan y Alcalá de Henares', Boletín de la Real Academia de la Historia, 149 (1959), pp. 155-188.
