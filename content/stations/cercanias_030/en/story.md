The station name is direct and practical: **Alcobendas-San Sebastián de los Reyes** names the two municipalities served by the Cercanías stop. Renfe’s own station material places it on Avenida de España and describes the two towns as a continuous urban area, historically separated along Avenida de España and Avenida de Madrid. That geography explains why the rail stop carries both names rather than choosing only one.

The older and harder half of the name is **Alcobendas**. Toponomasticon Hispaniae records the medieval form **Alcovendas** in 1208 and stresses that the place-name is unusually difficult: it is nearly unique, has few helpful old variants, and does not yield a secure Latin, Romance, Arabic, or Germanic explanation. Ramón Menéndez Pidal suggested a Celtic route involving words interpreted as roe deer or white, and another related possibility links the name to a Celtic *benda*, “hill,” giving a tentative sense such as “hill of the deer.” The important point for public use is that these are hypotheses, not a settled translation.

The second municipality has a clearer foundation story. San Sebastián de los Reyes was formed around an existing hermitage dedicated to Saint Sebastian, in territory connected to the Villa of Madrid rather than to the lordship of Alcobendas. Regional and municipal sources place its definitive legal origin in 1492, when the Catholic Monarchs protected or recognized the new settlement. Its name therefore combines the saint of the hermitage with “de los Reyes,” a reference to Isabel and Ferdinand, the Catholic Monarchs.

The pairing of the names also reflects a long shared history. Toponomasticon Hispaniae notes that the double tax burden and jurisdictional pressures affecting Alcobendas helped lead to the foundation of San Sebastián de los Reyes in 1492. Centuries later the two once-separate settlements became a conurbation, and the commuter rail branch opened to passengers in February 2001 with a station serving both. The modern transport name is therefore not an ancient formula, but a contemporary label for a shared urban and mobility space.

Confidence is high for the immediate origin of the station name: it is named after the two municipalities. Confidence is also high for the origin of San Sebastián de los Reyes as a saint-plus-Catholic-Monarchs name. Confidence is lower for the meaning of Alcobendas; the best evidence supports documenting the medieval form and the main scholarly hypotheses, while avoiding a definitive claim such as “hill,” “white hill,” or “hill of deer.”
