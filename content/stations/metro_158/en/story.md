Avenida de la Paz station takes its name from the street and roadway beside it: Avenida de la Paz, the eastern side of Madrid's M-30 ring road. The station sits by Avenida de Ramón y Cajal and the M-30, so the Metro name is primarily a locational name rather than a dedication to a person.

The older landscape behind the name is the Abroñigal valley. Before the motorway fixed this edge of the city in asphalt and concrete, the Abroñigal stream ran down Madrid's eastern side toward the Manzanares. The M-30's eastern corridor reused that natural depression, and later accounts describe the stream as canalised and buried under the road.

The chronology matters because the station did not create the name. Madrid's official callejero records Avenida de la Paz as a municipal street name from 26 November 1965, beginning at Avenida del Manzanares and ending at Avenida de Burgos. The M-30 was then built and opened in phases: Madrid Calle 30 places the first four sections of the third ring road on 11 November 1974, while later works completed major links on Avenida de la Paz during 1976 and 1977.

The Metro station arrived after that road name was already established. The Community of Madrid's Line 4 history says the Alfonso XIII-Esperanza extension opened on 5 January 1979 with three new stations; other Metro-derived references give the inauguration ceremony as 4 January and public service the following day. In either formulation, Avenida de la Paz entered the network as part of the northeastward expansion of Line 4.

The word 'peace' is not neutral in all Madrid street-name histories. A 2004 El País article, citing Luis Miguel Aparisi Laporta's municipal work *Toponimia madrileña. Proceso evolutivo*, states that Avenida de la Paz refers expressly to the peace after the Civil War. That interpretation is strong enough to keep the etymology as historical, but the public wording should note the source and avoid presenting the 1991 date as the official naming date.
