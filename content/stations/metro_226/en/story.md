Pradillo station is named after Plaza del Pradillo, the public square beside the station in the old centre of Móstoles. The Community of Madrid places the MetroSur stop in the historic town centre, under a park area between Agustina de Aragón, Dos de Mayo and Mártires, matching the area of the square rather than a personal dedication.

The older word behind the name is **prado**, defined by the Real Academia Española as wet or irrigated land where grass is grown for livestock, and also as a pleasant place used for walking in some towns. The RAE gives its origin as Latin **pratum**. **Pradillo** is the ordinary diminutive: literally, a small prado, but in urban place-names often a remembered open or green place.

The documented public history of the site points to water as well as open ground. Móstoles Town Council says that in 1851 a fountain was planned in the Pradillo, described as a spacious and central site in the town, and that the Fuente de los Peces was inaugurated in spring 1852. That municipal notice confirms the name Pradillo was already in use in the mid-19th century.

The square is also one of Móstoles' symbolic civic spaces. MonumentalNet describes Plaza del Pradillo as historically the most frequented place in the town, and the municipal catalogue records the monument to Andrés Torrejón in Plaza del Pradillo, inaugurated in 1908 for the centenary of the 1808 uprising. When MetroSur opened on 11 April 2003, the new station fixed that older local name onto the regional metro map.

The most specific older-name story is promising but not fully locked down from open primary sources. Wikipedia and the local-history thread it cites say the square appears in 16th-century documents as Plaza del Perezal, Pedreçal or Plaza de Abajo del Perezal, and by the first half of the 18th century as Pradillo del Caño. Because only secondary web traces of that claim were consulted, the recommended confidence is probable rather than fully verified for those early forms.
