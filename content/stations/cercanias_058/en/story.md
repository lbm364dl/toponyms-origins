The Cercanías station is named directly after Fuenlabrada, the municipality south-west of Madrid in which it stands. In transport terms the name is geographic and practical: Renfe identifies the stop with the city it serves on line C-5.

Behind that municipal name is a compact old place name: Fuenlabrada, understood as a contraction of Fuente Labrada. Toponomasticon Hispaniae explains it as a Castilian compound of fuente and labrada, where labrada keeps the older sense of something worked or carved, especially in stonework. In plain terms, the name points to a worked stone fountain.

The strongest historical evidence comes from the Relaciones Topográficas of Philip II. On 13 January 1576, local witnesses Pedro Montero and Juan Holgado answered that the village was called Fuenlabrada because near it there was an old fountain built of worked stone and mortar. They also reported the local belief that it had been made by Moors.

That fountain belongs to the settlement story of Fuenlabrada. The municipal history page links the origin of the town to people from nearby Loranca and Fregacedos, moving in the later Middle Ages toward what became the old centre of Fuenlabrada. It also identifies the remembered source as the Fuente de Fregacedos, in the area now known as Nuevo Versalles-Loranca.

The etymology is therefore secure, but one detail needs careful wording. The “Moorish” attribution is part of the 1576 local tradition, not a proven archaeological conclusion. The present railway station later inherited the municipal name; Renfe records that the station became part of the Cercanías network in 1989 as a C-5 terminus, and CRTM lists today’s interchange with Metro line 12 and buses.
