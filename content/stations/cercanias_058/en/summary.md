Fuenlabrada is a place-name station: Renfe’s stop takes the name of the Madrid municipality. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats the name as a clear Castilian compound of fuente and labrada, with labrada understood here as worked, hewn, or carved stone rather than simply cultivated land. The key historical testimony is the 1576 Relaciones Topográficas of Philip II, where local informants said the town was called Fuenlabrada because nearby there was an old fountain made of worked stone and mortar. The same passage says people thought the fountain had been made by Moors, but that part should be presented as local tradition rather than proven origin.
