Iglesia Centro means, quite literally, the church stop in the centre. The name points to the Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de la Asunción, the historic parish church that stands beside the tramway on Calle Real. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes lists the station access at Calle Real, 28, and municipal planning documentation describes the stop as lying in front of the plots between Calle Real and the church.

The church is also known locally as the old church. Parla's municipal heritage route dates it to the sixteenth century and notes that it was largely rebuilt in the twentieth century. Of the primitive building, the municipality highlights the Gothic-style chancel or head of the church as the surviving historic element, with walls of stone and brick in a Toledan masonry tradition.

The deeper history is richer than the station name suggests. A scholarly publication by Jose Gomez-Menor y Fuentes, issued by the Real Academia de Bellas Artes y Ciencias Historicas de Toledo, transcribes a contract dated 24 April 1522 in which the painter Juan de Borgoña undertook to make an altarpiece for the church of Parla. The contract was made in the name of the villa and church of Parla, with Payo Barroso de Ribera, lord of Parla, among the parties, and it specified an elaborate gilded and painted retable with an image of Our Lady of the Assumption.

The station also marks a modern transport layer added to the old north-south axis of Parla. The Tranvia de Parla runs along Calle Real through the historic centre, and the operator describes the network as a single surface circular line of 15 stops over 8.3 kilometres, in service since 2007. In this setting, Iglesia Centro functions less as a commemorative name than as a practical urban landmark: the church remains the orientation point for the stop.

The naming is therefore secure, but some surrounding historical details should be handled carefully. The municipality supports the sixteenth-century date, the Gothic head, and the major twentieth-century reconstruction; the 1522 Borgoña altarpiece is supported by a specialist archival publication. The 2020 replacement retable is documented by the workshop Artemartinez as a neo-Gothic work installed in May 2020, so it is safer to describe it as a modern historicising retable rather than as a strict replica of Borgoña's Renaissance altarpiece.
