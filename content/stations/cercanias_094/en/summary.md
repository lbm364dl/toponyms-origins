The station name comes from Las Zorreras, a local toponym in the Navalquejigo area of El Escorial. Official railway sources today use the fuller station name Las Zorreras-Navalquejigo, confirming the connection with that settlement. Municipal documentation also records a Camino de Las Zorreras or Los Tejones, and archaeological literature identifies an Iron Age site in the toponym known as Las Zorreras or Los Cierros de la Asunción. In Spanish, zorrera means a fox den, so the likely underlying sense is a place associated with foxes, but I did not find a primary source proving that the name was coined specifically because foxes were abundant there.
