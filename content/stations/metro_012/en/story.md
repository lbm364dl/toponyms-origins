The name **Ópera** points to the Teatro Real, the opera house that dominates the west side of central Madrid near Plaza de Isabel II, Plaza de Oriente and the Royal Palace. The station is not named after opera as an abstract art form so much as after this specific landmark and its role as Madrid's historic opera theatre.

Before the Teatro Real, this part of Madrid was associated with the Caños del Peral, a water-rich low area with fountains, laundries and later a theatre of the same name. The Comunidad de Madrid's archaeological account explains that public laundries used water from the Caños del Peral fountain, and that the eighteenth-century Coliseo de los Caños del Peral was built over the old washhouses. The present Teatro Real was then built on the site of that earlier theatre, not simply on an empty washhouse plot.

The Teatro Real was designed from 1818 by Antonio López Aguado and later continued by Custodio Teodoro Moreno. It officially opened on 19 November 1850, Queen Isabella II's birthday, with Donizetti's *La favorite*. Its opera identity was strong enough that, after Isabella II's exile in 1868, it was renamed Teatro Nacional de la Ópera for a time.

Metro history mirrors the politics of the square above it. The Line 2 station opened on 21 October 1925 as **Isabel II**, and the Ramal station opened later that year with the same name. During the Second Republic, the station was renamed **Ópera** on 24 June 1931; during the Civil War, it became **Fermín Galán** on 5 June 1937; and after the Francoist victory it returned to **Ópera** on 1 April 1939.

The current name is therefore well supported, but the fine detail matters. Saying that Ópera has been the station's name only 'from 1939' misses the first Ópera period from 1931 to 1937. The station also has a literal layer of older history inside it: the museum space in Ópera displays remains of the Fuente de los Caños del Peral, the Amaniel aqueduct and the Arenal sewer, recovered during accessibility works in the twenty-first century.
