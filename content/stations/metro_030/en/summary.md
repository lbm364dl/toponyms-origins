The transport station is named for the Glorieta de las Pirámides, a roundabout at the eastern approach to the Puente de Toledo. The name refers to two 14-metre obelisks built in 1831 and designed by Francisco Javier de Mariategui. Madrid's municipal heritage catalogue describes them as late-neoclassical stone markers created to embellish the monumental axis formed by the Puente de Toledo and the nearby Puerta de Toledo. Their name is slightly misleading: the monuments are obelisks, although each has a tall pyramidal needle. The roundabout is reported to have been officially renamed from Glorieta del Puente de Toledo to Glorieta de las Pirámides by municipal agreement on 28 December 1944, while the Metro station opened in 1968 and the Cercanías station in 1996.
