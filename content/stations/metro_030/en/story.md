Pirámides station is named after the Glorieta de las Pirámides, just downhill from the station toward the Manzanares and the Puente de Toledo. The “pyramids” are the two stone monuments in the roundabout: they are not pyramids in the strict architectural sense, but twin obelisks whose upper needles are pyramidal in form.

Madrid's municipal heritage catalogue identifies the monuments as the work of architect Francisco Javier de Mariategui and dates them to 1831. They were conceived as late-neoclassical markers in the monumental route joining the Puente de Toledo and the Puerta de Toledo, one of the formal southern approaches to Madrid in the final years of Ferdinand VII's reign.

The older place-name was tied to the bridge. Sources report that the roundabout was officially listed in the street directory in 1903 as Glorieta del Puente de Toledo and that it adopted the present name, Glorieta de las Pirámides, by municipal agreement on 28 December 1944. That means the monuments are much older than the official street name that made “Pirámides” the standard label for the area.

The obelisks have not always stood exactly as they do today. The municipal catalogue notes that the square changed repeatedly, that the obelisks were moved from their first position, and that other planned or installed elements disappeared, including royal statues, Doric columns, and side fountains. The current appearance comes mainly from the 1995-1996 remodelling, when the obelisks were restored and placed on a landscaped central island with a fountain.

For the station name, the evidence is straightforward: Metro's Pirámides station opened with Line 5 in 1968, and the Cercanías station entered service in 1996 as part of the Pasillo Verde railway works. Both adopted the established local name of the roundabout. The main caveat is interpretive rather than factual: “Pirámides” is a popular and official place-name for monuments that architectural sources describe more precisely as obelisks.
