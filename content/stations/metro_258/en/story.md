Las Suertes is one of the three Line 1 stations created for the new Ensanche de Vallecas extension, opened to passengers on 16 May 2007. The station name points to its immediate urban setting: Avenida de Las Suertes was officially assigned as a street name in the Ensanche by Madrid City Council in a bulletin published on 3 August 2006, less than a year before the Metro extension entered service.

The key to the name is the older Spanish land word suerte. The Real Academia Española records a rural meaning of suerte as a portion of arable land separated from others by its boundaries. In many Spanish landscapes, names like Las Suertes can therefore refer to divided fields, allotments, or parcels whose limits mattered for cultivation, ownership, taxation, or distribution.

For Vallecas, that meaning fits the historical geography, but it should be stated carefully. The Ensanche de Vallecas was planned as a large southeast urban expansion with around 26,000 homes, replacing and reorganizing land that had long been on the edge between village, fields, roads, and later metropolitan infrastructure. A neighborhood association also notes that names such as de las Suertes belonged to older Vallecas place-names, but no inspected archival document that traces this exact name to a specific repartition or cadastral parcel.

The transport chronology is better documented. The Community of Madrid describes the Congosto-Ensanche de Vallecas project as a 3.1 km extension serving a major new residential area; contemporary press reported the opening of La Gavia, Las Suertes, and Valdecarros on 16 May 2007. The official street-naming record shows Avenida de Las Suertes replacing the provisional street label 'Ensanche de Vallecas number 10.'

So the safest public explanation is that the station is named for the local avenue and toponym Las Suertes, probably rooted in old rural parcels. The lexical origin is solid, and the avenue and Metro dates are official or well documented. What remains probable rather than fully verified is the final historical link: an explicit document saying that this Vallecas toponym was created from agricultural suertes rather than merely preserving the word as an inherited local name.
