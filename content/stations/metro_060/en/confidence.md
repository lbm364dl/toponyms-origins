The direct station-name origin is verified because the station serves the Chamartín district and railway interchange, and official Madrid sources document the former municipality of Chamartín de la Rosa and its annexation. Confidence is only probable for the deeper linguistic etymology because the strongest consulted Toponomasticon entry is for Chamartín in Ávila and treats Madrid’s Chamartín as a related example, not as the primary documented medieval case. The vial records strengthen the Madrid place-name continuity while leaving the medieval Echamartín/Chamartín etymology to specialist sources.
