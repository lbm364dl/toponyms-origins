Chamartín station is named after the Madrid district where it stands, whose name preserves the former municipality of Chamartín de la Rosa. Madrid’s municipal records state that Chamartín de la Rosa was annexed to Madrid by a Ministry of the Interior decree dated 14 November 1947; municipal accounts place the effective incorporation in June 1948. The deeper etymology is best treated as probable rather than fully documented for the Madrid site: Emilio Nieto Ballester’s Toponomasticon entry for Chamartín in Ávila explains the type as a repopulation-era compound of the medieval personal name Echa, related to Basque aita, plus Martín, and explicitly lists Madrid’s Chamartín de la Rosa among related examples. Popular tavern stories such as 'Echa, Martín' are entertaining but much weaker as historical etymology. The official Callejero adds Madrid street-history evidence for the former municipality name, with Calle de Chamartín from 1859, Carretera de Chamartín in the 1930s-1940s, and Plaza Mayor de Chamartín de la Rosa after annexation.
