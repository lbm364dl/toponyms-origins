Chamartín station takes its name from the place around it: the modern district of Chamartín and, before that, the independent municipality of Chamartín de la Rosa. The Metro stop sits under the great railway complex in the north of Madrid, so its name is both a district name and an interchange name.

Behind the district name is an older village. Madrid’s own district history describes a rural core of Chamartín de la Rosa, while the municipal archive identifies Chamartín de la Rosa as a town council whose records run to the annexation period. A municipal history article places the origins of the settlement in the repopulation context of the Community of Villa y Tierra de Madrid between the eleventh and thirteenth centuries.

The most solid linguistic explanation treats Chamartín as a repopulation-era personal-name place-name. Emilio Nieto Ballester’s Toponomasticon entry for Chamartín in Ávila explains the form as Echa plus Martín: Echa is interpreted as a medieval personal name of Basque origin, related to aita, while Martín is the familiar Latin-derived personal name. That entry is not a direct medieval document for Madrid’s Chamartín, but it explicitly lists the Madrid locality once called Chamartín de la Rosa among related cases, so the same etymological family is plausible.

Chamartín de la Rosa changed scale dramatically in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Municipal material notes that much of the present district was still late to urbanize, except for the old rural core and later neighbourhoods, and that the former municipality also included areas now associated with Tetuán. It was annexed to Madrid by decree in 1947 and incorporated in 1948, after which the shorter name Chamartín remained as the district and station name.

The station name should therefore be explained first as a place-name, not as a direct dedication to a person named Martín. The folk story that customers shouted 'Echa, Martín' in a tavern is a classic popular etymology and is not as strong as the anthroponymic explanation. A further transport caveat matters: the nearby railway station’s current major building was constructed between 1970 and 1975 and was renamed Madrid-Chamartín-Clara Campoamor in 2020, while the Metro station itself keeps the shorter name Chamartín.
