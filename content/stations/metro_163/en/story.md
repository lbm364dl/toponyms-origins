Empalme is one of Madrid Metro's most literal station names. In Spanish, an empalme is a joining point, connection, or place where two things are linked, and the RAE dictionary also records its transport use for services that connect or combine. Here the word was not poetic: it described the railway function of the site.

The older line behind the name was the Madrid-Almorox railway, a narrow-gauge route whose first section opened in July 1891 between Madrid and Navalcarnero and was later extended toward Villa del Prado and Almorox. Its Madrid terminus was the Estación de Goya, near the former Quinta del Sordo area by the Puente de Segovia. The railway served agricultural and leisure traffic from the Guadarrama and Alberche areas, but its inconvenient city terminus and road competition helped lead to its decline.

When the Ferrocarril Suburbano de Carabanchel was planned and built, the route crossed the Madrid-Almorox railway near today's station. A source on Aluche's urban history says the Suburbano project added a stop for connection with the Almorox train, first referred to as Madrid-Empalme or Goya-Empalme, and finally shortened to Empalme. The Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid's historical Metro chronology likewise identifies Empalme as a Suburbano station with connection to the Madrid-Almorox railway.

The transport chronology matters because the present Line 5 label is later than the station itself. Empalme began as part of the Ferrocarril Suburbano, not as a Line 5 station; the CRTM chronology says it passed to Line 10 on 17 December 1981 and to Line 5 on 22 October 2002. That explains why a station on today's Line 5 carries a name inherited from an older railway interchange.

The origin is therefore well supported, but some fine details need caution. The name Empalme is secure; the exact early naming formula is less so, because a stronger available source gives Madrid-Empalme or Goya-Empalme rather than the previous wording's Empalme-Goya. There is also a date discrepancy: the CRTM historical chronology lists 4 February 1961 for Empalme, while another transport-history source lists 1 April 1961 and the Aluche excerpt says Empalme opened two months after the rest of the Suburbano.
