The station name is descriptive: **Hospital de Móstoles** means that this Line 12 stop serves the Hospital Universitario de Móstoles. The transport authority places the station in fare zone B2, with its access on Calle del Río Sella, and the hospital's own access information says MetroSur improved access to the hospital from the stop called "Hospital de Móstoles".

Behind the station name is one of the key public institutions of modern Móstoles. The Hospital Universitario de Móstoles is part of the public hospital network of the Servicio Madrileño de Salud and serves Móstoles and Arroyomolinos. Its current name also distinguishes it from other later hospitals in the municipality, especially after Móstoles grew into a large metropolitan city rather than a small town outside Madrid.

The hospital's official history says the building began as a private initiative and was later acquired by the Ministry of Health in response to long-standing local demands for a hospital. It was officially inaugurated on Saturday, 25 June 1983, by Health Minister Ernest Lluch, and the hospital describes it as the first county hospital in Madrid. Before opening, the project was redesigned by architects Fernando Flórez Plaza and Luis López Fando.

MetroSur arrived two decades later. Line 12 was placed in service on 11 April 2003 as a circular metro line linking Alcorcón, Móstoles, Fuenlabrada, Getafe and Leganés; within Móstoles, the Community of Madrid's MetroSur page lists Hospital de Móstoles as the fourth station and locates it on Calle Río Ebro, under an existing park. The station therefore turned a hospital named for the municipality into a transit landmark.

There is a later commemorative layer to the name. In 2020 the Móstoles municipal plenary approved a motion asking the Community of Madrid and CRTM to rename the hospital and the MetroSur stop in honour of Ernest Lluch, who had inaugurated the hospital and later became closely associated with Spain's 1986 General Health Law. That request does not appear to have changed the official name: transport and health sources still use **Hospital de Móstoles**.
