Infante Don Luis station takes its name from a very local royal memory: Luis Antonio Jaime de Borbón y Farnesio, known in Boadilla as Infante Don Luis. The station is on Metro Ligero Oeste's ML3 line in Boadilla del Monte, and the name connects the modern light-rail stop with the nearby avenue and with the palace that made the infante's name part of the town's identity.

Luis was born in Madrid on 25 July 1727, the youngest son of Felipe V and Isabel de Farnesio. He was drawn early into an ecclesiastical career, becoming associated with the archbishoprics of Toledo and Seville, but later left that path and built his own aristocratic and artistic household. Authoritative biographical references identify him as an infante of Spain, brother of Carlos III, Count of Chinchón, and an important patron of artists and musicians.

His link with Boadilla began on 20 February 1761, when he bought the Señorío de Boadilla del Monte from Josefa Micaela de Mirabal, Marquise of Mirabal. The purchase included earlier buildings, among them the old palace known as the Casona or Palacio de las Dos Torres. From that base, the infante promoted the construction or deep transformation of the residence now known as the Palacio del Infante Don Luis.

The palace is the key to the name's endurance. Regional and municipal sources connect the building with Ventura Rodríguez and date the main works to the 1760s, with the building effectively finished by 1765. It was not just a country house: it became a residence, a place for collections, hunting, music, and art, and later one of Boadilla del Monte's most recognizable historic monuments.

The station itself opened with Metro Ligero Oeste service on 27 July 2007, when the light rail began commercial operation between Colonia Jardín and Puerta de Boadilla. No separate naming decree was found, but the evidence is strong: the station's official transport name, the local avenue name, and the palace tradition all point to the same figure, Infante Don Luis de Borbón y Farnesio.
