The name **Ferial de Boadilla** is practical and descriptive. It identifies the ML3 stop by one of the nearby public facilities: Boadilla del Monte's municipal fairground, or **recinto ferial**. Metro Ligero Oeste describes the station as being on the Villaviciosa road and close to the town's fairground, Parque Juan Pablo II and the José Ortega y Gasset library.

The word **ferial** comes from **feria**, a Spanish word that can mean a public market, festive fair, fairground installations, or an exhibition space. The RAE traces *feria* to Latin *feria* and includes precisely the senses that matter here: the place or installations associated with a fair, and the festivities held around it. In this station name, the word is not ceremonial or metaphorical; it points to an actual municipal space.

Boadilla's fairground is documented in municipal regulation. The town's 2016 ordinance for the recinto ferial defines it as the physical space where fair activities take place during Boadilla del Monte's patron-saint festivities. The same ordinance places it on the explanada of the Villaviciosa de Odón road, next to the light-rail stop, and states that the fiesta of Nuestra Señora del Rosario is celebrated each October.

That makes the stop a small transport clue to local civic life. Outside fair time, it is a normal ML3 surface station, listed by the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid with access at Ctra. de Boadilla-Villaviciosa, 13A. During fiestas and municipal events, the name becomes more literal: the train stops by the ground used for attractions, stalls, the municipal marquee and other festive installations.

The **Boadilla** part of the name should be handled carefully. Current toponymic work does not support deriving the town's name from Beatriz de Bobadilla; the place name is older than that personal association. Toponomasticon Hispaniae treats Boadilla as part of a wider family of Boad-, Bovad- and Bobad- place names, with possible roots in a word related to *bóveda* or in a Latin-Romance *bovata*, meaning a communal cattle pasture or place of oxen. For this station, however, the secure etymology is simpler: it is the fairground stop of Boadilla del Monte.
