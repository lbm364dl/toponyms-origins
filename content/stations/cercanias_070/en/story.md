San Yago is named with a form that looks older than the modern Spanish Santiago. In plain terms, the name points to Saint James the Apostle: San is the saintly title, and Yago or Iago is one of the Iberian Romance forms of the name Jacob/James. The station's public name therefore preserves the pieces that are usually fused together in Santiago.

The linguistic background is unusually visible here. The Real Academia Galega explains Santiago as a contraction of Sant Iago, from Latin sanctus and Iacobus. Xacopedia, following the traditional explanation associated with historical grammar, also presents forms such as Yaco, Yagüe and Sant Yago on the path toward Santiago. That makes San Yago a good example of a religious name that also feels like a fossilized fragment of language history.

For the railway, the name belongs to a halt in Galapagar, reached from Avenida Saltos del Sil in Colonia España. ADIF places the halt at kilometre point 40.046 of the Madrid-Hendaye line, and CRTM lists it today under the name San Yago. The railway line through this corridor opened in 1861 between Madrid and El Escorial, but transport-history material notes that San Yago itself does not appear to have been one of the original stopping places.

The surrounding landscape also matters. The Community of Madrid's walking route from Galapagar to Las Zorreras and San Yago passes through Galapagar, Colonia España and Las Zorreras, linking the station with the suburban and rural edge of the Guadarrama foothills. This helps explain why the station name behaves like a place name: the halt appears to have taken over a local toponym or neighborhood reference, rather than inventing a new commemorative railway name.

The strongest conclusion is linguistic, not archival. San Yago very probably refers to Saint James through the older Sant Iago/San Yago family of forms, but I did not find a primary naming order, municipal street-name file or railway opening notice that states exactly when the halt received this name. The current dataset should therefore keep the religious etymology, but lower the confidence from fully verified to probable and note that the current official Cercanias service is C-3a, not the older C-8 label in the input row.
