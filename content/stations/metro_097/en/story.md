The name of Marqués de Vadillo station comes from the Glorieta del Marqués de Vadillo, where the Metro entrances open near the Carabanchel end of the Puente de Toledo. In practice, the station name points first to that urban place-name and then to the historical figure behind it: Francisco Antonio de Salcedo y Aguirre, first Marquess of Vadillo.

Salcedo was a senior royal administrator of the late Habsburg and early Bourbon period. A genealogical study in the Anales de la Real Academia Matritense de Heráldica y Genealogía identifies him as baptized in San Andrés de Soria on 17 October 1646 and dying in Madrid on 24 June 1729. He was appointed corregidor of Madrid by royal title on 8 October 1715 and remained in office until his death.

His Madrid reputation is inseparable from Pedro de Ribera. Municipal heritage pages describe the Puente de Toledo as a Baroque bridge commissioned in 1715 by the Marqués de Vadillo and carried out by Ribera after a long history of earlier crossings over the Manzanares damaged or destroyed by floods. One official monument page dates the main work to 1718-1727, while another Madrid Río page says works began in 1719 and concluded in 1732, a useful reminder that the bridge's construction and completion are described in more than one phase.

The bridge was not only practical infrastructure. It gave Madrid a monumental southern entrance toward the Toledo road, with nine semicircular arches, sculptural temple structures for San Isidro and Santa María de la Cabeza, ramps, fountains and an urban setting intended to dignify the approach to the city. The same Vadillo-Ribera partnership also appears in other early Bourbon works in Madrid, such as the Conde Duque barracks and the Ermita de la Virgen del Puerto.

The station opened on 5 June 1968 on Line 5, in the original Callao-Carabanchel section, according to transport-history sources; the current CRTM station page confirms the station's present name, Line 5 service and accesses on the Glorieta del Marqués de Vadillo. No earlier station name was found. The etymology is therefore secure, with two cautions: the station is named through the roundabout/place-name, and the person's birth year should be recorded as 1646 rather than c.1660.
