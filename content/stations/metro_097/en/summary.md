The Metro station is named from the Glorieta del Marqués de Vadillo, beside the western end of the Puente de Toledo. That roundabout commemorates Francisco Antonio de Salcedo y Aguirre, first Marquess of Vadillo, a royal administrator who became corregidor of Madrid in 1715 and held the office until his death in 1729. His name is especially attached to the rebuilding of the Puente de Toledo, commissioned under his authority and designed by Pedro de Ribera. Municipal heritage sources describe the bridge as one of Madrid's major Baroque works and document its complicated chronology after earlier bridges over the Manzanares were destroyed by floods. A useful correction to the record is that Salcedo was baptized in 1646, not around 1660.
