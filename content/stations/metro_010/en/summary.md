Chamberí was one of the original stations of Madrid's first metro line, opened on 17 October 1919 beneath Plaza de Chamberí. Its name comes from the district and plaza, not from a person directly commemorated by Metro. The older Madrid toponym is normally connected with Chambéry, the Savoyard city, but sources disagree about whether the name came through royal memory, troops returning from an Italian/Savoyard campaign, or later popular legend. The Napoleonic-camp version is weak because the name is documented before 1808, notably on the 1761 Chalmandrier map. The station closed on 22 May 1966 because its curved platforms could not be lengthened, and it reopened in 2008 as Metro's preserved museum station. The official historical Callejero places Plaza de Chamberí itself in the municipal street record from 1 January 1863, between the older map evidence and the 1919 Metro station.
