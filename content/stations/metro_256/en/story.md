The Metro station called **San Cristóbal** is named for the neighbourhood it serves: San Cristóbal de los Ángeles, in the southern Madrid district of Villaverde. When Line 3 was extended from Legazpi to Villaverde Alto, the Community of Madrid described this stop as the station placed opposite San Cristóbal de los Ángeles. The station opened on 21 April 2007, giving the barrio a direct Metro link to the city centre.

Behind the station name is a religious toponym. San Cristóbal is Saint Christopher, whose Greek-derived name is commonly explained as 'bearer of Christ'. In Christian tradition he is the giant figure who carries the Christ child across a dangerous river, which explains his long association with travellers, transport workers and drivers.

The place behind the name is much more recent than the saint. San Cristóbal de los Ángeles emerged in the late 1950s and 1960s as a large social-housing operation on Madrid’s southern edge. The Fundación Arquitectura COAM records the poblado dirigido as planned in 1958, with works running from 1959 to 1967 under a team of eight architects led by Eugenio Casar Estellés.

The neighbourhood was built on more than 43 hectares that had belonged to the La Norah brickworks. That industrial past is still visible in the surviving brick chimney, now treated as a testimonial landmark of the barrio. Official Madrid planning material also links the estate to the Gerencia de Poblados Dirigidos and the Instituto Nacional de la Vivienda, with 6,077 subsidised dwellings built in successive phases from 1959.

The best-supported reading is therefore layered: the Metro station is directly named after the barrio, and the barrio’s name contains the religious dedication to San Cristóbal. The current dataset is right in broad terms, but some numerical and chronological details should be tightened: authoritative sources give 6,077 subsidised dwellings overall, 4,066 dwellings for the core directed settlement, and a project/building chronology of 1958-1959 and 1959-1967 rather than simply 1958-1965. I did not find a primary naming act stating who chose the barrio name or why 'de los Ángeles' was added.
