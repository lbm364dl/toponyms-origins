San José de Valderas station takes its name from the neighbourhood in northern Alcorcón where it stands. Renfe identifies the stop as a C-5 Cercanías Madrid station opened on 29 October 1976 on the Aluche-Móstoles railway, and places it in the neighbourhood of the same name. The railway name therefore follows a local place-name already established around the station area.

The place-name behind the station is older than the railway. In 1913 José Sanchiz de Quesada, associated with the Valderas title through his wife Isabel Arróspide y Álvarez, acquired the Quinta de San José and many surrounding rural plots in Alcorcón. Municipal research describes that property as the place chosen for the family residence, an oratory-chapel, and service buildings; the ensemble became known as the Castillos de Valderas.

The San José part is best understood through that estate. A short municipal history says the former Valdecuervo estate later became known as San José, and that the small castle was used as an oratory and chapel under the dedication of Saint Joseph. A longer centenary volume adds a useful nuance: the current name may come above all from the 'Quinta de San José' of the Marquises of Valderas, while oral tradition also links it to the chapel dedicated to Saint Joseph.

The modern neighbourhood arrived with Alcorcón's rapid metropolitan growth. In 1957 María Jacinta Sanchiz de Arróspide sold the property to the developer Román Sanahuja; the first stone of the housing development was laid on 1 April 1958. The Castles, once aristocratic residence, school, chapel, sales office, and later municipal cultural buildings, remained the visible landmark that kept the Valderas name attached to the area.

The Valderas element does not describe Alcorcón's landscape directly. It comes through the noble title, which refers to Valderas in León. Historical-toponymic sources record medieval forms such as Valde Heras in 1113 and explain the name as probably meaning val de eras, 'valley of threshing floors'; a proposed Roman derivation is much weaker because it lacks documentary support. For the station, the secure chain is: station -> neighbourhood -> estate and Castles of the Marquises of Valderas, with Saint Joseph supplied by the Quinta and chapel tradition.
