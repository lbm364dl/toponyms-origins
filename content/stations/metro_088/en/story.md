Retiro station is named after the Parque del Buen Retiro, the large historic park immediately beside this part of Calle de Alcalá. Metro de Madrid places the station between Calle de Lagasca and Calle de Claudio Coello, opposite the park and close to Plaza de la Independencia. The name is therefore not abstract: it points to the green space that defines the station’s surroundings.

Behind the park name is an older royal and religious landscape. The monastery of San Jerónimo el Real, founded in the early sixteenth century, had royal quarters known as the Cuarto Real. Madrid’s Landscape of Light documentation explains that those quarters were created for royal retreat, and that the monastery and royal rooms became the nucleus from which the later Buen Retiro palace and royal site developed.

In the 1630s, during the reign of Felipe IV, the Count-Duke of Olivares promoted a new royal leisure complex around San Jerónimo. The Museo del Prado records the change from the older Cuarto Real de San Jerónimo to the Casa Real del Buen Retiro after the palace was completed in 1633. Madrid municipal heritage sources describe the park’s origin as a seventeenth-century space for the king and court, later enlarged with formal gardens, water features, avenues, groves and recreational buildings.

The Metro station belongs to a separate twentieth-century chapter. Its historical file records the opening date as 14 June 1924, on the first Line 2 section between Sol and Ventas. The same file preserves a useful naming detail: in early plans the stop was called Independencia-Velázquez, but during construction it was renamed Retiro. The station was also designed with an unused third track for a projected Salamanca district branch; that unused space later became the ExpoMetro exhibition area.

The present public park reflects many later layers. It was transferred to Madrid City Council after the 1868 revolution, was declared a historic-artistic garden in 1935, and includes nineteenth-century exhibition buildings such as the Palacio de Cristal, built in 1887 for the Philippine Islands flora exhibition. Since 2021, El Retiro has formed part of UNESCO’s World Heritage property Paseo del Prado and Buen Retiro, a landscape of Arts and Sciences. The station name is therefore well supported: it is a direct place-name from the adjacent park, whose own name preserves the memory of a royal retreat.
