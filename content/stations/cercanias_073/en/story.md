The station name is a compound service name. The railway station stands in the municipality of Santa María de la Alameda, in the settlement known as La Estación, and the second half, Peguerinos, points to the nearby municipality in Ávila that the station also serves. Adif's station page and Renfe's Cercanías map preserve the full form Santa María de la Alameda-Peguerinos.

The first place-name has an evident religious and landscape reading: Santa María refers to the Virgin Mary, and alameda means a place planted with poplars or a poplar-lined walk. The town's heritage material highlights the parish church of Nuestra Señora de la Alameda, a sixteenth-century building facing the town hall in the historic centre. That makes the traditional explanation plausible, but it should be presented carefully.

Local history is more cautious than the simple legend. The municipal history page says the foundation of Santa María de la Alameda is not known with certainty and may be linked to repopulation after the late eleventh-century Christian conquest of the area; it also says the name could have come from the surname of an early settler or owner. ADI Sierra Oeste adds another local toponymic clue, suggesting that the nearby deserted place El Alaminejo may have influenced the present name.

Peguerinos brings in a different mountain economy. The RAE defines peguero as a person who extracts or makes pitch, or deals in it, and peguera as a pit where pine wood is burned to obtain tar and pitch. In a pine country such as Peguerinos, that gives a strong linguistic basis for connecting the place-name with resin, pitch-making, and forest work, even though the available sources did not include an official municipal etymology page stating this directly.

For public display, the safest wording is that the station is named after two municipalities, not directly after a saint and an occupation. The deeper etymologies are unequal in certainty: alameda and peguero are secure dictionary words, the church dedication is well documented, and the Peguerinos explanation is linguistically strong; the exact historical reason why Santa María de la Alameda acquired its full municipal name remains partly uncertain.
