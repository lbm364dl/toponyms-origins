The current explanation's broad etymology is good, but the strongest source is more nuanced than the simplified 'Arabic article + Romance pedrete' formula. Toponomasticon Hispaniae says the local form is not documented as such in Arabic and explicitly leaves open, though less centrally, the possibility of a fully Arabic origin. For display, it is better to foreground the academically supported late-Latin/Romance stone-name with Arabic article and treat Ad Petrum / Al Pedrete as local explanatory traditions.
