Alpedrete station is named for the municipality of Alpedrete, in the Sierra de Guadarrama area of the Community of Madrid. The transport name is therefore a place name: Adif currently lists the railway station as Alpedrete, while the town council also refers to the station in recent works as Mataespesa or Mataespesa de Alpedrete, reflecting the local area around the railway.

The strongest etymological source is Toponomasticon Hispaniae. Its entry explains Alpedrete as an Arabized form, with the Arabic article al-, of late Latin *PĔTRĒTU, a word built from *PĔTRA, 'stone', with a collective or abundant suffix. In plain language, the name points to a 'place of stones', a 'stone field', or a 'quarry'.

That meaning fits the landscape and economy of the town. Alpedrete has long been known for granite quarrying, and the municipal website presents it as a town born from stone. Local material links its granite to major works in and around Madrid, including El Escorial, the Royal Palace, and other monuments, and a municipal tourism leaflet says that stone was so important to local life that it gave the town its name.

There is useful historical depth behind the station name. Toponomasticon Hispaniae cites documentary forms such as 'El Alpedrete' around 1539, 'Villalba y Alpedrete' in 1591, and later references in 1605, 1737, 1751, 1829, 1845, and 1861. The same source notes that Alpedrete was once a barrio of Collado Villalba and became separate in 1840; the municipal leaflet instead notes a villazgo in 1630 and independence from Collado Villalba in 1840.

There are local traditions that tell the story differently. The town council mentions one theory in which Romans called the place Ad Petrum and another in which Arabs named it Al Pedrete because of the abundance of quarries. Those traditions preserve the same central idea, stone, but the most reliable linguistic reading is the more technical one: an Arabized form of a late Latin/Romance stone-name. No exact date was found for when the railway station adopted the current public name Alpedrete, and Mataespesa remains relevant as an alternate or local station designation.
