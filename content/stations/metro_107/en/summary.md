The station name is topographical: it comes from Plaza de Castilla, not directly from a person. CRTM's historical panels describe the site before the plaza as the fork of the old road to France, today's Bravo Murillo, and the road to Chamartín. In the 1940s, the new northward urban axis from Nuevos Ministerios was called Avenida del Generalísimo and a large circular square at its junction with the old road was planned and already known as Plaza de Castilla. Metro arrived there on 4 February 1961 with the extension of Line 1 from Tetuán; Lines 10 and 9 were added in the 1980s. The deeper meaning of 'Castilla' is best stated cautiously as a reference to Castile, the historic Spanish region and former kingdom, rather than as a documented dedication specifically to the Crown of Castile.
