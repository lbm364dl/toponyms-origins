The name **Aeropuerto T1-T2-T3** is direct wayfinding. It tells passengers that this Line 8 station serves terminals 1, 2 and 3 of Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas Airport; Aena places the Metro stop at Terminal 2, floor 1, while Terminal 4 has its own Metro station.

The station did not begin with this full name. Metro chronology sources record that the stop opened on 14 June 1999 as **Aeropuerto**, on the extension from Campo de las Naciones to the airport. When Line 8 was later extended beyond Barajas to the new T4 station, the original airport stop was renamed **Aeropuerto T1-T2-T3** on 3 May 2007 to distinguish the older terminal complex from Terminal 4.

The airport behind the name is older than the Metro connection by nearly seven decades. Aena says the National Airport of Madrid opened to air traffic on 22 April 1931, on a barren site of about 500 fanegas beside the then municipality of Barajas, chosen partly for its road connection with Madrid. Commercial services began at the end of 1933, and later terminal development produced the present T2 from the 1950s national terminal and T1 from the international terminal begun in 1971.

The official airport name changed again in 2014. By Order FOM/480/2014 of 24 March 2014, published in the BOE on 26 March, Madrid-Barajas became **Aeropuerto Adolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas** in memory of Adolfo Suárez, the first president of Spain's democratic period and a central figure of the Transition. The Metro station name, however, remains terminal-based: it points to the airport area rather than to Suárez personally.

The older place-name **Barajas** should be treated cautiously. A common Arabic-style explanation is not strongly supported; a specialist article on the Madrid toponym rejects the legendary derivation from a supposed Moor called Bar Axa and argues instead for an older pre-Roman or Hispano-Celtic personal-name base. For this station, the secure etymology is therefore simple and operational: it is named after the airport terminals it serves.
