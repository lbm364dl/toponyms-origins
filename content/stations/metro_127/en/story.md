Ventilla station is named after La Ventilla, the traditional neighborhood name around today’s Almenara area in the district of Tetuán. The station sits on Avenida de Asturias, in a part of Madrid where the official administrative name and the everyday neighborhood name do not quite match: Almenara is the municipal barrio, but La Ventilla remains the local identity.

The usual explanation of the name is linguistic and roadside-historical. In Spanish, a venta was an inn or stopping place on a road, and the suffix -illa can carry a diminutive sense. Local neighborhood history says that, at the exit of the old Carretera de Francia, there was a venta known as Ventilla or La Ventilla, and that the surrounding settlement took its name from it.

Before it became part of Madrid, this land belonged to the old municipality of Chamartín de la Rosa. Municipal material confirms that Chamartín de la Rosa was annexed to Madrid in 1948 and that Tetuán became a separate district in 1955. The Ventilla-Almenara area grew from the early twentieth century with modest workers’ housing, small workshops and industries, forming a spontaneous urban edge north of older Tetuán.

The name also carries a strong social history. Sources on the neighborhood describe La Ventilla as an area of infravivienda, or severely substandard housing, later transformed by public housing programs, IVIMA intervention and the opening of Avenida de Asturias between Plaza de Castilla and the Barrio del Pilar/Sinesio Delgado axis. The Metro station, opened on 3 June 1983, arrived during the decades when the neighborhood was being physically remade.

The inn-origin is probable rather than fully proven: it is clear that the station is named for La Ventilla, and the meaning of venta and the diminutive -illa are independently supported by the RAE dictionary. The specific claim that a particular roadside inn gave the neighborhood its name is best documented in local history, not in an official toponymic file among the inspected sources. No reliable evidence was found that the Metro station formerly bore the name Sorolla; that appears more likely to refer to a nearby EMT stop, Sorolla-Avenida de Asturias, or to unrelated historical Metro naming elsewhere.
