Ventilla station opened on 3 June 1983 on the first northern section of Line 9, then operated as Line 9B/9N. Its name comes from La Ventilla, the long-standing popular name of the Almenara neighborhood in Tetuán. Local histories explain the toponym as a diminutive of venta, a roadside inn, because a small inn known as Ventilla or La Ventilla stood near the exit of the old Carretera de Francia. Municipal and academic sources strongly support the later urban history: the area belonged to Chamartín de la Rosa before its 1948 annexation to Madrid, grew spontaneously from the early twentieth century, and was deeply reshaped by public housing and the opening of Avenida de Asturias. The official Callejero adds municipal street evidence for the same name family, including Calle de los Mártires de la Ventilla from 1947 and Calle de la Ventilla from 1986 to 2008.
