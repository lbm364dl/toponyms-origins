Urgel station is a Carabanchel name before it is a medieval one. The Metro stop belongs to Line 5 and sits in the orbit of Calle de Urgel, which the official Madrid street directory lists in Carabanchel. That makes the most direct origin of the station name local and practical: the station took the name already visible in the surrounding street grid.

Behind that Madrid street name is Urgel, the Spanish spelling of Catalan Urgell. Urgell is not a single simple reference: it can evoke the present-day comarca of Urgell, the wider historic lands around the Segre basin, the Diocese of Urgell, La Seu d'Urgell, and above all the medieval County of Urgell. The Consell Comarcal de l'Urgell connects the modern comarca's name with the older Pyrenean county whose rulers carried the name south during medieval expansion.

The County of Urgell gives the name its deepest historical weight. The Gran Enciclopedia Catalana describes it as a territorial and administrative division of Old Catalonia created under Frankish rule after the incorporation of Alt Urgell into the Carolingian sphere in the late eighth century. It later passed through several dynastic phases: Ermengol I began the first comital dynasty in 992, while later counts expanded and consolidated lands along the Segre and in neighbouring regions.

The existing wording's story about Jaume II 'el Dissortat' belongs to this county history, but it is a step beyond what can be proven for the station itself. Jaume II, the last holder of the final comital dynasty, lost the political struggle that followed the succession crisis of the Crown of Aragon; in 1413 Ferdinand I dispossessed him and the county was annexed to the royal patrimony. This is relevant background for Urgell, but it should be presented as historical context, not as proof of why Madrid's street commission chose the name.

The degree of confidence is therefore probable rather than fully verified. The station name, the Madrid street, and the Urgell historical toponym are all well supported; what remains missing is a municipal naming record saying exactly what the Madrid street name intended to commemorate. A cautious public label should read: named after Calle de Urgel, referring to Urgell, the Catalan historical place-name best known through the medieval County of Urgell.
