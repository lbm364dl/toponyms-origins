Alto de Extremadura is a place-name station. The Madrid regional transport consortium lists the station on Metro line 6 and gives one of its accesses as Plaza Alto de Extremadura, on Paseo de Extremadura, which anchors the name in the immediate urban geography rather than in a person or event.

The words describe both a height and a route. A Madrid Río historical report refers to the old “Altos de Extremadura” and adds that the area was once known as Cerro Bermejo, because of the reddish clays in land used for vineyards and cereals from at least the sixteenth century. That makes the “Alto” part more than a generic adjective: it reflects a remembered rise on Madrid's western side.

The “Extremadura” part comes through the road. The same historical material identifies the present Paseo de Extremadura with older routes such as the camino de Alcorcón or de Extremadura, leaving Madrid beyond the Puente de Segovia and the Manzanares. In modern road geography, this corridor became associated with the N-V/A-5 axis toward Badajoz and Portugal, so the station name preserves the direction of travel as much as the local hill.

The area also sits in a frontier zone of Madrid's urban growth. Much of the surrounding western and south-western edge of the city belonged to the former Carabanchel municipalities until 1948, when Carabanchel Alto and Carabanchel Bajo were incorporated into Madrid. The old agricultural landscape, later crossed by tramways, roads and metro tunnels, gradually became part of the Latina district's dense urban fabric.

The station itself is not from 1981. A metro chronology records Alto de Extremadura opening on 10 May 1995 as one of the six stations in the Laguna-Ciudad Universitaria extension, the section that completed Line 6 as a circular line. The road also has documented links with Madrid's historic drove-road network, including the Cañada Real de Madrid, but that context should be presented as background to the corridor, not as the direct reason for the station's name.
