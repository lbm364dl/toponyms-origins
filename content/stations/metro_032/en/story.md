The Metro station name comes from the old Jesuit novitiate beside Calle de San Bernardo. Metro de Madrid's historical sheet says Noviciado station is named for the former Jesuit novitiate located at what is now number 49 on that street, and the station was laid out between Calle de los Reyes and Calle del Noviciado.

That house was formally the Noviciado de San Ignacio, also described as the Noviciado or Casa de Probación of the Society of Jesus. PARES, the Spanish archival authority portal, gives its dates as from 15 April 1602 to 1767 and attributes its creation to Ana Félix de Guzmán, Marchioness of Camarasa, a court lady and patron of the Jesuits.

The novitiate occupied almost the whole block bounded by today's San Bernardo, Noviciado, Amaniel and Reyes streets. A novitiate was not a parish church but a training house: it received candidates during their period of spiritual preparation before entering the order. In this case it became one of the major Jesuit houses in Madrid and, according to academic work on the Complutense's Jesuit heritage, the principal novitiate of the order in the Province of Toledo.

Its later history explains why the name still feels embedded in the city even though the original religious complex disappeared. After the Jesuits were expelled by Charles III in 1767, the site passed through other religious and state uses; after the nineteenth-century confiscations it became associated with the transfer of the old University of Alcalá to Madrid. Municipal heritage documentation records that the Universidad Central was definitively settled there by royal order in 1842, with major rebuilding from 1843 and later work by Francisco Javier de Mariátegui and Narciso Pascual y Colomer.

The station opened on 21 October 1925 as part of the Sol-Quevedo extension of Line 2. No former station name was found. The main etymology is therefore secure, but the street history is a little layered: older street-name references indicate that Calle del Noviciado had previously been called Calle de San Benito, and that the name Noviciado was once attached differently in early maps.
