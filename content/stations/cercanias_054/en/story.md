The Cercanías station called **Móstoles** is named directly after the municipality in which it stands. Today it is a C-5 station and an interchange with Metro Ligero/MetroSur services under the wider transport name Móstoles or Móstoles Central, but the railway name preserves the plain municipal toponym.

The town-name is much older than the modern station. A local-history publication from Móstoles City Council identifies the first secure written reference in an October 1144 grant by Alfonso VII to Segovia Cathedral, where the area is described with the Latin phrase **turrem de Monsteles**, near the road from Madrid to Olmos. A later 1208 boundary text gives the form **Mosteles**, showing that the medieval name was already close to the modern one.

The traditional etymology in the previous wording follows a learned proposal: Móstoles may derive from a late Latin or Mozarabic form related to **monisterium / monisteriis**, meaning 'monastery' or 'at the monasteries'. This fits a broader Iberian family of monastery-derived place-names, and Toponomasticon Hispaniae notes that Móstoles could belong to that group. The municipal historical account also mentions a possible Visigothic monastery in the Arroyo del Soto area, which makes the idea attractive, though not conclusive.

There are competing explanations. Jesús Rodríguez Morales, writing on Celtiberia.net, discussed the monastery derivation but ultimately preferred an alternative from a Hispano-Celtic or Romance landscape term related to **bustellum**, interpreted as a small pasture or meadow; another line of interpretation treats **Mósteles** as a personal name or nickname behind the medieval phrase 'Torre de Mósteles'. These alternatives matter because the earliest documentary form does not by itself prove that actual monasteries gave the town its name.

the reliable part is the immediate naming: **Móstoles station is named after Móstoles municipality**. The deeper etymology should be recorded as probable or contested rather than verified. The current station name has no separate commemorative origin found in the available sources; it is a geographic railway name inherited from the town and from earlier railway service to Móstoles.
