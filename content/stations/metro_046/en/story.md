Prosperidad station takes its name from Plaza de la Prosperidad and from the surrounding neighborhood of the same name in Chamartín. The transport link is direct: the official CRTM station page places the access at Plaza de la Prosperidad, and the Community of Madrid describes the 1973 Line 4 extension as serving the population of the Prosperidad neighborhood.

Behind that place-name is Próspero Soynard y Polen. Historical accounts identify him as one of the first landowner-promoters in this part of Madrid's northeastern fringe, a man who bought agricultural land near the old road to Hortaleza and divided it into small building plots. The name Prosperidad appears to have grown from his given name, Próspero, rather than from a neutral promise of wealth or good fortune.

The foundational date most often cited is 14 December 1862, when Soynard sold the first parcels to Pedro Prado, a bricklayer, and Gregorio Mayorga, a carpenter. The area lay outside the planned Ensanche, on cheap, poorly serviced land where workers, day laborers, artisans and migrants could buy lots and build modest houses. This explains why La Prosperidad developed with a working-class and semi-rural character very different from the nearby Salamanca district.

The neighborhood later became part of Madrid's expanding urban structure. Local historical research notes its incorporation into the Buenavista district in 1898, its reassignment to Chamartín in the 1955 municipal reorganization, and a later 1987 division that separated Ciudad Jardín from the present Prosperidad neighborhood. Line 4 reached the area in March 1973, when the Diego de León-Alfonso XIII extension added three stations for this growing corridor.

The evidence is strong for the station's immediate name source: the plaza and neighborhood. The older explanation linking La Prosperidad to Soynard is also well supported by academic and local historical sources, although the exact social process by which residents fixed the name is less like a formal dedication and more like a nineteenth-century neighborhood toponym that settled into common use.
