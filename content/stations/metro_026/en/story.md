The name **Estación del Arte** is not an old place-name but a deliberate modern label. Metro's former **Atocha** station on Line 1 was renamed with effect from **1 December 2018** so that the stop would read as the gateway to Madrid's nearby art museums: the Museo del Prado, the Museo Reina Sofía and the Museo Nacional Thyssen-Bornemisza.

The older name, **Atocha**, came from the historic railway and urban area around the Glorieta del Emperador Carlos V. When this Metro station opened in 1921, it linked the early underground network with the railway station then commonly known as the Estación del Mediodía, Madrid's southern rail terminus. For decades, the Metro stop's identity was tied to that rail gateway.

That relationship changed as the railway interchange evolved. Metro history notes that the old Atocha station once had a passage toward the railway station, but the role of direct rail access shifted after the opening in 1988 of the intermediate Metro station then called Atocha-Renfe, now simply Atocha. By 2018, having two nearby Metro names built around Atocha was considered confusing for users and visitors.

The art name also describes the surface city. The station stands near the southern end of the Paseo del Prado/Paseo del Arte area, where Madrid promotes a dense cultural route including the Prado, Reina Sofía, Thyssen-Bornemisza, CaixaForum and other institutions. Since 2021, the broader Paseo del Prado and Buen Retiro cultural landscape has also been part of UNESCO's World Heritage List under the name Landscape of Light.

The renaming was more than a map change. A 2018 agreement turned the station into an art-themed space with large-format reproductions from the three main museums, originally 36 works, 12 from each museum, placed in passages, platforms and vestibules. The etymology is therefore secure: **Estación del Arte** is a descriptive transport name created to clarify wayfinding and to brand the station as an underground entrance to Madrid's museum axis.
