The station name is direct and practical. **Arganzuela-Planetario** combines the district where the station stands with the nearby **Planetario de Madrid**, making the stop both a geographic label and a landmark signpost.

The first half of the name, Arganzuela, is much older than the Metro station. Madrid municipal history links it to the old Dehesa de Arganzuela, a grazing area by the Manzanares; the Ayuntamiento notes that on 15 May 1492 the Catholic Monarchs licensed the Villa of Madrid to enclose that dehesa. A later local explanation connects the word with a small settlement of former residents of Arganda, known by a diminutive form, but the dehesa is the clearest documented bridge between the old toponym and the modern district.

The second half points to a twentieth-century civic landmark. The Planetario de Madrid, a municipal centre for astronomy and related sciences, was inaugurated on 29 September 1986 inside Parque Tierno Galván. Current educational material from the planetarium describes its projection dome as 17.5 metres in diameter and records the 2016-2017 technological renewal that replaced the original Zeiss-era experience with a modern hybrid optical-digital system.

The Metro station belongs to a later phase of urban change in the south of central Madrid. Comunidad de Madrid explains that this part of Line 6, between Legazpi and Méndez Álvaro, was longer than usual and crossed an area later transformed after the Pasillo Verde operation, with industrial land shifting toward residential uses and parts incorporated into Parque Tierno Galván. The new station improved the functionality of Line 6 and served a growing residential area.

The best-supported reading is therefore simple: the station was inaugurated with its current compound name on 26 January 2007 to identify its district and nearby planetarium. The current dataset's broad explanation is sound, but some embellishments need caution: the exact administrative naming decision has not been located, and the claim that the compound name was chosen specifically to distinguish it from Legazpi is reasonable but not directly proven by the official sources consulted.
