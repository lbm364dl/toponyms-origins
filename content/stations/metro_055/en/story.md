Carabanchel station is named after the district around it, not after a person or a single building. When the Callao-Carabanchel section of Line 5 opened on 5 June 1968, the name identified the line’s south-western terminus with one of Madrid’s best-known former outlying settlements.

Behind the Metro name are the old “Carabancheles”: Carabanchel de Abajo and Carabanchel de Arriba, later called Carabanchel Bajo and Carabanchel Alto. Madrid City Council describes them as two small towns on the right bank of the Manzanares whose fields supplied produce to the capital and whose greener, cooler surroundings later attracted villas, palaces, and recreation estates.

The name itself reaches much further back than the Metro. A municipal publication records the first known mention of Carabanchel on 1 January 1181, in a parchment preserved in the archive of Toledo Cathedral, when Pedro Manrique de Lara and his sister María transferred the villa of Carabanchel to Gonzalo Díaz for 100 maravedíes. Another medieval form, Carauanchiel, is cited for 1264 in the toponymic literature.

The linguistic origin is debated. Ramón Menéndez Pidal placed Carabanchel among pre-Roman names related to Caravanca, Caravantius, Caravantis and a *carau-/*kar- family associated with stone or rock, which would fit a landscape reading such as “stony ground.” Joaquín Caridad Arias, writing in the Anales del Instituto de Estudios Madrileños, instead argued that Carabanchel should be based on the personal name Carabantius or Caravantius, with the modern ch resulting from palatalization.

The popular chickpea story is culturally memorable but should be treated cautiously. Carabanchel’s fields were indeed associated with food production, and the Ayuntamiento mentions chickpeas in its historical sketch, but deriving the name from garbanzo or garbanzal is likely a folk etymology rather than the strongest philological explanation. The safest public wording is therefore: the station is certainly named for the Carabanchel district; the district name is medieval and probably pre-Roman or ancient in origin, but the exact derivation remains contested.
