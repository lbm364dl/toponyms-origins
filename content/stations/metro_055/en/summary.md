Carabanchel station opened with Line 5 on 5 June 1968 and took the name of the district and old village area around it. Madrid City Council traces the district to the two settlements known as Carabanchel de Abajo and Carabanchel de Arriba, later Bajo and Alto, which were incorporated into Madrid in 1948. The medieval place-name is documented by at least 1181, but its linguistic origin is not certain. Ramón Menéndez Pidal connected Carabanchel with a pre-Roman *carau- or *kar- family associated with stone or rocky ground, while Joaquín Caridad Arias argued for a Celtic personal-name base such as Caravantius. Other proposals, including Arabic carab and the local garbanzo/garbanzal story, are part of the tradition but are weaker as etymology.
