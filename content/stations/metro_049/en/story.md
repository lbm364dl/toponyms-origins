Estrecho station is named after the local place-name **El Estrecho**, not after a street directly beside the station. Metro de Madrid’s historical file places the station under Calle Bravo Murillo, near Francos Rodríguez and Las Mercedes, and records its opening on 6 March 1929 as an intermediate stop on the Line 1 extension from Cuatro Caminos to Tetuán.

The strongest documentary clue is older than many modern neighbourhood descriptions. A 1931 notice in the Gaceta de Madrid mentions the Calle López de Haro area as a “site called El Estrecho” in the Cuatro Caminos neighbourhood, with the route continuing by Francos Rodríguez. That puts the name in local use around the same urban corridor as the Metro station shortly after it opened.

Why “the Strait”? The best-supported explanation is popular rather than administrative. Tetuán de las Victorias took its name from the Spanish victory at Tetouan in the 1859-1860 War of Africa, and Madrid’s own municipal history describes how that nucleus arose after the return of troops and merchants from Morocco. In local memory, people joked that to go from Madrid or Cuatro Caminos toward Tetuán one had to cross “the Strait,” echoing the Strait of Gibraltar between Spain and North Africa.

That context fits the wider naming landscape of old Tetuán, but it should not be flattened into a perfect military map. The municipal historical route notes early streets with names connected to the War of Africa, such as O'Donnell, Prim, Topete, Ceuta and Wad-Ras. Other names often mentioned nearby are more mixed: Bravo Murillo was a politician and public-works figure, while General Margallo belongs to a later Moroccan conflict, not the 1860 campaign.

The confidence is therefore **probable**, not fully verified. The station’s local name and opening date are well documented, and the existence of a nearby “El Estrecho” place-name is directly attested. The precise origin as a Gibraltar/Tetuán joke is plausible and repeated in neighbourhood history, but I did not find an official naming act or contemporary source that explicitly says the station was named in honour of the Strait of Gibraltar.
