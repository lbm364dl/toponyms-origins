The Metro station opened on 6 March 1929 on the Cuatro Caminos-Tetuán extension of Line 1. Its name reflects an older local place-name: a 1931 Gaceta de Madrid notice refers to a “sitio llamado el Estrecho” near López de Haro, Francos Rodríguez and Cuatro Caminos. Later neighbourhood memory explains the name as a humorous allusion to the Strait of Gibraltar, because Tetuán de las Victorias evoked the Moroccan city of Tetouan and the route out from Madrid was imagined as crossing the Strait. The station name is therefore best treated as a local toponym, with a probable Gibraltar/Tetuán wordplay behind it rather than a documented official dedication to the Strait itself.
