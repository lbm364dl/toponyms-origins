The station name comes most directly from Calle de Diego de León, where one of the original accesses was located when the Goya-Diego de León branch opened on 17 September 1932. The street had already been named by a Madrid municipal agreement of 21 July 1880 for Diego Antonio de León y Navarrete, first Count of Belascoáin. León won fame as a cavalry commander in the First Carlist War and became part of the political mythology of liberal and moderate Spain. After joining the 1841 attempt to overturn Espartero's regency and restore María Cristina's influence, he was condemned and shot in Madrid. The popular last-scene details should be handled cautiously: sources agree on the execution and on the famous phrase, but accounts vary in the exact gestures and words.
