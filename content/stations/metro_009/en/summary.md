Lavapiés is named after the plaza and historic popular district above which the station was built. The strongest explanation is descriptive and topographical: the area lay on steep ground, with streets and ravines that carried stormwater down toward Embajadores and the Manzanares. Older Jewish-ablution and inn-washing explanations are part of the neighborhood's legend, but modern documentary and archaeological work places Madrid's medieval Jewish quarter elsewhere. The station opened on Line 3's first Sol-Embajadores section in 1936, so the Metro name appears to have been inherited directly from the existing place name.
