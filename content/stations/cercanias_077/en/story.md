Los Molinos station takes its name from the municipality of Los Molinos, in the Sierra de Guadarrama. The transport name is therefore a place-name rather than a dedication to a person: the stop identifies the town it serves on the Cercanías C-8 route.

The deeper origin of the place-name is the old milling landscape of the upper Guadarrama valley. Local history explains that families of millers settled near the river and that the area came to be known by the practical description 'los molinos', the mills. In this context the mills were flour mills, using water power to grind cereals grown or handled in the surrounding mountain economy.

The documentary trail begins early but needs careful reading. The town council cites BNE manuscript 11,265 and a 1546 license granted to Álvaro de Mena for a cube mill on the Guadarrama. Municipal railway-history research also notes that the place-name Los Molinos appears in Fernando Colón's work between 1517 and 1523, as a place near Guadarrama, which means the name was already in circulation in the early sixteenth century.

The best later confirmation of the milling activity is the Catastro de Ensenada. The municipal page on the 1751 survey states that Los Molinos had seven flour mills, one of them destroyed. That number is stronger than the looser 'at least three' formulation and shows that milling was not just a legend attached to the name but a documented element of the local economy.

For the station itself, the railway context begins with the Villalba-Segovia line, officially opened on 29 June 1888. A municipal study of 125 years of railway history lists the stop as Los Molinos-Guadarrama, and current public transport information lists Los Molinos on Cercanías C-8. The etymology is therefore very secure in broad terms, although the 1546 Álvaro de Mena document should be cited with care because one published study of Madrid flour mills transcribes it as referring to 'la Foz' and to the Manzanares, then called Guadarrama.
