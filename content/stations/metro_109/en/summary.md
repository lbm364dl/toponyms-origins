The station is named after the Puente de Vallecas district and older neighbourhood, whose name began with a literal bridge on the road from Madrid to Vallecas. An official catalogue of Madrid architectural drawings identifies a 1729 Pedro de Ribera plan for a bridge over the Arroyo Abroñigal on the Camino de Vallecas. That crossing linked Madrid with the then separate municipality of Vallecas and helped fix the name of the suburb growing beyond the Abroñigal. Metro de Madrid opened the Atocha-Puente de Vallecas extension of Line 1 to the public on 8 May 1923, making this place-name part of the network. The old bridge itself disappeared in the twentieth century, but the name remains attached to the station, district and urban identity of Vallecas.
