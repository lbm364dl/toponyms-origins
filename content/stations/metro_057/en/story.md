Esperanza station is named, with high probability, after the Colonia Virgen de la Esperanza, the residential estate that surrounds its entrance in Canillas. The official CRTM station page places the Line 4 stop at the Andorra access, Calle de Andorra, 51, while architectural sources identify this area as the Barrio de Nueva Esperanza or Colonia Virgen de la Esperanza.

The place name belongs to Madrid's postwar and late-Franco housing expansion rather than to an old rural landscape. COAM records a 1965 urban layout by a broad team of architects and housing built in 1969-1971 by Antonio Camuñas Paredes and José Antonio Camuñas Solís for the Cooperativa Virgen de la Esperanza. Its formal description gives the scale of the estate: 3,393 dwellings arranged in 156 five-storey blocks and 20 seventeen-storey towers.

The wider chronology matters because Canillas had been a separate municipality until the mid twentieth century. The Ayuntamiento de Madrid explains that Canillas was annexed to Madrid in 1950 and later became part of the urban growth that transformed Hortaleza. In the 1970s, large estates such as Virgen del Cortijo and the Colonia Virgen de la Esperanza marked that change from peripheral settlement to metropolitan district.

The Metro arrived after the housing estate was already established. The Community of Madrid's history of Line 4 states that the Alfonso XIII-Esperanza extension opened on 5 January 1979, adding 2.221 kilometres and three stations. That made Esperanza a transport terminus name attached to a neighbourhood identity created only a few years earlier.

Some details should be handled carefully. The station is in today's district of Hortaleza, in Canillas, not Chamartín, although Canillas and Hortaleza were once administratively grouped with Chamartín before the 1970 district reorganisation. The Teruel street-name cluster around Andorra, Calanda, Alcorisa and Utrillas is visible in the local geography and in secondary descriptions, but the stronger available sources prove the architectural and transport context more directly than the residents' origins.
