Zarzaquemada is first of all a neighbourhood name: Renfe describes the station as being in the homonymous Leganes neighbourhood, and official transport sources list it on Cercanias line C-5. Linguistically, the name is transparent in Spanish: zarza is a bramble or blackberry bush, a pre-Roman word according to the RAE, and quemada is the feminine past participle of quemar, 'to burn'. The most plausible reading is therefore 'burned bramble patch' or 'burned bramble land'. Traditional Iberian rozas involved burning vegetation before cultivation, so that agricultural context fits the form, but no source found directly proves that this exact Leganes toponym began as a specific slash-and-burn plot.
