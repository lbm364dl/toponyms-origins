San Lorenzo station is named for the San Lorenzo barrio of Hortaleza. The most direct official source is Comunidad de Madrid's page on the Line 4 extension from Mar de Cristal to Parque de Santa María, which says that San Lorenzo station is located in the barrio from which it takes its name, under Calle or Avenida de la Barranquilla.

That barrio name was already part of the modern urban geography of Hortaleza before the Metro arrived. The COAM architectural guide lists Colonia San Lorenzo in the district of Hortaleza as a housing estate or neighbourhood dated 1967, designed by José Luis Sanz-Magallón y Hurtado de Mendoza, with the project and works running from the late 1960s into the early 1970s. The City Council's current district mapping places San Lorenzo within the administrative barrio of Pinar del Rey, so here San Lorenzo is best understood as a local neighbourhood or colony name rather than one of Madrid's official barrio names.

The Metro name belongs to the second phase of Line 4's arrival in this part of Hortaleza. The extension between Mar de Cristal and Parque de Santa María entered service on 15 December 1998 and added the stations of San Lorenzo and Parque de Santa María. Local reporting on the 25th anniversary of the Metro in Hortaleza also describes how neighbourhood campaigns pushed for stations in San Lorenzo and nearby areas after years of poor public transport.

The name itself is ultimately religious. San Lorenzo is Saint Lawrence of Rome, traditionally remembered as one of the deacons of Pope Sixtus II and as a martyr during the persecution of Emperor Valerian in 258. Vatican News gives the traditional account: Lawrence cared for the Church's goods for the poor, presented the poor as the Church's true treasure, and was martyred on 10 August. Britannica is more cautious on the details, noting that the gridiron death is legendary and that Lawrence was probably beheaded, which is important for a historically careful public explanation.

For public wording, the safest wording is that the station is directly named after the San Lorenzo barrio or Colonia San Lorenzo in Hortaleza, and only ultimately after Saint Lawrence. No authoritative source proving that the station was named after a specific local parish of San Lorenzo was found; nearby religious references in the area include other parish names, and the sources found support the barrio/colony explanation much more directly.
