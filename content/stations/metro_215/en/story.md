La Fortuna station is named directly after the La Fortuna district of Leganés, the area reached by Line 11 when it crossed beyond Madrid city into this isolated edge of the municipality. The station opened on 5 October 2010 as the terminus of the extension from La Peseta, giving the district a direct Metro link toward Plaza Elíptica.

Behind the district name is a personal story. Comunidad de Madrid's publication on the Line 11 extension explains that Domingo Dos Santos, known locally as "El Portugués", named the barrio for his wife, the Madrid-born Fortunata González Arroyo. In that sense, the Metro name is not a direct commemorative station name like a street named for a public figure; it is a place name that preserves a family dedication.

The settlement began around 1960 on the boundary between Leganés and Madrid. Accounts of its foundation describe families moved from the Orcasitas area, early plots linked to waste processing and animal raising, and a first urban nucleus built with small houses and narrow streets. Local histories also note that the earliest street names often followed saints' names connected with Dos Santos's children, relatives, or friends, which helps explain the later importance of San Fortunato in the district's festive identity.

For decades, La Fortuna was physically close to Madrid but poorly connected both to the capital and to the main urban centre of Leganés. Local and transport sources describe the arrival of buses, municipal works, schools, health and sports facilities, and the creation of the district board in the 1990s as milestones in the neighbourhood's integration. The Metro extension was presented in 2010 as the answer to a long-standing demand from residents.

The evidence strongly supports the etymology, but the dataset should distinguish the station's immediate and ultimate namesakes. The station is named after the barrio of La Fortuna; the barrio, in turn, is named after Fortunata González Arroyo. Some secondary accounts differ in the exact number of pioneer families, so those figures should be treated as historical context rather than the defining etymology.
