Aluche station is named directly for the Aluche neighbourhood around it, now part of Madrid's Latina district. The transport name is therefore a neighbourhood name, but the neighbourhood name points further back to a lost watercourse: the Arroyo Luche.

The Luche was a tributary of the Manzanares. It crossed the western edge of Madrid through areas that later became Aluche, Lucero and Puerta del Ángel, watering orchards and low-lying land before urban growth transformed the landscape. The Museo del Prado gives unusually direct support for this origin in its record for José Jiménez Fernández's 1873 painting *Landscape. Stream in the orchards of Luche*, describing the stream as the Manzanares tributary that ran through what is now Aluche and gave the neighbourhood its name.

The place-name is older than the twentieth-century housing district. Local histories cite a Royal Cedula of 26 March 1580, issued in the time of Philip II for the delimitation of royal hunting grounds near the Casa de Campo, where both the Arroyo de Luche and an 'exido' or common land called Aluche are mentioned. The Catastro de Ensenada is also reported to record a 'parage' or place called Aluche in the lands of the Carabancheles, reinforcing that this was a rural toponym before the modern city arrived.

The station's transport history belongs to Madrid's great suburban expansion. Aluche appeared on the Ferrocarril Suburbano de Carabanchel, opened in 1961, and later became a key interchange when the Aluche-Móstoles railway opened in 1976 and the Carabanchel-Aluche section was incorporated into Metro line 5. The neighbourhood itself grew rapidly from former Carabanchel Bajo land annexed to Madrid in 1948, with the present barrio framework taking shape in the early 1970s.

One common explanation says that old maps wrote the stream as 'A. Luche' for 'Arroyo Luche' and that the abbreviation fused into 'Aluche'. That is possible as a later folk-etymology, but it is not the best-supported origin because the form Aluche is already cited in sixteenth-century documentation. The safest public explanation is simpler: the station is named for the neighbourhood, and the neighbourhood preserves the name of the Arroyo Luche and its orchards.
