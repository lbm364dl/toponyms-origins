Hortaleza station opened on Line 4 on 11 April 2007, in the extension from Parque de Santa María toward Pinar de Chamartín. Its name comes from the district of Hortaleza, itself named after the former village and municipality absorbed into Madrid in the postwar annexation process. Official municipal sources record that the district includes land from the old municipalities of Hortaleza and Canillas, and the BOE decree of 22 July 1949 approved the total annexation of Hortaleza to Madrid. The older place-name is not as settled as the common orchard explanation suggests: Corominas notes Hortaleza as a Madrid toponym and considers it a variant of fortaleza, while popular accounts often connect it with market gardens. The safest public wording is therefore that the station is named after the district/former municipality, while the medieval origin of the word remains probable rather than fully verified.
