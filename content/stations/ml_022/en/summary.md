The station is named for Aravaca, the historic village and present neighbourhood where the ML2 line meets Cercanías and bus services. Official regional transport material describes the stop as the end of the Colonia Jardín-Pozuelo-Aravaca light-rail corridor and places it in the Aravaca neighbourhood near Calle Golondrina and Avenida del Talgo. Aravaca was once a municipality of its own and was incorporated into Madrid in the mid twentieth century. For the older place-name, Joaquín Caridad Arias proposed a Celtic explanation, reading Aravaca as an ethnic designation related to the Arevaci named in classical sources. The medieval administrative context is solid, but the exact linguistic origin remains a scholarly proposal rather than a universally proven fact.
