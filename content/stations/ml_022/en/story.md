Estación de Aravaca takes its name from Aravaca itself: the historic settlement and present Madrid neighbourhood at the western edge of the city. In transport terms, this is not a commemorative name but a place-name. The Community of Madrid describes the stop as the point where the Metro Ligero reaches Aravaca and forms a multimodal interchange with Cercanías and bus lines.

The older name belongs to a village that long predated the light rail. Aravaca was one of the rural places in the old Tierra de Madrid, and the expression "sexmo de Aravaca" appears in medieval administrative history as one of the rural compartments associated with Madrid's hinterland. Nearby towns such as Pozuelo, Majadahonda, Boadilla, Leganés and the Carabancheles are repeatedly linked in later historical accounts to that Aravaca sexmo.

There is a useful correction to make in the usual short version of the story. The Fuero of Madrid is associated with Alfonso VIII and the early thirteenth century, but several municipal historical summaries place the clearer 1222 delimitation of Madrid's rural sexmos under Fernando III, not Alfonso VIII. For public wording, it is safer to say that Aravaca is documented in the medieval organization of the Tierra de Madrid and to avoid presenting the 1222/Alfonso VIII wording as certain.

The deeper linguistic origin is more speculative. Joaquín Caridad Arias argued in a 2004 article in Anales del Instituto de Estudios Madrileños that Aravaca should not be explained by a folk phrase such as "arar vacas" but as a Celtic ethnic designation: "the Aravaca" or "the Arevaca", related to the Arevaci known from classical authors. He also linked the ancient ethnic name to the River Areva, following Pliny's statement that the river gave the Arevaci their name.

The modern station name is therefore very secure at the immediate level: it names the Aravaca neighbourhood and interchange. The ancient etymology is plausible and sourced to a named scholarly proposal, but it should be presented as a proposed Celtic explanation rather than a settled fact. Aravaca's later civic history is clearer: the municipality kept its autonomy until its incorporation into Madrid, approved by decree in 1949 and completed on 20 October 1951.
