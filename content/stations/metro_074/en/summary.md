Vicálvaro is a place-name station: it points to the old town of Vicálvaro, now a district in eastern Madrid. Madrid municipal sources record Vicálvaro as a former municipality annexed by decree of 10 November 1950, with the annexation remembered locally as taking effect on 20 October 1951. The name's ultimate etymology is not fully settled: an official Madrid study says the most accepted base is Latin vicus, 'village, quarter or estate', but gives two hypotheses, Vicus-Alvar and Vicus-Albus. The station opened on Metro line 9 on 1 December 1998 as part of the Pavones-Puerta de Arganda extension serving the growing Vicálvaro area. The official Callejero gives additional Madrid-road chronology for the old town name, including Camino de Vicálvaro from 1 January 1874 and Carretera de Vicálvaro from 1 January 1910.
