The station name is direct and local: **Vicálvaro** is named after the historic town of Vicálvaro, now the eastern Madrid district in which the Metro stop sits. The line 9 station opened on 1 December 1998 in the Pavones-Puerta de Arganda extension, a project explicitly described by the Community of Madrid as improving mobility for the growing population of Vicálvaro.

Behind the modern station is a much older place-name. Madrid municipal research describes the most accepted starting point as Latin **vicus**, meaning a village, quarter or rural estate. From there, however, the explanation divides: one hypothesis reads the name as **Vicus-Alvar**, linked to a personal name such as Alvar or Álvaro; another reads it as **Vicus-Albus**, a 'white place' connected with the area's gypsum quarries.

The settlement appears in documentary memory at least by the fourteenth century. A published local history extract identifies the first known document with the name Vicálvaro as a 1352 record in the Vatican Secretariat Archive referring to tithe payments. Later, Vicálvaro belonged to the jurisdictional world of the Villa y Tierra de Madrid during the Ancien Régime, but it also retained the identity of a separate municipality until the twentieth-century expansion of Madrid absorbed it.

That absorption is one of the important dates behind the name. Madrid's municipal archive states that Vicálvaro was annexed to Madrid by a Ministry of the Interior decree dated 10 November 1950, while local and press accounts remember 20 October 1951 as the day the annexation became effective in civic life. After annexation it passed through other district arrangements before becoming an independent district in the 1987 municipal restructuring.

Vicálvaro also carries a national historical echo: the **Vicalvarada**, the 1854 military rising associated with General Leopoldo O'Donnell. The Real Academia de la Historia records the Battle of Vicálvaro under 28 June 1854, and the later Manifesto of Manzanares helped turn the military episode into the political movement that opened the Progressive Biennium. For the station etymology, though, that episode is context rather than origin: the station is named for the place, and the place-name's exact medieval meaning remains probable rather than fully proven.
