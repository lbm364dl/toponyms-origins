Dos Castillas is a station name taken directly from its setting: the stop is on Vía de las Dos Castillas, 11D, in Pozuelo de Alarcón. Metro Ligero Oeste describes Dos Castillas as an ML2 station located on a similarly named road, and the Regional Transport Consortium gives the same street as the station access address.

The phrase "Dos Castillas" most naturally refers to the old geographical and historical distinction between Castilla la Vieja and Castilla la Nueva. In that usage, Castile was understood as a broad central Spanish region divided into a northern Old Castile and a southern New Castile, rather than as the two modern autonomous communities that include the word Castilla.

Madrid belongs to the story through New Castile. Reference works describe Castilla la Nueva as a historic central Spanish region including Madrid together with Ciudad Real, Cuenca, Guadalajara and Toledo, and as land linked to the former Muslim kingdom of Toledo incorporated into Castile. Nineteenth-century geographical sources also describe the Sierra de Guadarrama as one of the mountain lines separating the two Castiles.

That makes the street name especially apt for a road in Pozuelo de Alarcón, west of Madrid, near the Guadarrama-facing edge of the metropolitan area. The ML2 line opened with Metro Ligero Oeste on 27 July 2007 and runs through this corridor of campuses, business parks and residential areas, so the station name preserves an older regional vocabulary inside a very modern transport landscape.

The evidence is strongest for the immediate naming chain: station from road. The deeper interpretation of the road name as Old Castile and New Castile is historically well supported, but the available sources did not include the Pozuelo municipal naming record explaining why the road was assigned that name. For public wording, it is better to say that the station is named after Vía de las Dos Castillas and that the road name probably evokes the historical two Castiles, not today's Castilla y León and Castilla-La Mancha in a strict one-to-one sense.
