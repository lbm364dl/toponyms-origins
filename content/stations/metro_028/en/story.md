The direct origin of the station name is the Atlético de Madrid stadium next to it. Metro de Madrid's Line 7 stop was originally called **Estadio Olímpico**, but in the night of 25 to 26 June 2017 its signs began to change to **Estadio Metropolitano**, ahead of Atlético's move to its new home.

The word “Metropolitano” reaches further back than the 2017 stadium. Atlético chose it as a historical reference to the old **Stadium Metropolitano**, the club's home from 1923 until 1966, before the move to the Vicente Calderón. That earlier ground stood near the present Plaza de la Ciudad de Viena, close to Avenida de la Reina Victoria and Cuatro Caminos.

The older name was tied to Madrid's early metro-era urban development. Atlético's own centenary account says the Stadium Metropolitano was promoted by the Otamendi brothers, creators of the Madrid Metro, and that Sociedad Stadium Metropolitano was constituted on 16 March 1922 before the stadium opened on 13 May 1923. The name therefore carries both football memory and the imprint of the metropolitan railway and real-estate projects around north-west Madrid.

The modern stadium also has a layered history. The site began as the athletics stadium popularly known as **La Peineta**, associated with Madrid's Olympic ambitions, and the metro station's former name reflected that phase. Atlético's redeveloped stadium opened for the first team on 16 September 2017, hosted the 2019 UEFA Champions League final, and is currently commercially named **Riyadh Air Metropolitano** after earlier Wanda and Cívitas naming-rights periods.

The etymology is therefore secure, but the wording should be precise. The station is not named after a person and not after the sponsor; it is named after the nearby Metropolitano stadium, whose own name intentionally revives Atlético's 1923-1966 home. The former station name was Estadio Olímpico, and the effective station renaming date is best recorded as 26 June 2017, with the official public presentation by the Comunidad de Madrid on 27 June 2017.
