The tram stop called **Jaime I** is best understood as a street-derived name. CRTM lists separate platforms, Jaime I Norte and Jaime I Sur, on the Tranvía de Parla, while Parla's municipal transparency inventory records **Calle Jaime I El Conquistador** as an official street running through this part of the city. The stop therefore points first to the local street, and through it to the medieval monarch Jaime I of Aragon.

Jaime I, known as **el Conquistador**, was one of the major rulers of the thirteenth-century Crown of Aragon. The Real Academia de la Historia gives his life dates as 2 February 1208, in Montpellier, and 27 July 1276, in Alcira, and identifies him as king of Aragon, Mallorca and Valencia, count of Barcelona and Urgel, and lord of Montpellier. His byname reflects the military expansion that made him central to the historical memory of the western Mediterranean.

The best-known episodes behind that reputation are the conquest of Mallorca, begun in 1229, and the conquest of Valencia, whose surrender in 1238 opened the way to a new kingdom within the Crown of Aragon. His reign also mattered institutionally: Valencia developed its own legal and political framework, while the Aragonese fueros were compiled and promulgated in the royal context of 1247. These details make the street name more than a simple royal label; it evokes expansion, law, and the formation of medieval territorial identities.

In Parla, the name sits in a recognizable urban pattern. The municipal inventory places Calle Jaime I El Conquistador among streets such as Reyes Católicos, Felipe II, Alfonso XIII, Isabel II, Fernando III el Santo and Carlos V. That pattern suggests a local naming landscape organized around monarchs and royal history, although I did not find the original municipal act that created the street name.

The stop itself belongs to the modern Tranvía de Parla, a surface light-rail system developed as part of Madrid Region's 2003-2007 transport infrastructure programme. CRTM material describes the Parla tramway as a circular urban line operated by Tranvía de Parla S.A.; its public maps include Jaime I Norte and Jaime I Sur. The exact naming act for the stop remains to be checked in municipal or concession records, so the etymology is very strong but should be treated as probable rather than fully documented by a primary naming resolution.
