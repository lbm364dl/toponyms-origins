The station name **Alonso de Mendoza** comes first from the urban geography around the stop: Metro and CRTM plans place the station beside Calle de Alonso de Mendoza in Getafe, near Calle del Greco and Calle Ferrocarril. A recent Madrid local-history article states explicitly that the station owes its name to that adjacent street, and not to a district or interchange name.

The Alonso de Mendoza behind the Getafe name is best identified as **Alonso de Mendoza y Núñez de Prado**, remembered locally for the Hospital de San José, also called the Hospitalillo. He was connected with Archbishop Alonso Carrillo de Albornoz and held office in Bonilla de la Sierra, in Ávila. The hospital's own foundation history says that on 5 September 1507 he made his will there and left most of his assets to secure the hospital's future.

The most important nuance is that the hospital story is not always told in exactly the same way. Some summaries say Mendoza founded the Hospital de San José; the foundation's own account says his will provided for the enlargement and regulation of a hospital that already existed in Getafe. Either way, the public memory attached to his name is the same: a durable charitable institution for poor sick people, especially those from Getafe, Pinto, and Griñón.

That charitable setting matters because early-16th-century Getafe stood on the Madrid-Toledo road, a route used by local residents, travellers, and people in need of lodging or care. The Hospitalillo became one of the town's most enduring historic buildings, with a Renaissance-style courtyard, a chapel, and later architectural changes. It also survived into modern civic life as the seat of the Fundación Hospital de San José and as a social and cultural facility.

There is one common wrong turn in the name: Alonso de Mendoza is also the name of the conquistador associated with the 1548 foundation of La Paz in present-day Bolivia. For this Madrid Metro station, that identification is weak. The local Getafe evidence points instead to the street name and to the Alonso de Mendoza connected with the Hospital de San José; the exact municipal act naming the street was not found in the sources inspected.
