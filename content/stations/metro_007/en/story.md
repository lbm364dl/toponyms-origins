Chueca station is named after the square above it: Plaza de Chueca. The square's present name honors Pío Estanislao Federico Chueca y Robres, usually known as Federico Chueca, a Madrid-born composer closely associated with zarzuela and the sound of popular nineteenth-century Madrid.

Before becoming Plaza de Chueca, the place was Plaza de San Gregorio or San Gregorio Magno. Madrid street-name scholarship records that the City Council dedicated the square to Chueca on 26 August 1943, replacing the older San Gregorio name, and ratified the change on 14 October 1943. A municipal note from 2022 also confirms that the current name dates from 1943 and recalls the composer.

Federico Chueca was born in Madrid on 5 May 1846 and died in the city on 20 June 1908. Sources agree that he became a central figure of género chico, the short, popular zarzuela form. One famous early episode says that, after being jailed during student unrest against the Narváez government, he improvised waltzes later arranged by Francisco Asenjo Barbieri as Lamentos de un preso; this was an early success, though not the literal beginning of his musical life.

Chueca's strongest Madrid connection is musical and urban. La Gran Vía, composed with Joaquín Valverde and premiered at Teatro Felipe on 2 July 1886, turned the projected new avenue into a comic, musical portrait of the city before the actual street existed. The Biblioteca Nacional de España notes its extraordinary public reception: it stayed at Teatro Felipe through the summer of 1886 and then ran at Teatro Apolo for three more years.

The Metro name came later. A study in Anales del Instituto de Estudios Madrileños states that the metropolitan station received the name Chueca on 2 March 1970, on Line 5's Callao-Ventas extension, while a Metro de Madrid technical document says the station was created two years after Line 5's 1968 opening. Today the name also carries the modern identity of the surrounding area: officially Justicia, but popularly Chueca, a central Madrid neighborhood widely associated since the late twentieth century with diversity, LGBTQ life, commerce, and nightlife.
