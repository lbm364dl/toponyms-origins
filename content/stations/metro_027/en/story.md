The current name **Feria de Madrid** is a practical place-name: it directs passengers to Madrid's main exhibition and congress grounds, today branded IFEMA MADRID. The station sits by the fair complex on Avenida del Partenón, so the name works less as a historical dedication than as a transport wayfinding label for a major destination.

Until 2017 the station was called **Campo de las Naciones**. That older name belonged to the wider urban and cultural complex developed in Madrid's northeast, where the fairgrounds, the Palacio Municipal de Congresos, offices, hotels and Parque Juan Carlos I formed a new metropolitan district. Madrid City Council describes Campo de las Naciones as a large project already foreseen in the 1985 General Plan and linked to the 1992 European Capital of Culture programme.

The station opened on **24 June 1998** as part of the Line 8 section between Mar de Cristal and Campo de las Naciones. For almost a year it was the line's outer terminus; on **14 June 1999** Line 8 was extended to the airport station then called Aeropuerto. The later 2002 works brought Line 8 to Nuevos Ministerios, but they did not mark the opening of this particular station.

The renaming was announced by the Community of Madrid on **7 June 2017** and took effect on **26 June 2017**. The official explanation was explicit: the new name would make it easier for users to associate the Metro stop with IFEMA's exhibition grounds, a major generator of fair, congress and business-tourism traffic. In other words, the change privileged the everyday destination over the broader district name.

A trace of the former name remains inside the station. The Line 8 infrastructure page describes its mural, *Rostros de las Naciones, una sola bandera*, a long ceramic work using faces, flags and international quotations to express the older Campo de las Naciones theme. The etymology is therefore verified, with one correction: the station is named after the fairgrounds, not after a person, and its opening year should be recorded as 1998.
