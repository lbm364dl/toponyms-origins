Duque de Pastrana station takes its name from the square above and beside it: Plaza del Duque de Pastrana, in Chamartín. Transport sources place the station on Line 9, and a Metro de Madrid photographic archive record dates the opening of the Plaza de Castilla-Avenida de América section to 30 December 1983. The station therefore preserves a local urban name rather than introducing a new commemorative name of its own.

That local name reaches back into the history of Chamartín de la Rosa. Madrid’s district history notes that the old village nucleus grew around the area of today’s Plaza del Duque de Pastrana, which corresponds to the former Plaza Mayor of Chamartín. This makes the station name unusually layered: it points not only to an aristocratic title, but also to the civic center of a municipality that was annexed to Madrid in 1948.

The title behind the name is the Duchy of Pastrana. Spain’s official guide to grandeeships and titles records that the duchy was granted on 20 December 1572 to Don Ruy Gómez de Silva, prince of Éboli, councillor of state and close servant of Philip II. The Real Academia de la Historia identifies him as a Portuguese-born courtier from Chamusca, born in 1516 and deceased in Madrid on 29 July 1573, who entered Castilian court life as a page in the entourage of Isabel of Portugal in 1526.

In Chamartín, however, the most relevant thread is the presence of the ducal house rather than only the biography of the first duke. The area is still marked by the Palacio de los Duques de Pastrana, between Calle de Platerías and Paseo de la Habana, declared a national historic-artistic monument in 1979. Later sources also connect the Pastrana/Infantado household with estates and institutions around old Chamartín, including the Quinta del Recuerdo and the Jesuit school nearby.

For that reason, the safest public explanation is: the station is named after Plaza del Duque de Pastrana, whose name recalls the Dukes of Pastrana and their historic association with Chamartín. Ruy Gómez de Silva is essential background because he was the first holder of the title, but the available sources did not include an authoritative municipal naming record proving that the Madrid square was dedicated specifically to him rather than to the ducal title or house. The official historical Callejero now gives the formal current-name date as 7 April 1965 and records earlier names for the same plaza, including Plaza Mayor de Chamartín de la Rosa, Plaza de la Constitución, and Plaza de Calvo Sotelo.
