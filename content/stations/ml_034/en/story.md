Nuevo Mundo station is named after the street it serves: Avenida Nuevo Mundo in Boadilla del Monte. Metro Ligero Oeste describes the stop as an ML3 station located on the avenue of the same name, close to IES Máximo Trueba and CEIP Teresa de Berganza.

The words mean 'New World', a historical expression used in European geography for the Americas after the Atlantic voyages that began in 1492. Britannica notes that Amerigo Vespucci’s published letters helped popularize the Latin form *Mundus Novus*, or 'New World', for lands across the Atlantic.

Boadilla’s street map makes the local context clearer. Around this part of town and the adjoining Valenoso area are streets named Cristóbal Colón, Hernán Cortés, Francisco Pizarro, Diego de Almagro, Alonso de Ojeda, Francisco de Orellana, Pedro de Valdivia and Reyes Católicos, forming a visible cluster of names tied to exploration, conquest and early modern Spanish expansion.

The transport name is modern even though the phrase is historical. Metro Ligero Oeste opened its ML2 and ML3 network on 27 July 2007; ML3 runs from Colonia Jardín to Puerta de Boadilla, and Nuevo Mundo became one of the stops serving Boadilla’s newer residential corridor.

The strongest verified fact is the direct station-to-avenue naming. The broader explanation, that the avenue belongs to an Americas or Discovery-era naming theme, is strongly suggested by the official street list but would need a municipal naming file or plenary record to be fully documented.
