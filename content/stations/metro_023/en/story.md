The station is named for Vicente Aleixandre, one of the central poets of Spain's Generation of 1927. Metro de Madrid opened this Line 6 stop in 1987 as Metropolitano, but the Community of Madrid announced in November 2018 that it would become Vicente Aleixandre from the start of service on 1 December 2018.

The reason was deliberately local. Aleixandre lived a short distance from the station, at what is now Calle Vicente Aleixandre 3, in the house known as Velintonia. The official announcement also said the change would reduce confusion with Estadio Metropolitano, the Line 7 station associated with Atletico Madrid's stadium.

Aleixandre was born in Seville in 1898, spent part of his childhood in Malaga, and made Madrid his lasting literary home. He was elected to the Real Academia Espanola in 1949, took possession of seat O in 1950, and received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1977. His work helped renew Spanish poetry between the wars and made him a reference point for later poets.

The nearby house gives the station name an extra layer. Aleixandre moved there in 1927, when the street was officially Wellingtonia; he gradually Hispanicized the name as Velintonia in his letters, and that name became attached to the house itself. Velintonia was not only a private home but a meeting place for poets of the Generation of 1927 and for younger writers after the Civil War.

The former station name, Metropolitano, belonged to the wider urban history of this part of Madrid: the area was developed by the Compania Urbanizadora Metropolitana, linked to the early Metro enterprise. The 2018 renaming did not erase that history entirely, because the station vestibules still preserve the Metropolitano label in transport information, but the public name now foregrounds the poet and his Madrid address. The current etymology is therefore well supported, with the main correction that the change was a specific local and wayfinding decision, not simply part of a broad programme to honour artists.
