El Goloso station takes its name from the place around it: El Goloso, on the northern edge of Madrid, beside the Madrid-Colmenar Viejo road and the military complex that also bears the name. Current transport sources identify the stop as El Goloso and place it on the C-4/C-4b Cercanias service, with access from the Carretera de Colmenar Viejo.

The most important point is chronological. El Goloso is not simply a station named after a modern barracks. A study of military cartography records a 1930 sheet called El Goloso in the series Madrid y sus alrededores, while a later exhibition catalogue identifies the laying of the first stone of the Cuartel del Goloso on 30 January 1933, attended by Manuel Azana. That makes the name older than the visible military installation that later popularized it.

The surrounding administrative history also supports the place-name reading. Before Fuencarral was absorbed into Madrid, archival description of the former municipality notes that, by the time of the 10 November 1950 annexation decree, Fuencarral had several barrios, including El Goloso. Today Madrid City Council lists El Goloso as barrio 8.8 of Fuencarral-El Pardo, although the modern barrio includes areas beyond the immediate railway stop.

The station itself belongs to the railway geography of the Directo Madrid-Burgos. Railway histories list El Goloso as an apeadero at about kilometre 10.44 of the Madrid-Burgos route, whose official inauguration was on 4 July 1968. Its later Cercanias role connected the name with everyday metropolitan travel, while the Army source places the Armoured Units exhibition inside Base El Goloso, headquarters of the Guadarrama XII Brigade.

The word goloso is transparent in ordinary Spanish: the RAE derives it from Latin gulosus and defines it as someone fond of sweets, desirous of something, or appetizing. That dictionary meaning, however, does not explain the Madrid toponym. Claims connecting gol-/gor- roots with reddish ground are speculative unless supported by stronger linguistic or historical evidence, so the safest public explanation is that the station is named after a pre-existing El Goloso place-name whose original motivation remains unknown.
