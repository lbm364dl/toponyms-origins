Metro de Madrid's historical station sheet states that the station was named after the then Glorieta de San Bernardo, renamed Glorieta de Ruiz Giménez in 1934. The wider place-name comes from Calle de San Bernardo, historically called Calle Ancha de San Bernardo and, in the 17th-century Teixeira map, Calle de Convalecientes or de San Bernardo. A Community of Madrid heritage order explains that the street took its name from a hospital and convent of San Bernardo erected there in the 16th century. The name therefore has a religious origin connected with Saint Bernard of Clairvaux, but the previous wording's reference to the 1617 Cistercian monastery founded by Cardinal Sandoval y Rojas belongs to Alcalá de Henares, not this Madrid station area.
