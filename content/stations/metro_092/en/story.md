San Bernardo station takes its name from the old Glorieta de San Bernardo, the roundabout under which the station was built. Metro de Madrid records that this square was renamed Glorieta de Ruiz Giménez in 1934, after the Madrid minister and mayor who died that year, but the station kept the older transport name.

Behind the roundabout is Calle de San Bernardo, one of the historic northbound roads out of central Madrid. In older usage it was Calle Ancha de San Bernardo, and official heritage documentation notes that Pedro Teixeira's 17th-century map already showed it as Calle de Convalecientes or de San Bernardo.

The Madrid name is best explained by a local religious and charitable institution: a hospital and convent of San Bernardo erected in the street in the 16th century. That gives the station a religious etymology connected with Saint Bernard of Clairvaux, the medieval Cistercian reformer, but the naming chain is local: institution, street, roundabout, station.

San Bernardo also became one of Madrid's great academic addresses. The Jesuit Noviciado stood on Calle Ancha de San Bernardo from the early 17th century, and the Complutense University describes the later Paraninfo and university complex at Calle San Bernardo 49 as occupying the former Jesuit novitiate site, with major works between 1842 and 1855 and the assembly hall opened in 1851-1852.

A common confusion should be avoided. The celebrated Cistercian Monastery of San Bernardo founded in 1617 by Cardinal Bernardo de Sandoval y Rojas, with its important oval-domed church, is in Alcalá de Henares at Plaza de las Bernardas. It is relevant to Saint Bernard's wider presence in the Madrid region, but it is not the source of this Metro station's name.
