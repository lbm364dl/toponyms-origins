República Argentina station takes its name from the Plaza de la República Argentina, the roundabout above the Line 6 platforms in Chamartín. In transport terms this is a normal Madrid naming pattern: the station uses the name of the public space it serves, and that public space is named for the Argentine Republic.

The older name behind the station is therefore not a person or a local village, but a country. The plaza was conceived as a Madrid homage to Argentina, one of several American-themed civic names in northern Madrid, though the immediate official neighbourhood is El Viso rather than Hispanoamérica. Municipal records for the Fuente de los Delfines give the address as Plaza República Argentina 1 and the barrio/district as El Viso / Chamartín.

The plaza's commemorative character is unusually well documented through its fountain. Madrid's heritage catalogue states that the fountain originated in a 1949 competition for a Monument to the Argentine Nation in the same plaza. The winning monumental design was never fully built; only part of the water feature survived, and Manuel Herrero Palacios later simplified it into the trilobed fountain with bronze dolphins by Cristino Mallo.

That is why many Madrileños know the place as the plaza de los delfines. The dolphins were associated with the fountain from the 1960s, but the ensemble had a complicated physical history: it was dismantled for road works and reassembled in 1969. The metro arrived later, when the first section of Line 6 between Cuatro Caminos and Pacífico was inaugurated in October 1979; contemporary press noted that the official ceremony was held at República Argentina station.

The current record should keep the country-based etymology, but correct the local context. The strongest evidence supports: station named after Plaza de la República Argentina; plaza named in honour of Argentina; location in El Viso, Chamartín. The broader Latin-American street-name pattern is relevant background for parts of Chamartín, but it should not be stated as if every nearby street, or this station's barrio, belongs to Hispanoamérica.
