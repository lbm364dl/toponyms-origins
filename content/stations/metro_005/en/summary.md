Goya station is named for Calle de Goya, the street at the Alcalá-Goya junction where the station was built. The street’s official Madrid street-list form, “Calle de Francisco Goya,” makes clear that the honoree is Francisco de Goya y Lucientes, born in Fuendetodos in 1746 and died in Bordeaux in 1828. Goya became painter to the Spanish king in 1786, painter of the Chamber in 1789, and first painter of the Chamber in 1799. The station opened on Line 2 in June 1924; Line 4 reached Goya in 1944, and the earlier Goya-Diego de León branch was absorbed into Line 4 in 1958.
