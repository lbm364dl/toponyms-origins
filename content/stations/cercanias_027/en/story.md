Parla station is named directly after the municipality of Parla. The current Cercanías station stands in the town and functions as a southern head station of Madrid's C-4 services, so the railway name is geographic rather than commemorative: it identifies the place served by the stop.

Behind that simple station name is a debated place-name. The most compact scholarly explanation is the one given by Jairo J. García Sánchez in the Centro Virtual Cervantes: Parla is considered to have been formed from the Roman personal name Parilus, through Latin (villa) Parila, meaning the estate or villa associated with that owner. Emilio Nieto Ballester's Breve diccionario de topónimos españoles is cited in later academic work for the same type of explanation, treating Parla as a possible villa or estate-name.

There is, however, another serious tradition in local historical writing. A Comunidad de Madrid publication following Fernando Jiménez de Gregorio explains Parla from Latin padule and palus, paludis, with the meaning of lagoon, marsh or pond. That argument is tied to the old landscape: the text points to former lagoons, wet ground and nearby water-names such as Las Tablas, Los Barriales, Humanejos and Guatén. This does not disprove the Parilus theory, but it explains why the name has not had only one interpretation in local historiography.

The transport history adds another layer. Renfe's own station information says the C-4 route to Parla was inaugurated in 1989 on part of the old Madrid-Ciudad Real railway, and that the present station opened in 1995, becoming the line's head station. The older railway presence goes back to the nineteenth-century Madrid-Ciudad Real line, whose Parla stop was outside the town; after the modern station was built, the older facilities are reported in railway histories as having been known as Parla-Industrial.

The legendary explanation, in which a mute person drinks from a fountain and begins to parlar, belongs to folklore rather than etymology. It is culturally relevant because it connects the town's name with a local fountain tradition, but linguistically it is a classic folk etymology created from the modern Spanish verb parlar. For public display, the safest wording is that the station is certainly named after the municipality, whose name is probably from the Roman estate-name (villa) Parila, while noting the documented marsh-name alternative.
