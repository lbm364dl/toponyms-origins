Acacias station takes its name from the Acacias place-name around it, especially the Paseo de las Acacias in Arganzuela. The official street directory records the Paseo de las Acacias as a Madrid street running in the district, and the transport authority lists the Metro stop as Acacias within the current network.

Behind the station name is a descriptive street name. In the classic 1889 work *Las calles de Madrid*, Hilario Peñasco and Carlos Cambronero described the Paseo de las Acacias and explained the title by reference to the acacia, a tree used ornamentally in streets and walks. That makes the name botanical rather than commemorative: it is not known to honor a person, battle, saint, or estate owner.

The paseo belongs to the southern approaches of old Madrid, an area shaped by roads, slopes toward the Manzanares, working-class housing, markets, workshops, and later rail and Metro infrastructure. Nearby names such as Ribera de Curtidores and El Rastro recall the older world of slaughtering, tanning, and street trading north of Acacias, although that is context rather than the direct source of the station name.

The Metro station entered service with the first section of Line 5. Metro de Madrid's 1968 memory, quoted by Memoria de Madrid, records the inauguration of the Callao-Carabanchel line on 5 June 1968 and notes its interchange with Line 3 at Embajadores and Callao; Acacias formed part of that new southern route. Today the station is also understood through its long pedestrian connection with Embajadores.

The strongest conclusion is therefore straightforward: Acacias is named for a local street and barrio whose name comes from acacia trees. The broad origin is well supported by official street and transport sources plus nineteenth-century street-name literature. What remains less firmly documented is the exact date or administrative minute by which Metro assigned the station name, and the common claim that the paseo was specifically planted with rows of acacias in the nineteenth century needs a more direct archival source before being stated as fact.
